# Middle School Social Studies Engine — High School Social Studies Adaptation

**Status:** Subject engine foundation implemented for all Grade 6–8 Social Studies week pages.

This engine is adapted from the High School Social Studies architecture, especially its source study, contextualization, corroboration, geography/data reasoning, systems analysis, qualified historical claims, counterclaims, and explicit uncertainty.

## Middle School curriculum authority

The existing Grade 6, Grade 7, and Grade 8 Social Studies week pages remain the curriculum source of truth. The engine does not import Grade 9 historical content, quizzes, assignments, or source packets. Instead, it interprets each existing five-day Middle School week through a consistent inquiry routine:

**Question → Source → Context → Corroborate → Map/Data → Claim → Counterclaim → Qualify**

The five daily stages are:

1. Question & Source
2. Context & Corroborate
3. Map, Data & Systems
4. Claim & Counterclaim
5. Defend, Qualify & Reflect

Each daily lesson retains its original title, focus, objective, activity, workshop, teacher guidance, and evidence target. The engine adds a learner inquiry notebook beneath the existing content.

## Authority boundaries

The Social Studies engine is formative and portfolio-supportive only.

- No official mastery writes.
- No grade writes.
- No placement changes.
- No assessment answer keys or protected items.
- No curriculum unlock authority.
- Learner-scoped inquiry notes are recoverable but remain non-authoritative.
- Official weekly mastery remains owned by the Middle School assessment engine.

## High School architecture carried forward

The adaptation preserves the strongest Social Studies system ideas:

- question-driven inquiry;
- sourcing and contextualization;
- corroboration across evidence;
- geography, chronology, quantitative evidence, and systems reasoning;
- claims proportional to evidence;
- serious counterclaims and alternative explanations;
- explicit uncertainty and missing voices;
- exportable inquiry evidence.

The Middle School implementation is age- and curriculum-adjusted for Grades 6–8 and does not depend on High School runtime files at execution time.
