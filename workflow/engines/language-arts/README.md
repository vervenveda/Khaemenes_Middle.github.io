# Middle School Language Arts Engine — High School English Adaptation

**Status:** Subject engine foundation implemented for all Grade 6–8 Language Arts week pages.

This engine is adapted from the High School English architecture, especially its daily lesson workflow, evidence-centered writing model, learner-scoped draft recovery, reading/evidence custody, revision cycle, and explicit separation between formative work and official mastery.

## Middle School curriculum authority

The existing Grade 6, Grade 7, and Grade 8 Language Arts week pages remain the curriculum source of truth. The engine does not import High School readings, assignments, standards, or assessment items. Instead, it interprets each existing five-day Middle School week through a consistent instructional routine:

**Read → Annotate → Analyze → Draft → Revise → Defend**

The five daily stages are:

1. Read & Prepare
2. Annotate & Analyze
3. Draft & Apply
4. Revise & Defend
5. Publish & Reflect

Each daily lesson retains its original title, focus, objective, activity, workshop, teacher guidance, and evidence target. The engine adds a learner evidence workspace beneath the existing content.

## Authority boundaries

The Language Arts engine is formative and portfolio-supportive only.

- No official mastery writes.
- No grade writes.
- No placement changes.
- No assessment answer keys or protected items.
- No curriculum unlock authority.
- Learner-scoped browser drafts are recoverable but remain non-authoritative.
- Official weekly mastery remains owned by the Middle School assessment engine.

## High School architecture carried forward

The adaptation deliberately preserves the strongest English-system ideas:

- explicit daily instructional flow;
- reading/evidence before interpretation;
- student-authored writing rather than generated replacement prose;
- revision as a required learning act;
- learner-scoped draft recovery;
- exportable evidence;
- privacy and assessment separation;
- portfolio handoff rather than automatic mastery.

The Middle School implementation is age- and curriculum-adjusted for Grades 6–8 and does not depend on High School runtime files at execution time.
