Surface Area, Volume, and Modeling

How can three-dimensional models help solve real-world problems?

Week 10 Mathematical Depth Lab · Day 1: Find a 2×3×4 cm prism volume: V=24 cm³.

Check and revise: Verify the result, show accurate units or probabilities, and explain one possible error. Formative practice only; official mastery remains with the existing assessment engine.

Calculate prism volume and dimensions · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A rectangular prism 5 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm has volume V=5×4×3=60 cm³. Volume counts cubic units.

Misconception checkpoint: Multiplying only two side lengths finds base area, not three-dimensional volume.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find volume of a 6×3×4 cm prism.
  2. Find volume of a 9×2×5 cm prism.
  3. A prism has volume 120 cm³ and base area 20 cm². Find height.
  4. A prism measures 3×4×5 cm; double its height. Find new volume.

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 72 cm³
  2. 90 cm³
  3. 6 cm
  4. 120 cm³

Week 10 Mathematical Depth Lab · Day 2: Calculate the surface area of a 2×3×4 cm prism: 2(6+8+12)=52 cm².

Check and revise: Verify the result, show accurate units or probabilities, and explain one possible error. Formative practice only; official mastery remains with the existing assessment engine.

Unfold prisms to calculate surface area · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: For a closed 4×3×2 cm box, surface area is 2(4×3+4×2+3×2)=52 cm².

Misconception checkpoint: Surface area uses square units and counts all faces, unlike volume.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find surface area of a closed 5×2×3 cm box.
  2. Find surface area of a closed cube side 4 cm.
  3. A lidless 4×3×2 cm box omits the 4×3 top. Find material area.
  4. For a 6×2×3 cm prism, find total area of both 6×2 faces.

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 62 cm²
  2. 96 cm²
  3. 40 cm²
  4. 24 cm²

Model triangular prisms · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A triangular prism with triangular base 6 cm, height 4 cm and length 5 cm has volume (6×4/2)×5=60 cm³.

Misconception checkpoint: For a triangle, the perpendicular height is required; a slant side cannot replace it.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Triangle base 8 cm, height 3 cm, prism length 6 cm: volume?
  2. Triangle area 15 cm², prism length 7 cm: volume?
  3. Triangular prism volume 84 cm³, base area 12 cm²: length?
  4. A triangle base 10 cm, perpendicular height 5 cm: area?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 72 cm³
  2. 105 cm³
  3. 7 cm
  4. 25 cm²

Compare materials and capacity · Mathematics laboratory

Worked mathematical model: A closed 5×4×3 cm box has surface area 94 cm² and capacity 60 cm³. Those quantities measure different needs.

Misconception checkpoint: Two shapes can share volume while requiring different covering material.

Independent reasoning challenges:

Retrieve composite geometry · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A model combines nonoverlapping 2×3×4 and 3×3×3 cm prisms. Total volume is 24+27=51 cm³.

Misconception checkpoint: Add volumes only when parts do not overlap; exposed exterior faces need separate counting for surface area.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find volume of 2×4×5 and 3×3×2 cm prisms combined without overlap.
  2. A 6×4 m floor area: area?
  3. Find surface area of a closed 3×3×3 cm cube.
  4. Find volume of a 4×3×2 cm prism.

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 58 cm³
  2. 24 m²
  3. 54 cm²
  4. 24 cm³
