Week 19 · Mathematics

Proportional Models of Biological Systems

How do systems work together to maintain balance?

Monday: Seminar Launch and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: proportional relationships

Objective: Students strengthen mathematics through proportional relationships while investigating 'Body Systems and Homeostasis'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model middle-school thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Mathematics A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about body systems and homeostasis.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Mathematics. Connect to the essential question: How do systems work together to maintain balance? Model the focus skill: proportional relationships. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Proportional reasoning · Learn and represent

Vocabulary in context: Name the quantities, relationships, domain and units used in the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A constant rate relates two quantities multiplicatively; use multiplication to scale and division to find a unit rate. Today's model uses a=5 and b=4; explain how the example applies to this week's evidence theme.

Misconception checkpoint: An additive pattern alone does not establish proportionality.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. At 5 items per pack, how many in 4 packs?
  2. A set of 20 items fills 4 equal packs. Find each pack size.
  3. At 5 km/h, find distance in 4 h.
  4. At 5 items each, how many packs hold 20 items?

Independent self-check: Recompute using inverse operations, a substitution, an alternative diagram or unit analysis before opening the key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 20 items
  2. 5 items
  3. 20 km
  4. 4 packs
