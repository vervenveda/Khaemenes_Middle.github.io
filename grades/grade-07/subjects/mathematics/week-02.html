Week 02 · Mathematics

Cited Evidence and Proportional Thinking

How do evidence and proportional thinking help us compare complex situations?

Monday: Seminar Launch and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: unit rates and percent

Objective: Students strengthen mathematics through unit rates and percent while investigating 'Cited Evidence and Proportional Thinking'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model middle-school thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Mathematics A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about cited evidence and proportional thinking.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Mathematics. Connect to the essential question: How do evidence and proportional thinking help us compare complex situations? Model the focus skill: unit rates and percent. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Read fractions as percentages · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A sample of 18 out of 24 is 18÷24=0.75, or 75%.

Misconception checkpoint: Percentages compare shares, not raw counts alone.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Convert 9/12 to percent.
  2. Find 15% of 80.
  3. Compare 12/20 with 18/30.
  4. Convert 0.4 to percent.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 75%
  2. 12
  3. equal at 60%
  4. 40%

Calculate percentage change · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: An amount increases from 40 to 50: (50−40)÷40×100=25%.

Misconception checkpoint: Always divide change by the original amount, not the new amount.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Increase from 60 to 75: percent increase?
  2. Decrease from 100 to 80: percent decrease?
  3. Increase from 30 to 36: percent increase?
  4. Find 25% of 200.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 25%
  2. 20%
  3. 20%
  4. 50

Separate percent from percentage points · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A share rises from 20% to 35%: 15 percentage points, a 75% relative increase.

Misconception checkpoint: Percentage points and relative percentage change are different units of comparison.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. From 40% to 55%, find point change.
  2. For 40% to 55%, find relative percent increase.
  3. From 50% to 45%, find point decrease.
  4. For 50% to 45%, find relative percent decrease.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 15 percentage points
  2. 37.5%
  3. 5 percentage points
  4. 10%

Test equivalent evidence · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: 12 of 20 and 18 of 30 each equal 60%; the groups have different sample sizes.

Misconception checkpoint: An equal sample share does not guarantee equal quality or identical underlying populations.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find 14/28 as percent.
  2. Find 27/45 as percent.
  3. Which share is higher: 6/8 or 9/12?
  4. Find 30% of 90.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Apply proportional judgement · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A 20% discount from $50 removes $10, leaving $40.

Misconception checkpoint: Subtracting 20 dollars is not equivalent to a 20 percent discount on every price.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Calculate 10% discount from $80.
  2. Find final $80 price after 10% off.
  3. Find 15% of 120.
  4. Convert 21/35 to percent.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. $8
  2. $72
  3. 18
  4. 60%
