Week 05 · Mathematics

Operations with Rational Numbers

How do rational numbers extend what we can model and explain?

Monday — Integers on a number line

Learn and model: A rational number can be written as a fraction a/b with integers a and b, where b is not zero. This includes negative fractions, integers, and terminating or repeating decimals. Adding +3 and −5 means starting at 3 and moving five units left: 3 + (−5) = −2. Subtraction is addition of the opposite: −4 − (−7) = −4 + 7 = 3. A temperature change from −2°C to 5°C is +7°C.

Practice and evidence: Draw a number line from −8 to 8. Show −3 + 6, 4 − 9, and −5 − (−2). Explain direction and distance for each.

Tuesday — Multiply and divide signed numbers

Learn and model: A product of two negative factors is positive: (−3)(−4)=12; factors with different signs give a negative product: (−3)(4)=−12. The same sign pattern applies to division. Check −18 ÷ 3 = −6 because (−6) × 3 = −18. For fractions, (−2/3) × (9/4) = −18/12 = −3/2. Dividing by a nonzero fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal: (−3/5) ÷ (2/5) = (−3/5) × (5/2) = −3/2.

Practice and evidence: Compute (−7)(−6), −24 ÷ (−8), (−3/4)(8/9), and (5/6) ÷ (−10/9). Show a multiplication check for the last answer.

Wednesday — Solve a real data problem

Learn and model: A fictional hiking team's elevation relative to sea level changes by −3/4 kilometer each hour for two hours. Total change is 2 × (−3/4) = −3/2 kilometers. If the team starts at +1/2 kilometer, its final position is +1/2 − 3/2 = −1 kilometer. A signed value gives direction relative to a reference point, not the length of the traveled path. Distinguish these two quantities.

Practice and evidence: Create a table of starting elevation, signed change, and final elevation for three different starting points. Explain why a negative elevation is not a negative travel distance.

Thursday — Diagnose and correct errors

Learn and model: Error 1: −5 − 3 = 2. Correction: subtracting 3 moves three units left from −5, so the result is −8. Error 2: (−2/3) ÷ (−4/5) = −8/15. Correction: divide by multiplying by the reciprocal, yielding (−2/3) × (−5/4) = 10/12 = 5/6. Multiplication confirms (5/6) × (−4/5) = −2/3. Use inverse operations to check every division.

Practice and evidence: Explain both incorrect approaches in complete sentences. Then calculate −1/2 + 3/4, −3/5 − 1/10, and (−4/7) ÷ (2/3). Identify a plausible error and show how to detect it.

Friday — Independent mastery and reflection

Learn and model: Complete the demonstration without using the examples as an answer key. A: −8 + 13 and −6 − (−11). B: (−3/5)(−10/9) and (−7/8) ÷ (1/4). C: A fictional account has a balance of −$12, receives $25, then pays $8; model each operation and find the ending balance. D: Explain why dividing two negative rational numbers gives a positive quotient.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20 points): accurate models 4; calculations 8; independent application 4; explanation and correction 4. File the corrected work and one reflection in the existing learner portfolio. The established weekly assessment remains separate.
