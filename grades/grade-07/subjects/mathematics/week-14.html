Week 14 · Mathematics

Inequalities, Data Claims and Constraints

How can readers evaluate whether an argument is strong?

Monday — Translate claims into inequalities

Learn and model: An inequality compares quantities using <, >, ≤, or ≥. If a club can spend at most $60, and supplies cost $12 plus $6 per participant, 12+6p≤60. Subtract 12 and divide by 6: p≤8. Since participants are whole people, at most eight can join.

Practice and evidence: Write and solve 5x+10≤45. Explain what the solution means in a realistic example.

Translate quantitative claims to inequalities · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: 'At least 12 entries' translates to n≥12. A filled number-line endpoint includes 12.

Misconception checkpoint: Words fewer than and more than imply strict bounds.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Write at most 20 participants.
  2. Write more than 8 pages.
  3. Write fewer than 6 errors.
  4. Is 20 included in n≤20?

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Tuesday — Solve and represent

Learn and model: Solve −2x<8 by dividing both sides by −2 and reversing the inequality: x>−4. The reversal preserves the relationship when multiplication or division uses a negative number. On a number line, use an open circle at −4 and shade right.

Practice and evidence: Solve 3x−7≥5 and −3x≤12; graph the solutions and check a boundary value.

Solve inequalities and preserve direction · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: For −3x≤12, dividing by negative three reverses the sign: x≥−4.

Misconception checkpoint: Multiplication or division by a negative flips the inequality symbol.

Independent reasoning challenges:

Wednesday — Evaluate a misleading statistic

Learn and model: A fictional advertisement states 'Most students agree' based on 11 responses out of 20. This is 55% among respondents; sample selection and wording determine whether the evidence supports a broader claim. Quantitative reasoning requires denominators.

Practice and evidence: Find the percentage for 17 of 25 and write a careful claim that does not generalize beyond the sample.

Check misleading statistical claims · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: A poll reports 18 of 30 favor a plan: 60%. A claim that 80% favor it conflicts with the sample.

Misconception checkpoint: Sampling method and population matter even when the arithmetic percentage is correct.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Compute 18/30 as a percent.
  2. Compute 9/12 as a percent.
  3. Find difference between 80% and 60% in percentage points.
  4. If 40% of 50 respondents support a proposal, how many?

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

  3. 20 percentage points
  4. 20

Thursday — Work with constraints

Learn and model: A fictional school has a $90 materials budget. A fixed kit costs $18 and each experiment set costs $9. Thus 18+9s≤90 gives s≤8. If sets must be purchased in whole numbers, choose an integer solution and verify the cost.

Practice and evidence: Solve 24+7n≤80. Explain why rounding 8.3 up would violate the constraint.

Model budget and time constraints · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: A $48 budget with $6 fixed charge and $7 per task satisfies 6+7t≤48; whole t≤6.

Misconception checkpoint: A continuous inequality bound is not a license to round up the number of whole tasks.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find largest whole n for 5n+10≤40.
  2. Solve 4x+8<20.
  3. At $12 fixed and $3 per visit with $30 budget, maximum visits?
  4. Does n=7 satisfy 5n+10≤40?

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Friday — Independent defense

Learn and model: Develop an evidence-based recommendation for the maximum number of items possible under a cost limit. Show a variable definition, correct inequality, steps, and a substituted check.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): modeling 6, algebra 6, verification 4, interpretation 4. The existing independent weekly assessment remains unchanged.

Use evidence to defend constraints · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: A policy requiring at least 4 and at most 9 valid records has 4≤n≤9; both endpoints count.
