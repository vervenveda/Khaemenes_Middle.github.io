Week 14 · Mathematics

Informational Text and Argument Analysis

How can readers evaluate whether an argument is strong?

Monday — Translate claims into inequalities

Learn and model: An inequality compares quantities using <, >, ≤, or ≥. If a club can spend at most $60, and supplies cost $12 plus $6 per participant, 12+6p≤60. Subtract 12 and divide by 6: p≤8. Since participants are whole people, at most eight can join.

Practice and evidence: Write and solve 5x+10≤45. Explain what the solution means in a realistic example.

Tuesday — Solve and represent

Learn and model: Solve −2x<8 by dividing both sides by −2 and reversing the inequality: x>−4. The reversal preserves the relationship when multiplication or division uses a negative number. On a number line, use an open circle at −4 and shade right.

Practice and evidence: Solve 3x−7≥5 and −3x≤12; graph the solutions and check a boundary value.

Wednesday — Evaluate a misleading statistic

Learn and model: A fictional advertisement states 'Most students agree' based on 11 responses out of 20. This is 55% among respondents; sample selection and wording determine whether the evidence supports a broader claim. Quantitative reasoning requires denominators.

Practice and evidence: Find the percentage for 17 of 25 and write a careful claim that does not generalize beyond the sample.

Thursday — Work with constraints

Learn and model: A fictional school has a $90 materials budget. A fixed kit costs $18 and each experiment set costs $9. Thus 18+9s≤90 gives s≤8. If sets must be purchased in whole numbers, choose an integer solution and verify the cost.

Practice and evidence: Solve 24+7n≤80. Explain why rounding 8.3 up would violate the constraint.

Friday — Independent defense

Learn and model: Develop an evidence-based recommendation for the maximum number of items possible under a cost limit. Show a variable definition, correct inequality, steps, and a substituted check.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): modeling 6, algebra 6, verification 4, interpretation 4. The existing independent weekly assessment remains unchanged.
