Week 12 · Mathematics

Statistics and Inference

How can samples help us make claims about populations?

Monday: Seminar Launch and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: statistical centers, variability and inference

Objective: Students strengthen mathematics through statistical centers, variability and inference while investigating 'Statistics and Inference'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model middle-school thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Mathematics A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about statistics and inference.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Mathematics. Connect to the essential question: How can samples help us make claims about populations? Model the focus skill: statistical centers, variability and inference. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Describe statistical centers · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: Ordered values 2,4,4,6,14 have mean 6, median 4 and mode 4. These reflect different aspects of the data.

Misconception checkpoint: The mean can be influenced by an outlier, so do not report it as the only typical value.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find mean of 3,5,7,9.
  2. Find median of 1,3,5,7,9.
  3. Find mode of 2,3,3,5,7.
  4. Find mean of 2,2,4,8.

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

  4. 4

Use quartiles and interquartile range · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: For 1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8, median=4.5; Q1=2.5 and Q3=6.5, so IQR=4.

Misconception checkpoint: Quartile conventions should be stated; do not confuse total range with the IQR.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. For 2,4,6,8,10,12,14,16, median?
  2. For that set, lower-half median Q1?
  3. For that set, upper-half median Q3?
  4. Find IQR for that set.

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Judge samples and populations · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A random sample of 100 from a large group with 62 reporting a preference yields sample share 62%, not a guaranteed population share.

Misconception checkpoint: Convenience samples and small samples can introduce bias; a sample statistic is an estimate.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. 45 of 75 respondents: sample percentage?
  2. 18 of 30 respondents: sample percentage?
  3. If 30% of 200 sampled prefer A, how many?
  4. If 24 of 80 prefer B, sample proportion?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

  1. 60%
  2. 60%
  3. 60
  4. 30%

Defend a comparison with center and variability · Mathematics laboratory

Worked mathematical model: Data A: 4,4,4,4 has mean 4, range 0. Data B: 1,3,5,7 has mean 4, range 6; a center alone conceals reliability differences.

Misconception checkpoint: Observed association does not establish causation; describe sampling and variation.

  2. Find range of 1,3,5,7.
  3. Find median of 2,4,6,8.
