Identify proportional relationships · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For 4 notebooks costing $12, divide 12 by 4 to obtain $3 per notebook.

Misconception checkpoint: A relationship is proportional only when its multiplicative rate stays constant and passes through zero.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find the cost per notebook for 5 notebooks costing $15.
  2. At $3 per notebook, price 8 notebooks.
  3. Do pairs (2,6),(4,12),(6,18) show a common cost per item?
  4. What would 0 notebooks cost in a proportional price model?

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. $3
  2. $24
  3. yes, $3 per item
  4. $0

Represent unit rates · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A cyclist travels 42 km in 3 hours; 42÷3=14 km/h.

Misconception checkpoint: Do not reverse the units: 3÷42 is hours per kilometer, not km per hour.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find unit rate for 63 km in 7 hours.
  2. At 9 km/h, find distance in 4 hours.
  3. How many hours for 45 km at 9 km/h?
  4. A journey covers 9 km in 1 hour. What are the units of the distance-per-time rate?

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 9 km/h
  2. 36 km
  3. 5 hours
  4. km/h

Build ratio tables · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: At 5 tickets per group, 2, 3 and 4 groups require 10,15 and 20 tickets.

Misconception checkpoint: Additive increases alone do not prove proportionality; test all ratios.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  2. Fill 7 groups at 5 tickets/group.
  3. Does (2,10),(4,20),(6,30) have common constant 5?
  4. What tickets correspond to 0 groups?

Compare proportional and nonproportional models · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For A=4x and B=4x+6, A(0)=0 while B(0)=6; only A is proportional.

Misconception checkpoint: A flat setup fee creates a nonzero intercept even when rate per item is steady.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. At x=3, compute A=4x.
  2. At x=3, compute B=4x+6.
  3. Which model passes through (0,0)?
  4. Find the unit price slope in A.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

  1. 12
  2. 18
  3. A
  4. 4

Defend proportional reasoning · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Model 6 boxes at 7 items per box as y=7x. Check x=6 yields 42.

Misconception checkpoint: A single matching pair cannot alone establish the full rule; check multiple pairs and the origin.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. At 7 per box, find 9-box total.
  2. A pack of 3 identical items costs $18; what is the cost per item?
  3. At that unit price, find the cost of 5 identical items.
  4. Explain the meaning of 0 in y=7x.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 63
  2. $6
  3. $30
  4. zero boxes produce zero items
