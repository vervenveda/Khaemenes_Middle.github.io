Calculate percent change · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: An increase from 80 to 100 has change 20. Divide by the original 80: 20/80 = 25%.

Misconception checkpoint: Percent change uses the original amount as the denominator, not the final amount.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find percent increase from 50 to 65.
  2. Find percent decrease from 200 to 150.
  3. Find the original 80 increased by 25%.
  4. What is the change in percentage points from 40% to 55%?

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 30%
  2. 25%
  3. 100
  4. 15 percentage points

Compare discounts and tax · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: A $60 item discounted 20% loses $12 and costs $48 before tax.

Misconception checkpoint: Multiplying by 0.20 calculates the discount, not the final sale price.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find 15% discount on $80.
  2. An $80 item has a 15% discount. Find the sale price.
  3. Find 5% tax on a $40 purchase.
  4. Find the final price of a $40 purchase after adding 5% sales tax.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Interpret civic data with denominators · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: A program reaches 18 of 30 people, or 60%, while another reaches 20 of 40, or 50%.

Misconception checkpoint: A larger count alone does not establish a larger proportion or better outcome.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  3. Compare 24/30 with 28/40 as percentages.
  4. Find 25% of 160.

Work backward from a new amount · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: A value rises 25% to 150: divide 150 by 1.25 to recover 120.

Misconception checkpoint: Do not subtract 25% of the new total to reverse a 25% increase.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. A total of 132 follows a 10% rise. Find the original.
  2. An $80 price follows a 20% discount. Find the original.
  3. A value of 75 follows a 25% decrease. Find the original.
  4. Original 120 grows to 150. Find the growth percentage.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 120
  2. $100
  3. 100
  4. 25%

Assess percentage claims · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: An 8-person increase from 32 to 40 is 25%; the numerical claim says nothing about cause.

Misconception checkpoint: Percent changes can be accurate while causal or population conclusions remain unsupported.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find percent increase from 32 to 40.
  2. Find percent decrease from 50 to 40.
  3. Find 18 of 24 as a percentage.
  4. If 60% of 50 respondents agree, how many agree?

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 25%
  2. 20%
  3. 75%
  4. 30
