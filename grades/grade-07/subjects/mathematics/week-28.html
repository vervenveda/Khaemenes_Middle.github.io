Week 28 · Mathematics

Ratios and Trade Network Comparisons

How do trade routes connect geography, culture, and power?

Monday: Seminar Launch and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: proportional relationships

Objective: Students strengthen mathematics through proportional relationships while investigating 'Africa, Asia, and Global Trade'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model middle-school thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Mathematics A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about africa, asia, and global trade.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Mathematics. Connect to the essential question: How do trade routes connect geography, culture, and power? Model the focus skill: proportional relationships. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Ratios and unit rates · Concept and diagrams

Vocabulary in context: Define mathematical symbols, units and constraints in your explanation.

Worked mathematical model: Use multiplication to scale a relationship and division to recover a unit rate. Today's model sets a=2, b=4; show how these values support the week's mathematics.

Misconception checkpoint: Additive patterns alone do not prove proportionality.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. 2 items per package: 4 packages hold how many?
  2. 8 items in 4 equal packs: items per pack?
  3. At 2 km/h, distance in 4 hours?
  4. At 2 per pack, 8 items make how many packs?

Independent self-check: Use an inverse, substitution, alternate diagram or units before revealing solutions.
