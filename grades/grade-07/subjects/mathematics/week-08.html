Week 08 · Mathematics

Scale Drawings and Geometry Design

How can scale help us represent something larger or smaller accurately?

Monday: Seminar Launch and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: scale drawings, proportional lengths and geometric design

Objective: Students strengthen mathematics through scale drawings, proportional lengths and geometric design while investigating 'Scale Drawings and Geometry Design'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model middle-school thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Mathematics A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about scale drawings and geometry design.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Mathematics. Connect to the essential question: How can scale help us represent something larger or smaller accurately? Model the focus skill: scale drawings, proportional lengths and geometric design. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Week 08 Crosswalk Lab · Day 1: A map scale 1 cm : 4 m converts a 6 cm drawing length to 24 m.

Evidence and correction: Demonstrate the concept with labeled units or a clear explanation, test the result, and correct an error. Formative only; official assessment authority is unchanged.

Scale lengths with consistent units · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: At 1 cm:4 m, a 7 cm line represents 28 m. The scale factor must be applied to each length.

Misconception checkpoint: A scale is a ratio of consistent dimensions; do not mix centimeters and meters without stating the conversion.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. At 1 cm:5 m, model 6 cm.
  2. At 1 cm:2 km, model 3.5 cm.
  3. A real 45 m line at 1 cm:5 m uses what drawing length?
  4. Scale a 4 cm segment by factor 3.

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 30 m
  2. 7 km
  3. 9 cm
  4. 12 cm

Apply scale to perimeter and area · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A rectangle 3 by 5 scales by factor 2: perimeter grows from 16 to 32, area from 15 to 60.

Misconception checkpoint: Lengths scale by k; areas scale by k squared, not simply k.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Area 12 cm² scaled by factor 3.
  2. Perimeter 14 cm scaled by factor 4.
  3. Rectangle 2 by 6 scaled by factor 2: new area?
  4. Length 9 scaled by factor 1/3.

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 108 cm²
  2. 56 cm
  3. 48 square units
  4. 3

Find unknown dimensions with proportions · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A 4 cm drawing length models 18 m, so 10 cm models 10×18/4=45 m.

Misconception checkpoint: An additive guess cannot replace proportional multiplicative scale.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. 3 cm models 12 m; 7 cm models?
  2. 5 cm models 20 m; 2 cm models?
  3. An 8 m feature drawn at 1 cm:2 m occupies?
  4. A 15 m feature drawn at 1 cm:3 m occupies?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 28 m
  2. 8 m
  3. 4 cm
  4. 5 cm

Design and critique scale drawings · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A plan 6 by 4 cm at 1 cm:3 m represents 18 by 12 m; modeled area 216 m².

Misconception checkpoint: A plan's area must use squared conversion factors, and it does not prove on-site dimensions.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Drawing 2×5 cm at 1 cm:4 m: modeled lengths?
  2. Find modeled area for that plan.
  3. Drawing 3×3 cm at factor 5: real area?
  4. At factor 2, by what factor does area change?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 8 m and 20 m
  2. 160 m²
  3. 225 square units
  4. 4

Retrieve geometry and ratios · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A scale factor 3 multiplies side lengths by 3 and area by 9; justify with the multiplication model.

Misconception checkpoint: Equal shape does not mean equal size; explain which measures are preserved.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. A 7 cm drawn line at 1 cm:2 m models?
  2. A 5 cm line scaled by 3 becomes?
  3. An area of 20 scales by factor 2: new area?
  4. An original perimeter 18 scaled by factor 3 becomes?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 14 m
  2. 15 cm
  3. 80
  4. 54
