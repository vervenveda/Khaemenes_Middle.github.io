Build a theoretical probability model · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A fair six-sided die has three even outcomes: {2,4,6}. Thus P(even)=3/6=1/2.

Misconception checkpoint: Count all equally likely outcomes, not just the outcomes you notice first.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. P(rolling 5) on a fair six-sided die?
  2. P(rolling even) on a fair die?
  3. P(rolling above 4) on a fair die?
  4. P(rolling below 7) on a fair die?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Compare experimental and theoretical probability · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: If a fair coin lands heads 18 times in 30 flips, experimental P(heads)=18/30=0.6, versus theoretical 0.5.

Misconception checkpoint: Observed frequencies in small samples need not match the theoretical probability exactly.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. 12 heads in 20 flips: experimental P(heads)?
  2. 7 red outcomes in 25 trials: experimental P(red)?
  3. 15 wins in 40 games: experimental win rate?
  4. Theoretical P(tails) for a fair coin?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Analyze compound independent events · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: For two fair coin flips, outcomes HH, HT, TH, TT are equally likely. P(two heads)=1/4.

Misconception checkpoint: Multiplying outcome counts is different from adding chances; list the complete sample space.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. P(H then T) with two fair independent flips?
  2. P(at least one head) in two fair flips?
  3. P(two even die rolls) with independent fair dice?
  4. P(two tails) in two fair flips?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Week 11 Mathematical Depth Lab · Day 4: Critique a game offering a prize on only one of six equal outcomes as a 50% chance.

Examine fairness using expected outcomes · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A game wins $6 with probability 1/4 and otherwise loses $2. Expected net=1/4×6+3/4×(−2)=0.

Misconception checkpoint: A rare big prize does not prove a game is favorable; consider both probabilities and losses.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Game win +$4 at P=1/2, lose −$2 at P=1/2: expected net?
  2. Fair die P(rolling 6)?
  3. Game win +$3 P=1/4, lose −$1 P=3/4: expected net?
  4. Win +$1 P=1/2, lose −$3 P=1/2: expected net?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. +$1
  2. 1/6
  3. $0
  4. −$1

Design and critique probability experiments · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A fair spinner with sectors 2 red and 6 blue yields P(red)=2/8=1/4; use many trials to compare observations.

Misconception checkpoint: A small experiment alone cannot certify a device is biased or determine future outcomes.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Spinner has 3 red of 12 equal sectors: P(red)?
  2. Spinner has 9 blue of 12: P(blue)?
  3. 24 red results in 80 spins: experimental P(red)?
  4. Expected red results for 100 fair spins with P(red)=1/4?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

  4. 25
