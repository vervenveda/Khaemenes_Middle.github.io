Translate phrases into expressions · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: Five boxes hold x items each, plus 9 loose items: total is 5x+9.

Misconception checkpoint: Five times the combined quantity x+9 is 5(x+9), a different expression.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Write seven times n plus four.
  2. Write three groups of x+2.
  3. Evaluate 5x+9 at x=3.
  4. Find coefficient of a in 8a−5.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 7n+4
  2. 3(x+2)
  3. 24
  4. 8

Use distributive properties · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: 4(2x−3)=8x−12. Verify by substitution at x=2: 4 and 4.

Misconception checkpoint: Distribute a factor to every term, including a negative constant.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  2. Expand 2(4y−3).
  3. Expand −2(a+4).
  4. Evaluate 3(x+5) at x=1.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Combine like terms · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: 7x−2x+4−9 simplifies to 5x−5.

Misconception checkpoint: Variables and constants are not like terms; coefficients can combine only for matching variable powers.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Simplify 6a+4a−3.
  2. Simplify 9p−4p+2−7.
  3. Simplify 3(x+2)+2x.
  4. Evaluate 5x−5 for x=4.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Build equivalent expressions · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: 2(x+6)+3x becomes 5x+12; both expressions match for every x.

Misconception checkpoint: Checking one input helps detect errors but does not prove an identity for every input.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Simplify 4(y+2)−y.
  2. Simplify 5a+2−3a+9.
  3. Find value of 2(x+6)+3x at x=2.
  4. Simplify 3(2n−1)−n.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Model and justify a plan · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: Plan A=4n+10, Plan B=6n+2. At n=4, both equal 26.

Misconception checkpoint: An expression comparison must examine the input domain, not only the starting fees.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find A when n=3.
  2. Find B when n=3.
  3. Solve 4n+10=6n+2.
  4. Find A when n=5.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

  2. 20
  3. n=4
  4. 30
