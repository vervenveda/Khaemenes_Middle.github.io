Week 07 · Mathematics

Equations and Inequalities

How does solving an equation or inequality reveal hidden information?

Monday: Seminar Launch and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: solving equations and interpreting inequalities

Objective: Students strengthen mathematics through solving equations and interpreting inequalities while investigating 'Equations and Inequalities'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model middle-school thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Mathematics A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about equations and inequalities.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Mathematics. Connect to the essential question: How does solving an equation or inequality reveal hidden information? Model the focus skill: solving equations and interpreting inequalities. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Week 07 Crosswalk Lab · Day 1: Solve x+7=19 to find x=12; verify by substitution.

Evidence check: Show the model or annotated explanation, verify a claim, and correct one misconception before reflection. Formative work only; the existing assessment retains authority.

Solve one-step and two-step equations · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For 3x+5=26, subtract five from both sides and divide by three: x=7.

Misconception checkpoint: Perform the same inverse operation on both sides; never divide only the variable term.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Solve 4x+3=31.
  2. Solve 5y−8=17.
  3. Solve −2a+6=14.
  4. Check x=7 in 3x+5=26.

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. x=7
  2. y=5
  3. a=−4
  4. 26=26

Represent strict and inclusive inequalities · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For x+4≤11, subtract four to obtain x≤7; mark a closed dot at 7 and shade left.

Misconception checkpoint: A strict boundary is open; ≥ and ≤ include equality and require a closed boundary.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Solve x−6>2.
  2. Solve y+3≤9.
  3. Solve 4n≥20.
  4. Is n=5 a solution of 4n≥20?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. x>8
  2. y≤6
  3. n≥5
  4. yes

Solve inequalities with negative factors · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For −3x<12, divide by −3 and reverse the direction to get x>−4.

Misconception checkpoint: Dividing or multiplying an inequality by a negative reverses its comparison symbol.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Solve −2x≥10.
  2. Solve −4y<8.
  3. Solve −5a≤15.
  4. Test x=−6 in −2x≥10.

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. x≤−5
  2. y>−2
  3. a≥−3
  4. 12≥10; yes

Week 07 Crosswalk Lab · Day 4: Solve x−4>8 as x>12; test boundary values 12 and 13.

Evidence check: Show the model or annotated explanation, verify a claim, and correct one misconception before reflection. Formative work only; the existing assessment retains authority.

Model constraints with whole-number domains · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: With a $50 budget, $8 fixed fee and $6 per visit, 8+6v≤50 gives v≤7 whole nonnegative visits.

Misconception checkpoint: The algebraic interval may include non-whole or negative values that are invalid for counts.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. For $40 and $10 fee plus $5/visit, maximum visits?
  2. Write inequality for $55 with $7 fee and $8/visit.
  3. Solve 7+8v≤55 for v.
  4. What is the least valid visit count?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 6 visits
  2. 7+8v≤55
  3. v≤6
  4. 0

Retrieve and verify equation reasoning · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For 5(x−2)=25, divide by five to get x−2=5, so x=7. Substitute to check.

Misconception checkpoint: Solving is not complete until the original equation or inequality confirms the answer.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Solve 2x+9=25.
  2. Solve −3x≥12.
  3. Solve 3(n+2)=21.
  4. Test n=5 in 3(n+2)=21.

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. x=8
  2. x≤−4
  3. n=5
  4. 21=21
