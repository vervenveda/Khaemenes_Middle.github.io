Week 05 · Language Arts & Literacy

Operations with Rational Numbers

How do rational numbers extend what we can model and explain?

Read · Check Sources · Explain · Revise · Defend

Investigate three Academy-original documents about a fictional signed-number observation log. Practice distinguishing observations, calculations, independent corroboration and unsupported explanations.

Source credibility Signed-number integrity Provenance and method Revision and counterclaim

Monday · Read a primary-source observation log

Learning target: Identify creator, context, measurement sequence, and the limits of a signed-number record.

Start with the original observation log
Academy-original primary-style text · Cedar Hall Display Log

During a fictional classroom weather-display exercise, three student reporters copied readings from a demonstration thermometer. Their notebook listed -2.5°C at 8 a.m., +1.5°C at noon, and -0.5°C at 6 p.m. The reporters identified the instrument as a classroom demonstration tool; they did not record calibration information or claim the readings described outdoor conditions.

At the next editorial meeting, Noor pointed to the minus sign in the first entry. “That sign is part of the information,” she said. “If we drop it, we're writing a different number.” A student wondered whether the noon reading was four degrees warmer than the first reading. Noor drew a number line to check the movement from -2.5 to +1.5.

The club wanted to describe the sequence precisely but avoid claiming why it changed. Their log did not include a complete record of room conditions, equipment adjustments or measurement uncertainty. The students decided their article should state what the fictional notes said and clearly distinguish calculations from explanations.

Source: Academy-original fictional observation log for Grade 7 English Week 05. No readings are real weather or safety data.

Worked example: Source check: The log is more useful than a secondhand headline for identifying its three recorded values. From -2.5°C to +1.5°C is an increase of 4.0°C. The log alone cannot establish what caused that change.

Student practice
  1. Identify the recorded source's creator, purpose, units and three times in a concise citation note.
  2. Place -2.5, +1.5 and -0.5 on a labeled number line and explain which is greatest.
  3. Cite two different sentences showing the difference between observation and interpretation.
  4. List two missing details that would affect confidence in claims about the cause of the change.

Today's portfolio evidence: Primary-source annotation, signed-number timeline and two notes about limitations.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Keep this a source-credibility reading activity. Values are invented. Ask students to distinguish a recorded number from a justified causal inference.

Tuesday · Compare a headline with the original source

Learning target: Explain how incorrect signs and incomplete units distort a public-facing claim.

Expand source, model and active practice
Academy-original social notice · Temperature Rose by Only One?

“The Cedar Hall temperature display showed -2.5 in the morning and +1.5 at noon. That's a rise of only one degree because 2.5 minus 1.5 equals one. The equipment definitely warmed the room by that amount.”

Academy-original editor reply · Keep the Signs

“The original log lists degrees Celsius and includes a negative morning value. To describe the change from -2.5°C to +1.5°C, subtract -2.5 from +1.5. The recorded increase is 4.0°C, not 1°C. We cannot tell from the log why the values differed.”

Both notices are fictional. A source can reproduce some correct values while giving a misleading interpretation, incorrect calculation or unjustified causal explanation.

Worked example: Worked contrast: The original shows +1.5 - (-2.5) = +4.0°C. The first notice ignores the minus sign and adds a causal explanation not observed in the source. The corrected reply preserves the units and the claim's scope.

Student practice
  1. Underline the numerical mistake, missing unit and causal overclaim in the misleading notice.
  2. Write a source-accurate two-sentence correction with the full +4.0°C change.
  3. Explain why +1.5°C to -0.5°C is a decrease of 2.0°C, not an increase.
  4. Evaluate which source is best for the original values and which is best for recognizing an error; explain why.

Today's portfolio evidence: Annotated misinformation notice, mathematical correction and a two-source credibility comparison.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Teach that a first-hand document is useful for what it records, not automatically reliable for every causal explanation. Correct the text without ridiculing the writer.

Wednesday · Rank evidence and examine provenance

Learning target: Use a credibility checklist and distinguish independent evidence from copied claims.

Expand source, model and active practice
Academy-original informational note · Three Ways a Number Travels

A fictional school newsletter received three documents: a dated log with readings and units, a photograph caption that copied the log's first two numbers, and an unsigned online post stating that “everyone agrees” the demonstration room was warmer all day. The caption did not document the third reading, while the post named no observer or instrument.

The dated log can be checked for consistency within its own entries. The caption may be helpful for understanding what was shared, but it is not an independent confirmation of a value it merely repeats. The unsigned post offers a broad claim with no evidence of who agreed or what “all day” means.

A cautious editor asks: Who created the record? When and how was the value gathered? Can the original be inspected? Does the author distinguish observation, calculation and explanation? Does another document provide independent evidence, or only copy the first source?

Source: Khaemenes Academy original fictional media-literacy note.

Worked example: Credibility model: A copied caption does not double the amount of independent evidence. “All day” cannot be inferred from three fictional times, especially when the reported values cross below and above zero. State what the document directly shows.

Student practice
  1. Create a three-row credibility chart for the dated log, copied caption and unsigned post.
  2. Identify two features that make the log traceable and two limitations it still has.
  3. Explain why repeated information is not always independent corroboration.
  4. Write a 90–120-word editor note separating directly observed data, arithmetic and possible explanations.

Today's portfolio evidence: Provenance matrix, an independence-of-evidence check and a 90–120-word source evaluation.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Credibility is graded by relevance, provenance and method, not by visual polish or whether a source's claim aligns with the student's initial opinion.

Thursday · Write a sourced explanation with a counterclaim

Learning target: Compose a precise argument that responds to a misleading interpretation without inventing causes.

Expand source, model and active practice
Academy-original writing prompt · Publish the Cedar Hall Correction

The student newsletter wants to explain the fictional sequence -2.5°C → +1.5°C → -0.5°C. It also wants readers to understand why the first public notice was misleading and what cannot be concluded from an incomplete demonstration log. Use the primary-style record, the competing notices and the provenance note.

A competent writer names each source and clearly attributes measurements, computations and interpretation. A reasonable argument might favor publishing a corrected sequence alongside a request for clearer measurement documentation.

Worked example: Revision example: Weak: “The room warmed all day because of the device.” Stronger: “The fictional log's value rose 4.0°C between 8 a.m. and noon, then fell 2.0°C by 6 p.m. Those three observations do not establish what caused either change (Cedar Hall Display Log, paragraph 1).”

Student practice
  1. Draft 210–290 original words with an explanatory claim and three precise source notes.
  2. Explain both signed-number changes with units and a carefully qualified description of the observed sequence.
  3. Include the strongest plausible objection, such as concern over the log's missing calibration details, and respond fairly.
  4. Revise one numerical error, one overconfident causal statement and one unclear transition; save before/after versions.

Today's portfolio evidence: A 210–290-word source-based correction, fair counterclaim, and three documented revisions.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Students can acknowledge that a source is incomplete while correctly interpreting its recorded entries. No score is transferred to official Math or English assessment records.

Friday · Defend a credible account and preserve revisions

Learning target: Independently audit a claim, select the best support, and document improvements.

Expand source, model and active practice
Academy-original independent challenge · The Viral Summary

“The school proved its thermometer caused a steady four-degree rise all day. Multiple independent sources confirm it, and the negative sign in the first reading does not matter.”

The claim is fictional and inaccurate on several grounds: it implies causation, ignores the later decrease, treats copied material as independent corroboration, and dismisses sign information.

Worked example: Evidence-based correction: The demonstration log records -2.5°C, +1.5°C, then -0.5°C. The first change is +4.0°C, the second -2.0°C. The sources do not establish a cause, independent confirmation or an all-day rise.

Student practice
  1. Identify four separate unsupported statements in the fictional viral summary.
  2. Rewrite the summary using all three data points, correct signs and explicit source limitations.
  3. Present a 60-second defense of your revised Thursday explanation, answering a question about provenance.
  4. File the original and revised essay, source matrix and a four-sentence reflection on what makes a claim trustworthy.

Today's portfolio evidence: Corrected public summary, evidence chart, revised writing and reflection. Teacher-coaching rubric /20: source provenance 4, signed-number and unit integrity 6, reasoning 4, counterclaim 3, revision 3. Not an official course grade.
