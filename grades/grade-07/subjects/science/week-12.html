Week 12 · Science & Engineering

Statistics and Inference

How can samples help us make claims about populations?

Monday — Variation within populations

Learn and model: Individuals within a population vary. Fictional seedling heights (cm): 8, 9, 9, 10, 14. The mean is 10 cm and median is 9 cm; an unusual high value can affect the mean.

Practice and evidence: Calculate mean, median and range and describe what each conveys.

Week 12 Evidence Crosswalk · Day 1 — Describe variation quantitatively

Student investigation: Calculate the mean, median and range of fictional seedling heights 8, 9, 9, 10, 14 cm. Explain the effect of the tallest seedling.

Worked teacher check: The values total 50 cm; mean 10 cm, median 9 cm, range 14−8 = 6 cm. The highest value raises the mean above the median.

Evidence product: Calculation sheet with units and a claim about which measure best describes a typical seedling.

This is formative instructional evidence. The existing weekly assessment and learner grades remain unchanged.

Tuesday — Sampling bias

Learn and model: Measuring only the tallest seedlings would distort a statement about all seedlings. Randomly selected observations can reduce some forms of selection bias, but small samples still vary.

Practice and evidence: Design a fair sampling method for 20 plants across four garden rows; note one limitation.

Week 12 Evidence Crosswalk · Day 2 — Plan an unbiased sampling method

Student investigation: A garden has four rows with five plants per row. Select a fair sample of eight plants with two randomly chosen labels per row; explain what this method does and does not guarantee.

Worked teacher check: Selecting plants with a random-number table from each row avoids choosing only tall plants, but sampling uncertainty and differing row conditions remain.

Evidence product: A reproducible sampling protocol, example selected IDs and one limitation.

This is formative instructional evidence. The existing weekly assessment and learner grades remain unchanged.

Wednesday — Graph the evidence

Learn and model: A dot plot shows repeated values and unusually high observations. A plot needs units, labels and clear scales so peers can check its conclusions.

Practice and evidence: Create a dot plot of the fictional heights and compare it with a misleading graph lacking its axis labels.

Week 12 Evidence Crosswalk · Day 3 — Build and critique dot plots

Student investigation: Create a dot plot for 8, 9, 9, 10, 14 cm using a horizontal centimeter scale and compare it with a plot that omits units.

Worked teacher check: The correctly labeled plot has one dot at 8, two at 9, one at 10 and one at 14; without units or scale the evidence cannot be checked.

Evidence product: Accurate dot plot with axis title and a critique of one intentionally misleading alternative.

This is formative instructional evidence. The existing weekly assessment and learner grades remain unchanged.

Thursday — Separate explanation from evidence

Learn and model: A height difference may reflect variation in light, soil, water, inherited traits or measurement. Correlation alone cannot identify a cause.

Practice and evidence: Write a claim-evidence-reasoning response and outline a controlled comparison that tests one possible factor.

Week 12 Evidence Crosswalk · Day 4 — Test competing causal explanations

Student investigation: Claim: seedlings grew taller only because they received more water. Identify light, soil, seed variety and baseline height as possible alternatives; propose a controlled experiment.

Worked teacher check: Compare randomly assigned groups with the same seed variety, soil, light and measurement schedule while varying only a predefined water level. Report uncertainty.

Evidence product: A claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph and controlled-study design including a control group.

This is formative instructional evidence. The existing weekly assessment and learner grades remain unchanged.

Friday — Independent investigation design

Learn and model: Design a safe paper-based study describing a research question, sample selection, measurements, predicted pattern and a limitation; do not claim simulated results are real observations.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): fair method 6, calculations 5, evidence reasoning 5, limitations 4. Assessment scores are handled only by the existing engine.

Week 12 Evidence Crosswalk · Day 5 — Design and defend a paper investigation

Student investigation: Create a plan to test whether water volume changes seedling height: sample, control, fixed variables, measurement period, data table and anticipated limitations.

Worked teacher check: A defensible design specifies identical species and light, randomly assigned watering groups, repeated height measurements and no invented outcomes.

Evidence product: Study protocol, blank data table, safety considerations and a 20-point self-evaluation using the existing practice rubric.

This is formative instructional evidence. The existing weekly assessment and learner grades remain unchanged.
