Week 12 · Science & Engineering

Statistics and Inference

How can samples help us make claims about populations?

Monday — Variation within populations

Learn and model: Individuals within a population vary. Fictional seedling heights (cm): 8, 9, 9, 10, 14. The mean is 10 cm and median is 9 cm; an unusual high value can affect the mean.

Practice and evidence: Calculate mean, median and range and describe what each conveys.

Tuesday — Sampling bias

Learn and model: Measuring only the tallest seedlings would distort a statement about all seedlings. Randomly selected observations can reduce some forms of selection bias, but small samples still vary.

Practice and evidence: Design a fair sampling method for 20 plants across four garden rows; note one limitation.

Wednesday — Graph the evidence

Learn and model: A dot plot shows repeated values and unusually high observations. A plot needs units, labels and clear scales so peers can check its conclusions.

Practice and evidence: Create a dot plot of the fictional heights and compare it with a misleading graph lacking its axis labels.

Thursday — Separate explanation from evidence

Learn and model: A height difference may reflect variation in light, soil, water, inherited traits or measurement. Correlation alone cannot identify a cause.

Practice and evidence: Write a claim-evidence-reasoning response and outline a controlled comparison that tests one possible factor.

Friday — Independent investigation design

Learn and model: Design a safe paper-based study describing a research question, sample selection, measurements, predicted pattern and a limitation; do not claim simulated results are real observations.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): fair method 6, calculations 5, evidence reasoning 5, limitations 4. Assessment scores are handled only by the existing engine.
