Week 13 · Social Studies, Civics & History

Literary Theme, Perspective, and Modern Connections

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Rights versus responsibilities

Learn and model: Constitutional rights limit certain government actions; civic responsibilities include informed participation and respectful treatment of others. Rights are not identical to privileges or informal expectations.

Practice and evidence: Classify five invented community scenarios as rights questions, responsibilities, or both; explain each choice.

Tuesday — Study a primary text

Learn and model: The First Amendment begins, 'Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof'. This excerpt is a primary text; legal application depends on additional law and context.

Practice and evidence: Paraphrase the excerpt, name its source and explain why an excerpt alone cannot resolve every dispute.

Wednesday — Compare perspectives

Learn and model: A fictional town debates permitting a demonstration in a public square. Different stakeholders may emphasize expression, access, safety and equal treatment. Constitutional analysis requires facts and applicable legal standards, not slogans.

Practice and evidence: Draft a stakeholder chart showing at least three perspectives and the information needed before reaching a conclusion.

Thursday — Test a claim

Learn and model: The statement 'Rights never have limitations' is too absolute. Legal rights can involve carefully defined boundaries, and the precise standard varies by right and circumstance.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite the claim with qualifications and propose two research questions to verify any specific legal assertion.

Friday — Independent civic argument

Learn and model: Write a 200-word balanced analysis of the fictional town dispute, accurately identifying competing interests and the difference between a personal preference and a legal conclusion.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): source interpretation 5, nuance 5, evidence 6, clarity 4; preserve weekly assessment.
