Week 14 · Social Studies, Civics & History

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Identify a historical claim

Learn and model: During U.S. westward expansion, government policy, migration, economics, and resistance intersected. Assertions that expansion helped 'everyone' erase the experiences of Indigenous nations and other affected communities. Analyze who speaks, who is omitted, and what period a claim describes.

Practice and evidence: Annotate this explicitly invented textbook claim: 'Expansion benefited all residents equally.' List groups whose experiences would need independent evidence.

Tuesday — Contextualize a genuine primary source

Learn and model: The Declaration of Independence states that 'all men are created equal.' This historical statement can be examined alongside laws, institutions, and exclusions of its era and later periods. A document's ideals and their implementation are different historical questions.

Practice and evidence: State the quoted principle, identify a historical contradiction, and name a primary-source category that could reveal affected people's experiences.

Wednesday — Compare reform arguments

Learn and model: Abolitionists, women's-rights advocates, Indigenous leaders, workers, and other reformers challenged institutions through differing goals and strategies. Their movements cannot be reduced to one timeline or single shared position. Source authorship, intended audience, and the specific year matter.

Practice and evidence: Create a comparison grid for two reform efforts covering goals, tactics, opposition, and evidence needed; distinguish broad context from claims needing verification.

Thursday — Evaluate an image and map

Learn and model: Maps can depict territorial boundaries while hiding who lived on the land and how boundaries were imposed. An unlabeled map is not sufficient evidence of consent or demographic change. Examine dates, legend, scale, authorship, and excluded perspectives.

Practice and evidence: Draft a map critique describing four labels or source notes required before the map could support a historical conclusion.

Friday — Evidence-based history defense

Learn and model: Write 200–250 words assessing whether historical expansion should be judged solely by economic growth. Include consequences for at least two groups and clearly separate moral evaluation from historical evidence.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): context 5, multiple perspectives 5, reasoning 6, source discipline 4. The existing independent weekly assessment remains unchanged.
