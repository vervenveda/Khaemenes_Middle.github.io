Week 13 · World Languages & Global Culture

Literary Theme, Perspective, and Modern Connections

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Grammar and meaning

Learn and model: Spanish adjective agreement: libro nuevo (masculine singular) and libros nuevos (masculine plural). Agreement provides grammatical information; word-for-word translation alone can lose structure.

Practice and evidence: Write four original noun–adjective pairs in Spanish, mark number and gender, and check agreement.

Tuesday — Compare narrative viewpoints

Learn and model: Original fictional exchange: 'Ana: Leo historias antiguas. / Luis: Prefiero historias modernas.' Translation: Ana reads old stories; Luis prefers modern stories. These are preferences, not claims about whole cultures.

Practice and evidence: Translate the dialogue and write two new sentences using prefiero and a plural noun.

Wednesday — Express time and context

Learn and model: Ayer means yesterday; hoy means today; mañana can mean tomorrow or morning depending on context. The sentence 'Mañana leo' needs context to distinguish a habitual intention from a precise scheduled event.

Practice and evidence: Write a three-line bilingual plan with clear day/time labels, then explain a potential ambiguity.

Thursday — Revise for audience

Learn and model: A responsible translation records original wording, intended audience, and any unresolved ambiguity. Cultural conventions vary within communities and do not have a single universal rule.

Practice and evidence: Prepare two versions of a polite library notice; identify which expressions need verification.

Friday — Independent language portfolio

Learn and model: Create a six-line original dialogue comparing reading preferences across generations; annotate noun agreement, contextual meaning and respectful phrasing.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): grammar 6, meaning 6, independent expression 4, revision 4. Existing assessment unchanged.
