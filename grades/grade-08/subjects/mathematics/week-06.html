Represent powers and products · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: 10³=1000; in 3×10⁴, coefficient 3 scales 10000 to 30000.

Misconception checkpoint: The exponent applies to the base 10, not as a multiplier of 10.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find 10⁵.
  2. Find 4×10³.
  3. Write 7000 in scientific notation.
  4. Find 10⁻².

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Misconception checkpoint: Scientific notation coefficient cannot be 52 for a nonzero normalized value.

Multiply and divide powers · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: (3×10⁴)(2×10²)=6×10⁶; add exponents when multiplying equal bases.

Misconception checkpoint: Exponent sums apply to multiplication; addition of scientific-notation values requires place alignment.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Compute 10³×10⁴.
  2. Compute 10⁶÷10².
  3. Compute (2×10³)(3×10²).
  4. Compute 8×10⁵ ÷ (2×10²).

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 10⁷
  2. 10⁴
  3. 6×10⁵
  4. 4×10³

Compare scale across magnitudes · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: 6×10⁻³=0.006 and 2×10⁻²=0.02, so 0.02 is larger.

Misconception checkpoint: A more negative exponent means a smaller positive power of ten, not a larger one.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Which is larger: 3×10⁻⁴ or 2×10⁻³?
  2. Write 5×10⁻² as decimal.
  3. Compare 4×10⁵ with 2×10⁶: which is larger?
  4. Write 0.00008 in scientific notation.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

  1. 2×10⁻³
  2. 0.05
  3. 2×10⁶
  4. 8×10⁻⁵

Defend scientific-notation models · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the key operations, units and variables in the worked example; explain their mathematical meaning.

Worked mathematical model: A quantity of 2.5×10⁶ multiplied by 4 becomes 1×10⁷ after normalization.

Misconception checkpoint: Scientific notation does not establish that hypothetical measurements are accurate.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Calculate 2×10⁴ + 3×10⁴.
  2. Calculate 5×10⁶ − 2×10⁶.
  3. Calculate 1.5×10³ × 2.
  4. Write 0.00032 in scientific notation.

Independent self-check: Verify the result by substitution, inverse operations or a second mathematical representation before viewing the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 5×10⁴
  2. 3×10⁶
  3. 3×10³
  4. 3.2×10⁻⁴
