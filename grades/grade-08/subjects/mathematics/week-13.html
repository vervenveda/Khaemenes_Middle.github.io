Week 13 · Mathematics: Algebra I Readiness

Systems of Equations and Decision Models

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Model two conditions

Learn and model: A system consists of equations that must both hold. If x+y=11 and x−y=3, adding gives 2x=14, hence x=7 and y=4. Verify in both equations.

Practice and evidence: Solve x+y=15 and x−y=5, then check.

Represent simultaneous constraints · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: A system x+y=14 and x−y=2 yields x=8,y=6 by addition.

Misconception checkpoint: A pair solving only one of two equations is not a system solution.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. For x+y=11 and x−y=3, find x.
  2. For x+y=11 and x−y=3, find y.
  3. Check 7+4.
  4. Check 7−4.

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Tuesday — Use substitution

Learn and model: Given y=2x+3 and x+y=15, substitute: 3x+3=15, so x=4, y=11. Substitution makes the shared solution explicit.

Practice and evidence: Solve y=3x−1 and x+y=11; show both checks.

Apply substitution to systems · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: For y=2x+1 and x+y=13: x+2x+1=13, x=4,y=9.

Misconception checkpoint: Substitution replaces an entire variable expression, not just one coefficient.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. For y=x+3 and x+y=13, find x.
  2. For y=x+3 and x+y=13, find y.
  3. For y=3x and x+y=16, find x.
  4. For y=3x and x+y=16, find y.

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Wednesday — Interpret a fictional decision

Learn and model: An invented club compares plan A=$10+$5 per visit with B=$22+$3 per visit. Set 10+5v=22+3v, giving v=6; both cost $40. At other visit counts the preferred option may differ.

Practice and evidence: Create a table for 2, 6 and 10 visits and explain the crossover.

Interpret fictional choice models · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: Plans A=3n+10 and B=5n+2 meet when n=4 and both total 22 units.

Misconception checkpoint: Equal cost at one count does not mean the two plans are always equal.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. At n=2, find A=3n+10.
  2. At n=2, find B=5n+2.
  3. Solve 3n+10=5n+2.
  4. Find common cost at n=4.

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Thursday — Challenge an incorrect answer

Learn and model: A student reports (5,6) for x+y=11 and x−y=3. The first equation works, but the second equals −1. Checking each original equation detects the error.

Practice and evidence: Find the actual solution and explain the student's mistake.

Detect a false system solution · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: For x+y=10 and x−y=4, pair (8,2) satisfies first but fails second; actual pair is (7,3).

Misconception checkpoint: A solution must be substituted into both original equations.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Solve x+y=12, x−y=6 for x.
  2. For x+y=12 and x−y=6, find y.
  3. Does (8,4) satisfy x−y=6?
  4. Compute 9−3.

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Friday — Independent transfer

Learn and model: A fictional school sells $4 tickets and $7 tickets, 18 total for $99. Let a+b=18 and 4a+7b=99; solve for both quantities.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): equations 6, solution 6, verification 4, explanation 4. Keep existing weekly assessment separate.

Defend independent system transfer · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: Eliminate y in x+y=15 and x−y=5 to get 2x=20, x=10 and y=5.

Misconception checkpoint: The steps of elimination require correct signs and preservation of equality.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. For x+y=17 and x−y=1, find x.
  2. For x+y=17 and x−y=1, find y.
  3. For 2x+y=15 and x−y=3, find x.
  4. For 2x+y=15 and x−y=3, find y.
