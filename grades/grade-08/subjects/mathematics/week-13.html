Week 13 · Mathematics: Algebra I Readiness

Literary Theme, Perspective, and Modern Connections

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Model two conditions

Learn and model: A system consists of equations that must both hold. If x+y=11 and x−y=3, adding gives 2x=14, hence x=7 and y=4. Verify in both equations.

Practice and evidence: Solve x+y=15 and x−y=5, then check.

Tuesday — Use substitution

Learn and model: Given y=2x+3 and x+y=15, substitute: 3x+3=15, so x=4, y=11. Substitution makes the shared solution explicit.

Practice and evidence: Solve y=3x−1 and x+y=11; show both checks.

Wednesday — Interpret a fictional decision

Learn and model: An invented club compares plan A=$10+$5 per visit with B=$22+$3 per visit. Set 10+5v=22+3v, giving v=6; both cost $40. At other visit counts the preferred option may differ.

Practice and evidence: Create a table for 2, 6 and 10 visits and explain the crossover.

Thursday — Challenge an incorrect answer

Learn and model: A student reports (5,6) for x+y=11 and x−y=3. The first equation works, but the second equals −1. Checking each original equation detects the error.

Practice and evidence: Find the actual solution and explain the student's mistake.

Friday — Independent transfer

Learn and model: A fictional school sells $4 tickets and $7 tickets, 18 total for $99. Let a+b=18 and 4a+7b=99; solve for both quantities.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): equations 6, solution 6, verification 4, explanation 4. Keep existing weekly assessment separate.
