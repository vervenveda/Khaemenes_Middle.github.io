Calculate cylinder volume · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 5 cm has volume π×3²×5=45π cm³.

Misconception checkpoint: Using the diameter in place of radius multiplies the volume by four.

Independent reasoning challenges:

Calculate cone volume and compare · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A cone of radius 3 and height 9 has volume (π×3²×9)/3=27π, one third of the matching cylinder.

Misconception checkpoint: Cone volume includes the factor 1/3; it is not equal to cylinder volume for matching base and height.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Cone r=3, h=6: volume?
  2. Cone r=2, h=9: volume?
  3. Matching cylinder volume 60π: cone volume?
  4. Cone r=4, h=3: volume?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Calculate sphere volume · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A sphere radius 3 has volume 4π×3³/3=36π cubic units.

Misconception checkpoint: Do not use 4πr², which calculates a sphere's surface area, not volume.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Sphere r=3: volume?
  2. Sphere r=6: volume?
  3. Sphere r=1.5: volume?
  4. Sphere r=2: surface area?

Week 10 Mathematical Depth Lab · Day 4: Correct a formula confusing cylinder volume πr²h with surface area.

Compare scaling in three dimensions · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: Doubling every length multiplies volume by 2³=8 while surface area multiplies by 2²=4.

Misconception checkpoint: A scale factor applies cubically to volume, not linearly.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Volume 12 enlarged by factor 2: new volume?
  2. Surface area 15 enlarged by factor 3: new area?
  3. Sphere radius 3 enlarged to 6: volume factor?
  4. Cube side 2 enlarged to 6: volume factor?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.
