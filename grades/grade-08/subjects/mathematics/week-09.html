Week 09 Crosswalk Lab · Day 1: Use a 3-4-5 right triangle and verify 3² + 4² = 5².

Evidence check: Show a labeled model or argument, verify the reasoning and correct one likely misconception. This is formative work and does not alter official assessment scores.

Use the Pythagorean theorem · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For legs 6 and 8, hypotenuse c satisfies c²=36+64=100, so c=10.

Misconception checkpoint: The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and must be the longest side.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Legs 3 and 4: hypotenuse?
  2. Legs 5 and 12: hypotenuse?
  3. Legs 8 and 15: hypotenuse?
  4. Legs 7 and 24: hypotenuse?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Week 09 Crosswalk Lab · Day 2: Find a rectangle diagonal for sides 5 and 12: √(25+144) = 13.

Evidence check: Show a labeled model or argument, verify the reasoning and correct one likely misconception. This is formative work and does not alter official assessment scores.

Find an unknown leg · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: If c=13 and a=5, b²=169−25=144, so b=12.

Misconception checkpoint: Finding a leg uses subtraction of squares, unlike finding the hypotenuse.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Hypotenuse 10, leg 6: other leg?
  2. Hypotenuse 17, leg 8: other leg?
  3. Hypotenuse 25, leg 7: other leg?
  4. Hypotenuse 5, leg 3: other leg?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Week 09 Crosswalk Lab · Day 3: Find distance from (1,2) to (4,6): horizontal change 3, vertical change 4, distance 5.

Evidence check: Show a labeled model or argument, verify the reasoning and correct one likely misconception. This is formative work and does not alter official assessment scores.

Check right-triangle claims · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Sides 9,12,15 form a right triangle because 9²+12²=15².

Misconception checkpoint: The largest side is the hypotenuse candidate; not every three positive lengths make a right triangle.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Do 5,12,13 make a right triangle?
  2. Do 6,7,8 make a right triangle?
  3. Is 8,15,17 a right triangle?
  4. For 3,4,6: right triangle?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Week 09 Crosswalk Lab · Day 4: Correct a false claim that a² + b² = c instead of c².

Evidence check: Show a labeled model or argument, verify the reasoning and correct one likely misconception. This is formative work and does not alter official assessment scores.

Find distance on a coordinate grid · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Between (1,2) and (4,6), changes are 3 and 4, so distance is √(3²+4²)=5.

Misconception checkpoint: Coordinate distance is not always the horizontal plus vertical walking route.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Distance between (0,0) and (6,8)?
  2. Distance between (−2,1) and (1,5)?
  3. Distance between (3,3) and (3,10)?
  4. Distance between (1,1) and (4,5)?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Week 09 Crosswalk Lab · Day 5: Design a shortest straight-path calculation with labeled right-triangle legs and units.

Evidence check: Show a labeled model or argument, verify the reasoning and correct one likely misconception. This is formative work and does not alter official assessment scores.

Connect theorems to design · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A rectangle 9 by 12 has diagonal 15, since 9²+12²=225.

Misconception checkpoint: A geometric model must state its assumptions; a shortest modeled line is not automatically a real travel route.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Diagonal of rectangle 5 by 12?
  2. Diagonal of rectangle 8 by 15?
  3. Hypotenuse for legs 9 and 40?
  4. For (0,0) to (9,12), coordinate distance?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.
