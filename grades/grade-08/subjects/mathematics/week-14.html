Week 14 · Mathematics: Algebra I Readiness

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Read numbers behind a claim

Learn and model: An invented advertisement says a community project grew from 200 to 300 members, 'a 100% increase.' The increase is 100 members, or 100/200=50%. Percent change is (new−old)/old ×100% when the old value is nonzero.

Practice and evidence: Check a change from 80 to 100 and from 120 to 90. State the baseline and whether each is an increase or decrease.

Tuesday — Scientific notation and scale

Learn and model: Scientific notation writes a nonzero number as a×10^n with 1≤|a|<10. Thus 3,600,000=3.6×10^6 and 0.00045=4.5×10^−4. A chart can manipulate impressions by shifting scales even when its arithmetic is correct.

Practice and evidence: Convert 72,000,000 and 0.0000062; explain the error in writing 72×10^6 as normalized scientific notation.

Wednesday — Test a quantitative headline

Learn and model: A fictional report compares 4.0×10^5 units with 5.0×10^5 units. The difference is 1.0×10^5, a 25% increase relative to 4.0×10^5. A baseline must be specified; '25% more' without a reference is ambiguous.

Practice and evidence: Create a table with original, new, absolute change, and percent change for two invented examples. Explain one way a cropped axis could exaggerate a result.

Thursday — Analyze errors and units

Learn and model: The claim '0.003×10^4 = 3×10^4' is false: the left side equals 30, or 3×10^1. Exponent rules require shifting the decimal and adjusting the power together. Always inspect units, rounding, and whether quantities are comparable.

Practice and evidence: Correct 45×10^3, 0.08×10^−2, and 6×10^3 divided by 2×10^2. Check your results in ordinary notation.

Friday — Independent data defense

Learn and model: Evaluate this invented claim: visits rose from 2.4×10^3 to 3.0×10^3, so they 'doubled.' Calculate actual percentage change and propose a more accurate headline.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): calculation 8, notation 4, interpretation 5, critique 3. Preserve the existing weekly assessment and portfolio process.
