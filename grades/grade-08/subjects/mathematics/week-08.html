Week 08 Crosswalk Lab · Day 1: Dilate (2,3) by factor 2 about the origin to (4,6).

Evidence and correction: Demonstrate the concept with labeled units or a clear explanation, test the result, and correct an error. Formative only; official assessment authority is unchanged.

Dilate coordinates about the origin · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Scale factor 3 sends (−2,4) to (−6,12); multiply both coordinates.

Misconception checkpoint: Dilation does not add the scale factor to coordinates, and it does not preserve lengths unless k=1.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Dilate (2,−3) by factor 2.
  2. Dilate (−4,6) by factor 1/2.
  3. Dilate (0,5) by factor 3.
  4. Dilate (3,3) by factor 0.5.

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. (4,−6)
  2. (−2,3)
  3. (0,15)
  4. (1.5,1.5)

Apply similarity ratios · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A triangle 3-4-5 scaled by factor 2 has side lengths 6-8-10 and the same angles.

Misconception checkpoint: Equal angles with proportional sides imply similarity, not necessarily congruence.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Scale side 7 by factor 3.
  2. Scale side 12 by factor 1/4.
  3. Small side 5 matches large side 15: factor?
  4. A 40° angle under dilation remains?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Scale perimeters and areas · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Scaling a square side 4 to 12 uses factor 3: perimeter triples, area grows ninefold.

Misconception checkpoint: Area increases by k², not k; volume would scale by k³.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Area 8 scaled by factor 2: new area?
  2. Perimeter 11 scaled by factor 4: new perimeter?
  3. Area 50 scaled by factor 1/2: new area?
  4. Volume 6 scaled by factor 3: new volume?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Week 08 Crosswalk Lab · Day 4: Explain why dilation changes size but preserves angle measures and shape.

Evidence and correction: Demonstrate the concept with labeled units or a clear explanation, test the result, and correct an error. Formative only; official assessment authority is unchanged.

Prove similarity from proportional sides · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Triangles with sides 2,3,4 and 6,9,12 have common factor 3; their corresponding ratios match.

Misconception checkpoint: Comparing non-corresponding sides can create a false similarity claim.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Corresponding 4→10: scale factor?
  2. At factor 2.5, side 6 becomes?
  3. Are 3,4,5 and 6,8,10 similar?
  4. Are 3,4,5 and 6,8,11 similar?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 2.5
  2. 15
  3. yes
  4. no

Week 08 Crosswalk Lab · Day 5: Correct a claim that dilation by factor 2 yields congruence; explain similarity instead.

Evidence and correction: Demonstrate the concept with labeled units or a clear explanation, test the result, and correct an error. Formative only; official assessment authority is unchanged.

Compare rigid and scaled transformations · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define quantities, symbols and units from the worked model in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: A reflection preserves length 8; a dilation k=1.5 changes it to 12 but preserves corresponding angles.

Misconception checkpoint: Congruence implies equal size; similarity allows enlargement and reduction.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Original segment 10 under translation: length?
  2. Original segment 10 under scale 0.4: length?
  3. A 35° angle under dilation remains?
  4. Area 9 under factor 3 becomes?

Independent self-check: Verify the result with substitution, inverse operations, units, a coordinate sketch or a second model before opening the teacher key.

  4. 81
