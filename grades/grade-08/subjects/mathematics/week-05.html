Week 05 · Mathematics: Algebra I Readiness

Systems of Equations and Competing Explanations

How do we resolve situations where two conditions must be true at once?

Monday — Two conditions, one solution

Learn and model: A system of equations asks for values satisfying two equations simultaneously. For x+y=10 and x−y=2, adding equations gives 2x=12, so x=6 and y=4. Check both: 6+4=10 and 6−4=2. On a graph this pair is the intersection of two lines.

Practice and evidence: Solve x+y=12 and x−y=4. Show each substitution check. Explain why satisfying only one equation is insufficient.

Tuesday — Solve by substitution

Learn and model: When y=2x+1 and x+y=10, substitute the first equation into the second: x+(2x+1)=10; 3x=9; x=3, y=7. This is useful when one variable is already isolated. If no pair satisfies both equations, the lines are parallel; if both equations describe the same line, there are infinitely many solutions.

Practice and evidence: Solve y=3x−2 and x+y=14. Check the ordered pair in both original equations. Write a one-sentence interpretation of its graph.

Wednesday — Model competing plans

Learn and model: A fictional club charges Plan A: $12 enrollment plus $4 per visit, and Plan B: $6 enrollment plus $6 per visit. Their total costs after v visits are A=12+4v and B=6+6v. Equality requires 12+4v=6+6v, yielding v=3 and total cost $24. Below and above the break-even visit count, the cheaper plan changes.

Practice and evidence: Construct a table for 0, 1, 3, and 5 visits; identify the lower-cost plan each time and explain why the intersection matters. All prices are invented for practice.

Thursday — Error analysis and independent work

Learn and model: A student solves x+y=8 and x−y=4 and reports (4,4), but 4−4=0, not 4. Correct by adding to get 2x=12, so x=6, y=2. Verification is mandatory. Classify y=2x+1 and y=2x−3 as having no solution because they have equal slopes and different intercepts.

Practice and evidence: Solve 2x+y=11 and x−y=1 by elimination or substitution. Describe a likely arithmetic mistake and show how to detect it.

Friday — High-school-readiness defense

Learn and model: A fictional fundraiser sells student tickets for $5 and adult tickets for $8. A total of 20 tickets collects $124. Let s+a=20 and 5s+8a=124. From s=20−a, substitute: 5(20−a)+8a=124, so a=8 and s=12. Check count and revenue.

Practice and evidence: Independently solve an altered case: 18 tickets total, $117 revenue at the same prices. Submit equations, steps, a verification, and a paragraph explaining variable meanings. Rubric (20): modeling 5; accurate solution 7; checks 4; explanation 4. Existing weekly assessment remains separate.
