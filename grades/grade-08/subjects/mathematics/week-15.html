Translate points and preserve lengths · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: Moving every point by (+4,−2) maps (−1,3) to (3,1), preserving distances.

Misconception checkpoint: A translation changes location, not size or orientation.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Translate (2,5) by (+3,−1).
  2. Translate (−3,4) by (−2,+1).
  3. A segment of length 6 translated: new length?
  4. Translate (0,−2) by (+4,+2).

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. (5,4)
  2. (−5,5)
  3. 6
  4. (4,0)

Reflect and rotate figures · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: Reflect (3,−2) across the x-axis to (3,2), then 180° rotate to (−3,−2).

Misconception checkpoint: Reflection over the x-axis changes y; reflection over y-axis changes x.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Reflect (−2,4) over x-axis.
  2. Reflect (−2,4) over y-axis.
  3. Rotate (2,3) 90° counterclockwise.
  4. Rotate (2,3) 180°.

Compare similarity with congruence · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: A factor-2 dilation maps a 3 cm side to 6 cm: shapes are similar, not congruent.

Misconception checkpoint: Rigid movements preserve side length; dilation by factor other than 1 does not.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Dilate (−2,3) by factor 2 about origin.
  2. Dilate (4,−6) by factor 1/2 about the origin.
  3. Length 5 scaled by factor 3.
  4. Area 8 scaled by factor 3.

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. (−4,6)
  2. (2,−3)
  3. 15
  4. 72

Use transformations as proof evidence · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: Translations and reflections can carry a triangle to a congruent image; corresponding sides and angles remain unchanged.

Misconception checkpoint: A visual resemblance alone is not a mathematical congruence argument.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. A 60° angle rotated: new measure?
  2. A 4 cm segment reflected: new length?
  3. Square area 16 after translation: new area?
  4. Side 3 enlarged factor 2: new side?

Synthesize geometric transformations · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Explain the meaning of the variables, measurements or symbols in the mathematical model.

Worked mathematical model: Reflect (1,2) over y-axis to (−1,2), then translate right 3 to (2,2). Order the moves carefully.

Misconception checkpoint: The order of transformations can matter even when both transformations are valid.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Reflect (2,−3) over y-axis.
  2. After reflecting (2,−3) over the y-axis to (−2,−3), translate that point 5 units up.
  3. Rotate (4,1) 180°.
  4. Dilate (3,−2) by factor 2 about the origin.

Independent self-check: Verify your solution through substitution, inverse operations, unit analysis or a second diagram before opening the key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. (−2,−3)
  2. (−2,2)
  3. (−4,−1)
  4. (6,−4)
