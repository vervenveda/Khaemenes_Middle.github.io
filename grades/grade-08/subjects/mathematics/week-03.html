Find slope from two points · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For points (1,3) and (4,9), slope=(9−3)/(4−1)=2.

Misconception checkpoint: Slope is vertical change divided by horizontal change; reversing gives the wrong rate.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Find slope through (0,2) and (3,8).
  2. Find slope through (1,5) and (3,9).
  3. Find slope through (2,4) and (5,4).
  4. Find slope through (1,5) and (1,9).

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 2
  2. 2
  3. 0
  4. undefined

Identify the y-intercept · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: The line y=−2x+7 intersects the y-axis when x=0, at (0,7).

Misconception checkpoint: The y-intercept is not always the value seen at the first listed data point.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. State intercept of y=3x−4.
  2. State slope of y=3x−4.
  3. Find y at x=2.
  4. Find x-intercept of y=3x−6.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. −4
  2. 3
  3. 2
  4. 2

Write line equations · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Slope 4 through (0,−3) gives y=4x−3.

Misconception checkpoint: The intercept must be the output at x=0, not an arbitrary observed x.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Write rule for slope 2 and intercept 5.
  2. Write rule for slope −3 and intercept 1.
  3. Write rule for slope 0 and intercept 4.
  4. Find y at x=3 for y=2x+5.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Compare steepness and direction · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: Lines with slopes +2 and −4 have opposite directions; |−4| is steeper than |+2|.

Misconception checkpoint: A negative slope does not mean smaller steepness; magnitude determines steepness.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Which is steeper: slope −5 or +3?
  2. Which line falls as x rises: slope −2 or +2?
  3. Compute change for slope −3 over Δx=4.
  4. Compute change for slope 2 over Δx=4.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. −5
  2. −2
  3. −12
  4. 8

Use slope to predict and verify · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities and units in the worked model; explain them in your own words.

Worked mathematical model: For y=3x−2, x=5 gives y=13; check by substitution.

Misconception checkpoint: A fitted prediction is an estimate, not an observation or a guarantee of causal change.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. For y=3x−2 find y at x=0.
  2. Find y at x=5.
  3. Find x when y=10.
  4. Find rise over run between x=1 and x=4.

Independent self-check: Verify by substitution, inverse operations, unit comparison or a second representation before inspecting the answer key.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. −2
  2. 13
  3. x=4
  4. 9 over 3
