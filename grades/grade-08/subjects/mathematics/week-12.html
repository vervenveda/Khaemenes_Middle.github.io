Compute basic probability · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: For a fair die, rolling a number greater than four means 5 or 6: probability 2/6=1/3.

Misconception checkpoint: Theoretical models assume equally likely outcomes; they are not promises for small samples.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. P(rolling 1 on a fair die)?
  2. P(rolling even on a fair die)?
  3. P(rolling >3 on a fair die)?
  4. P(rolling 1 through 6)?

Worked mathematical model: Two fair independent coins have four equally likely outcomes; P(both heads)=1/4.

Understand conditional probabilities · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A group has 20 students, 12 play music and 6 of those also play sports. P(sports | music)=6/12=1/2.

Misconception checkpoint: The denominator of a conditional probability is the size of the given subgroup, not the full population.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. If 8 of 10 artists use a tablet, P(tablet | artist)?
  2. If 5 of 20 participants have a badge, overall probability?
  3. If 3 of 6 cyclists wear helmets, P(helmet | cyclist)?
  4. If 9 of 12 readers finish, P(finish | reader)?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. 4/5
  2. 1/4
  3. 1/2
  4. 3/4

Connect statistics to risk · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A trial shows 15 wins among 50 plays: experimental win rate 30%. Decisions should consider payoff, uncertainty and sample size.

Misconception checkpoint: A single past win does not guarantee future success; empirical estimates can shift with more data.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. 12 wins out of 40: experimental probability?
  2. 18 successes of 60: sample rate?
  3. If P(success)=0.3, expected successes in 100 trials?
  4. Find complement of P=0.3.

Evaluate expected value and uncertainty · Mathematics laboratory

Vocabulary in context: Define the important quantities, units and symbols in this worked model before attempting the questions.

Worked mathematical model: A game gives +$5 with probability 1/2 and −$3 with probability 1/2, expected net +$1 per play.

Misconception checkpoint: Positive expected value is an average over many trials, not a guaranteed win on each turn.

Independent reasoning challenges:

  1. Win +$6 P=1/2, lose −$2 P=1/2: expected net?
  2. Win +$3 P=1/4, lose −$1 P=3/4: expected net?
  3. P(failure) if P(success)=0.65?
  4. If 75 of 100 trials succeed, experimental probability?

Independent self-check: Check the result using substitution, inverse operations, a diagram, another representation or a careful comparison of units before opening the solutions.

Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
  1. +$2
  2. $0
  3. 0.35
  4. 75%
