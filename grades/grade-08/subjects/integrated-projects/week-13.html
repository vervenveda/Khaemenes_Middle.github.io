Week 13 · Integrated High School Readiness Projects

Literary Theme, Perspective, and Modern Connections

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Define a defensible question

Learn and model: Investigate whether a fictional town should restore an old observatory as a learning center. Separate historical claims, costs, educational possibilities and community opinions; none is settled by one attractive story.

Practice and evidence: Write a research question and a four-column evidence-needs table.

Tuesday — Build a mathematical model

Learn and model: Fictional costs: a one-time $240 setup fee and $30 per weekly session; another option costs $60 per session without setup. Equating 240+30w=60w yields w=8. The numbers are illustrative, not an actual budget.

Practice and evidence: Verify the eight-session break-even point and compare costs at 4 and 12 sessions.

Wednesday — Plan evidence collection

Learn and model: A proposal requires trustworthy information about site condition, accessibility, educational use, community interests and operating cost. A self-selected poll is not representative by default.

Practice and evidence: Design a three-question neutral survey and list two professional sources you would seek before making real safety claims.

Thursday — Create and test a prototype

Learn and model: Sketch a paper-based visitor schedule and accessible educational activity. A prototype is an invitation to test, not proof that a proposal will work.

Practice and evidence: Annotate the prototype with who might be excluded and two improvements.

Friday — Defend the proposal

Learn and model: Present a 250-word recommendation with the fictional cost model, one counterargument, limitations, and a revision plan.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): evidence 6, correct modeling 5, inclusive design 5, reflection 4. Use the established portfolio and assessment processes.
