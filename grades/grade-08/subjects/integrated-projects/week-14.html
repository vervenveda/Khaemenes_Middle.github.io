Week 14 · Integrated High School Readiness Projects

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Define a researchable question

Learn and model: The project asks: 'Should a fictional school change library hours?' A testable question identifies users, decision criteria, resources and a realistic evidence plan.

Practice and evidence: State a measurable question and distinguish it from a predetermined conclusion.

Tuesday — Audit the information

Learn and model: Use fictional survey results: 21 of 30 student volunteers prefer later hours. This 70% describes surveyed volunteers, not necessarily every student.

Practice and evidence: Create a source-quality table listing sample, calculation, potential bias and missing information.

Wednesday — Build a prototype

Learn and model: Propose a two-week trial timetable and accessible feedback form. A prototype lets stakeholders test a choice before committing scarce resources.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a schedule and feedback card that work on paper and do not require accounts.

Thursday — Test competing explanations

Learn and model: An evening-visit increase might reflect changed hours, an event or seasonal demand. Describe what comparison observations could distinguish these possibilities.

Practice and evidence: Develop a fair comparison design with baseline, observations and limitations.

Friday — Defend and revise

Learn and model: Present a claim, annotated chart, prototype, counterargument and revision plan. Be clear that the case and data are fictional.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): credible evidence 6, mathematical accuracy 4, design 4, counterargument and reflection 6. Portfolio approval and weekly assessment remain unchanged.
