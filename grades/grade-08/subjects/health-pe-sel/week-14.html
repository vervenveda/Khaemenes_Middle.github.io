Week 14 · Health, PE & SEL

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Evaluate health claims

Learn and model: A social-media claim that a single routine works for everyone ignores individual differences, safety and evidence quality. Health information requires context and careful sourcing.

Practice and evidence: Identify absolute language in a fictional fitness advertisement and rewrite it as a cautious, testable statement.

Tuesday — Executive-function planning

Learn and model: Planning a task involves defining a realistic goal, breaking it into steps and checking progress. A timetable is a tool, not proof that learning occurred.

Practice and evidence: Design a five-step study routine with rest breaks and a brief self-check.

Wednesday — Interpret activity data

Learn and model: An invented activity log records 15, 20 and 25 minutes on three days: total 60 minutes, average 20. Activity records alone cannot determine overall health.

Practice and evidence: Calculate total and average; name two important details the log does not provide.

Thursday — Make inclusive choices

Learn and model: Students vary in mobility, energy and access to equipment. Provide seated or standing options, stop activity that causes pain, and avoid comparing personal health information publicly.

Practice and evidence: Describe two accessible ways to practice coordination or mindful movement without special equipment.

Friday — Reflection and responsible communication

Learn and model: Evaluate one health-related claim for author, evidence, risks and overgeneralization, then outline a flexible weekly wellness routine.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): evidence critique 6, planning 5, numerical reasoning 4, inclusion and reflection 5.
