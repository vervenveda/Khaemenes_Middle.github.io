Week 07 · Language Arts & Literacy

Transformations and Congruence

How can movement preserve structure and prove congruence?

Read · Compare · Reason · Revise · Defend

Investigate Academy-original geometry narratives and conflicting proof claims. Practice accurate correspondence, counterexamples, conditional language and high-school-ready explanatory writing.

Proof language and conventions Congruence versus similarity Source-based rebuttal Oral defense and revision

Monday · Read two claims about a moving figure

Learning target: Analyze an argument about translation, identify the source's evidence, and avoid claiming more than the coordinates show.

Begin with the original triangle narrative
Academy-original narrative · The Triangle That Moved

In a fictional design studio, Hana marked a triangle using three points: A(0,0), B(2,0) and C(0,1). Her group planned to shift every point two grid units right and one unit down. After applying that movement, Hana drew A′(2,-1), B′(4,-1) and C′(2,0). Her classmate said, “It has moved, so it must be a different shape.”

Hana replied that movement and shape were different questions. The rule (x,y) → (x+2,y-1) changes location but does not change the distances between corresponding points. A′B′ is still two grid units long; A′C′ is still one; the diagonal BC and B′C′ have the same length, √5 grid units.

The teacher asked the group to state a limited conclusion. These particular triangles are congruent under the described translation. That result does not prove that any arbitrary change of a figure preserves congruence. The students marked the original and image vertices with matching letters so readers could check the correspondence.

Source: Khaemenes Academy original fictional coordinate-geometry narrative, English 8 Week 07. All shapes are classroom examples.

Worked example: Supported claim: the translation (x,y) → (x+2,y-1) maps A(0,0) to A′(2,-1), and the matching side lengths remain 2, 1 and √5. The text's conclusion is about the given translation and corresponding figure, not every conceivable change.

Student practice
  1. Create a source table pairing A/B/C with A′/B′/C′, citing the narrative.
  2. Identify the classmate's unsupported inference and explain why movement alone does not settle congruence.
  3. Quote one exact reason given for preservation of distance and translate it into your own explanatory sentence.
  4. Write a carefully scoped thesis about what the triangle example establishes.

Today's portfolio evidence: Correspondence chart, corrected claim and a cited thesis about rigid movement.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Check exact coordinate correspondence, including the negative y-value. The English focus is evaluating a proof's language, not replacing Geometry assessment.

Tuesday · Read a proof and distinguish translation, reflection and dilation

Learning target: Analyze the logic and vocabulary of transformations and identify an accurate counterexample to an overbroad statement.

Expand original sources, model and practice
Academy-original explanatory text · Same Shape, Different Claim

A translation moves each point by the same vector. In the Week 07 triangle, adding (2,-1) to every vertex produces the image without changing any side length. A reflection across the y-axis sends (x,y) to (-x,y): the original A(0,0), B(2,0), C(0,1) become A″(0,0), B″(-2,0), C″(0,1). This also preserves side lengths.

A dilation centered at the origin with scale factor two instead sends B(2,0) to (4,0) and C(0,1) to (0,2), while A remains at (0,0). The resulting nondegenerate triangle has side lengths twice as long. It is similar to the original but is not congruent to it.

Translations and reflections are rigid motions because they preserve distances. The dilation shown here is not a rigid motion. A careful explanation distinguishes congruence, which requires corresponding equal lengths and angles, from similarity, which permits proportional lengths with equal angles.

Source: Academy-original explanatory mathematical literacy text, Grade 8 Week 07.

Worked example: The dilation gives a counterexample to “all transformations create congruent figures.” Translating and reflecting the original triangle preserve distances; scaling all its lengths by two yields a similar, larger triangle.

Student practice
  1. Define translation, reflection, rigid motion, dilation and congruence using the given examples.
  2. List the reflected vertices and identify the coordinate sign change.
  3. Explain why the scale-factor-two image is similar but not congruent to the nondegenerate original.
  4. Write a two-sentence counterexample refuting the claim that any transformed figure is congruent.

Today's portfolio evidence: Five-term proof vocabulary, transformation comparison chart and counterexample.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Review the distinction between congruence and similarity without requiring a formal proof beyond the example. A larger figure can remain similar without remaining congruent.

Wednesday · Evaluate competing written arguments

Learning target: Separate supported geometric statements from rhetorical shortcuts, then construct a fair rebuttal.

Expand original sources, model and practice
Academy-original student opinion · Every Move Preserves Everything

“You can always shift a shape, reflect it, stretch it, or resize it and still call it congruent. All transformations keep the same figure. Anyone saying the size matters is thinking too narrowly.”

Academy-original reviewer response · State the Transformation

“The word transformation describes more than one kind of mapping. Translation and reflection keep side lengths in the specific examples, but a dilation by two doubles lengths and does not preserve congruence for this nondegenerate triangle. A writer must identify the rule and compare the correct corresponding parts.”

Both opinions are fictional. The first uses sweeping words and dismissal of disagreement in place of evidence. The second answers the generalization with the concrete source examples and limits.

Worked example: The response provides a counterexample to the word “all.” If one transformation—dilation by two—changes corresponding side lengths, the general claim that every transformation preserves congruence fails. This does not make the original writer's other observations meaningless.

Student practice
  1. Underline three overbroad or dismissive phrases in the first opinion.
  2. Find two specific details in the Week 07 explanatory text that refute an unsupported claim.
  3. Write a source matrix for the narrative, explanatory text and opinions: purpose, strongest evidence and limitation.
  4. Draft a 110–150-word rebuttal using accurate conditionals, one counterexample and a respectful tone.

Today's portfolio evidence: Three-source matrix, rhetorical analysis and concise conditional rebuttal.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Teach if–then reasoning and precise quantifiers such as “this,” “some,” and “all.” Keep rhetoric and mathematics evidence distinct.

Thursday · Compose a precise multi-source explanation

Learning target: Build an evidence-based argument with corresponding coordinates, conditional claims, counterargument and clear conventions.

Expand original sources, model and practice
Academy-original writing brief · What Counts as a Proof?

A fictional student journal asks: When does moving a figure preserve congruence, and how should a writer support that conclusion? Use the triangle narrative, transformation explanation and competing opinions. Describe what happens under a translation and reflection, and why the given dilation does not produce a congruent image.

Write for a reader who may not have a picture in front of them. Define all primes or image labels, state coordinates or correspondence in words, explain how side lengths are compared and distinguish a verified example from an unsupported universal rule.

Worked example: Weak: “The figures are obviously equal because they look alike.” Stronger: “Applying (x,y) → (x+2,y-1) to each vertex of the original triangle gives corresponding side lengths 2, 1 and √5, so the image is congruent; a scale-factor-two dilation doubles those lengths, showing that similarity does not necessarily imply congruence.”

Student practice
  1. Draft 300–400 original words with a qualified thesis, at least three source details and exact correspondence in one example.
  2. Explain how translation and reflection preserve distance, and analyze the dilation counterexample.
  3. Include a fair counterargument about why an image might appear to be “the same shape,” then explain the distinction between similarity and congruence.
  4. Revise one missing conditional, one overbroad claim and one ambiguous image label; preserve before-and-after excerpts.

Today's portfolio evidence: A 300–400-word sourced explanatory argument with correspondence evidence, rebuttal and revision trace.

Teacher / family facilitation guidance

Grammar and conventions are integral: inspect if–then clauses, antecedents, diagram labels and proof transitions. Assessment scores remain under the original engine.

Friday · Defend a congruence claim and reflect

Learning target: Respond to proof challenges, qualify the conclusion, and preserve a high-school-readiness writing artifact.

Expand original sources, model and practice
Academy-original oral defense · The Reviewer's Challenges

Challenge 1: “The translated triangle appears somewhere else. Why can it still be congruent?”

Challenge 2: “The dilated triangle has the same angles, so why isn't it congruent?”

Challenge 3: “Does this example prove that every possible mapping preserves distance?”

An effective defense states the rule, identifies corresponding parts and explains why evidence about one kind of transformation must not be assigned to all others.

Worked example: A translation preserves lengths and angles even as location changes. A scale-factor-two dilation preserves angle measures but doubles nonzero side lengths, so the resulting triangle is similar, not congruent. No single example proves that all mappings preserve distance.

Student practice
  1. Prepare a 90-second defense referencing the original triangle and one transformed image with exact coordinates.
  2. Answer all three challenges using a reason, source evidence and a qualified conclusion.
  3. Submit the original and revised Thursday argument, transformation matrix and oral outline.
  4. Write a four-to-five-sentence reflection on how grammar, counterexamples and precise correspondence strengthen a Grade 9 proof.

Today's portfolio evidence: English 9 readiness explanation and oral defense. Teacher-coaching rubric /20: qualified thesis 4, accurate evidence 6, counterexample and reasoning 4, defense 3, revision 3. Not an official grade or placement decision.
