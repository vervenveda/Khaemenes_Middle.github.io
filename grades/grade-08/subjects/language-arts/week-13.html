Week 13 · Language Arts & Literacy

Literary Theme, Perspective, and Modern Connections

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Read an original narrative

Learn and model: Original Academy story: 'Mira returned to the shuttered observatory with a notebook of questions. Her grandfather had called its silence a failure. When she pushed open the door, she saw maps marked with careful corrections, not abandoned dreams. She began recording what the building could still teach.' A theme is an insight developed through events, not simply the topic 'observatory'.

Practice and evidence: Identify two details supporting a theme about learning through revision, and distinguish the narrator from Mira's perspective.

Tuesday — Compare interpretations

Learn and model: Mira's grandfather interprets silence as failure; Mira discovers evidence of continuing value. Character perspectives may differ without one being entirely unreasonable. Explain claims with quotations and account for context.

Practice and evidence: Write two interpretive claims, each with a quotation or accurate paraphrase of the original passage.

Wednesday — Evaluate modern parallels

Learn and model: A contemporary adaptation can preserve a theme while changing setting and conflict. Moving an observatory story into a community workshop could retain the idea of revising inherited assumptions. Similarity alone is not plagiarism; credit material and transform it meaningfully.

Practice and evidence: Outline a 200-word adaptation featuring a different setting and new conflict, and identify the retained theme.

Thursday — Revise for nuance

Learn and model: 'Everyone who quits is wrong' overstates the lesson. A nuanced thematic statement might be: 'Evidence can change how people judge apparent failure.' Discuss how narration, imagery and contrast help the reader infer that message.

Practice and evidence: Draft 180–230 words with a thematic claim, two passage details, an alternative reading, and a response.

Friday — Independent literary defense

Learn and model: Create a final analytical paragraph and a short original adapted scene. Explain how changing the setting changed the meaning or emphasis.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): textual evidence 6, interpretation 6, creative transfer 4, revision 4; use the existing weekly assessment separately.
