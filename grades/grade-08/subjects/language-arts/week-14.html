Week 14 · Language Arts & Literacy

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Distinguish claims from evidence

Learn and model: Original fictional school announcement: 'A student poll of 30 volunteers found that 21 wanted a later library closing time. The school should extend hours immediately.' The reported 70% is correct for volunteers, but the sample may not represent all students. Claims, evidence, and warrants play different roles.

Practice and evidence: Annotate the claim, statistic, assumption, and missing information; explain why volunteer selection matters.

Tuesday — Identify persuasive techniques

Learn and model: A headline saying 'Everybody demands longer hours!' goes beyond the evidence. Words like everybody, always, and proven can exaggerate. Images and cropped charts can also alter readers' impressions without changing underlying counts.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite two exaggerated headlines accurately and explain the difference between emotional appeal and verifiable support.

Wednesday — Compare two arguments

Learn and model: Argument A says the 21 students prove a policy change is needed. Argument B says the poll warrants a wider survey and a trial schedule. A strong response fairly describes both positions, compares evidence, and identifies what would change the conclusion.

Practice and evidence: Build a claim–reason–evidence chart for A and B; evaluate which recommendation is better supported by the limited survey.

Thursday — Practice source evaluation

Learn and model: An accountable report identifies its author, collection method, date, denominators, and limitations. A screenshot without provenance cannot establish whether the numbers are authentic. Distinguish publication credibility from the independent accuracy of a claim.

Practice and evidence: Draft a 180–220-word media-literacy analysis citing the fictional notice and labeling its limitations.

Friday — Independent editorial defense

Learn and model: Write a short editorial recommending whether to run a trial, survey further, or retain existing hours. Include a credible counterargument and a response grounded in the known facts.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): accurate evidence 6, sound reasoning 6, source critique 4, clarity/revision 4. Complete the separate existing weekly assessment.
