Week 14 · Science & Engineering

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Understand geologic time

Learn and model: Earth's history spans about 4.54 billion years. Relative dating orders events using rock relationships, including superposition in undisturbed sedimentary layers; radiometric dating can estimate numerical ages from suitable materials. These methods answer different questions and have limitations.

Practice and evidence: Diagram three undisturbed sedimentary layers A (bottom), B, C (top). Identify the relative order and why that does not provide calendar dates.

Tuesday — Evaluate a fossil claim

Learn and model: A fictional poster says 'One fossil proves all life appeared at the same moment.' A fossil shows an organism existed in a context; it cannot alone establish simultaneous origins for all life. Fossil interpretation involves rock context, identification, dating, and comparison.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite the poster claim into a testable question and list three kinds of evidence investigators would need.

Wednesday — Calculate time intervals

Learn and model: An illustrative timeline labels events 250 million and 65 million years ago. The elapsed interval is 185 million years. These are illustrative markers, not dates assigned to a particular classroom specimen. Avoid treating unequal intervals as equal distances on a time axis.

Practice and evidence: Construct a scale with 1 cm representing 50 million years; determine how far apart the two illustrative events should be.

Thursday — Compare scientific sources

Learn and model: Source A is a museum explanation identifying rock units, methods, and uncertainty; Source B is an anonymous image with an unlabeled fossil and an exact age. Neither source should be accepted solely by appearance, but A gives a path to verification. Describe method, provenance, and limitations.

Practice and evidence: Create a source-rating table based on evidence transparency, not popularity; write a claim-evidence-reasoning analysis of the poster.

Friday — Scientific argument defense

Learn and model: Explain how relative ordering and numerical dating can jointly help reconstruct past events, and why one fossil cannot establish a universal timeline.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): scientific accuracy 7, temporal reasoning 5, source critique 4, explanation 4. Complete the normal weekly assessment independently.
