Week 12 · Science & Engineering

Probability, Statistics, and Risk

How can probability and statistics help us judge uncertainty?

Monday — Measurements and variation

Learn and model: Repeated measurements differ because of natural variation and measurement limitations. Fictional lengths in cm: 10.1, 10.3, 10.2, 10.4, 10.0. These are invented observations, not experimental results.

Practice and evidence: Compute the mean (10.2 cm) and range (0.4 cm), showing units.

Tuesday — Estimate uncertainty

Learn and model: A mean summarizes the center, but it does not capture the whole distribution. Repeating an observation and documenting method helps identify variability. Precision of a ruler can affect reported digits.

Practice and evidence: Make a dot plot for the five fictional lengths; explain the difference between a measurement and its rounded value.

Wednesday — Probability and risk

Learn and model: In an invented classroom trial, an event occurred 6 times in 20 observations, giving an observed proportion 0.30. This is an estimate from a small sample, not a guarantee for the next trial.

Practice and evidence: Compare 6/20 with 60/200; explain why a larger sample can still have limitations.

Thursday — Evaluate an overconfident claim

Learn and model: A fictional headline states 'This result proves the event always occurs 30% of the time.' Scientific conclusions require sampling context, uncertainty, repeatability and transparent methods.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite the headline carefully and identify two missing methodological details.

Friday — Independent evidence report

Learn and model: Produce a table, summary calculations, a claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph and a limitation statement for the fictional measurements and trial.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): accurate calculations 6, evidence use 5, uncertainty 5, communication 4; retain existing weekly assessment.
