Week 13 · Technology, Design & Tools

Literary Theme, Perspective, and Modern Connections

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Build a readable data table

Learn and model: A spreadsheet organizes values in rows and columns. In a fictional three-day library count, Monday 20, Tuesday 25, Wednesday 15, the total is 60 and mean is 20. Mark these counts as simulated.

Practice and evidence: Create a table with labels and calculate total and average by hand or spreadsheet.

Tuesday — Explain formulas

Learn and model: A spreadsheet formula such as =SUM(B2:B4) totals numeric cells, while =AVERAGE(B2:B4) computes their mean. A cell reference points to a location, and bad references can create inaccurate reports.

Practice and evidence: Predict the result if B2:B4 contain 20,25,15. Describe an error caused by omitting B4.

Wednesday — Visualize fairly

Learn and model: A bar graph should label its axis, category and unit. Starting the vertical axis at zero often prevents exaggerating small changes between counts.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a bar chart of the fictional counts and write an accessible text description.

Thursday — Check credibility and privacy

Learn and model: A spreadsheet may contain individual student information. For classroom demonstrations, use fictional, aggregated counts; avoid publishing identifiable records. Recalculate claims before presenting a graph.

Practice and evidence: Write a quality checklist covering arithmetic, source, anonymization and accessible labels.

Friday — Independent dashboard

Learn and model: Submit a one-page table, calculations, graph and paragraph explaining what the data do and do not establish.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): calculations 6, graph 5, interpretation 5, accessibility/privacy 4. Existing assessment untouched.
