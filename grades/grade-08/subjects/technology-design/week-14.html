Week 14 · Technology, Design & Tools

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Detect manipulation

Learn and model: A bar graph compares 48 and 50 responses. Starting the vertical axis at 47 exaggerates the small difference. Always check scale, denominator and labels.

Practice and evidence: Draw zero-based and truncated-axis versions; explain their different impressions.

Tuesday — Examine online sources

Learn and model: A screenshot without author, date, methodology or original dataset is not sufficient proof. Trace claims to named sources and separate reported facts from commentary.

Practice and evidence: Create a four-question source checklist and evaluate an invented viral headline.

Wednesday — Verify percentages

Learn and model: In a fictional sample, 18 of 30 respondents prefer option A: 18÷30=0.6=60%. This does not mean 60% of an entire city agrees.

Practice and evidence: Calculate results for 9 and 3 other responses; explain sampling limitations.

Thursday — Design accessible evidence

Learn and model: Charts should contain text labels, descriptive captions, readable contrast and non-color-only distinctions. An alternative-text summary should state the main numerical comparison.

Practice and evidence: Produce an accessible chart with a written summary and source note.

Friday — Independent design critique

Learn and model: A credible digital argument reveals its underlying numbers and caveats.

Practice and evidence: Submit a chart, source evaluation and corrected headline. Rubric (20): arithmetic 5, accurate labels 5, reasoning 6, accessibility 4.
