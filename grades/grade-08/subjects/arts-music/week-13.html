Week 13 · Arts, Music & Media

Literary Theme, Perspective, and Modern Connections

How do authors transform old patterns into new meanings?

Monday — Analyze dramatic perspective

Learn and model: In drama, a character's objective is what they seek; an obstacle prevents easy success. Different actors can express the same dialogue with different pacing and emphasis, changing its interpretation.

Practice and evidence: Write a four-line original scene about an abandoned studio; name each character's objective.

Tuesday — Stage a contrast

Learn and model: Stage directions describe movement, gesture or tone. A pause may suggest uncertainty but cannot prove a character's exact thoughts without additional context.

Practice and evidence: Annotate the same scene with two contrasting sets of stage directions.

Wednesday — Study sound and mood

Learn and model: A repeated three-beat rhythm with a sudden rest can build expectation. Dynamics (loudness) and tempo (pace) support artistic meaning but do not force a single emotional response.

Practice and evidence: Clap or tap a safe short rhythm, notate beats and rests, and describe its intended mood.

Thursday — Give evidence-based critique

Learn and model: An actionable critique states an observable choice, the effect it produced for the reviewer, and a specific revision. Treat interpretations as perspectives, not objective facts.

Practice and evidence: Write a 120-word critique of your own staged scene and implement one revision.

Friday — Portfolio performance

Learn and model: Present the final scene as writing, a storyboard or an accessible performance transcript, including the contrasting interpretation.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): craft 6, interpretation 6, supporting examples 4, revision 4. Existing assessment preserved.
