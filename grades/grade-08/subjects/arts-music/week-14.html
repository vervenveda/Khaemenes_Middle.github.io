Week 14 · Arts, Music & Media

Argument Analysis and Media Literacy

How can we evaluate whether an argument deserves our trust?

Monday — Describe before judging

Learn and model: A critique begins with observable details such as tempo, instrumentation, visual composition or repeated motifs. 'The music is bad' supplies an opinion without evidence.

Practice and evidence: Describe an original rhythm of four beats or a self-created sketch using five observable details.

Tuesday — Analyze creative choices

Learn and model: A repeated rhythmic motif can create coherence. Shifts in volume or emphasis change attention. Interpretation should be connected to evidence rather than asserted as universal.

Practice and evidence: Compose two four-beat patterns, mark strong beats, and explain how the effect changes.

Wednesday — Critique an advertisement

Learn and model: An image's framing, typography and soundtrack can imply urgency or credibility without providing facts. Separate the work's aesthetic effectiveness from the truth of its claims.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a fictional poster advertising a school event; annotate three persuasive design choices.

Thursday — Respond constructively

Learn and model: Useful feedback names a specific feature, its effect, and a practical suggestion. Creators can accept or reject feedback after reflection.

Practice and evidence: Write a 100-word critique of your poster, then revise one feature and justify the change.

Friday — Portfolio performance

Learn and model: Create and annotate a short rhythm score or poster, explaining purpose, audience and the distinction between persuasive style and factual evidence.

Practice and evidence: Rubric (20): craft 6, analysis 6, supported critique 4, revision 4.
