The Debate About a Missing Number

At the community club, two students checked a missing quantity in a budget record: x + 7 = 19. One student proposed x = 26 because adding 7 seemed like the obvious move. Another student explained that equality means both sides have the same value. Subtracting 7 from both sides keeps them balanced and gives x = 12. The group substituted 12 back into the original equation: 12 + 7 = 19. They decided a trustworthy mathematical argument must state a claim, show the operation on both sides, and verify the result. In a second example, 5n = 35, they divided both sides by 5 and explained that n = 7 describes the price of one notebook in dollars.

Original fictional Academy reading, included for offline study.

1. State the passage's central claim about reliable mathematical arguments.

2. Cite two details used to support that claim.

3. Explain why x = 26 is incorrect and how to verify the correct answer.
