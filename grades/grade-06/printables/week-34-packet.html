The Capstone Proposal Constraint

In a fictional Academy capstone, a group planned to construct demonstration models for its inquiry. Each model required four resource units, preparation required seven more, and the group had at most thirty-one units. One student calculated 31÷4 and said seven models were possible. Another wrote 4x+7≤31 and found x≤6, accounting for fixed preparation. Their teacher asked the class to check the six-model plan (24+7=31) and the seven-model plan (28+7=35), then to name units and clarify that model counts are whole numbers. The students used these calculations to design a feasible plan, but identified actual costs as facts requiring independent verification.

Original fictional Academy reading, available offline.

1. Explain the first student's mistake.

2. Cite the inequality and two budget checks.

3. Explain what information a real proposal would need to verify.
