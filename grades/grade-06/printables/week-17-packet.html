What Counts as Evidence of Learning?

At a fictional student conference, a sixth grader presented a mathematics portfolio. The first page showed a ratio of 3:5 and a revised equivalent ratio of 12:20. The second showed the difference between the surface area and volume of a rectangular prism. The student had originally confused square and cubic units, but later calculated 108 cm² of surface area and 72 cm³ of volume for a 6-by-4-by-3 centimeter closed box. A family reviewer praised the corrected work but asked how the learner knew the answers were reliable. The student described inverse operations, unit checks, labeled diagrams, and a written explanation. Together they agreed that evidence of growth includes not only a correct result, but also a reasoned explanation and a record of revision.

Original fictional Academy reading, available offline.

1. Summarize the central claim of the passage.

2. Cite two quantitative examples proving growth.

3. Explain how corrections should be preserved in a learner portfolio.
