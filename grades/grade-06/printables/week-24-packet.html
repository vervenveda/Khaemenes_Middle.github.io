A Village Model, Not a History Record

In an imaginary river settlement, learners created two models for moving grain. One plan cost seven units per trip and twelve units to prepare, written 7t+12. Another used five units per trip and a twenty-unit preparation cost, written 5t+20. At four trips, both cost forty units. One learner announced that the plans were always equal. Her classmates checked three trips and six trips, discovering different totals. They explained that substituting different values into the two expressions was necessary before making a broad claim. The lesson used a fictional setting to practice algebra; it did not claim that real ancient communities used these numbers.

Original fictional Academy reading, available offline.

1. State the mistaken claim and correction.

2. Cite the expressions and evaluate them at four trips.

3. Explain how a mathematical model differs from a historical source.
