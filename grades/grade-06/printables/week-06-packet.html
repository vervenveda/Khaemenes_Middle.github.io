Below Zero and the History of Place Value

A fictional museum exhibit explored two ways people describe quantity. One display showed modern temperatures: a reading of −4 degrees is four degrees below zero, while a reading of 3 degrees is three degrees above zero. On a number line, 3 lies to the right of −4 and is therefore greater. Another display introduced a simplified model of a historical base-sixty place-value system. The two positional digits (1, 5) represented one group of sixty plus five units, or 65 in our base-ten notation. The museum guide reminded visitors that historical writing systems varied across places and periods, so a simplified classroom model should not be mistaken for every historical practice. Students compared the two displays and explained how symbols and position help people record meaning precisely.
