The Community Hall Rule

In a fictional classroom council, learners designed a model for a community hall that could hold at most forty people. Twelve seats were reserved for volunteers. Some students said that up to forty additional guests could attend. Another wrote g+12≤40 and reasoned that g≤28, because the reserved seats also counted toward capacity. The council checked the boundary: twenty-eight guests plus twelve volunteers filled exactly forty spaces, but twenty-nine guests exceeded the limit. They explained that an inequality describes all permitted guest counts, not a single answer. This scenario practices mathematical reasoning and does not represent an actual community's laws or beliefs.

Original fictional Academy reading; available offline.

1. Explain the initial misunderstanding.

2. Cite the inequality and its valid boundary.

3. Explain why an equation and an inequality answer different questions.
