The Garden Club's Spending Limit

At a fictional neighborhood garden club, students were given a supply budget of no more than $48. A starter kit cost $12 and each planting tray cost $6. The coordinator wrote 12 + 6t ≤ 48, where t stood for the number of trays. She explained that “no more than” includes the boundary of exactly $48. Subtracting 12 and dividing by positive 6 gives t ≤ 6. Since a planting tray is purchased as a whole item, the group could buy between zero and six trays. One student suggested seven trays, but $12 + 7 × $6 = $54, exceeding the limit. Before choosing six trays, the group also discussed whether it needed other supplies. They concluded that a mathematical rule captures the stated constraints, not every possible real-world concern.

Original fictional Khaemenes Academy passage, supplied for offline reading.

1. Explain the main claim about responsible decisions and constraints.

2. Cite two numerical details supporting that claim.

3. Why is the boundary included, and why are trays restricted to whole numbers?
