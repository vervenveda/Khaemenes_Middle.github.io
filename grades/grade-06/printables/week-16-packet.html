Which Cart Was Faster?

During a fictional classroom investigation, two groups measured the motion of toy carts. Cart A traveled 32 meters in eight seconds, and Cart B traveled 35 meters in seven seconds. One student argued that the cart traveling the longer distance must be the faster one. The investigator calculated average speed by dividing distance by time: Cart A averaged four meters per second, while Cart B averaged five meters per second. The learners discussed whether different starting positions, uneven tracks, or inaccurate timing could have influenced the observations. They agreed that their calculations compared the recorded speeds but did not, by themselves, prove which forces caused the differences. Their report included measurements, units, formulas, and a suggestion for another trial.

Original fictional Academy reading provided for offline study.

1. Summarize the key claim about comparing motion.

2. Cite two numerical facts that justify the conclusion.

3. Explain a fair-testing limitation and one improvement.
