Labels and Fair Comparisons

In a fictional multilingual classroom, students prepared thirty greeting cards: eighteen included illustrations and twelve used text only. One learner reported a ratio of eighteen to thirty without naming the quantities being compared. Another explained that drawings-to-total was 18:30, or 3:5, while drawings-to-text-only was 18:12, or 3:2. Later the students compared two made-up practice activities: eighteen correct matches among twenty-four cards versus twenty-one correct matches among thirty-five. Although the second activity had more matches, the first had a higher proportion, 75% versus 60%. Their report named denominators, reported the actual sample sizes, and avoided assumptions about any language or culture.

Original fictional Academy reading; no internet needed.

1. Explain the difference between part-to-part and part-to-whole.

2. Cite two numerical facts and their simplified ratios.

3. Explain why raw matching counts alone are misleading.
