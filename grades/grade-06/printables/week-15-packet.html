What Can a Temperature Graph Prove?

In a fictional school investigation, learners measured a sample's temperature at five evenly spaced times. Their data were −4°C at minute zero, 0°C at two minutes, 2°C at four minutes, 2°C at six minutes, and 6°C at eight minutes. A student described the interval from four to six minutes as a plateau because the recorded temperature stayed the same. Another said the plateau proved that the material had changed from solid to liquid. The group agreed that the numbers alone did not establish that explanation: they would need to know what material was tested and gather additional observations. They plotted the results with time on the horizontal axis and temperature on the vertical axis, checked the scales, and recorded the uncertainty in their conclusions.

Original Academy practice reading; no external text required.

1. State the central claim about evidence and inference.

2. Cite two observations from the data.

3. Explain why the graph cannot by itself prove a specific phase change.
