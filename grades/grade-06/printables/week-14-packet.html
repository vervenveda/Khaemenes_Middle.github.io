The Material Comparison

At a fictional workshop, two teams compared samples by measuring mass and volume. Sample A had mass 36 grams and volume 9 cubic centimeters. Sample B had mass 30 grams and volume 10 cubic centimeters. One student assumed that the second sample had the larger mass-per-volume ratio because 30 sounded substantial. The others calculated 36 ÷ 9 = 4 grams per cubic centimeter and 30 ÷ 10 = 3 grams per cubic centimeter. The team explained that a ratio comparing different units must be calculated before drawing a conclusion. They also noted that measurements can include uncertainty and that a number alone cannot identify a material. Their final report gave the calculations, units, and limitations.

Original fictional Academy passage; available offline.

1. Explain the central claim about fair comparison.

2. Cite two specific details supporting the claim.

3. Identify one mathematical or measurement limitation.
