Counting Evidence Carefully

A fictional research club reviewed two imaginary note collections. In the first, members checked eighteen of twenty-four records, giving 75%. In the second, members checked twenty-one of thirty-five, giving 60%. A student incorrectly claimed the second collection had stronger verification because twenty-one exceeds eighteen. The class compared the shares and reconsidered the claim. They also realized that reviewing a record does not automatically make its contents accurate: genuine research still requires evaluating the source, context and methods. Their revised paragraph named both sample sizes, used accurate percentages and avoided declaring either collection trustworthy based solely on these counts.

Original fictional Academy reading for offline work.

1. Explain the mistaken claim.

2. Cite both fractions and calculated percentages.

3. Explain what percentage evidence cannot prove about a source.
