The Model That Needed a Defense

In a fictional Academy capstone studio, a team constructed a cardboard prototype measuring six centimeters by four centimeters by three centimeters. One learner claimed that twenty-four square centimeters described the amount of space inside the model. Another explained that the floor's footprint was 6×4=24 square centimeters but the enclosed volume was 6×4×3=72 cubic centimeters. During peer review, classmates asked how much surface material a closed model would require, prompting a six-face net. The team noted that an open-top version would have a different surface area but the same geometric capacity. Their final oral defense included units, assumptions, and a warning that material thickness and cutting waste were not represented.

Original fictional Academy reading; suitable for offline study.

1. State the original measurement confusion.

2. Cite the area and volume calculations with correct units.

3. Name a limitation of the model and explain its importance.
