A Formula Needs a Test

In a fictional technology club, students compared two imaginary file-processing tools. Tool A used 7t+12 seconds for t files, while Tool B used 5t+20 seconds. At four files, both took forty seconds. One student declared that the tools were equivalent. Others calculated both at three and six files, finding different results. The group also found a spreadsheet formula error: 3(x+4) had been typed as 3x+4 instead of 3x+12. Testing a few inputs helped them find the mistake, although testing alone could not prove the hypothetical programs accessible, secure, or effective. The revised report explained variables, units, calculations, and limitations.

Original fictional Academy reading; works offline.

1. Identify the mistaken claim about the two models.

2. Cite calculations at two different input sizes.

3. Explain why correct mathematics does not prove a tool is accessible.
