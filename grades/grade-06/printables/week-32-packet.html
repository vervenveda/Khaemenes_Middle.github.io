The Choices Planning Grid

In a fictional classroom, learners used a coordinate grid to organize choices for a pretend community-wellness event. Point A stood at (−4,3), B at (2,3) and C at (2,−2). They calculated A-to-B as six horizontal grid units and B-to-C as five vertical grid units. A student assumed that two people placed on the same line would necessarily have the same needs or choices. The group disagreed: map coordinates identify assigned positions, not private traits, medical conditions or preferences. They labeled axes, used signed coordinates accurately and described their diagram as a planning tool rather than evidence about people.

Original fictional Academy reading, available offline.

1. Summarize the group’s correction.

2. Cite two coordinate facts and calculations.

3. Explain why coordinates do not reveal personal needs.
