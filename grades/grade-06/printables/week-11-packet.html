Original reading: The Survey That Needed a Second Look

Students at a fictional school wanted to learn how much time classmates spent reading each day. They surveyed eight volunteers and recorded these minutes: 10, 15, 15, 20, 20, 20, 25, and 35. A classmate calculated a mean of 20 minutes and claimed every student read exactly 20 minutes. Another student pointed to the different observations and explained that an average summarizes a distribution rather than describing every individual. The students also noted that volunteers from one classroom might not represent the entire school. Before recommending a schoolwide reading policy, they proposed gathering a larger sample from different classes and reporting both the center and variation.

Original Academy fiction, included for independent offline reading.

1. Identify the statistical question and explain why results vary.

2. Cite evidence that refutes the claim “every student reads 20 minutes.”

3. Explain a potential sampling weakness and suggest one improvement.
