A Map Is Not a Forecast

During a fictional resilience workshop, a class plotted three weather stations on a coordinate map. Station A stood at (−4,3), B at (2,3), and C at (2,−2), with each grid square representing one kilometer. Students calculated that A and B were six kilometers apart horizontally, and B and C were five kilometers apart vertically. One student assumed that stations aligned on a map must have the same weather. The group explained that coordinate pairs show only location; a separate table of actual observations would be necessary to support claims about conditions. Their final map displayed named axes, orientation, units, and a caution about drawing conclusions from few observations.

Original fictional reading, available offline.

1. Summarize the central claim.

2. Cite two numerical facts from the map.

3. Explain why coordinate position alone cannot prove a climate pattern.
