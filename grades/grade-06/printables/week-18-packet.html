How Much Can a Signal Graph Tell Us?

In a fictional learning laboratory, students plotted a simulated signal recorded at equally spaced times. The measurements were 0, 2, 0, −2, 0, 2, 0, −2, and 0 arbitrary units. They noticed that the highest reading was two and the lowest was negative two, making the range four units. The positive peaks occurred four sample intervals apart. One learner declared that the graph identified an exact musical note. A classmate replied that the measurements were merely a classroom model, without information about actual measurement conditions, scale or sound source. The students revised their report to separate what the mathematical pattern showed from the properties that would require additional scientific evidence.

Original fictional Academy reading; no internet required.

1. State the passage's main claim about data and interpretation.

2. Cite two numerical pieces of evidence.

3. Explain why a signal graph cannot establish the source of a sound without additional information.
