The Hall Model

In a fictional classroom investigation inspired by classical architecture, students designed a rectangular community hall 8 meters long, 5 meters wide, and 3 meters high. A student calculated 8×5=40 and called that the hall's volume. Their teacher asked what the multiplication described. The class identified forty square meters of floor area, but found volume by including the height: 8×5×3=120 cubic meters. To estimate covering the outside of a rectangular model, they next drew a net with six faces. They noticed that an open-top structure would need less covering material than an otherwise identical closed structure. The fictional dimensions illustrated geometry, not verified ancient architecture.

Original Academy reading; no internet required.

1. State the initial mistake and correction.

2. Cite both calculations and their units.

3. Explain why an open-top model has different surface area.
