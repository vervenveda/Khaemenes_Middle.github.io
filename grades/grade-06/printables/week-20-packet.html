Reading a Tiny Sample Carefully

In a fictional classroom, students examined a simulated microscope image with 80 labeled objects. They recorded 20 in the highlighted category and calculated that 25% of their sample was highlighted. When a second simulation showed 45 highlighted objects among 180 total, the students found that it also represented 25%. A learner argued that this proved every similar image would have exactly one quarter highlighted. The group disagreed: neither a small sample nor a computer simulation can guarantee what another sample will contain. They documented the sample sizes, definitions, and methods used to classify each object before reporting their observations.

Original Academy fictional reading; no internet required.

1. Identify the central claim.

2. Cite the two sample fractions and equivalent percentages.

3. Explain a limitation of predicting new samples.
