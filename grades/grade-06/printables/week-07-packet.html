The Community Map That Needed a Legend

At the beginning of a neighborhood planning meeting, students studied a map drawn on a square grid. A library was marked at (−4, 3), a garden at (2, 3), a clinic at (2, −2), and a school at (−4, −2). The map maker wrote that every grid interval represented 250 meters along a straight street. The student team checked that each coordinate used the horizontal position first and the vertical position second. They also discovered that a point on an axis did not belong to a quadrant.

One student announced that the library was “six meters from the garden.” Another student used the map scale to explain that the two places were six grid intervals apart, so the distance along their shared straight street was 6 × 250 = 1,500 meters. The group agreed that the first claim had confused map units with real-world units. Before recommending a walking route, the students added a note: the grid did not show crossings, closed roads, accessibility barriers, or whether the streets were safe to use. Their revised map would include a clear legend and a warning that a simplified model was not a substitute for an actual route check.

Original Khaemenes Academy practice passage. The locations and events are fictional, and the passage is included here for offline study.

3. Explain the error in “six meters” and why map scale matters.

4. Identify one limitation of the simplified map and one improvement that would help readers.
