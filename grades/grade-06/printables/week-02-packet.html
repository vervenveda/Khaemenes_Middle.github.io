A Garden Ratio and a Careful Annotation

A fictional school garden used two red seedlings for every three yellow seedlings in one planting row. A student recorded the ratio as 2:3. When the gardeners prepared a larger row, they planted six red and nine yellow seedlings. The ratio 6:9 was equivalent because both original quantities had been multiplied by three. One classmate initially claimed that adding four to each original quantity would always preserve a ratio. The group tested 2:3 against 6:7 and saw the two comparisons were not equivalent. During a reading conference, the teacher asked students to annotate the words 'for every,' circle the quantities being compared, and check the calculation with a second example. The students concluded that a mathematical explanation is stronger when it identifies the relationship, not merely a pair of numbers.
