Planning the Habitat Samples

In a fictional habitat classroom project, learners had three liters of harmless colored water to represent material moving through a model ecosystem. Each demonstration bottle held half a liter. One student predicted they could fill only one and a half bottles because three divided by two equals one and a half. Another explained that dividing by one half counts how many half-liter portions fit into three liters: six. Next, the class used three fourths of a kilogram of modeling clay in one-eighth-kilogram portions. They found six portions again. They checked the answers by multiplying each portion size by the number of portions, then carefully described the results as arithmetic for a model rather than evidence about real ecological energy transfer.

Original fictional Academy reading, suitable for offline study.

1. Summarize the misunderstanding about dividing by one half.

2. Cite two numerical calculations supporting the correct explanation.

3. Explain why the numerical model does not prove a scientific claim about energy flow.
