Two Ways of Describing a Journey

In a fictional class project on trade-route models, students examined travel times on two imagined routes. Route A had journeys of 4, 6, 8 and 10 days; Route B had four journeys of 7 days each. Both averages were seven days, so one student concluded that the routes were equally predictable. Others computed the ranges: six days for A and zero for B. The class realized that a mean alone can conceal important variation. They drew dot plots and added a warning: four hypothetical journeys cannot represent every traveler, location or historical period. A careful comparison reports sample size, center, spread, units and limits.

Original fictional Academy reading, available offline.

1. Summarize the mistaken claim.

2. Cite the sample values and two means.

3. Explain why the routes differ in predictability within these samples.
