More Than a Single Score

In a fictional year-end learning conference, two groups reported practice results. Group A recorded 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and Group B recorded 2, 4, 7, 10, 12. Both means and medians were seven, but the ranges were four and ten. A learner claimed the equal averages meant that the distributions were identical. Other students drew dot plots and found that the values were spread differently. Their teacher emphasized that a few imaginary scores cannot label individual abilities or predict future learning. A useful readiness portfolio instead includes checked calculations, examples of revision, and a plan to address uncertain skills.

Original fictional Academy reading, accessible offline.

1. Explain the original mistake.

2. Cite means, medians and ranges for both samples.

3. Explain why the samples cannot determine someone's readiness alone.
