The Water Station's Unit-Rate Question

At a fictional community race, volunteers filled water bottles at a steady rate. The first station filled 36 bottles in six minutes; a second station filled 45 bottles in nine minutes. A student compared the totals and argued that the second station was faster because 45 was greater than 36. Another student divided by the time and found that the first station filled six bottles per minute while the second filled five per minute. The volunteers then checked whether the stations had used the same bottle size and whether the rates had remained steady. They explained that a unit rate describes an amount per one unit of another quantity, and that comparisons are most meaningful when units and assumptions match. Their report included the calculations and a warning about conclusions based on totals alone.
