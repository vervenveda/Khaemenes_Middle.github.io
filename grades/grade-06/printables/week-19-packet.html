The Misleading Picture

In a fictional classroom exhibition, one poster showed 15 supporters out of 20 people surveyed, while another showed 18 supporters out of 30. A learner assumed the second poster represented stronger support because eighteen exceeded fifteen. The class calculated 15/20 = 75% and 18/30 = 60%. They discovered that proportions—not only counts—were needed for a fair comparison. They also noticed that the second graph cropped its vertical axis, exaggerating differences. Their teacher asked them to label the denominators and scales, explain what the surveys could show, and avoid inventing conclusions about people outside the samples.

Original fictional Academy reading, available offline.

1. Summarize the central claim.

2. Cite two numerical pieces of evidence.

3. Explain how a cropped graph may mislead.
