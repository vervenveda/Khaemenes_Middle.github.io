The Design Adjustment Log

In a fictional media-design club, students recorded how far each editing slider moved from its zero reference. A color setting began at −3 units. After moving it seven units toward the positive side, its new setting was +4. Someone mistakenly calculated −3−7=−10 and argued that moving any slider from a negative starting position must make the value more negative. The club drew a number line to show why an increase means moving right. Later, five fictional audio adjustments averaged to zero even though individual settings had changed. The group learned to identify the reference point, use signed numbers carefully, and avoid treating a mathematical setting as a measured physical quantity.

Original fictional Academy reading; works offline.

1. Explain the mistake and its correction.

2. Cite two signed values and the calculation.

3. Explain why an average of zero can hide individual adjustments.
