Original reading: The Community Center Formula

A community center created a simple pricing plan for its evening activities. Registration costs $12 once, and every attended session costs $3. The coordinator wrote the total as C = 12 + 3s, where s represents the number of sessions. A learner checked the expression by counting the price of four sessions: $12 + 4 × $3 = $24. The coordinator explained that the letter s does not stand for dollars; it represents the number of sessions. Another student proposed C = 15s, but that would charge the registration fee again for every session. The group agreed that symbols can communicate a useful rule only when the quantities, units, and assumptions are explained.

Read carefully. This original fictional passage is provided for independent and offline study.

1. Identify the writer's main explanation about using variables.

2. Cite two details showing why C = 12 + 3s fits the pricing rule.

3. Explain why C = 15s is incorrect when the registration fee is paid only once.
