Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 17

Midyear Portfolio and Mathematical Defense

How can I prove growth using calculations, evidence, and corrected work?

Learning targets

Retrieve work from the first sixteen weeks, defend mathematical choices, revise earlier errors, and demonstrate readiness for later cumulative assessment. This is a portfolio checkpoint, not a substitute for the separate course midterm.

Monday — Select portfolio evidence

Choose one piece of work from ratios, signed numbers, expressions, or equations and explain the initial question, method, and corrected reasoning. For example, 3:5 scales to 12:20 by multiplying both terms by four. An answer alone is not sufficient evidence of learning.

Practice: select two older problems, label the skill, and produce a correction note with calculations.

Defend ratio and algebra evidence · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: portfolio, equivalence, justification. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A student claims 9:12 and 12:16 are equivalent. Reduce both to 3:4 or cross-check 9×16=12×12=144 to defend equivalence.

Independent problems:

  1. Simplify 18:24 and explain the divisor.
  2. Evaluate 3(x+2)−x at x=4 and justify substitution.
  3. Solve 5y−4=21; verify in the original equation.
  4. Explain a common error when converting 0.35 to percent.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3:4; 3(6)−4=14; y=5 and 5(5)−4=21; 0.35 = 35%, not 0.35%.

Tuesday — Geometry and measurement defense

A box with dimensions 6 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm has volume 72 cm³, but its closed surface area is 2(24+18+12)=108 cm². Defend why square units and cubic units cannot be substituted. A triangle with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm has area 20 cm².

Practice: sketch a prism, calculate both measures, and explain one common mistake.

Contrast area, surface area and volume · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: dimension, area, surface area, volume. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A 4×3×2 cm closed rectangular prism has surface area 2(12+8+6)=52 cm² and volume 24 cm³. The units refer to different measurements.

Independent problems:

  1. Find volume and surface area of a 5×2×3 cm closed prism.
  2. Find area of a triangle with base 14 cm and height 5 cm.
  3. Find the area of a 9×6 rectangle with a 2×3 corner removed.
  4. Explain why a surface-area answer in cm³ is wrong.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

30 cm³ and 62 cm²; 35 cm²; 48 square units; surfaces measure two-dimensional area, not filled space.

Wednesday — Statistical and graphical reasoning

For observations 2, 4, 4, 6, 9, mean is 5, median is 4, mode is 4 and range is 7. Give a defensible statistical claim, and describe the limits of a small sample. A graph must use labeled, equally spaced axes.

Practice: defend why a single mean does not describe every value; sketch a labeled plot.

Defend a statistical claim · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: mean, median, sample, outlier. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: For 2,3,3,4,18, mean is 6 and median is 3. A summary stating 'typical reading is 6' could hide the unusually high value.

Independent problems:

  1. Find mean and median of 1,2,2,5,10.
  2. Find the range and modes of 2,4,4,7,7,11.
  3. Explain why three classroom observations cannot establish a whole-school trend.
  4. Describe a dot plot for the values 1,1,3,4,4,4.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Mean 4 and median 2; range 9 and modes 4 and 7; sample limited and possibly biased; dots: 1→2, 3→1, 4→3.

Thursday — Revisit applied equations and models

A cart moving at a modeled 5 m/s for 7 seconds travels 35 meters. An inequality such as 12 + 6t ≤ 48 limits whole-number trays to at most six. Explain what is measured, what is modeled, and when only whole numbers are sensible.

Practice: compare the motion equation and the budget inequality; include substitution checks.

Check equations, models and inequalities · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: model, solution, feasible, units. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A club has $70, spends $10 on setup and $6 per tray: 10+6t≤70, so at most ten whole trays. Check t=10 costs $70.

Independent problems:

  1. Solve −3x=18 and verify.
  2. Solve 4+p=−3.
  3. Solve 12+5n≤47 for nonnegative whole n.
  4. At 4 m/s, find distance after 9 seconds under a steady-speed model.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

x=−6; p=−7; n≤7 so n=0 through 7; 36 m.

Friday — Portfolio defense and formative review

Complete a ten-point review: ratio equivalence (2 points), a signed-number equation (2 points), a labeled area/volume comparison (3 points), and a statistical or rate claim supported by numerical evidence (3 points). For each, show an earlier attempt, a correction or validation, and clear units.

Teacher-reviewed evidence is formative. Week 17's saved assessment retains its separate 80% mastery requirement; the course midterm remains a separate examination controlled by the existing program schedule.

Present a portfolio defense · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: claim, correction, counterexample. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: To justify 2(x+3)=2x+6 for every x, use the distributive property. Checking one numerical value alone does not prove a universal identity.

Independent problems:

  1. Compute 2/3 ÷ 1/6 and verify multiplication.
  2. Find 15% of 240.
  3. Reflect (−2,5) across the y-axis.
  4. Find mean of 3,4,4,9.
  5. Write a three-sentence correction of the false claim that a bigger total always means a bigger unit rate.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

4; 36; (2,5); 5; compare totals divided by their corresponding times or quantities; offer a numerical counterexample.
