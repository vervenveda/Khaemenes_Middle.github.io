Week 01 · retrieval and transfer

Comparisons and equivalent ratios

A ratio describes a relationship between quantities; order and units matter.

Try independently before revealing explanations. Each task calls for a calculation or a decision you can justify in your notebook. Review it again after 2 days and 7 days.

Challenge 1

A club has 12 painters and 8 sculptors. Write the ratio of painters to sculptors in simplest form.

Show reasoning after attempting

Divide both parts of 12:8 by 4. The order remains painters:sculptors.

Challenge 2

There are 5 red tiles and 15 blue tiles. What fraction of all tiles are red?

Show reasoning after attempting

There are 20 tiles altogether; red/all = 5/20 = 1/4. This is different from red:blue.

Challenge 3

Does 6:9 equal 10:15? Answer yes or no.

Show reasoning after attempting

Both ratios simplify to 2:3. Verify by cross multiplication: 6×15 = 9×10.

Challenge 4

A recipe uses 2 cups of oats for 3 cups of milk. How much milk is required for 8 cups of oats? Give cups.

Show reasoning after attempting

Multiply the oats by 4, so scale milk by 4 as well: 3×4 = 12 cups.

Challenge 5

A classmate says 4 girls and 6 boys means girls are 4/6 of the class. Is that correct? Answer yes or no.

Show reasoning after attempting

4/6 is girls to boys; the fraction of the whole class that are girls is 4/10 = 2/5.

Week 02 · retrieval and transfer

Equivalent-ratio tables and reasoning

Multiply both quantities by the same nonzero scale factor, and verify context and units.

Try independently before revealing explanations. Each task calls for a calculation or a decision you can justify in your notebook. Review it again after 2 days and 7 days.

Challenge 1

For 3 pencils costing $6, how much do 9 pencils cost at the same rate? Enter dollars.

Show reasoning after attempting

The scale factor is 3. Both quantity and cost triple, from 3:$6 to 9:$18.

Challenge 2

An equivalent ratio to 4:7 has first term 20. What is its second term?

Show reasoning after attempting

Scale the first term by 5, so the second is 7×5 = 35.

Challenge 3

A table lists (2,5), (4,10), (6,14). Is it proportional? Answer yes or no.

Show reasoning after attempting

The first two pairs imply 2.5 units per one, while the last implies 14/6; the unit rate changes.

Challenge 4

In the ratio flour:water = 5:2, how much water is needed for 15 cups of flour? Give cups.

Show reasoning after attempting

Flour was multiplied by 3; water must also triple from 2 to 6 cups.

Challenge 5

A student multiplies only the numerator of 2/3 by 4 and claims 8/3 is equivalent. Correct? Answer yes or no.

Show reasoning after attempting

Equivalent fractions multiply numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number: 2/3 = 8/12.

Week 03 · retrieval and transfer

Unit rates, data, and dimensional reasoning

Name the quantity per one and check units and the assumptions of any comparison.

Try independently before revealing explanations. Each task calls for a calculation or a decision you can justify in your notebook. Review it again after 2 days and 7 days.

Challenge 1

A bus travels 84 miles in 3 hours. What is its average speed in miles per hour?

Show reasoning after attempting

84 miles ÷ 3 hours = 28 miles per hour.

Challenge 2

A 6-ounce package costs $4.50. What is the price per ounce in dollars?

Show reasoning after attempting

$4.50 ÷ 6 ounces = $0.75 per ounce.

Challenge 3

At a constant 12 kilometers per hour, how far is traveled in 2.5 hours? Give kilometers.

Show reasoning after attempting

12 km/h × 2.5 h = 30 km; the hour units cancel.

Challenge 4

Shop A sells 4 notebooks for $10; Shop B sells 6 for $12. Which is cheaper per notebook? Enter A or B.

Show reasoning after attempting

A costs $2.50 per notebook and B costs $2.00. Compare like units.

Challenge 5

A runner finishes 400 meters in 80 seconds. Find their average speed in meters per second.

Show reasoning after attempting

400 m ÷ 80 s = 5 m/s. Average rate alone does not prove constant speed at every moment.
