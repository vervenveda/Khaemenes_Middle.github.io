Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 25

Belief Systems, Law, and Culture: Equations and Inequalities

How can equations and inequalities describe rules and resource constraints?

Learning goals

Represent verbal rules using expressions, solve and check one-variable equations, translate inequalities, graph solution sets, and reverse inequality directions appropriately when dividing by negative values. Examples are fictional and do not describe actual legal systems.

Monday — Equal rules and equations

A fictional community has 3 identical resource bundles plus 5 shared units, totaling 23 units: 3x+5=23. Subtract 5 and divide by 3: x=6. An equation asserts equality; check 3(6)+5=23.

Solve 2x+4=18 and 5y−3=22; substitute answers to verify.

Solve equations using balance · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: equality, inverse, verification. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: For 4x+7=35, subtract 7 then divide by 4 to get x=7; check 4(7)+7=35.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Solve 3x+6=27.
  2. Solve 5y−4=21.
  3. Solve −2a+8=18.
  4. Explain why a correct answer must satisfy the original equation.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

x=7; y=5; a=−5; substitution checks equality rather than only an intermediate step.

Tuesday — Inequality symbols

A fictional meeting hall holds at most 40 people. If 12 seats are reserved and g guests arrive, g+12≤40, so g≤28. 'At least' means ≥, 'more than' means >, and 'fewer than' means <.

Translate: at least 15 delegates; no more than 32 combined attendees; explain endpoints.

Translate boundaries into inequalities · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: at most, at least, inclusive. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: A fictional hall holds at most 50 people. With 18 seats occupied, new guests g satisfy 18+g≤50, so 0≤g≤32 for whole-number guests.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Translate 'at least 17 delegates' into an inequality.
  2. Translate 'fewer than 12 applicants' into an inequality.
  3. If a limit is 26 attendees and 8 already present, write a rule for additional people.
  4. Explain when a boundary receives an open rather than closed circle.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

d≥17; a<12; 8+p≤26, so 0≤p≤18 for whole people; strict symbols < or > use open circle.

Wednesday — Solve inequalities

For 4x+6≤30, subtract 6 and divide by positive 4: x≤6. A number-line solution includes 6 with a filled endpoint. If multiplying/dividing by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

Solve 3x−2>10 and −2x≥8; graph the answers with correct open or filled endpoint.

Solve signed inequalities · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: inequality, reverse, number line. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: −3x≥12 implies x≤−4 because dividing both sides by negative 3 reverses the inequality symbol. Test x=−5: 15≥12.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Solve 5n−3>12.
  2. Solve −4p≤20.
  3. Solve x+7≤−1.
  4. Graph t>−2, labeling boundary and shade.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

n>3; p≥−5; x≤−8; open circle at −2 shaded right.

Thursday — Compare hypothetical policies fairly

Two fictional service plans charge 2t+8 and 3t+3 units. They cost the same at t=5. To know when Plan A costs no more than Plan B, solve 2t+8≤3t+3, giving t≥5. These are mathematical models, not claims about historical or modern laws.

Evaluate both at t=4 and 6; explain the break-even point and feasible domain.

Compare constraints fairly · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: feasible, break-even, domain. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: Two fictional plans A=2t+8 and B=3t+3 are equal at t=5. A≤B for t≥5; t represents a nonnegative count of whole trips.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Compare both plan costs at t=2.
  2. Compare plan costs at t=8.
  3. A budget has $62 with a $14 fee plus $6 per visit. Find maximum whole visits.
  4. Explain why negative trips are not valid even though an inequality solution might include them.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

A12 and B9; A24 and B27; 14+6v≤62 gives v≤8, so max 8; real domain is nonnegative whole-number trip counts.

Friday — Formative demonstration

A: solve 4x+7=31. B: translate 'at most 26 after adding 6' into an inequality. C: solve 5n−4≥21. D: solve −3x<12 and explain why the sign reverses.

Ten-point paper review is formative. Verified mastery uses the independently saved randomized examination.

Demonstrate inequality reasoning and retrieval · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: solution set, justified, verify. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: If −2x<10, then x>−5 after dividing and reversing. The boundary −5 is excluded: at −5, both sides equal 10.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Solve 6y+4=28.
  2. Solve −5x≥15 and test a value.
  3. Recall Week 24: expand 5(a+2).
  4. Recall Week 23: distance from (−3,2) to (4,2) in grid units.
  5. Explain why the official mastery check is separate from these written responses.
