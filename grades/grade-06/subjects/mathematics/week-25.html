Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 25

Belief Systems, Law, and Culture: Equations and Inequalities

How can equations and inequalities describe rules and resource constraints?

Learning goals

Represent verbal rules using expressions, solve and check one-variable equations, translate inequalities, graph solution sets, and reverse inequality directions appropriately when dividing by negative values. Examples are fictional and do not describe actual legal systems.

Monday — Equal rules and equations

A fictional community has 3 identical resource bundles plus 5 shared units, totaling 23 units: 3x+5=23. Subtract 5 and divide by 3: x=6. An equation asserts equality; check 3(6)+5=23.

Solve 2x+4=18 and 5y−3=22; substitute answers to verify.

Tuesday — Inequality symbols

A fictional meeting hall holds at most 40 people. If 12 seats are reserved and g guests arrive, g+12≤40, so g≤28. 'At least' means ≥, 'more than' means >, and 'fewer than' means <.

Translate: at least 15 delegates; no more than 32 combined attendees; explain endpoints.

Wednesday — Solve inequalities

For 4x+6≤30, subtract 6 and divide by positive 4: x≤6. A number-line solution includes 6 with a filled endpoint. If multiplying/dividing by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

Solve 3x−2>10 and −2x≥8; graph the answers with correct open or filled endpoint.

Thursday — Compare hypothetical policies fairly

Two fictional service plans charge 2t+8 and 3t+3 units. They cost the same at t=5. To know when Plan A costs no more than Plan B, solve 2t+8≤3t+3, giving t≥5. These are mathematical models, not claims about historical or modern laws.

Evaluate both at t=4 and 6; explain the break-even point and feasible domain.

Friday — Formative demonstration

A: solve 4x+7=31. B: translate 'at most 26 after adding 6' into an inequality. C: solve 5n−4≥21. D: solve −3x<12 and explain why the sign reverses.

Ten-point paper review is formative. Verified mastery uses the independently saved randomized examination.

Answer key

Reveal after practicing

Monday: x=7; y=5. Tuesday: d≥15; total≤32. Wednesday: x>4; x≤−4. Thursday: at four trips A=16, B=15; at six A=20, B=21. Friday: x=6; n+6≤26; n≥5; x>−4.
