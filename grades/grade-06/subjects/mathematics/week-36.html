Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 36

Final Review and Grade 7 Readiness: Statistics

What mathematical evidence demonstrates readiness for seventh grade?

Learning goals

Calculate and interpret mean, median, mode and range; compare distributions; revisit ratio, rational-number, equation and geometric models; and prepare a defensible learning portfolio. These fictional practice results are not individual ability labels.

Monday — Mean and median review

A fictional class records practice times 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 minutes. Mean is 40÷5=8 minutes, and median is the middle value 8. These measures describe the sample, not every learner.

Practice: find mean and median for 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and label units.

Find center and interpret it · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: mean, median, unit. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: For 2,4,6,8,10 minutes, mean and median are both 6 minutes; neither proves every learner practiced exactly six minutes.

Independent problems:

  1. Find mean and median of 4,6,6,8,11.
  2. Find mean and median of 3,5,7,9.
  3. Explain why the median for 2,4,8,10 is 6.
  4. Write a valid claim about the data 2,4,6,8,10 without calling them individual ability labels.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

Mean 7, median 6; both 6; median is average of middle two, (4+8)/2; sample mean/median 6 and range 8, without inference about ability.

Tuesday — Mode, range and outliers

A review set 2, 3, 3, 5, 12 has median and mode 3, mean 5, and range 10. The outlying 12 influences the mean more than the median.

Practice: find mean, median, mode, and range for 4, 4, 6, 8, 18.

Understand mode, range and outliers · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: mode, range, outlier. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: For 1,3,3,5,18, the mean is 6, median 3, mode 3, range 17. High 18 pulls the mean above the median.

Independent problems:

  1. Find mean, median, mode, range of 2,2,4,7,10.
  2. Find range and mode(s) of 3,3,5,5,8.
  3. If 2,3,3,5,18 replaces 18 with 8, how does the mean change?
  4. Why should a single high value not automatically be removed from data?
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

Mean 5, median 4, mode 2, range 8; range 5 and two modes 3 and 5; mean decreases from 31/5=6.2 to 21/5=4.2; investigate the source and measurement validity first.

Wednesday — Compare readiness data fairly

Fictional practice groups A: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and B: 2, 4, 7, 10, 12 both have median 7, while their ranges are 4 and 10. The greater spread shows different variability, not that one group is more capable.

Practice: draw two labeled dot plots; find both means and ranges and explain one limitation of the comparison.

Compare distributions fairly · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: variability, center, dot plot. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: Two simulated sets A=5,6,7,8,9 and B=2,4,7,10,12 have equal mean and median 7, but ranges 4 and 10. Spread differs without proving competence.

Independent problems:

  1. Find mean, median, range of 4,5,6,7,8.
  2. Find mean, median, range of 1,3,6,9,11.
  3. Draw dot plots of A 2,3,3,4 and B 1,3,3,5, and compare spread.
  4. Explain why comparing two small fictional samples cannot rank real learners.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

Both mean and median 6; range 4; second mean 6, median 6, range 10; A range 2, B range 4 and both have mean 3; samples and selection do not establish individual capability.

Thursday — Cumulative Grade 6 bridge

Review: 3:5 equivalent to 12:20; 25% of 80 = 20; −3+8 = 5; solve 3x+6=21 to obtain x=5; the volume of 2×3×4 prism is 24 cubic units. Explain how a number line, proportion, inverse operation or labeled solid checks an answer.

Practice: create one original problem for ratios, rational numbers, equations and geometry; solve and check each.

Bridge across Grade 6 strands · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: ratio, signed value, equation, geometry. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: The equivalent ratio 3:5=12:20, solution to 3x+6=21 is x=5, and 2×3×4 prism volume is 24 cubic units. Multiple representations can verify these.

Independent problems:

  1. Find 30% of 150.
  2. Compute −3/4+1/2.
  3. Solve 4x+7=31 and verify.
  4. Find area of a 12×7 rectangle minus a 3×4 corner.
  5. Recall Week 27: mean of 3,5,7,9.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

45; −1/4; x=6, check 4(6)+7=31; 72 square units; 6.

Friday — Grade 7 readiness defense

Present a brief portfolio with one accurate statistical comparison, one algebraic solution, one geometry calculation, a corrected past mistake, and a plan for further practice. Independently complete the saved weekly mastery examination and, when eligible, the separate cumulative closed-book final.

Portfolio reflection is formative and cannot substitute for verified weekly mastery or the cumulative final exam.

Demonstrate readiness and identify next steps · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: explanation, evidence, correction. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A learning portfolio can show a correctly calculated result, a revised misconception, and a self-identified practice goal. It is not a substitute for the verified final.

Independent problems:

  1. Find median and range of 1,3,3,5,8.
  2. Find 2/3 ÷ 1/6 and check by multiplication.
  3. Solve −3x≥12 and include the correct boundary.
  4. Calculate surface area of a closed 2×4×5 cm prism.
  5. Explain the difference between formative review, saved weekly mastery and the separately authorized closed-book final.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

Median 3 and range 7; 4, check 4×1/6=2/3; x≤−4; 76 cm²; only authorized assessment records establish verified progression or final completion.
