Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 · Mathematics · Week 13

Volume and Three-Dimensional Modeling

How can 3D models solve real problems?

Learning goals

Model and calculate rectangular-prism volume using unit cubes, whole and fractional edge lengths, unknown dimensions, composite figures, and real design constraints. Always distinguish cubic volume units from square surface-area units.

Monday — Cubic units and volume

A solid occupies volume measured in cubic units, unlike surface area, which measures its outside faces. A rectangular prism with length 5 cm, width 4 cm, and height 3 cm contains 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 cubic centimeters. Imagine 3 layers of 20 unit cubes.

Practice: calculate volumes of prisms measuring 6 × 2 × 4 cm and 8 × 3 × 2 m. Explain why cm³, not cm², is correct.

Tuesday — Fractional edge lengths

For rectangular prisms V = length × width × height even with fractional dimensions. A prism measuring 3/2 cm × 2 cm × 4 cm has volume 12 cm³. Check by multiplying 1.5 × 2 × 4. Fractions describe lengths; the product describes three-dimensional space.

Practice: volume for 1/2 m × 4 m × 3 m; and 5/2 cm × 2 cm × 3 cm. Sketch the dimensions.

Wednesday — Find an unknown dimension

If volume and two dimensions are known, divide: a box has volume 120 cm³, length 6 cm and width 5 cm; height = 120 ÷ (6 × 5) = 4 cm. Substitution verifies 6 × 5 × 4 = 120.

Practice: volume 96 cm³, length 8 cm, width 3 cm. Find height. Explain why knowing only volume does not determine a unique set of dimensions.

Thursday — Composite models and design limits

A model can be split into two nonoverlapping prisms. A 6 × 4 × 2 cm base plus a 2 × 2 × 3 cm raised section has total volume 48 + 12 = 60 cm³, assuming no overlap. A real storage box has wall thickness, packing gaps, and constraints not included in an ideal mathematical model.

Practice: combine 5 × 3 × 2 cm and 2 × 2 × 2 cm prisms without overlap. Compare capacity and surface area; explain why they answer different design questions.

Friday — Independent evidence

A (2 points): compute volume of 7 × 3 × 2 cm. B (2 points): compute volume of 3/2 × 4 × 2 cm. C (3 points): a 90 cm³ box is 5 cm long and 3 cm wide; find height and verify. D (3 points): model two joined nonoverlapping prisms of volumes 4 × 3 × 2 and 2 × 2 × 3, then defend the total and describe a limitation.

Teacher-reviewed practice is worth 10 points, with 8/10 indicating 80% formative achievement, but it never writes verified learner scores. The separate formal assessment determines progression.

Answer key

Reveal after independent practice

Monday: 48 cm³ and 48 m³. Tuesday: 6 m³ and 15 cm³. Wednesday: height = 4 cm; many combinations yield the same product. Thursday: 30 + 8 = 38 cm³. Friday: A 42 cm³; B 12 cm³; C 6 cm; D 24 + 12 = 36 cubic units.
