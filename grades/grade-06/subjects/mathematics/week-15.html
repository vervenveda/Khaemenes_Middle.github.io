Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 15

Thermal Energy: Temperature Data and Graphs

How can mathematical evidence help evaluate changes in matter?

Learning goals

Interpret signed temperatures, compute changes and average rates, make labeled time-series graphs, evaluate evidence, and distinguish mathematical observations from scientific inferences. All example datasets are fictional for offline study.

Monday — Signed temperatures and units

Temperature can be represented on a number line, including negative values. A change is final temperature minus initial temperature: from −4°C to 3°C is 3 − (−4) = +7°C. Temperature is not identical to thermal energy, but its measurements can be graphed and compared.

Practice: calculate changes from −6°C to 2°C and 12°C to 5°C. Explain positive versus negative change.

Tuesday — Read and graph a data table

Fictional classroom measurements at minutes 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 are −4, 0, 2, 2, 6°C. Plot time on the horizontal axis and temperature on the vertical axis. Label axes and use equal spacing. The flat interval from minute 4 to minute 6 indicates unchanged recorded temperature over that interval.

Practice: calculate temperature change between minutes 0 and 8; describe the interval from minutes 4 to 6 without claiming a specific physical process from data alone.

Wednesday — Rates and averages

Average temperature change per minute over an interval equals temperature difference divided by elapsed minutes. From 0 to 2 minutes, −4°C rises to 0°C: +4°C/2 min = +2°C per minute. From minute 4 to 6, rate = 0°C per minute. Average rate need not describe every instant.

Practice: compute average rates for minutes 6–8 and 0–8, including units. Explain when a negative rate describes cooling.

Thursday — Evaluate evidence and models

A recorded temperature plateau can be consistent with a change of state under some circumstances, but the graph alone does not establish phase change. To support a physical explanation, identify the material, conditions, measurement uncertainty, and additional observations. Students should distinguish the observation 'temperature remained at 2°C' from the inference 'the sample was freezing.'

Practice: write a claim using the table; cite two observations; explain one limitation. Compare a 5°C increase over 2 minutes with a 6°C increase over 4 minutes using rates.

Friday — Formative mastery

A (2 points): change from −5°C to 4°C. B (2 points): rate for a change of 8°C in 4 minutes. C (3 points): plot and describe the data (0,−2), (2,2), (4,2), (6,5), with time and temperature axes. D (3 points): examine a plateau and explain what data show versus what an investigator must still verify.

Teacher-reviewed evidence totals 10 points; 8/10 is formative only. Formal progression requires the Academy's separate saved assessment and its dual 80% conditions.

Self-check

Reveal after your practice

Monday: +8°C, −7°C. Tuesday: +10°C overall; constant measured 2°C from minutes 4 to 6. Wednesday: +2°C/min from 6 to 8; +1.25°C/min from 0 to 8. Thursday: 5/2 = 2.5°C/min versus 6/4 = 1.5°C/min. Friday: A +9°C; B +2°C/min; C plateau from minutes 2 to 4; D observation alone does not prove phase change.
