Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 15

Thermal Energy: Temperature Data and Graphs

How can mathematical evidence help evaluate changes in matter?

Learning goals

Interpret signed temperatures, compute changes and average rates, make labeled time-series graphs, evaluate evidence, and distinguish mathematical observations from scientific inferences. All example datasets are fictional for offline study.

Monday — Signed temperatures and units

Temperature can be represented on a number line, including negative values. A change is final temperature minus initial temperature: from −4°C to 3°C is 3 − (−4) = +7°C. Temperature is not identical to thermal energy, but its measurements can be graphed and compared.

Practice: calculate changes from −6°C to 2°C and 12°C to 5°C. Explain positive versus negative change.

Represent signed temperature changes · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: signed number, difference, Celsius. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: From −8°C to +4°C, the change is 4 − (−8) = +12°C. The final temperature is above zero but thermal energy is a separate physical concept.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find the change from −5°C to 6°C.
  2. Find the change from 9°C to −3°C.
  3. A temperature drops 7°C from −2°C; find the final value.
  4. Explain the difference between final reading and temperature change.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

+11°C; −12°C; −9°C; final is a point on temperature scale, change is difference between points.

Tuesday — Read and graph a data table

Fictional classroom measurements at minutes 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 are −4, 0, 2, 2, 6°C. Plot time on the horizontal axis and temperature on the vertical axis. Label axes and use equal spacing. The flat interval from minute 4 to minute 6 indicates unchanged recorded temperature over that interval.

Practice: calculate temperature change between minutes 0 and 8; describe the interval from minutes 4 to 6 without claiming a specific physical process from data alone.

Graph and compare intervals · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: time series, interval, plateau. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: For temperatures (time min, °C) (0,−3),(2,1),(4,1),(6,5), the temperature change over 0–6 min is 8°C; 2–4 is a recorded plateau.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Plot (0,−2),(3,4),(6,4),(9,1), labeling axes.
  2. Find total change from minute 0 to minute 9 in that series.
  3. Which interval is unchanged in recorded values?
  4. Explain why temperature points should not automatically be joined by a claim of constant rate.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Plot as given; +3°C; minute 3 to 6; observations between measurement times are not known.

Wednesday — Rates and averages

Average temperature change per minute over an interval equals temperature difference divided by elapsed minutes. From 0 to 2 minutes, −4°C rises to 0°C: +4°C/2 min = +2°C per minute. From minute 4 to 6, rate = 0°C per minute. Average rate need not describe every instant.

Practice: compute average rates for minutes 6–8 and 0–8, including units. Explain when a negative rate describes cooling.

Calculate and interpret average rates · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: rate, ΔT, elapsed time. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: From 1°C at minute 2 to 9°C at minute 6, average change = (9−1)/(6−2) = 2°C/min. This average does not establish the instantaneous rate.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find average change from −4°C to 2°C over 3 minutes.
  2. A sample cools 8°C in 4 minutes: give signed rate.
  3. Find mean rate from 2°C at t=1 to −4°C at t=4.
  4. Why should a rate include both temperature and time units?
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

+2°C/min; −2°C/min; −2°C/min; °C/min distinguishes a rate from temperature.

Thursday — Evaluate evidence and models

A recorded temperature plateau can be consistent with a change of state under some circumstances, but the graph alone does not establish phase change. To support a physical explanation, identify the material, conditions, measurement uncertainty, and additional observations. Students should distinguish the observation 'temperature remained at 2°C' from the inference 'the sample was freezing.'

Practice: write a claim using the table; cite two observations; explain one limitation. Compare a 5°C increase over 2 minutes with a 6°C increase over 4 minutes using rates.

Separate observation and inference · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: evidence, inference, uncertainty. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: A graph shows 3°C at t=4 and t=6. 'Temperature readings match' is observed; 'a phase change occurred' needs more evidence.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Calculate rates of +6°C in 3 minutes and +8°C in 8 minutes.
  2. List two alternative explanations for an apparent plateau.
  3. A sensor rounds to whole degrees; why might small changes be hidden?
  4. Recall Week 14: 30 g in 10 cm³ gives what rate?
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

2°C/min versus 1°C/min; examples stable heating conditions, sensor resolution or readings, actual process cannot be concluded; rounding masks smaller differences; 3 g/cm³.

Apply signed data and retention · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: verify, model, comparison. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: A drop from +5°C to −7°C is −12°C. Check by adding change to initial: 5 + (−12) = −7.

Independent reasoning:

  2. Find average rate for +15°C over 5 minutes.
  3. Compute the mean of temperatures −2,0,4,6.
  4. Recall Week 10: solve −3x > 12.
  5. Recall Week 13: volume of 2×3×5 cm prism.
