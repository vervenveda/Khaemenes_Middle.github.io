Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 18

Waves, Sound, and Communication: Data and Patterns

How do numeric patterns and graphs help us describe observations?

Learning goals

Interpret time-series tables, graph signed data, describe repeating patterns, calculate count-per-time rates, summarize data statistically, and distinguish mathematical descriptions from unsupported scientific claims. The examples are simulated and self-contained.

Monday — Read periodic data

An idealized wave-like height pattern sampled at equal time steps may be 0, 2, 0, −2, 0, 2, 0, −2, 0 (arbitrary units). Plot sample number horizontally and displacement vertically. Describe repeating patterns without assuming the numbers alone prove how a physical sound wave behaved.

Practice: identify the maximum, minimum, range, and number of sample intervals between repeated peaks.

Tuesday — Interpret tables and graphs

For a simulated sensor, values at seconds 0,1,2,3,4 are 0,3,0,−3,0. The highest measured displacement is +3 and the lowest is −3 units; the range is six units. A mathematical pattern can be described from samples, but its origin needs evidence.

Practice: plot the five ordered pairs on axes with signed vertical scale and determine the range.

Wednesday — Ratios and regular intervals

An invented signal produces 24 pulses in 6 seconds. Its average count rate is 24 ÷ 6 = 4 pulses per second. If the steady-rate assumption holds, predict 40 pulses in ten seconds. Differentiate pulse-count rate from loudness and other unrelated measurements.

Practice: compute 36 pulses in 9 seconds and predict the count in 7 seconds under a steady rate.

Thursday — Statistical descriptions and media claims

Recorded peak readings (in arbitrary units) are 2,4,4,6,9. The mean is 5, median 4, mode 4 and range 7. A claim that every signal had amplitude five is unsupported. Sample size, units and observation method should accompany a report.

Practice: calculate four summary statistics for 1,3,3,5,8; explain how averages can hide individual variability.

Friday — Independent demonstration

A (2 points): read a periodic table and identify its range. B (2 points): compute count rate for 35 pulses in 7 seconds. C (3 points): plot (0,0), (1,2), (2,0), (3,−2), (4,0) with labels and describe the pattern. D (3 points): evaluate an unsupported claim from five readings using mean, median and evidence.

The ten-point teacher-reviewed task is formative. Week 18 verified weekly mastery is separate from the cumulative formal midterm, which follows successful mastery of Weeks 1–18.

Answer explanations

View after attempting exercises

Monday: maximum +2, minimum −2, range 4; peaks occur four intervals apart. Tuesday: maximum +3, minimum −3, range 6. Wednesday: 4 pulses/s and 28 pulses in 7 s. Thursday: 1,3,3,5,8 has mean 4, median 3, mode 3, range 7. Friday B: 5 pulses/s; graph peaks and valleys require correct axis labels and evenly spaced time intervals.
