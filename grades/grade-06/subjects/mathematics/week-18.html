Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 18

Waves, Sound, and Communication: Data and Patterns

How do numeric patterns and graphs help us describe observations?

Learning goals

Interpret time-series tables, graph signed data, describe repeating patterns, calculate count-per-time rates, summarize data statistically, and distinguish mathematical descriptions from unsupported scientific claims. The examples are simulated and self-contained.

Monday — Read periodic data

An idealized wave-like height pattern sampled at equal time steps may be 0, 2, 0, −2, 0, 2, 0, −2, 0 (arbitrary units). Plot sample number horizontally and displacement vertically. Describe repeating patterns without assuming the numbers alone prove how a physical sound wave behaved.

Practice: identify the maximum, minimum, range, and number of sample intervals between repeated peaks.

Describe a repeating sampled pattern · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: sample, period, displacement. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: In simulated readings 0,3,0,−3,0,3,0,−3,0 at equally spaced times, maxima recur four intervals apart and the range is 6 units.

Independent problems:

  1. Find max, min and range of 1,4,1,−2,1,4.
  2. How many index intervals separate the peaks at indices 1 and 5?
  3. Explain why seven data points alone cannot establish the physical source of a signal.
  4. Give a new five-value numerical pattern with a repeated first and last reading.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Max 4, min −2, range 6; four intervals; mathematical samples do not establish causation; e.g. 0,2,0,−2,0.

Tuesday — Interpret tables and graphs

For a simulated sensor, values at seconds 0,1,2,3,4 are 0,3,0,−3,0. The highest measured displacement is +3 and the lowest is −3 units; the range is six units. A mathematical pattern can be described from samples, but its origin needs evidence.

Practice: plot the five ordered pairs on axes with signed vertical scale and determine the range.

Graph signed measurements correctly · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: axis, interval, range. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: The simulated pairs (0,0),(1,2),(2,0),(3,−2),(4,0) require a signed vertical axis and equal time spacing. The values range from −2 to 2.

Independent problems:

  1. Plot (0,−1),(2,3),(4,−1),(6,−5) and label axes.
  2. Find the range of the measured values in that dataset.
  3. Find the signed change from time 0 to time 6.
  4. Identify an interval that may hide unmeasured changes.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Vertical values −1,3,−1,−5; range 8; −4; any interval between recorded times may hide changes.

Wednesday — Ratios and regular intervals

An invented signal produces 24 pulses in 6 seconds. Its average count rate is 24 ÷ 6 = 4 pulses per second. If the steady-rate assumption holds, predict 40 pulses in ten seconds. Differentiate pulse-count rate from loudness and other unrelated measurements.

Practice: compute 36 pulses in 9 seconds and predict the count in 7 seconds under a steady rate.

Relate pulses to elapsed time · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: unit rate, assumption, average. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A simulated counter records 55 pulses over 11 seconds: average 5 pulses/s. Predict 30 pulses in 6 seconds only if the rate is assumed constant.

Independent problems:

  1. Find the average rate for 42 pulses in 7 seconds.
  2. Estimate pulses in 9 seconds at 4 pulses/s.
  3. Explain how 3 pulses/s differs from a three-second interval per pulse.
  4. At 7 pulses/s, how long for 63 pulses if rate remains constant?
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

6 pulses/s; 36 pulses; units are reciprocal quantities; 9 s.

Thursday — Statistical descriptions and media claims

Recorded peak readings (in arbitrary units) are 2,4,4,6,9. The mean is 5, median 4, mode 4 and range 7. A claim that every signal had amplitude five is unsupported. Sample size, units and observation method should accompany a report.

Practice: calculate four summary statistics for 1,3,3,5,8; explain how averages can hide individual variability.

Critique averages and media claims · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: mean, median, misleading claim. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: For measured peaks 1,2,2,3,12 the mean is 4 and the median 2. One high observation affects the mean without making every reading 4.

Independent problems:

  1. Find mean and median of 0,1,1,3,10.
  2. Find the range of 2,3,3,6,7.
  3. Correct 'all readings are five' if mean is five but values vary.
  4. Recall Week 11: why can a biased sample limit a general conclusion?
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Mean 3 and median 1; range 5; mean is a summary and does not imply identical observations; biased selection undermines representativeness.

Friday — Independent demonstration

A (2 points): read a periodic table and identify its range. B (2 points): compute count rate for 35 pulses in 7 seconds. C (3 points): plot (0,0), (1,2), (2,0), (3,−2), (4,0) with labels and describe the pattern. D (3 points): evaluate an unsupported claim from five readings using mean, median and evidence.

The ten-point teacher-reviewed task is formative. Week 18 verified weekly mastery is separate from the cumulative formal midterm, which follows successful mastery of Weeks 1–18.

Synthesize graphing and rate claims · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: evidence, model, critique. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A sequence 0,2,0,−2,0 has measured range 4. A rate of 36 pulses in 9 s equals 4 pulses/s; these are different mathematical descriptions.

Independent problems:

  1. Find mean of −2,0,2,0,0.
  2. Calculate rate for 84 pulses in 12 seconds.
  3. Recall Week 17: compute 4×3×2 prism volume.
  4. Recall Week 15: change from −6°C to 5°C.
  5. Explain why a pattern cannot alone establish loudness or origin.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Mean 0; 7 pulses/s; 24 cm³; +11°C; the sampled values and measurement units do not prove the physical process.
