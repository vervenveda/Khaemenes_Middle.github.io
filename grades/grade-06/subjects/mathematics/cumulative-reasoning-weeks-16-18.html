WEEK 16 · RETRIEVAL + TRANSFER

Motion, units and quantitative models

Calculate average rates from measured distance and elapsed time. State modeling assumptions.

Show your calculation, units and explanation in your notebook. Retry after 2 days and 7 days.

Challenge 1

A cart covers 90 meters in 15 seconds. Find average speed in m/s.

View reasoning after attempting

90 ÷ 15 = 6 meters per second. Average speed does not claim speed was constant.

Challenge 2

At a constant modeled rate of 4 m/s, how far does a cart travel in 12 seconds, in meters?

View reasoning after attempting

Distance = speed × time = 4 × 12 = 48 meters; seconds cancel.

Challenge 3

A graph has time (seconds) on the x-axis and distance (meters) on the y-axis. Points (2,8) and (5,20): what is the slope in m/s?

View reasoning after attempting

(20−8)/(5−2) = 12/3 = 4 m/s, an average interval rate.

Challenge 4

A trip covers 36 meters at average speed 3 m/s. How many seconds elapsed?

View reasoning after attempting

Time = distance/speed = 36/3 = 12 seconds.

Challenge 5

Two hypothetical carts both move 50 m; cart A takes 10 s and B takes 5 s. Which has greater average speed? Enter A or B.

View reasoning after attempting

A averages 5 m/s and B averages 10 m/s. Compare matching units.

View reasoning after attempting

2−(−5)=+7°C; use final minus initial.

WEEK 17 · RETRIEVAL + TRANSFER

Midyear mathematical portfolio defense

Reconstruct evidence and justify conclusions across ratio, algebra, statistics, geometry and models.

Show your calculation, units and explanation in your notebook. Retry after 2 days and 7 days.

Challenge 1

Simplify 18:24 to a:b. Enter the second term b.

View reasoning after attempting

Divide both parts of 18:24 by 6, yielding 3:4. Explain why the same divisor is applied.

Challenge 2

Evaluate 3(x+2)−x at x=4.

View reasoning after attempting

3(4+2)−4=18−4=14. Substitution must precede multiplication.

Challenge 3

Solve 5y−4=21.

View reasoning after attempting

Add four: 5y=25; divide by 5. Check 5(5)−4=21.

Challenge 4

For the data 2, 4, 4, 6, 9, what is the median?

View reasoning after attempting

With five ordered values, the third is the median.

Challenge 5

A prism measures 2 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm. Find volume in cm³.

View reasoning after attempting

2×5×3=30 cubic centimeters; label cubic units.

Prior-week recall 6

Retrieval Week 10: Does −3 satisfy x < −3? Answer yes or no.

View reasoning after attempting

A strict boundary excludes equality; −3 is not less than itself.

WEEK 18 · RETRIEVAL + TRANSFER

Waves, periodic data and responsible inference

Interpret simulated repeating sequences without treating a numeric pattern as proof of physical causes.

Show your calculation, units and explanation in your notebook. Retry after 2 days and 7 days.

Challenge 1

For simulated readings 0, 4, 0, −4, 0, 4, find the range.

View reasoning after attempting

Maximum 4 minus minimum −4 gives range 8.

Challenge 2

In readings indexed 0 to 8: 0,3,0,−3,0,3,0,−3,0, how many index intervals separate the two positive peaks?

View reasoning after attempting

Peaks occur at indices 1 and 5. The difference is four sample intervals.

Challenge 3

An instrument records 24 pulses in 6 seconds. What is the average pulses-per-second rate?

View reasoning after attempting

24/6=4 pulses per second, assuming all 24 occurred within the stated duration.

Challenge 4

A simulated series is −2, 1, 4, 1, −2. What is its maximum value?

View reasoning after attempting

The maximum reading is +4; distinguish maximum from magnitude and range.

Challenge 5

Can a repeating table of numbers alone prove the cause of a physical sound? Answer yes or no.

View reasoning after attempting

Patterns describe the values. Physical explanation requires independent measurements and a supported model.

Prior-week recall 6

Retrieval Week 16: A cart moves 72 meters in 9 seconds. What is average speed in m/s?

View reasoning after attempting

72/9=8 m/s; retain units.
