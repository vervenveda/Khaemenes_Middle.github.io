Week 13 · cumulative reasoning

Volume and three-dimensional modeling

Compute and interpret cubic volume, distinguish area units, and model fractional and composite rectangular prisms.

Write a model or calculation, label its units, then explain and verify your answer. Revisit after two days and seven days.

Challenge 1

A prism measures 6 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm. Find its volume in cm³.

Show reasoning after attempting

Multiply length × width × height: 6×4×3=72 cm³.

Challenge 2

A prism has volume 96 cm³, length 8 cm and width 4 cm. What is its height in cm?

Show reasoning after attempting

Height = volume/(length×width) = 96/(8×4) = 3 cm.

Challenge 3

Find the volume of a prism measuring 1/2 m by 3 m by 4 m, in m³.

Show reasoning after attempting

(1/2)×3×4=6 m³. Fractional edges do not change the cubic unit.

Challenge 4

Two nonoverlapping blocks have volumes 24 cm³ and 18 cm³. What is their combined volume?

Show reasoning after attempting

Because blocks do not overlap, add volumes: 24+18=42 cm³.

Challenge 5

Can a surface-area measurement in cm² be directly added to volume in cm³? yes or no.

Show reasoning after attempting

Square units and cubic units measure different quantities, so adding them is meaningless.

Earlier-learning retrieval 6

Retrieval Week 12: What is the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm, in cm²?

Show reasoning after attempting

Area=1/2×10×6=30 cm². Area is not volume.

Week 14 · cumulative reasoning

Mass, volume, ratios and model limits

Compute mass-per-volume rates, use proportional scaling, and distinguish mathematical agreement from scientific identification.

Write a model or calculation, label its units, then explain and verify your answer. Revisit after two days and seven days.

Challenge 1

A fictional sample has mass 72 g and volume 24 cm³. Find mass per unit volume in g/cm³.

Show reasoning after attempting

72÷24=3 g/cm³. The numerator and denominator units matter.

Challenge 2

For a uniform modeled sample with mass-per-volume rate 4 g/cm³, what mass in g corresponds to 15 cm³?

Show reasoning after attempting

4 g/cm³ ×15 cm³=60 g; cubic-centimeter units cancel.

Challenge 3

A sample's mass is 45 g at volume 9 cm³. What mass in g is predicted at 12 cm³ under a proportional model?

Show reasoning after attempting

Rate=45÷9=5 g/cm³. Multiply 5×12=60 g, assuming constant rate.

Challenge 4

Samples A and B have equal mass-per-volume rates. Does this alone prove they are the same material? yes or no.

Show reasoning after attempting

The measured rate alone is insufficient to establish identity; context and additional observations matter.

Challenge 5

On a graph with volume on x-axis and mass on y-axis, what is the slope for points (2,8) and (5,20)?

Show reasoning after attempting

Slope=(20−8)/(5−2)=12/3=4 g/cm³.

Earlier-learning retrieval 6

Retrieval Week 13: Find the volume of a 3 cm × 5 cm × 2 cm rectangular prism in cm³.

Show reasoning after attempting

3×5×2=30 cm³, which differs from its surface area.

Week 15 · cumulative reasoning

Signed temperature and time-series reasoning

Calculate signed temperature change and average rates while separating the graph's observations from scientific explanations.

Write a model or calculation, label its units, then explain and verify your answer. Revisit after two days and seven days.

Challenge 1

Temperature changes from −6°C to 4°C. What is the change in °C?

Show reasoning after attempting

Final minus initial = 4−(−6)=+10°C.

Challenge 2

Temperature changes from 8°C to −3°C. What is the signed change in °C?

Show reasoning after attempting

Final minus initial = −3−8=−11°C.

Challenge 3

A model temperature rises from 2°C to 14°C in 4 hours. What is its average change in °C per hour?

Show reasoning after attempting

(14−2)/4=3°C/hour. Average rate does not imply every hourly interval changed equally.

Challenge 4

At hour 0 the reading is −2°C, at hour 2 it is 6°C. What is the average change per hour?

Show reasoning after attempting

(6−(−2))/(2−0)=8/2=4°C/hour.

Challenge 5

Does an increasing temperature graph alone establish that a chemical reaction occurred? yes or no.

Show reasoning after attempting

A graph shows measured temperature changes; a cause requires additional evidence.

Earlier-learning retrieval 6

Retrieval Week 14: A sample measures 50 g for 10 cm³. Find mass per volume in g/cm³.

Show reasoning after attempting

50÷10=5 g/cm³, with the rate's units labeled.
