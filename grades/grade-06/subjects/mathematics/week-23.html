Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 23

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns: Coordinate Planes

How do coordinates help describe and evaluate spatial patterns?

Learning goals

Plot signed coordinates in all four quadrants, label axes and units, calculate horizontal and vertical distances, perform reflections, and distinguish location from measurements. All stations and maps are fictional models.

Monday — Coordinate axes and quadrants

A coordinate pair (x,y) names a location on a model grid. The first coordinate is horizontal (east/right positive); the second is vertical (north/up positive). Point (3,−2) lies in Quadrant IV, while (−4,2) lies in Quadrant II. Coordinates describe position on a chosen grid, not actual geographic longitude and latitude.

Practice: plot (2,3), (−3,4), (−2,−1), (4,−3) and identify quadrants.

Tuesday — Map simulated stations

A fictional weather map positions Station A at (−4,3), B at (2,3), and C at (2,−2) on a square grid measured in kilometers. A to B is 6 units horizontally. B to C is 5 units vertically. Axis orientation and units must be stated to interpret distances.

Practice: find horizontal distance from (−5,1) to (3,1), and vertical distance from (2,−4) to (2,5).

Wednesday — Reflections and patterns

Reflecting (−3,2) across the y-axis gives (3,2); reflecting it across the x-axis gives (−3,−2). The sign of only the coordinate perpendicular to the mirror axis changes. These operations can model symmetries in a diagram without implying symmetry in actual weather.

Practice: reflect (4,−5) across each axis. Explain why (−4,5) reflects across both axes, not just one.

Thursday — Compare data claims

Fictional stations at (−2,4), (0,4), and (3,4) are plotted along a common horizontal line. That geometry does not imply equal rainfall or temperature. For a meaningful graph, distinguish spatial position from measured variables and cite a separate data table.

Practice: design a grid with three positions and separate temperature labels; identify one limitation of a small station network.

Friday — Formative review

A (2 points): identify quadrant of (−5,−2). B (2 points): horizontal distance from (−3,2) to (4,2). C (3 points): reflect (2,−4) across each axis. D (3 points): plot (−2,3), (2,3), (2,−1), with axes, units, and an evidence-based limitation.

Ten-point formative practice does not award verified mastery. The saved randomized examination requires both overall and mathematics strand mastery.
