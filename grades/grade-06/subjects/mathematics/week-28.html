Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 28

World Languages and Cultural Communication: Ratios and Rates

How can proportional comparisons describe communication-project data?

Learning goals

Simplify part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios, build equivalent ratios, calculate unit rates, compare proportions and identify limits of fictional samples. All counts represent imaginary classroom activities, not judgments about languages or people.

Monday — Language sample ratios

A fictional language club recorded 18 greeting cards with drawings and 12 without. Drawings-to-no-drawings is 18:12 = 3:2; drawings-to-total is 18:30 = 3:5. Ratios require a named numerator and denominator.

Practice: compare 24 translated labels with 16 untranslated labels using part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios.

Compare named quantities · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: ratio, part-to-part, part-to-whole. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A fictional display has 28 drawings and 12 text-only cards. Drawing:text-only=28:12=7:3 while drawing:total=28:40=7:10.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Simplify the ratio of 18 translated to 24 untranslated labels.
  2. For 18 translated and 24 untranslated labels, find translated:total.
  3. Explain why 3:4 is not translated:total in that example.
  4. Construct a 2:5 ratio using 21 total cards.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3:4; 3:7; total is 42 so 18:42=3:7; 6 in first category and 15 in second.

Tuesday — Equivalent ratios

A bilingual classroom display contains 3 pictograms for every 5 text labels. With 12 pictograms, the equivalent number of labels is 20 because 3:5 = 12:20. Multiplying both quantities preserves the ratio.

Practice: scale 2:7 to a first quantity of 10, and explain why adding the same number to both terms generally fails.

Construct equivalent tables · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: equivalent ratio, scale factor, proportional. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A pictogram:caption ratio 4:7 scales to 12:21 by multiplying both numbers by three. Adding three to each number does not preserve the ratio.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Complete 3:8=12:?.
  2. Scale 5:6 to first term 20.
  3. Determine whether 4:9 and 12:27 are equivalent.
  4. Explain why 2:3 and 4:5 are not equivalent.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

32; 20:24; yes, factor 3; 2/3 differs from 4/5.

Wednesday — Unit rates

A fictional transcription team labels 48 items in 6 minutes, an average of 8 items per minute. At an unchanged rate it could label 80 in 10 minutes. Unit rates assume sustained conditions, so predictions should be qualified.

Practice: compute 63 labels in 7 minutes and the prediction for 12 minutes.

Model unit-rate comparisons · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: rate, per, quotient. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A fictional cataloger processes 84 cards in 7 minutes, averaging 12 cards/min. At steady pace, 9 minutes predicts 108 cards.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find rate for 90 labels in 10 minutes.
  2. Find predicted total in 12 minutes at 7 labels/minute.
  3. Find time needed for 96 cards at 8 cards/minute.
  4. Explain why an average rate may not hold during an entire task.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9 labels/min; 84 labels; 12 minutes; fatigue, task difficulty and interruptions can alter rates.

Thursday — Compare communication models

Fictional practice group A accurately matches 18 of 24 cards (75%); group B matches 21 of 35 (60%). Comparing successes alone favors B, but comparing proportions favors A. These exercises do not judge real languages or cultures.

Practice: compare 15 of 20 and 18 of 30; identify fair denominators and limits.

Evaluate proportional claims · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: denominator, percent, evidence. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A fictional group A gets 30 correct of 40 (75%), B gets 33 of 55 (60%). The larger raw correct count does not imply the higher success proportion.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Compare 16 of 20 and 18 of 30 as percentages.
  2. Compare 24 of 60 to 14 of 28.
  3. Explain why different group sizes require comparable denominators.
  4. Recall Week 27: find median of 2,4,8,10.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

80% versus 60%; 40% versus 50%, second higher; percentage normalizes the counts by their sample sizes; median 6.

Defend fair communication models · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: proportion, scale, limitation. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A 6×9 cm graphic enlarged to 10×15 cm has scale factor 5/3 for both sides, preserving shape.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Simplify 36:54.
  2. Find x in 4:7=20:x.
  3. At 11 labels/minute, predict labels in 8 minutes assuming a steady rate.
  4. Recall Week 26: surface area of closed 2×3×4 cm prism.
  5. Explain why a classroom ratio cannot establish cultural or linguistic superiority.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

2:3; x=35; 88 labels; 52 cm²; arbitrary classroom counts do not measure the value of any language or culture.
