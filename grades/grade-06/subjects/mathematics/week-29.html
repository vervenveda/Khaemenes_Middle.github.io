Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 29

Research Writing: From Question to Thesis — Percent

How can percentages support accurate descriptions of research evidence?

Learning goals

Convert fractions into percentages, calculate percentages of quantities, compare proportions with different denominators, determine percent increases, and limit claims to what fictional sample counts can justify.

Monday — Fractions, decimals, percents

A fictional research team checks 18 of 24 citations: 18/24=0.75=75%. Report both the numerator and denominator before using a percentage as evidence.

Practice: convert 12/20 and 21/35 to percents; describe what each counts.

Represent research sample percentages · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: numerator, denominator, percent. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: In a fictional audit, 21 of 28 references are checked, so 21/28=0.75=75%. The claim applies only to those 28 references.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Convert 15/25 to a percent.
  2. Convert 27/45 to a decimal and percent.
  3. A report labels 18 of 30 as 18%. Explain and correct the error.
  4. Create a sample count representing 40% of 50 notes.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

60%; 0.6 and 60%; 18/30=60%, not 18%; 20 verified of 50 total.

Tuesday — Percent of a quantity

A team sets a goal to verify 40% of 150 notes: 0.4×150=60 notes. Percent of a whole is not the same as percent change.

Practice: calculate 30% of 90 and 15% of 200.

Calculate percentages of quantities · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: percent, whole, part. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A checklist covers 35% of 160 items: 0.35×160=56 items. Check 56/160=35%.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find 20% of 145.
  2. Find 12.5% of 80.
  3. If 18 is 30% of the whole, find the whole.
  4. A rounded percentage predicts 42.5 reviewed documents. Explain why a counted-document total must be an integer.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

29; 10; 60; count is whole and a fractional model output suggests an estimate or rounded percent.

Wednesday — Comparing research samples

Two fictional drafts have checked evidence rates 18/24=75% and 21/35=60%. Counting checked items alone would favor the second draft, but the first has the larger fraction.

Practice: compare 16 of 20 to 24 of 40 and justify denominators.

Compare unequal sample sizes fairly · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: normalized comparison, sample, proportion. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: Draft A checks 16 of 20 items (80%); B checks 27 of 45 (60%). B has more checked items but a lower share.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Compare 15 of 30 with 24 of 40.
  2. Compare 35 of 50 with 45 of 75.
  3. Explain why comparing only checked item counts can mislead.
  4. Write a cautious two-sentence sample conclusion using one comparison.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

50% versus 60%, second higher; 70% versus 60%, first higher; denominators differ; conclusions should refer to reviewed samples, not overall truth.

Thursday — Percent increase and research limits

A fictional source log rises from 40 to 50 reviewed notes: increase 10, percent increase 10/40×100=25%. Distinguish a change relative to the original from the percentage of notes checked.

Practice: calculate the increase from 60 to 75 reviewed items and explain why sample review percentages cannot alone establish research truth.

Distinguish relative change from percentage points · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: base, relative change, percentage point. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A research log grows from 40 to 54 entries: gain 14, relative growth 14/40=35%. By contrast 40% to 54% is 14 percentage points.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find percent increase from 80 to 100.
  2. Find percent decrease from 60 to 45.
  3. A checked share rises from 50% to 65%. State point change and relative increase.
  4. Recall Week 28: find the unit rate for 72 cards in 9 minutes.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

25% increase; 25% decrease; 15 percentage points and 30% relative; 8 cards/minute.

Friday — Independent evidence

Compute percentages for 9/12 and 14/20; find 35% of 80; calculate percent increase from 20 to 25; and write a cautious evidence claim with a named denominator.

This is formative work. Only the separately saved randomized examination awards verified mathematics mastery.

Defend a research-related numeric claim · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: evidence, precision, inference. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: In a fictional set of 100 sources, 72 have complete citations. 72% is a count-based coverage measure, not proof that each citation is accurate.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Convert 9/12 into percent.
  2. Find 35% of 120.
  3. Compare 18/24 with 24/40 using percent.
  4. Recall Week 27: find median of 2,5,7,10.
  5. Explain how larger citation coverage might still leave unsupported arguments.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

75%; 42; 75% versus 60%; 6; coverage does not establish credibility or truth.
