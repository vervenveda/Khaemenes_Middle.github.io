Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 29

Research Writing: From Question to Thesis — Percent

How can percentages support accurate descriptions of research evidence?

Learning goals

Convert fractions into percentages, calculate percentages of quantities, compare proportions with different denominators, determine percent increases, and limit claims to what fictional sample counts can justify.

Monday — Fractions, decimals, percents

A fictional research team checks 18 of 24 citations: 18/24=0.75=75%. Report both the numerator and denominator before using a percentage as evidence.

Practice: convert 12/20 and 21/35 to percents; describe what each counts.

Tuesday — Percent of a quantity

A team sets a goal to verify 40% of 150 notes: 0.4×150=60 notes. Percent of a whole is not the same as percent change.

Practice: calculate 30% of 90 and 15% of 200.

Wednesday — Comparing research samples

Two fictional drafts have checked evidence rates 18/24=75% and 21/35=60%. Counting checked items alone would favor the second draft, but the first has the larger fraction.

Practice: compare 16 of 20 to 24 of 40 and justify denominators.

Thursday — Percent increase and research limits

A fictional source log rises from 40 to 50 reviewed notes: increase 10, percent increase 10/40×100=25%. Distinguish a change relative to the original from the percentage of notes checked.

Practice: calculate the increase from 60 to 75 reviewed items and explain why sample review percentages cannot alone establish research truth.

Friday — Independent evidence

Compute percentages for 9/12 and 14/20; find 35% of 80; calculate percent increase from 20 to 25; and write a cautious evidence claim with a named denominator.

This is formative work. Only the separately saved randomized examination awards verified mathematics mastery.

Answer key

Open after completing practice

Monday: 60% and 60%. Tuesday: 27 and 30. Wednesday: 80% versus 60%. Thursday: 25% increase. Friday: 75%, 70%, 28, and 25% increase.
