Week 10 · cumulative review

Inequalities and Decision Rules

Reason about strict/inclusive boundaries, valid solution sets and inverse operations.

Attempt each independently, show units and a mathematical justification, then revisit after two and seven days.

Challenge 1

Does 4 satisfy x < 4? Answer yes or no.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

The strict inequality excludes its boundary; 4 is not less than 4.

Challenge 2

Solve x + 7 ≤ 12. Enter the greatest possible x.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Subtract 7 on both sides: x ≤ 5. Equality is included.

Challenge 3

Solve −2x > 8. Enter the integer boundary value (not included).

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Divide both sides by negative 2 and reverse the inequality: x < −4.

Challenge 4

A hall has room for at most 30 people. Already 18 are inside. What is the greatest number that can still enter?

Show worked reasoning after attempting

18 + n ≤ 30; subtract 18 to get n ≤ 12.

Challenge 5

Solve x/3 ≥ 5. Enter the least possible x.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Multiply by positive 3, leaving the inequality direction unchanged: x ≥ 15.

Week 11 · cumulative review

Statistics: Questions, Data and Distribution

Differentiate statistical questions, measure center/spread and evaluate evidence limits.

Attempt each independently, show units and a mathematical justification, then revisit after two and seven days.

Challenge 1

For 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, find the median.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

The data are already sorted. The middle (third) value is 3.

Challenge 2

For 4, 6, 8, 10, find the mean.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Add the four values to get 28, then divide by four: 7.

Challenge 3

For 3, 4, 9, 11, find the range.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Range = maximum − minimum = 11 − 3 = 8.

Challenge 4

Is 'How many books do students in this class read each month?' a statistical question? yes or no.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Responses vary between students and months, so the question anticipates variability.

Challenge 5

A sample includes only basketball team members to estimate exercise among all students. Is it clearly representative? yes or no.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

The selection may overrepresent students who exercise. A representative sample must consider the full student population.

Retrieval, Week 10: Does x = 3 satisfy x ≥ 3? yes or no.

Week 12 · cumulative review

Geometry: Area, Surface Area and Design

Use correct area formulas, decompose without double counting and distinguish open from closed prisms.

Attempt each independently, show units and a mathematical justification, then revisit after two and seven days.

Challenge 1

A triangle has base 12 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. What is its area in cm²?

Show worked reasoning after attempting

A = 1/2 × 12 × 5 = 30 cm². Perpendicular height is required.

Challenge 2

A 7 cm by 4 cm rectangle has a 2 cm by 2 cm square cut from a corner. What area remains in cm²?

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Rectangle 28 cm² minus removed square 4 cm² gives 24 cm².

Challenge 3

Find the surface area of a CLOSED rectangular prism 2 cm × 3 cm × 4 cm, in cm².

Show worked reasoning after attempting

2(lw + lh + wh) = 2(6 + 8 + 12) = 52 cm².

Challenge 4

An OPEN-TOP box measures 2 cm × 3 cm × 4 cm. How much surface area in cm² (no lid)?

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Closed surface 52 cm² minus top face 2 × 3 = 6 cm² gives 46 cm².

Challenge 5

A rectangle's length doubles while its width remains fixed. Does its area double? yes or no.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

Area = length × width; doubling one factor doubles the product.

Earlier-learning retrieval 6

Retrieval, Week 11: Find the mean of 5, 7, and 9.

Show worked reasoning after attempting

(5 + 7 + 9) ÷ 3 = 7.
