Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 · Week 07 · Mathematics

Coordinate Planes and Geography

Essential question: How can coordinates accurately describe places, movements and patterns?

Learning targets and vocabulary

By Friday you will read and plot ordered pairs in all four quadrants, identify the origin (0,0), distinguish the horizontal x-axis from the vertical y-axis, identify quadrant signs, reflect locations across an axis, and interpret a map using a stated scale. Prerequisite: positive/negative numbers and number lines from Week 6.

Start here: The coordinate pair (x,y) always records horizontal movement first and vertical movement second. Right is positive x; left is negative x; up is positive y; down is negative y. An axis point (such as (0,4)) is not inside any quadrant.

Monday — Coordinates as precise directions

Imagine two perpendicular number lines meeting at the origin. To plot (3,2), start at (0,0), travel 3 steps right, then 2 steps up. To plot (−4,1), travel 4 left, then 1 up. The order matters: (2,3) is usually a different place from (3,2).

Worked example: (−2,−3) means 2 units left and 3 units down. It is in Quadrant III. (0,−5) lies on the negative y-axis, not in Quadrant III or IV.

Guided practice: 1. Describe the route from (0,0) to (4,−2). 2. Plot (−3,5) and (5,−3); describe their differences. 3. Name the coordinate of the origin. Draw and label both axes before placing points.

Worked example: P = (−5,3) is in Quadrant II; Q = (4,−1) is in Quadrant IV. The point R = (−3,0) lies on the x-axis and has no quadrant.

Guided practice: 4. Classify (6,−2), (−1,−7), (4,5), and (0,3). 5. Give one different coordinate in each quadrant. 6. Explain why (−4,0) is not in Quadrant II or III.

Wednesday — Make and interpret a community map

These are fictional practice coordinates on a grid, not real GPS positions. Community library L = (−4,3), garden G = (2,3), clinic C = (2,−2), and school S = (−4,−2). Plot and label all four points. Each grid interval represents 250 meters along a horizontal or vertical path.

Worked example: Library to garden is 6 horizontal grid units; 6 × 250 m = 1,500 m along the grid. Garden to clinic is 5 vertical grid units; 5 × 250 m = 1,250 m. Travel here follows straight grid streets, not diagonal shortcuts.

Investigation: 7. How far is the clinic from the school along their shared horizontal grid line? 8. Starting at the school, give step-by-step directions to the library, then to the garden. 9. Explain why real map scales, route restrictions, and landmarks must be checked before using such a model for actual navigation. Label axes and units on your map.

Thursday — Reflections, reasoning and error analysis

A reflection across the x-axis keeps x the same and reverses the sign of y: (3,−4) → (3,4). Reflection across the y-axis reverses x: (3,−4) → (−3,−4). Points on the line of reflection do not move.

Worked example: Reflect A = (−2,5) across the x-axis to get A′ = (−2,−5), then across the y-axis to get A″ = (2,−5). The resulting point is in Quadrant IV.

Independent practice: 10. Reflect (−6,2) across the x-axis. 11. Reflect (−6,2) across the y-axis. 12. A student plots (−3,4) by moving 3 right and 4 up. Explain the mistake and correct it. 13. Point M is (−5,−1) and N is (−5,4). Find their vertical separation in grid units. 14. Explain how a negative coordinate is different from a negative distance.

Friday — Independent mastery evidence

A (2 points): Plot and identify the quadrants of (−3,2) and (4,−2), including labeled axes. B (2 points): Identify whether (0,−4) belongs to any quadrant and explain. C (3 points): Reflect (−4,3) across both axes, separately, and label the new coordinates. D (3 points): On a grid with 200 meters per unit, a library at (−3,1) and a park at (2,1) lie on the same street. Find their along-street distance, show the calculation, and explain the model's limitation.

Teacher-reviewed rubric: A: 2, B: 2, C: 3, D: 3, totaling 10. A score of 8/10 represents 80% on this instructional task. This task is formative evidence, not an automatic verified assessment or curriculum unlock. Official progression remains with the Academy's assessment engine and student record.

Move 3 left and 4 up. 13. 5 units. 14. A negative coordinate marks location relative to an origin, while distance is a nonnegative length. Remediation: If your work is below 80%, practice identifying x and y first, redraw four sample coordinates, correct mistakes with a labeled diagram, and complete a fresh authorized mastery assessment when ready.
