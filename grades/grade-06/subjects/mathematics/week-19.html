Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 19

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation: Ratios and Rates

How can proportional reasoning help us evaluate visual information?

Learning goals

Simplify ratios, compare part-to-part and part-to-whole quantities, apply scale factors, calculate unit rates, and assess visual-media claims from numerical evidence.

Monday — Ratios in image grids

A fictional picture has 24 light pixels and 16 dark pixels. The light-to-dark ratio is 24:16 = 3:2. The light-to-total ratio is 24:40 = 3:5. Ratios require identifying which quantities are compared.

Practice: find light-to-dark and light-to-total ratios for 18 light and 12 dark squares. Explain the difference.

Tuesday — Equivalent scales

A drawing is enlarged by a scale factor of three: a 4 cm side becomes 12 cm, and a 7 cm side becomes 21 cm. Multiplying only one dimension changes the proportions. Image resizing can distort a model if dimensions are scaled inconsistently.

Practice: enlarge a 5 cm by 8 cm rectangle by a factor of two. Reduce a 12 cm by 18 cm rectangle by one-third.

Wednesday — Unit rates for media

A hypothetical classroom display shows 48 frames during six seconds. The average display rate is 48 ÷ 6 = 8 frames/s. A mathematical unit rate does not by itself determine image quality or explain human perception.

Practice: calculate 63 frames in 7 seconds and predict frames in 12 seconds under a constant-rate assumption.

Thursday — Assess visual claims

Two fictional graphics report 15 of 20 responses and 18 of 30 responses. The proportions are 75% and 60%; comparing raw counts alone misleads. Examine axis labeling, denominator choice, and whether the evidence supports a claim.

Practice: compare 12 out of 16 with 15 out of 25, and explain how cropped scales can distort interpretation.

Friday — Independent evidence

A (2 points): simplify 20:30. B (2 points): scale 6 cm to 24 cm and find factor. C (3 points): calculate 54 frames in 9 s and predict at 12 s. D (3 points): compare 18/24 to 21/35, with percentages and a fair conclusion.

This 10-point task is formative. The randomized examination independently controls verified mastery, and Week 19 requires Week 18 mastery and the separately verified midterm before progression.

Crosswalk studio — Angles, coordinates and visual evidence

Monday bridge: In an ideal plane-mirror diagram, both the incoming and reflected angles are measured from the normal. If incidence is 30°, reflection is 30°; do not measure from the mirror surface by mistake.

Tuesday bridge: Draw a coordinate grid with fictional rays from (0,0) through (3,3) and (−3,3). Describe the symmetry about the vertical normal without claiming these schematic coordinates are physical observations.

Wednesday bridge: A model enlarged from a 4 cm image to 12 cm uses scale factor 3. A marked 2 cm feature becomes 6 cm at the same scale. Label lengths in cm and angles in degrees.

Thursday bridge: A fictional design shows 3 of 12 rays labeled incorrectly. Compute the error proportion 3/12=25%; explain why the sample says nothing about real mirrors.

Friday evidence: Submit a labeled ideal reflection diagram with a normal, one angle pair, coordinate points, a scale calculation and a one-paragraph correction of an unsupported visual claim. Check: 30°, 30°; scale 3; 6 cm; 25%.

Answer key

Check after attempting

Monday: 3:2 and 3:5. Tuesday: 10 cm × 16 cm and 4 cm × 6 cm. Wednesday: 9 frames/s and 108 frames. Thursday: 75% versus 60%. Friday: A 2:3; B 4; C 6 frames/s and 72 frames; D 75% versus 60%.
