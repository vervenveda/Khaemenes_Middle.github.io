Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 19

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation: Ratios and Rates

How can proportional reasoning help us evaluate visual information?

Learning goals

Simplify ratios, compare part-to-part and part-to-whole quantities, apply scale factors, calculate unit rates, and assess visual-media claims from numerical evidence.

Monday — Ratios in image grids

A fictional picture has 24 light pixels and 16 dark pixels. The light-to-dark ratio is 24:16 = 3:2. The light-to-total ratio is 24:40 = 3:5. Ratios require identifying which quantities are compared.

Practice: find light-to-dark and light-to-total ratios for 18 light and 12 dark squares. Explain the difference.

Interpret part-to-part and part-to-whole · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: ratio, total, reduction. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A pixel grid contains 30 light and 20 dark squares. Light:dark=3:2, while light:total=30:50=3:5.

Independent problems:

  1. A grid has 24 colored and 16 uncolored squares. Find colored:uncolored.
  2. Find colored:total for the same grid.
  3. A student writes 3:5 for colored:uncolored. Diagnose the denominator error.
  4. Construct an example where red:blue is 2:3 with 25 total red and blue tiles.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3:2; 3:5; 3:5 is colored:total; 10 red and 15 blue.

Tuesday — Equivalent scales

A drawing is enlarged by a scale factor of three: a 4 cm side becomes 12 cm, and a 7 cm side becomes 21 cm. Multiplying only one dimension changes the proportions. Image resizing can distort a model if dimensions are scaled inconsistently.

Practice: enlarge a 5 cm by 8 cm rectangle by a factor of two. Reduce a 12 cm by 18 cm rectangle by one-third.

Scale visual models accurately · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: scale factor, proportion, dimensions. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A design measures 6 by 9 cm. Scaling both dimensions by 4/3 gives 8 by 12 cm; relative shape is preserved.

Independent problems:

  1. Scale a 4×7 rectangle by factor 3.
  2. Reduce a 15×24 image by factor 1/3.
  3. Explain why scaling width but not height distorts a picture.
  4. A segment grows from 5 cm to 17.5 cm. Find its scale factor.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

12×21; 5×8; proportions change; scale factor 3.5.

Wednesday — Unit rates for media

A hypothetical classroom display shows 48 frames during six seconds. The average display rate is 48 ÷ 6 = 8 frames/s. A mathematical unit rate does not by itself determine image quality or explain human perception.

Practice: calculate 63 frames in 7 seconds and predict frames in 12 seconds under a constant-rate assumption.

Compare frame-per-time rates · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: frames, second, average, rate. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: An animation displays 90 frames over 15 seconds: 6 frames/s on average. A display-rate measure alone cannot determine audience perception.

Independent problems:

  1. Find rate for 144 frames in 12 s.
  2. At 10 frames/s, calculate expected frames in 7 s.
  3. Find seconds required for 108 frames at 9 frames/s.
  4. Explain why total frame count alone is not enough for a fair rate comparison.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

12 frames/s; 70 frames; 12 seconds; elapsed duration must be included.

Thursday — Assess visual claims

Two fictional graphics report 15 of 20 responses and 18 of 30 responses. The proportions are 75% and 60%; comparing raw counts alone misleads. Examine axis labeling, denominator choice, and whether the evidence supports a claim.

Practice: compare 12 out of 16 with 15 out of 25, and explain how cropped scales can distort interpretation.

Analyze media statistics responsibly · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: denominator, scale, sample. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: Graphic A shows 27/36 supports =75%; Graphic B shows 30/50 =60%. Though B has more supporters, A has a larger support percentage.

Independent problems:

  1. Compare 14/20 and 18/30 as percentages.
  2. Identify a misleading interpretation of 40 of 80 versus 30 of 50.
  3. Explain why a y-axis starting at 90 instead of zero can exaggerate small bar differences.
  4. Recall Week 04: distinguish a change from 20% to 30% in percentage points and relative percent.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

70% versus 60%; 50% versus 60%, so the second has the larger proportion despite fewer total supporters; truncated axes can overstate visual changes; +10 percentage points and +50% relative.

Friday — Independent evidence

A (2 points): simplify 20:30. B (2 points): scale 6 cm to 24 cm and find factor. C (3 points): calculate 54 frames in 9 s and predict at 12 s. D (3 points): compare 18/24 to 21/35, with percentages and a fair conclusion.

Synthesize media ratios and readiness · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: claim, proportional, evidence. Define these terms and use one in your reasoning.

Worked model: A drawing enlarged from 4×6 cm to 10×15 cm preserves dimensions by factor 2.5; a changed aspect ratio would not preserve the geometric proportions.

Independent problems:

  1. Simplify 42:56.
  2. A design 8×12 is scaled to 12×18. Find factor.
  3. Find 75 frames in 5 seconds and predict 9-second total at a constant rate.
  4. Recall Week 18: find range of readings −4,0,1,6.
  5. Explain why Week 19's progress requires the separate Week 18 mastery and midterm checks.

Crosswalk studio — Angles, coordinates and visual evidence

Monday bridge: In an ideal plane-mirror diagram, both the incoming and reflected angles are measured from the normal. If incidence is 30°, reflection is 30°; do not measure from the mirror surface by mistake.

Tuesday bridge: Draw a coordinate grid with fictional rays from (0,0) through (3,3) and (−3,3). Describe the symmetry about the vertical normal without claiming these schematic coordinates are physical observations.

Wednesday bridge: A model enlarged from a 4 cm image to 12 cm uses scale factor 3. A marked 2 cm feature becomes 6 cm at the same scale. Label lengths in cm and angles in degrees.

Thursday bridge: A fictional design shows 3 of 12 rays labeled incorrectly. Compute the error proportion 3/12=25%; explain why the sample says nothing about real mirrors.

Friday evidence: Submit a labeled ideal reflection diagram with a normal, one angle pair, coordinate points, a scale calculation and a one-paragraph correction of an unsupported visual claim. Check: 30°, 30°; scale 3; 6 cm; 25%.
