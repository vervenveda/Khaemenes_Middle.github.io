Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 09

Equations and Balanced Arguments

How do balance and evidence make arguments stronger?

Learning goals

Understand the equal sign as a relationship; solve one-step addition, subtraction, multiplication and division equations; represent word problems; verify solutions by substitution; critique incorrect reasoning. Vocabulary: equation, solution, equality, inverse operation, variable, substitution. All instruction is included for offline learning.

Monday — Equality and balance

An equation states that two expressions have equal value. A balance model means changing one side requires the same change on the other. Example: x + 7 = 19; subtract 7 from each side to get x = 12. Verify 12 + 7 = 19.

Practice: 1. y + 9 = 23. 2. z − 6 = 11. Explain how each inverse operation preserves equality.

Tuesday — Multiplication and division equations

For 4a = 28, divide both sides by 4: a = 7. For b/5 = 8, multiply both sides by 5: b = 40. A solution must make the original equation true.

Practice: 3. 6t = 54. 4. p/3 = 12. 5. Explain why dividing only one side of 3x = 15 by 3 is not valid.

Wednesday — Model real decisions

A fictional after-school club pays a $6 registration fee and $4 per visit. A student's total bill is $30. Model 6 + 4v = 30, subtract 6: 4v = 24, then divide by 4: v = 6 visits. Verify using the original situation and label visits and dollars.

Practice: 6. Five equal-priced notebooks cost $35; write and solve 5n = 35. 7. A student's savings increase by $15 to $47: write and solve s + 15 = 47.

Thursday — Critique an argument

A student claims x + 5 = 18 has solution x = 23 because they added 5. Check the claim with substitution: 23 + 5 ≠ 18. Correctly subtract 5 to find x = 13. Explain that a convincing mathematical claim includes its inverse operation and numerical verification.

Practice: 8. Solve r − 12 = −3. 9. Solve −4m = 20. 10. A student's solution says h/6 = 4 gives h = 2; diagnose and fix.

Friday — Independent evidence and remediation

A (2 pts): Solve x + 11 = 30, showing matching operations. B (2 pts): Solve 7y = 42 and verify. C (3 pts): A $9 membership plus $5 per visit totals $34; write and solve an equation and state units. D (3 pts): Explain why 2x = 16 implies x = 8, and critique the incorrect claim x = 14.

Teacher-reviewed total 10 points, with 8/10 representing 80% instructional performance. This activity is formative; only the separately saved formal assessment establishes progression. Below 80%, revisit worked examples, complete corrections, and undertake a fresh authorized check.

Self-check answer key

Reveal after completing your attempts

1. y = 14; 2. z = 17; 3. t = 9; 4. p = 36; 5. one-sided changes break equality; 6. n = $7; 7. s = $32; 8. r = 9; 9. m = −5; 10. h = 24. Friday A: x = 19; B: y = 6; C: 9 + 5v = 34 so v = 5 visits; D: divide both sides by 2, and 14 would give 28, not 16.
