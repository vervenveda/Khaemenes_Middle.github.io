Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 · Mathematics · Week 16

Forces, Motion, and Systems: Quantitative Models

How can measurements and graphs help describe motion?

Learning goals

Compute distance–time rates and interpret units; use ratio tables and coordinate graphs; solve one-step distance equations; compare fair trials; and distinguish mathematical models from causal science claims. These hypothetical examples require no outside reading.

Monday — Measure distance and time

A fictional cart moves 24 meters in 6 seconds. Its average speed is distance divided by elapsed time: 24 ÷ 6 = 4 meters per second. This is a mathematical description of motion, not an explanation of the force causing it.

Practice: calculate average speeds for 30 m in 5 s and 42 m in 7 s. Label the units and compare the rates.

Calculate average speed · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: distance, time, speed. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: A cart travels 36 m in 9 s. Its average speed is 36/9 = 4 m/s; it may speed up and slow down within those 9 seconds.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find the average speed of 75 m in 15 s.
  2. Find the average speed of 84 m in 12 s.
  3. Why is 5 m/s different from 5 s/m?
  4. A cart travels 3 m/s on average for 8 s; find distance if a constant-rate model is appropriate.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

5 m/s; 7 m/s; units are reciprocal measures; 24 m.

Tuesday — Ratio tables and motion graphs

For a hypothetical constant speed of 3 m/s, distance = 3 × time. A table gives (time in seconds, distance in meters): (0,0), (2,6), (4,12), (6,18). Plot time along the horizontal axis and distance vertically. An equal-time interval gives equal distance increase in this ideal model.

Practice: create a table for 5 m/s at times 0, 1, 3, and 6 seconds; draw and explain the graph.

Model distance-time patterns · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: ordered pair, rate table, graph. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: For constant speed 4 m/s: times 0,2,4,7 s correspond to distances 0,8,16,28 m. Plot (time,distance) with labeled axes.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Make a table at 6 m/s for t=0,1,3,5 s.
  2. Find slope from points (2,10) and (6,30).
  3. Explain why (0,0) is included when distance is measured from starting position.
  4. Identify why a graph through (0,5) does not represent zero starting distance.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

0,6,18,30 m; (30−10)/(6−2)=5 m/s; at t=0 displacement traveled is zero; nonzero starting distance changes the interpretation.

Wednesday — Expressions and unknown quantities

A simplified distance model is d = vt for steady speed v and time t. At 4 m/s, d = 4t. To find time, solve t = d ÷ v: 36 meters at 4 m/s takes 9 seconds. To find speed, divide 45 meters by 9 seconds to get 5 m/s.

Practice: solve 6t = 54; determine a speed for 56 meters in 8 seconds. Test your answers by substitution.

Solve motion equations · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: equation, inverse operation, speed. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: With d=vt, a cart moving at 7 m/s for t seconds travels 7t meters. If d=63 m, t=63/7=9 s.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find t for 48 m at 6 m/s.
  2. Find average v for 90 m in 15 s.
  3. At steady 2.5 m/s find modeled distance after 12 s.
  4. Explain when using d=vt is inappropriate for a changing speed.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

8 s; 6 m/s; 30 m; a single fixed speed cannot reproduce a changing-rate path exactly.

Thursday — Compare models and investigate fairness

In a fictional cart trial, Trial A covers 32 m in 8 s and Trial B covers 35 m in 7 s. Their average speeds are 4 m/s and 5 m/s. The longer distance does not alone establish the faster average speed. Record controlled variables, measurement error, and the distinction between observed motion and a claim about a physical force.

Practice: compare 54 m in 9 s with 40 m in 5 s; explain one source of measurement uncertainty and how to conduct a fair comparison.

Compare trials fairly · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: fair comparison, control, uncertainty. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: Trial A travels 70 m in 10 s (7 m/s); Trial B travels 72 m in 12 s (6 m/s). Total distance alone does not determine greater average speed.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Compare 54 m in 6 s with 80 m in 10 s.
  2. Describe one source of timing error.
  3. For travel at 3 m/s, predict distance in 11 s.
  4. Recall Week 15: from −6°C to 2°C, find the signed change.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9 versus 8 m/s, first faster; reaction time or instrument precision; 33 m; +8°C.

Friday — Explain and verify

A (2 points): find average speed for 48 m in 8 s. B (2 points): find distance at 5 m/s for 7 s. C (3 points): solve 4t = 44 and verify. D (3 points): graph (0,0), (2,8), (4,16), (6,24), label axes and explain one limitation of a constant-speed model.

Integrate quantitative modeling · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: model, evidence, verify. Define and use each term with an original example.

Worked model: For an ideal 5 m/s speed, a 45 m path takes 9 s. Real experiments require specifying start and finish and measuring uncertainty.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Calculate 96 m in 12 s.
  2. Draw a table for 4 m/s at 0,2,6 s.
  3. Solve 6t=72, and verify the result.
  4. Recall Week 14: mass rate for 56 g over 14 cm³.
  5. Recall Week 12: triangle area with base 12 cm and height 5 cm.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

8 m/s; distances 0,8,24 m; t=12 s, check 6×12=72; 4 g/cm³; 30 cm².
