Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 · Mathematics · Week 16

Forces, Motion, and Systems: Quantitative Models

How can measurements and graphs help describe motion?

Learning goals

Compute distance–time rates and interpret units; use ratio tables and coordinate graphs; solve one-step distance equations; compare fair trials; and distinguish mathematical models from causal science claims. These hypothetical examples require no outside reading.

Monday — Measure distance and time

A fictional cart moves 24 meters in 6 seconds. Its average speed is distance divided by elapsed time: 24 ÷ 6 = 4 meters per second. This is a mathematical description of motion, not an explanation of the force causing it.

Practice: calculate average speeds for 30 m in 5 s and 42 m in 7 s. Label the units and compare the rates.

Tuesday — Ratio tables and motion graphs

For a hypothetical constant speed of 3 m/s, distance = 3 × time. A table gives (time in seconds, distance in meters): (0,0), (2,6), (4,12), (6,18). Plot time along the horizontal axis and distance vertically. An equal-time interval gives equal distance increase in this ideal model.

Practice: create a table for 5 m/s at times 0, 1, 3, and 6 seconds; draw and explain the graph.

Wednesday — Expressions and unknown quantities

A simplified distance model is d = vt for steady speed v and time t. At 4 m/s, d = 4t. To find time, solve t = d ÷ v: 36 meters at 4 m/s takes 9 seconds. To find speed, divide 45 meters by 9 seconds to get 5 m/s.

Practice: solve 6t = 54; determine a speed for 56 meters in 8 seconds. Test your answers by substitution.

Thursday — Compare models and investigate fairness

In a fictional cart trial, Trial A covers 32 m in 8 s and Trial B covers 35 m in 7 s. Their average speeds are 4 m/s and 5 m/s. The longer distance does not alone establish the faster average speed. Record controlled variables, measurement error, and the distinction between observed motion and a claim about a physical force.

Practice: compare 54 m in 9 s with 40 m in 5 s; explain one source of measurement uncertainty and how to conduct a fair comparison.

Friday — Explain and verify

A (2 points): find average speed for 48 m in 8 s. B (2 points): find distance at 5 m/s for 7 s. C (3 points): solve 4t = 44 and verify. D (3 points): graph (0,0), (2,8), (4,16), (6,24), label axes and explain one limitation of a constant-speed model.

Ten-point teacher-reviewed formative check: 8/10 signals practice proficiency, not formal verified mastery. The separate randomized exam controls saved scores and progression.

Answer key

Open after completing the exercises

Monday: both 6 m/s. Tuesday: distances 0, 5, 15, 30 m. Wednesday: t=9 s and speed 7 m/s. Thursday: 6 m/s versus 8 m/s; Trial B has greater average speed. Friday: A 6 m/s; B 35 m; C 11 s; D graph shows 4 m/s in the ideal constant-speed model.
