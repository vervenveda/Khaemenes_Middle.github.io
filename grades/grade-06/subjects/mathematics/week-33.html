Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 33

Technology, Data, and Ethical Creation: Expressions

How can algebra help us test and critique a computational model?

Learning goals

Translate algorithm descriptions into expressions, evaluate variables, distribute multiplication, combine like terms, and test competing models. All timing data are fictional; calculations do not replace real usability or accessibility testing.

Monday — Variables in algorithms

A fictional accessibility tool spends 4 seconds checking each of n files and 6 seconds initializing: T=4n+6. For n=5, T=26 seconds. Variables represent changing inputs and coefficients represent rates.

Practice: write 3 seconds per file plus 8 startup seconds, then evaluate for 7 files.

Translate algorithm costs into algebra · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: variable, coefficient, fixed cost. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: A fictional tool spends 5 seconds per record plus 9 seconds setup: T=5r+9 seconds. For r=6, T=39 seconds.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Write T for 3 seconds per item and 7 startup seconds.
  2. Evaluate T=6n+4 when n=5.
  3. Identify coefficient and constant in C=8x+11.
  4. Explain why T=5(r+9) is not the same model as T=5r+9.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

T=3n+7; 34; coefficient 8 and constant 11; 5(r+9)=5r+45, so setup is multiplied incorrectly.

Tuesday — Equivalent expressions and debugging

A program's hypothetical time estimate is 3(x+4). Distribute correctly: 3x+12, not 3x+4. Check equivalence by substituting x=5: both correct forms yield 27.

Practice: expand 4(y+3) and 2(3a+5). Explain how substitution detects a bug.

Expand and debug expressions · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: distribute, equivalent, substitution. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: Three batches each use (2x+4) seconds: 3(2x+4)=6x+12. Checking x=2 gives 24 for both forms.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Expand 4(y+5).
  2. Expand 2(3p−4).
  3. Show a counterexample to 3(x+2)=3x+2.
  4. Compute 2(a+3) and 2a+6 when a=−2.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

4y+20; 6p−8; x=0 gives 6 versus 2; both equal 2.

Wednesday — Combining like terms

A fictional processing log has 5a+2a−3 units. The variable terms combine to 7a−3. Do not combine a variable term with a constant.

Practice: simplify 6p+3p+4 and 8m−3m+2+5.

Simplify repeated steps · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: like term, constant, combine. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: A simulated log contains 7a+4a−5 operations: 11a−5, not 6a. Separate a-terms from constant terms.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Simplify 9n−4n+6.
  2. Simplify 3(x+2)+2x.
  3. Evaluate 4b+6b−3 at b=3.
  4. Explain why 3r+2t cannot become 5rt.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

5n+6; 5x+6; 27; different variables are not like terms and multiplication changes meaning.

Thursday — Compare data-tool models

Two simulated export processes take 7t+12 and 5t+20 seconds. They tie for t=4, but differ at other input sizes. Compare at t=3 and t=6; identify units and assumptions instead of claiming any algorithm is always faster.

Practice: construct a labeled comparison table and explain why one matching data point is insufficient.

Compare competing performance models · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: break-even, domain, model. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: Two fictional tools have times A=6t+14 and B=4t+22. They tie at t=4, when both take 38 seconds. For t>4, B is smaller.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Calculate A and B at t=2.
  2. Calculate A and B at t=6.
  3. Solve 6t+14=4t+22 to verify break-even.
  4. Explain why fictional timing equations cannot establish a genuine software accessibility result.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

A26 vs B30; A50 vs B46; t=4; real runtime and usability require measured tests.

Friday — Independent model review

A: express 5 seconds per record plus 9 seconds setup. B: expand 4(x+2). C: simplify 6a+2a−5. D: compare 3n+8 with 5n+2 at n=4 and identify a limitation.

This review is formative. The separate randomized assessment controls verified progression.

Defend an algebraic model · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: substitution, justify, validate. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: To prove 5(x+3)=5x+15 for every x, apply the distributive property; testing a single number is supporting evidence, not proof.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Simplify 4(a+2)−a.
  2. Evaluate 2x²+3 for x=−2.
  3. Recall Week 32: reflect (−3,4) over the y-axis.
  4. Recall Week 30: divide 5/6 by 1/3.
  5. Identify one uncertainty in a fictional technology timing model.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3a+8; 11; (3,4); 5/2; e.g. real processing times vary with file sizes and hardware.
