Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 27

Trade Routes, Migration, and Exchange: Statistics

How do measures of center and spread support careful comparisons?

Learning goals

Calculate mean, median, mode, and range, compare small data distributions, create displays, and distinguish claims supported by a sample from claims about all historical exchange. All route data are invented for practice.

Monday — Mean of route data

A fictional trading study lists travel times of 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 days. The mean is (4+6+8+10+12)/5=8 days. A mean summarizes the sample but does not describe each trip.

Practice: calculate the mean of 3, 5, 7, 9, 11; describe the unit and sample.

Compute and interpret mean · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: mean, total, frequency. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: Fictional travel times 5,7,9,11,13 days total 45 days; mean 45/5=9 days. The mean need not equal every trip time.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find mean of 2,4,6,8,10.
  2. Find mean of 3,3,6,8.
  3. Five trips total 45 days. Find their mean.
  4. Explain why mean travel time is not a guarantee of arrival time.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

6; 5; 9 days; individual observations vary around the mean.

Tuesday — Median and mode

A simulated exchange survey lists 2, 3, 3, 5, 12 items. Its median is 3 and mode is 3; its mean is 5. The high value of 12 pulls the mean upward. Order values before locating the median.

Practice: find mean, median, mode of 4, 4, 6, 8, 18; compare the measures.

Compare median, mode and outliers · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: median, mode, outlier. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: For 2,3,4,5,16 the median is 4, mean 6, and no mode appears because all values occur once. The high 16 raises mean.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find mean, median, mode of 2,4,4,5,15.
  2. Find median of 3,5,7,9.
  3. Find mode(s) of 3,3,4,5,5,6.
  4. Identify which center is less affected by replacing 15 with 40 in a dataset.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Mean 6, median 4, mode 4; median 6; bimodal: 3 and 5; median is less affected.

Wednesday — Range and spread

Two hypothetical routes have travel times A: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and B: 2, 4, 7, 10, 12. Both have median 7 but ranges 4 and 10 respectively. Spread matters when planning travel reliability.

Practice: compute the range of 3, 5, 8, 11, 13 and describe why range alone is limited.

Describe spread and limitations · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: range, variability, data set. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: Route A times 6,7,8,9,10 have range 4 and mean 8; Route B times 2,5,8,11,14 have range 12 and mean 8. Equal means can hide different variability.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find range of 4,5,9,12,18.
  2. Find mean and range of 1,3,5,7,9.
  3. Explain why a single unusually extreme value can change the range dramatically.
  4. Which model shows more spread: 8,8,8,8 or 5,7,9,11?
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

14; mean 5, range 8; range depends only on minimum and maximum; second set, range 6 versus zero.

Thursday — Distribution and fair comparisons

Suppose Route A has 4, 6, 8, 10 days and Route B has 7, 7, 7, 7. Both means are 7 days, but Route B has no variation in this fictional sample. A data table or dot plot can reveal patterns that averages conceal.

Practice: compare medians and ranges, draw dot plots, and state a limitation of small samples.

Build displays and test claims · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: dot plot, distribution, representation. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: Two fictional sample sets A: 4,6,8,10 and B: 7,7,7,7 both have mean 7; their dot plots differ dramatically.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Make a frequency table for 2,2,3,4,4,4,5.
  2. Find median and range of Route A 4,6,8,10.
  3. Find median and range of Route B 7,7,7,7.
  4. Why might a convenience sample of a few trips be unrepresentative?
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

2→2,3→1,4→3,5→1; median 7, range 6; median 7, range 0; selection may omit different seasons, routes or conditions.

Friday — Evidence defense

A: mean of 4, 6, 8. B: median of 2, 3, 5, 9, 20. C: range of 3, 7, 12, 15. D: explain why samples with equal means may have different ranges and what that means for a fictional route.

Paper exercises remain formative; the independent randomized verified examination records mastery.

Synthesize statistical evidence · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: data, sample, claim. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: For 2,4,4,6,14 the mean is 6, median 4 and range 12; the mean is pulled upward by the highest value.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find mean and median of 2,3,3,4,13.
  2. Find range and mode of 1,2,2,5,11.
  3. Recall Week 26: volume of a 3×4×5 cm prism.
  4. Recall Week 23: identify quadrant of (−3,−4).
  5. Draft a claim about fictional route reliability supported by both center and spread.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Mean 5, median 3; range 10, mode 2; 60 cm³; Quadrant III; answers should mention observed mean/median, spread, sample limitations.
