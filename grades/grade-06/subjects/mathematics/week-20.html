Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 20

Cells, Systems, and Scientific Models: Percentages

How do percentages help describe observations of small structures?

Learning goals

Convert fractions to percentages, determine a percent of a whole, compare samples fairly, estimate with proportional reasoning, and acknowledge uncertainty. All sample numbers are hypothetical and suitable for offline study.

Monday — Understand percentages

In a fictional microscope image with 80 marked cells, 20 are classified in a particular category. The share is 20/80 = 1/4 = 25%. The percent describes the observed image, not necessarily an entire organism.

Practice: express 12 of 60 and 18 of 90 as percents; identify denominators.

Tuesday — Percent of a whole

For a model population of 150 cells, 40% is 0.40 × 150 = 60 cells. In reverse, if 45 of 180 cells have a feature, the percentage is 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%.

Practice: find 30% of 120; determine the percent represented by 21 of 70.

Wednesday — Compare sample percentages

Sample A has 18 of 60 marked objects (30%). Sample B has 24 of 80 (30%). Raw counts differ but sample proportions agree. Use the same category definition and a meaningful denominator.

Practice: compare 15 of 50 to 20 of 80; write a conclusion with calculations.

Thursday — Estimate and address uncertainty

A sample of 40 image regions yields 10 marked. The observed proportion is 25%. Predicting 25 marked in a new set of 100 is a proportional estimate, not a guarantee: sampling methods and image classification can influence the result.

Practice: make a model-based prediction for 200 regions; state one limitation of extrapolation.

Friday — Formative review

A (2 points): express 9/36 as a percent. B (2 points): find 35% of 80. C (3 points): compare 12/40 against 21/70. D (3 points): explain why a 25% rate observed in one simulated image does not automatically prove a population-wide rate.

Ten-point formative review; 8/10 supports readiness. The separately saved randomized examination determines verified mastery.

Crosswalk studio — Cell diagrams, scale and measurement

Monday bridge: A fictional cell drawing 8 cm across represents an object with modeled diameter 40 micrometers. The illustration uses 80 mm ÷ 0.04 mm = 2,000× enlargement; a model label must identify both units.

Tuesday bridge: If 1 cm on a fictional diagram represents 5 µm, then a 3 cm drawing represents 15 µm. A scale bar expresses a modeled relation; it is not independent microscopy evidence.

Wednesday bridge: Compare two fictional diagrams of 4 cm and 6 cm representing the same 20 µm object; their enlargement factors are 2,000× and 3,000×, not different actual cell sizes.

Thursday bridge: A schematic of 80 cells with 20 marked gives 20/80=25%. A separate fictional sample of 40 with 12 marked gives 30%. Different proportions alone do not prove differences between real tissues.

Friday evidence: Label a fictional cell illustration, convert µm to mm, calculate an enlargement, draw a scale bar and compare two proportions with an evidence-limit sentence. Check: 2,000×; 15 µm; 2,000× versus 3,000×; 25% versus 30%.

Answer key

Open after completing practice

Monday: both 20%. Tuesday: 36 and 30%. Wednesday: 30% versus 25%. Thursday: estimate 50 in 200, subject to sampling assumptions. Friday: 25%; 28; both 30%; sample estimates do not establish population truths.
