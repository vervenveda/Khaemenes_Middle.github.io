Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 34

A++ Big Question Capstone Research: Equations and Inequalities

How can mathematical constraints make a research proposal testable?

Learning goals

Translate research-budget problems into equations and inequalities, solve and verify one-variable relationships, graph solution boundaries, respect whole-number domains, and distinguish calculated claims from genuine empirical evidence. Numerical scenarios are fictional.

Monday — Frame a research question mathematically

A hypothetical capstone tests an exhibition budget: each prototype costs 4 units, with 7 units of setup, and 31 units available. Write 4x+7≤31. Solve x≤6; if x counts prototypes, x is a nonnegative whole number.

Practice: translate 'five units per sample plus nine setup units, at most 44' into an inequality, then solve.

Model project limits and budgets · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: constraint, nonnegative, inequality. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: At most 65 units are available with 5 units setup and 10 per prototype: 5+10p≤65, so 0≤p≤6 for whole-number prototypes.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Write and solve 7s+8≤43 for nonnegative whole s.
  2. A project uses 12 units setup and 4 per trial within 40; find maximum whole trials.
  3. Explain why a result p≤6 alone does not permit negative prototype counts.
  4. Check whether seven prototypes fit the 5+10p≤65 budget.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

s≤5, so 0–5 samples; 7 trials; item counts must be whole nonnegative; no, total 75 exceeds 65.

Tuesday — Equations and checking

A fictional project spends 3 units per source bundle plus 8 in preparation for a total of 29. The equation 3n+8=29 gives n=7. Substitute to verify 3(7)+8=29. A solution must also make sense in the defined context.

Practice: solve 2x+5=21 and 4y−3=25; explain checks.

Solve and verify equations · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: solution, isolate, substitute. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: A fictional project has a setup cost of 6 and three units per case for total 33: 3n+6=33, so n=9, check 3(9)+6=33.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Solve 4x+5=37.
  2. Solve 6y−7=29.
  3. Solve −2t+3=15.
  4. Explain how a substitution check catches arithmetic mistakes.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

x=8; y=6; t=−6; the solution must recreate both sides of original equation.

Wednesday — Inequality boundaries

A fictional prototype space permits no more than 35 display units. If each station requires five units, 5s≤35, so s≤7. Show a filled circle at 7 on a number line; restrict s to nonnegative integers.

Practice: solve 3t+6≤24 and 4t−5>11. Mark inclusive versus strict bounds.

Distinguish inclusive and strict constraints · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: boundary, closed circle, open circle. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: 5s≤35 includes s=7, while 5s<35 excludes s=7. The first graph uses a filled endpoint at 7 and the second an open one.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Solve 4x+6≤26.
  2. Solve 3t−5>10.
  3. Explain which endpoint is used for y≥−2.
  4. A group must have fewer than 20 people; express valid nonnegative whole counts.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

x≤5; t>5; filled at −2 shaded right; 0 through 19 people.

Thursday — Negative coefficients and review

A mathematical test −2x≥8 requires reversing the inequality on division: x≤−4. For a count of actual items, a negative solution set may reveal an inappropriate model or infeasible constraint. Always discuss interpretation.

Practice: solve −3x<12 and −4y≤16; test one value per inequality.

Reverse signed inequality directions · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: negative coefficient, feasible, verify. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: For −4x≥12, divide by −4 and reverse to x≤−3. Check x=−4: 16≥12.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Solve −3x<12 and test x=−3.
  2. Solve −5y≤20.
  3. A restriction yields n≤−2 but n counts nonnegative items. Explain feasibility.
  4. Recall Week 33: simplify 2(a+5)+3a.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

x>−4; −3 works because 9<12; y≥−4; no feasible nonnegative values; 5a+10.

Friday — Capstone proof and evidence

Construct a fictional research budget with a startup amount and a per-item amount. Write an equation and an inequality, solve them, graph an inequality bound and explain how evidence could test the model assumptions.

This portfolio is formative; independently saved randomized examination results determine mastery.

Present a defensible capstone calculation · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: claim, evidence, domain. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: A modeled research budget 8+6n≤50 implies 0≤n≤7 whole samples. Seven cost 50 and eight cost 56, so the constraint is verified at its boundary.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Design a budget equation with startup fee 9, cost 7 per sample, and total 44; solve for samples.
  2. Solve the inequality 9+7s≤44 for whole nonnegative s.
  3. Recall Week 32: quadrant of (−3,−2).
  4. Recall Week 29: find 20% of 145.
  5. Explain what additional real measurements would be needed to validate a hypothetical budget.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9+7s=44 gives s=5; 0≤s≤5; Quadrant III; 29; real prices, available resources, and scope need verification.
