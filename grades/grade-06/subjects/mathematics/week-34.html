Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 34

A++ Big Question Capstone Research: Equations and Inequalities

How can mathematical constraints make a research proposal testable?

Learning goals

Translate research-budget problems into equations and inequalities, solve and verify one-variable relationships, graph solution boundaries, respect whole-number domains, and distinguish calculated claims from genuine empirical evidence. Numerical scenarios are fictional.

Monday — Frame a research question mathematically

A hypothetical capstone tests an exhibition budget: each prototype costs 4 units, with 7 units of setup, and 31 units available. Write 4x+7≤31. Solve x≤6; if x counts prototypes, x is a nonnegative whole number.

Practice: translate 'five units per sample plus nine setup units, at most 44' into an inequality, then solve.

Tuesday — Equations and checking

A fictional project spends 3 units per source bundle plus 8 in preparation for a total of 29. The equation 3n+8=29 gives n=7. Substitute to verify 3(7)+8=29. A solution must also make sense in the defined context.

Practice: solve 2x+5=21 and 4y−3=25; explain checks.

Wednesday — Inequality boundaries

A fictional prototype space permits no more than 35 display units. If each station requires five units, 5s≤35, so s≤7. Show a filled circle at 7 on a number line; restrict s to nonnegative integers.

Practice: solve 3t+6≤24 and 4t−5>11. Mark inclusive versus strict bounds.

Thursday — Negative coefficients and review

A mathematical test −2x≥8 requires reversing the inequality on division: x≤−4. For a count of actual items, a negative solution set may reveal an inappropriate model or infeasible constraint. Always discuss interpretation.

Practice: solve −3x<12 and −4y≤16; test one value per inequality.

Friday — Capstone proof and evidence

Construct a fictional research budget with a startup amount and a per-item amount. Write an equation and an inequality, solve them, graph an inequality bound and explain how evidence could test the model assumptions.

This portfolio is formative; independently saved randomized examination results determine mastery.

Answer key

Open after practicing

Monday: 5s+9≤44, s≤7. Tuesday: x=8 and y=7. Wednesday: t≤6 and t>4. Thursday: x>−4 and y≥−4. Friday: answers depend on the defined budget; check units, substitution and feasible domains.
