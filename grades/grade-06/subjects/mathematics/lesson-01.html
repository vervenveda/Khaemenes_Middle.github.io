01Remember · Number Sense

Before using a worked example, recall division and the meaning of a comparison.

Quick retrieval

1. What is 12 ÷ 4?

2. What is 8 ÷ 4?

3. If a box has 12 notebooks and 8 folders, which number comes first in notebooks:folders?

Your explanation

12 ÷ 4 = 3; 8 ÷ 4 = 2. Notebooks:folders begins with 12, so 12:8 simplifies to 3:2.

02Discover · Equivalent Ratios

Lesson goals and mathematical language

By the end of this lesson: identify exactly which two quantities are compared; distinguish part-to-part from part-to-whole; construct equivalent ratios by scaling both terms equally; and defend a comparison with a diagram, table, and mathematical sentence.

Ratio
A multiplicative comparison of two quantities in a stated order, such as 3 notebooks for every 2 folders (3:2).
Equivalent ratios
Ratios describing the same multiplicative relationship, such as 3:2, 6:4, and 12:8.
Part-to-part
A comparison of one category to another: notebooks:folders = 12:8 = 3:2.
Part-to-whole
A comparison of one category to the combined total: notebooks:all supplies = 12:20 = 3:5.
Scale factor
The nonzero number multiplying or dividing both ratio terms to form an equivalent ratio.

Concrete model: Imagine four equal groups. Each group contains 3 notebooks and 2 folders. Four groups give 12 notebooks and 8 folders. The ratio is still 3:2 because the composition of every group is unchanged.

A class has 12 notebooks for every 8 folders. Divide both terms by 4 to get 3 notebooks for every 2 folders. Both ratios describe the same comparison.

03Learn · Worked Model & Guided Work

Worked example · Identify, simplify, verify

Step 1 — Name the quantities: A recipe uses 6 cups of water and 4 cups of concentrate. Because the question asks for water:concentrate, write 6:4, not 4:6.

Step 2 — Simplify: The greatest common factor of 6 and 4 is 2. Divide both by 2: 6:4 = 3:2.

Step 3 — Scale: Multiply both parts of 3:2 by 3 to get 9:6. This preserves the mixture.

Step 4 — Verify: 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 and 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5. The water-per-concentrate comparison is unchanged. Reversing to 2:3 answers a different question.

Worked example · Part-to-part is not part-to-whole

A supply box has 12 notebooks and 8 folders. Notebooks:folders = 12:8 = 3:2, while notebooks:all items = 12:20 = 3:5. Both statements are correct, but their denominators represent different quantities.

Guided problem

05Apply · Ratio Challenge Game

Independent reasoning before the game

Try these on paper without opening the solutions. For every item, label the compared quantities and write the operation that preserves the relationship.

  1. 14 red counters and 21 blue counters: simplify red:blue and red:total.
  2. Complete 4:6 = 12:__. State the multiplier.
  3. A model has 5 triangles for every 2 squares. How many squares correspond to 20 triangles?
  4. Determine whether 7:4 and 21:12 are equivalent by showing two methods.
  5. Explain the error: A student claims 3:2 and 6:5 are equivalent because 3 was added to both terms. Explain why the claim is false, and give a correct equivalent ratio.
Open the reasoning key after completing the five tasks

1. Red:blue = 14:21 = 2:3; red:total = 14:35 = 2:5. 2. 12:18; multiply both terms by 3. 3. Eight squares; 5:2 × 4 = 20:8. 4. Yes; multiply both terms by 3, or compare 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75 with 21 ÷ 12 = 1.75. 5. The additive shift changes the multiplicative relationship: 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 but 6 ÷ 5 = 1.2. A correct equivalent ratio is 6:4.

06Reflect · Evidence & Next Step

Independent exit ticket · No hints

Without reviewing the example above, answer these in your notebook: (A) A team has 18 blue and 12 green pieces: write and simplify blue:green and blue:all pieces. (B) Complete 3:4 = 15:__. (C) In two sentences, explain the error in turning 2:5 into 4:7.

Show exit-ticket checks only after your attempt

A. Blue:green = 18:12 = 3:2; blue:all = 18:30 = 3:5. B. 15:20; multiply both entries by 5. C. Adding 2 to each term does not preserve a multiplicative ratio. A true double is 4:10, not 4:7.
