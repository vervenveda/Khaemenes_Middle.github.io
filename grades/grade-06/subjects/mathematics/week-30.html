Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 30

Argument, Debate, and Public Speaking: Fraction Division

How can careful fraction reasoning strengthen a mathematical argument?

Learning goals

Divide whole numbers and fractions by unit fractions, use reciprocal multiplication, distinguish exact quotients from full groups, and defend results with visual models and numerical checks. Timing scenarios are fictional.

Monday — Division as groups

A fictional debate club has 3 minutes for brief statements, each lasting 1/2 minute. There is time for 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6 statements. Dividing by a fraction asks how many fractional groups fit into a whole.

Practice: find the number of 1/4-minute turns in 2 minutes and 1/3-minute turns in 4 minutes.

Tuesday — Fractions divided by fractions

A speaker has 3/4 minute, and each point needs 1/8 minute. Compute (3/4) ÷ (1/8) = (3/4) × 8 = 6 points. Justify the reciprocal by drawing eighth-minute sections.

Practice: (2/3) ÷ (1/6) and (3/5) ÷ (1/10).

Wednesday — Mixed-number quotients

A fictional speaking session provides 2 1/2 minutes for 3/4-minute turns. Convert 2 1/2 to 5/2. Then (5/2) ÷ (3/4) = 10/3, meaning three complete turns and 1/4 minute left. A partial quotient does not equal another full turn.

Practice: 3 1/2 ÷ 1/2, and explain the meaning of full turns versus exact fractional capacity.

Thursday — Defend a solution with evidence

One schedule divides 4 minutes into half-minute contributions (8 contributions); another divides 4 minutes into quarter-minute contributions (16). A mathematical rebuttal names the divisor, includes units, and checks the product. The model does not measure quality of participation.

Practice: use an equation or strip model to refute the claim that smaller portions produce fewer turns.

Friday — Independent mathematical argument

A: 3 ÷ (1/3). B: (3/4) ÷ (1/8). C: 5/2 ÷ 3/4; report full turns and leftover time. D: Write a respectful counterclaim and a checked equation for 4 ÷ (1/4).

Ten-point formative review; only a separately saved randomized examination grants verified mastery.

Answer key

Check after independent practice

Monday: 8 and 12 turns. Tuesday: 4 and 6 portions. Wednesday: 7 complete half-minute turns. Thursday: 8 versus 16. Friday: 9; 6; 3 complete turns with 1/4 minute remaining; 16 quarter-minute turns.
