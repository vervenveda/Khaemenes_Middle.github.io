Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 30

Argument, Debate, and Public Speaking: Fraction Division

How can careful fraction reasoning strengthen a mathematical argument?

Learning goals

Divide whole numbers and fractions by unit fractions, use reciprocal multiplication, distinguish exact quotients from full groups, and defend results with visual models and numerical checks. Timing scenarios are fictional.

Monday — Division as groups

A fictional debate club has 3 minutes for brief statements, each lasting 1/2 minute. There is time for 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6 statements. Dividing by a fraction asks how many fractional groups fit into a whole.

Practice: find the number of 1/4-minute turns in 2 minutes and 1/3-minute turns in 4 minutes.

Model fractional speaking turns · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: quotient, unit fraction, group. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A 5-minute session divided into 1/4-minute turns fits 5÷1/4=20 full turns. Verify 20×1/4=5 minutes.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find 3÷1/2.
  2. Find 4÷1/8.
  3. Explain why dividing 2 by 1/4 produces a number greater than 2.
  4. Show with a strip why 3/2÷1/2=3.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

6; 32; four quarter-minute turns fit in each minute; three half-minutes fit into one and a half minutes.

Tuesday — Fractions divided by fractions

A speaker has 3/4 minute, and each point needs 1/8 minute. Compute (3/4) ÷ (1/8) = (3/4) × 8 = 6 points. Justify the reciprocal by drawing eighth-minute sections.

Practice: (2/3) ÷ (1/6) and (3/5) ÷ (1/10).

Divide fractions using inverses · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: reciprocal, divisor, equivalent. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: (5/6)÷(1/3)=5/6×3=5/2. Check (5/2)×(1/3)=5/6.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Compute (3/4)÷(1/6).
  2. Compute (2/5)÷(4/5).
  3. Explain why (2/3)÷(1/4) is not (2/3)×(1/4).
  4. Check (7/8)÷(7/16) using multiplication.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9/2; 1/2; multiply by reciprocal to get 8/3; quotient 2 because 2×7/16=7/8.

Wednesday — Mixed-number quotients

A fictional speaking session provides 2 1/2 minutes for 3/4-minute turns. Convert 2 1/2 to 5/2. Then (5/2) ÷ (3/4) = 10/3, meaning three complete turns and 1/4 minute left. A partial quotient does not equal another full turn.

Practice: 3 1/2 ÷ 1/2, and explain the meaning of full turns versus exact fractional capacity.

Interpret full turns and leftovers · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: remainder, capacity, context. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A 2 3/4-minute session divided into 2/3-minute turns yields 11/4×3/2=33/8=4 1/8 turn capacities; four complete turns use 8/3 minutes and leave 1/12 minute.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. For 5/2 minutes and 3/4-minute turns, find full turns and leftover time.
  2. For 7/2 minutes and 1/2-minute turns, find full turns.
  3. For 9/4 minutes and 1/2-minute turns, find full turns and remainder.
  4. Explain why a quotient of 3 1/3 turns is not 3 1/3 minutes.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3 full with 1/4 minute left; 7 full; 4 full with 1/4 minute left; turn count differs from duration.

Thursday — Defend a solution with evidence

One schedule divides 4 minutes into half-minute contributions (8 contributions); another divides 4 minutes into quarter-minute contributions (16). A mathematical rebuttal names the divisor, includes units, and checks the product. The model does not measure quality of participation.

Practice: use an equation or strip model to refute the claim that smaller portions produce fewer turns.

Critique a mathematical argument · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: verify, counterexample, reasoning. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: Four minutes split into half-minute turns gives 8; quarter-minute turns gives 16. The smaller divisor produces more turns at a fixed total time.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find 6÷1/3 and 6÷1/6; compare.
  2. Provide a counterexample to the claim that dividing by a smaller fraction yields fewer groups.
  3. A student claims 3/4÷1/8=3/32. Explain the mistake.
  4. Recall Week 29: compute 25% of 120.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

18 versus 36; e.g. 2÷1/2=4 versus 2÷1/4=8; must multiply by reciprocal, so 6; 30.

Friday — Independent mathematical argument

A: 3 ÷ (1/3). B: (3/4) ÷ (1/8). C: 5/2 ÷ 3/4; report full turns and leftover time. D: Write a respectful counterclaim and a checked equation for 4 ÷ (1/4).

Ten-point formative review; only a separately saved randomized examination grants verified mastery.

Defend fraction division and prior learning · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: reason, verify, remainder. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: For 3/4÷1/8=6, check 6×1/8=3/4. A clear mathematical explanation gives method, units and verification.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Compute 2/3÷1/9.
  2. Find 3 3/4÷3/8.
  3. Recall Week 28: simplify 30:45.
  4. Recall Week 26: find volume of a 4×3×5 cm prism.
  5. Invent a turn-length division problem whose answer is four complete turns.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

6; 10; 2:3; 60 cm³; sample 2 minutes at half-minute turn size gives four.
