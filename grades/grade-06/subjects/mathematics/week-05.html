Week 05 · Mathematics · Five-Day Learning Studio

Fraction Division and Procedural Writing

How can models explain division when the numbers are fractions?

Learning targets

Represent fraction division with fraction strips or number lines; divide unit and nonunit fractions; handle mixed numbers; check using multiplication; explain the method precisely in procedural writing. Vocabulary: dividend, divisor, quotient, unit fraction, reciprocal, equivalent fraction. Read the five days in sequence; all essential examples are included for offline use.

Monday — Division as measuring groups

Division asks “how many groups fit?” For 3 ÷ 1/2, draw three whole bars and divide each into halves: 6 half-sized parts fit. So 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6. In a 2-cup quantity, four 1/2-cup servings fit. Label what the quotient counts: servings, not cups. For 3/4 ÷ 1/4, three quarter-sized pieces fit, so the quotient is 3.

Try: (1) 2 ÷ 1/4; (2) 5/6 ÷ 1/6; (3) Explain why dividing by 1/2 produces twice as many groups as dividing by 1.

Measure division using fraction strips · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: dividend, divisor, quotient. Define each term and explain one with an original example.

Worked mathematical model: 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 6 because six eighths fit in three quarters. Check 6 × 1/8 = 3/4.

Independent reasoning and applications:

  1. How many 1/6-cup portions fit in 2/3 cup?
  2. Calculate 5/8 ÷ 1/4 and interpret the fractional number of groups.
  3. Explain why dividing by 1/2 doubles a positive quantity.
  4. Represent 1/2 ÷ 1/4 with a number line.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

4 portions; 5/2 or 2.5 portions; a half-unit fits twice in each unit; two quarter-length jumps fill one half.

Tuesday — Equivalent fractions and reciprocals

To find 3/4 ÷ 1/2, rewrite 1/2 as 2/4: (3/4) ÷ (2/4) = 3 ÷ 2 = 1 1/2. The general rule is a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c, provided denominators and divisor are nonzero. The reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2. This “multiply by reciprocal” rule agrees with a fraction-strip model because we are counting groups.

Worked example: 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6. Check: (5/6) × (4/5) = 2/3. Try: (4) 5/8 ÷ 1/4; (5) 3/5 ÷ 2/3; (6) 7/9 ÷ 7/9. Explain one answer in words.

Reason with reciprocals · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: reciprocal, equivalent fraction, inverse. Define each term and explain one with an original example.

Worked mathematical model: 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6. Multiplying 5/6 × 4/5 gives 2/3.

Independent reasoning and applications:

  1. Find 3/5 ÷ 2/3.
  2. Find 7/8 ÷ 7/16.
  3. Explain why 2/5 ÷ 3/4 is not 2/5 × 3/4.
  4. Check your answer to 5/6 ÷ 2/3 by multiplication.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9/10; 2; use reciprocal 4/3, result 8/15; quotient 5/4 and 5/4 × 2/3 = 5/6.

Wednesday — Interpret multi-step situations

A ribbon is 3 1/2 meters long. Each project needs 1/4 meter. Convert 3 1/2 = 7/2, then (7/2) ÷ (1/4) = (7/2) × 4 = 14 projects. Check: 14 × 1/4 m = 3 1/2 m. If a problem instead asks for ribbon left over after making 10 projects, subtract 10 × 1/4 = 2 1/2 meters from 3 1/2 to get 1 meter.

Research notebook: (7) How many 3/8-cup portions fit into 1 1/2 cups? (8) A 4 1/2-km trail is split into 3/4-km segments: how many segments? (9) Explain what each answer's units represent.

Solve multi-step fraction problems · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: mixed number, unit, remainder. Define each term and explain one with an original example.

Worked mathematical model: A 2 1/4-meter ribbon divided into 3/8-meter pieces gives 9/4 ÷ 3/8 = 6 pieces exactly.

Independent reasoning and applications:

  1. A cook has 3/2 L of juice and serves 1/4 L each. How many portions?
  2. A 5/6-meter strip is cut into 1/3-meter lengths. How many lengths worth of fabric?
  3. A 1 1/2-hour project has stages of 3/4 hour. Find the number of stages.
  4. Explain why 2 1/2 ÷ 1/2 is 5 rather than 1 1/4.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

6 portions; 5/2 or 2.5 lengths (two full pieces and a half-length); 2 stages; five halves fit in two and one half.

Thursday — Procedural writing and error analysis

A student claims 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2/18 by dividing both numerators and denominators. This is incorrect: 2/3 is 4/6, and there are four 1/6 pieces, so the answer is 4. A second common error is flipping the dividend instead of the divisor. Use the inverse-operation check: quotient × divisor must reproduce the dividend.

Independent practice: (10) 3/4 ÷ 3/8; (11) 5/6 ÷ 2/3; (12) 2 1/4 ÷ 3/4; (13) write a numbered four-step explanation of the reciprocal method and verify one example with multiplication. Circle your units and correct at least one common misconception.

Communicate a complete solution · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: estimate, procedure, verify. Define each term and explain one with an original example.

Worked mathematical model: To compute 4/5 ÷ 2/3: estimate slightly above 1; rewrite as 4/5 × 3/2 = 6/5; verify 6/5 × 2/3 = 4/5.

Independent reasoning and applications:

  1. Identify the error in 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/8.
  2. Compute 7/10 ÷ 7/5 and explain why the answer is below 1.
  3. Write a four-step explanation for 5/6 ÷ 1/4.
  4. Estimate then calculate 9/10 ÷ 3/5.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/2; 1/2 (divisor is greater than dividend); 10/3; 3/2, which is 1.5.

Friday — Independent mastery demonstration

A (2 points): Draw or describe a model for 2 ÷ 1/2. B (3 points): Calculate 5/6 ÷ 1/3, simplify, and verify by multiplication. C (3 points): A 2 1/4-liter pitcher fills 3/8-liter cups; how many full cups? Show conversion and units. D (2 points): Explain why dividing by a fraction less than 1 can result in a number greater than the dividend.

Transfer and cumulative review · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: reasoning, context, check. Define each term and explain one with an original example.

Worked mathematical model: A 3/4-kg package divided into 1/8-kg bags creates six bags. To find cost per bag at $9 total, divide 9 by 6 to obtain $1.50/bag.

Independent reasoning and applications:

  1. Find 2 1/2 ÷ 5/8 and verify.
  2. Find 3/4 ÷ 2/5 and interpret the fractional quotient.
  3. Recall Week 04: find 20% of 45.
  4. Recall Week 03: find the unit rate of 48 miles in 4 hours.
  5. Write a word problem that uses 5/6 ÷ 1/3 and solve it.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

4; 15/8 = 1 7/8; 9; 12 miles/hour; 5/2 = 2.5 units or portions depending on context.
