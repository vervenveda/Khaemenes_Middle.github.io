Week 05 · Mathematics · Five-Day Learning Studio

Fraction Division and Procedural Writing

How can models explain division when the numbers are fractions?

Learning targets

Represent fraction division with fraction strips or number lines; divide unit and nonunit fractions; handle mixed numbers; check using multiplication; explain the method precisely in procedural writing. Vocabulary: dividend, divisor, quotient, unit fraction, reciprocal, equivalent fraction. Read the five days in sequence; all essential examples are included for offline use.

Monday — Division as measuring groups

Division asks “how many groups fit?” For 3 ÷ 1/2, draw three whole bars and divide each into halves: 6 half-sized parts fit. So 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6. In a 2-cup quantity, four 1/2-cup servings fit. Label what the quotient counts: servings, not cups. For 3/4 ÷ 1/4, three quarter-sized pieces fit, so the quotient is 3.

Try: (1) 2 ÷ 1/4; (2) 5/6 ÷ 1/6; (3) Explain why dividing by 1/2 produces twice as many groups as dividing by 1.

Tuesday — Equivalent fractions and reciprocals

To find 3/4 ÷ 1/2, rewrite 1/2 as 2/4: (3/4) ÷ (2/4) = 3 ÷ 2 = 1 1/2. The general rule is a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c, provided denominators and divisor are nonzero. The reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2. This “multiply by reciprocal” rule agrees with a fraction-strip model because we are counting groups.

Worked example: 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6. Check: (5/6) × (4/5) = 2/3. Try: (4) 5/8 ÷ 1/4; (5) 3/5 ÷ 2/3; (6) 7/9 ÷ 7/9. Explain one answer in words.

Wednesday — Interpret multi-step situations

A ribbon is 3 1/2 meters long. Each project needs 1/4 meter. Convert 3 1/2 = 7/2, then (7/2) ÷ (1/4) = (7/2) × 4 = 14 projects. Check: 14 × 1/4 m = 3 1/2 m. If a problem instead asks for ribbon left over after making 10 projects, subtract 10 × 1/4 = 2 1/2 meters from 3 1/2 to get 1 meter.

Research notebook: (7) How many 3/8-cup portions fit into 1 1/2 cups? (8) A 4 1/2-km trail is split into 3/4-km segments: how many segments? (9) Explain what each answer's units represent.

Thursday — Procedural writing and error analysis

A student claims 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2/18 by dividing both numerators and denominators. This is incorrect: 2/3 is 4/6, and there are four 1/6 pieces, so the answer is 4. A second common error is flipping the dividend instead of the divisor. Use the inverse-operation check: quotient × divisor must reproduce the dividend.

Independent practice: (10) 3/4 ÷ 3/8; (11) 5/6 ÷ 2/3; (12) 2 1/4 ÷ 3/4; (13) write a numbered four-step explanation of the reciprocal method and verify one example with multiplication. Circle your units and correct at least one common misconception.

Friday — Independent mastery demonstration

A (2 points): Draw or describe a model for 2 ÷ 1/2. B (3 points): Calculate 5/6 ÷ 1/3, simplify, and verify by multiplication. C (3 points): A 2 1/4-liter pitcher fills 3/8-liter cups; how many full cups? Show conversion and units. D (2 points): Explain why dividing by a fraction less than 1 can result in a number greater than the dividend.

Teacher-reviewed rubric: 10 points total; 8/10 demonstrates the 80% threshold for this assignment. This page does not set official mastery flags or unlock later weeks; verified advancement requires the authorized Academy assessment. Below 80%: revisit a model, correct two errors, explain the correction, and take a fresh authorized check.

Answer key and explanations

Reveal after independent attempts

1. 8; 2. 5; 3. half-size groups are smaller, so twice as many fit; 4. 2 1/2; 5. 9/10; 6. 1; 7. 4 portions; 8. 6 segments; 9. portions and trail segments; 10. 2; 11. 1 1/4; 12. 3. Friday A: four halves; B: 5/2 = 2 1/2, verified by (5/2) × (1/3) = 5/6; C: 6 full cups, because 9/4 × 8/3 = 6; D: smaller units fit more times. Procedural explanations must identify the reciprocal of the divisor, retain the first fraction, multiply, simplify, and verify.
