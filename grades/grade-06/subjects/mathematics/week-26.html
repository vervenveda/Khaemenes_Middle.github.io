Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 26

Classical Civilizations and Civic Ideas: Surface Area and Volume

How can geometric models help us estimate space and materials?

Learning goals

Distinguish area from volume, apply rectangular- and triangular-prism formulas, reason with nets, and model open versus closed structures. Architectural contexts are hypothetical and not claims about actual ancient measurements.

Monday — Area versus volume

A fictional public hall floor measures 8 m by 5 m. Its floor area is 40 m². If the hall is 3 m tall, the rectangular interior has a volume of 8×5×3=120 m³. Square units describe a surface; cubic units describe space.

Practice: calculate floor area and volume of a 6×4×3 m model room; explain the units.

Compare area and capacity · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: square unit, cubic unit, dimension. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A fictional hall 7 m by 4 m with height 3 m has floor area 28 m² and rectangular volume 84 m³. Changing the height affects volume but not the floor area.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find floor area and volume for a room 9 m by 5 m by 2 m.
  2. A floor measures 6 m by 8 m; find flooring needed.
  3. Explain why floor area is independent of ceiling height.
  4. If the height of a 4×3×2 cm prism doubles, how do volume and base area change?
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

45 m² and 90 m³; 48 m²; area uses two lengths, not height; volume doubles from 24 to 48 cm³ while base area stays 12 cm².

Tuesday — Nets and surface area

A closed rectangular model box measures 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm. Its surface area is 2(4×3+4×2+3×2)=52 cm². Draw its six faces and label equal opposite rectangles.

Practice: find surface area of a closed 5×2×3 cm prism; explain why each face is counted twice.

Build nets and reason about surfaces · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: net, face pair, surface area. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A closed prism 6×2×3 cm has opposite face pairs of 12,18,6 cm², so surface area is 2(12+18+6)=72 cm².

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find surface area of a closed 5×4×2 cm prism.
  2. List three pairs of face dimensions for a 7×3×2 cm prism.
  3. A 4×3×2 cm box is missing its top 4×3 face. Find open-top surface area.
  4. Explain why material tabs can make real cardboard use exceed ideal surface area.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

76 cm²; 7×3,7×2,3×2 (two each); closed 52−12=40 cm²; tabs and waste need extra material.

Wednesday — Triangular and rectangular prisms

A triangular-prism model has a right-triangle base with legs 3 cm and 4 cm, giving base area 6 cm². With prism length 10 cm, volume is 60 cm³. For a triangular prism V=base area×length.

Practice: find the volume when a triangle's base is 6 cm, height 4 cm, and prism length 5 cm.

Use triangular prism models · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: triangle base, perpendicular height, prism length. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A triangular prism with triangle base 10 cm, perpendicular height 6 cm and length 4 cm has volume (1/2×10×6)×4=120 cm³.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find triangular-prism volume with triangle base 8 cm, height 5 cm and prism length 7 cm.
  2. If base area is 18 cm² and volume 90 cm³, find prism length.
  3. Explain why a sloping side cannot automatically be used as the triangle's perpendicular height.
  4. Compare a 5×4×3 rectangular prism volume with a triangular prism of base area 12 cm² and length 5 cm.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

140 cm³; 5 cm; perpendicular height is the distance at right angles from base to opposite vertex; both 60 cm³.

Thursday — Building material models

An open-top rectangular model reservoir measures 6 m by 4 m by 2 m. Its capacity is 48 m³. Its exterior area excluding the top is 6×4+2(6×2)+2(4×2)=64 m². Specify whether a structure is open or closed before calculating materials.

Practice: calculate an open-top 5×3×2 m box's volume and surface area; distinguish area from capacity.

Estimate materials for open designs · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: open top, capacity, model limitation. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A lidless 8×5×3 m rectangular tank has ideal interior volume 120 m³ and base-and-sides area 40+2(24)+2(15)=118 m².

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. For a lidless 4×3×2 cm box, find material area and volume.
  2. For a closed 4×3×2 cm box, find additional area needed beyond an open-top box.
  3. Why can a real tank hold less than its modeled outer rectangular volume?
  4. Recall Week 25: solve 2t+8≤20 for whole nonnegative t.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Open area 40 cm², volume 24 cm³; 12 cm² lid; wall thickness, edges or operating fill limit; t=0,1,2,3,4,5,6.

Friday — Evidence defense

A: calculate volume of 4×3×5 cm prism. B: find surface area of closed 2×3×4 cm prism. C: volume of triangular prism with triangle base 8 cm, height 3 cm, and length 6 cm. D: compare a model's surface area with its volume and explain why the numbers use different units.

Formative review does not award mastery; the separate randomized assessment checks verified mathematics and overall proficiency.

Synthesize geometry evidence · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: area, volume, justify. Define the terms and use one in a complete mathematical explanation.

Worked mathematical model: A 9×6 m floor has area 54 m²; at height 4 m a matching rectangular model has volume 216 m³. Correct units are essential to a defensible comparison.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find area and volume of a 6×4×5 m hall.
  2. Find area of a triangle with base 12 cm and height 9 cm.
  3. Find surface area of a closed 3×4×5 cm prism.
  4. Recall Week 22: calculate −3/4+1/2.
  5. Explain why two models with equal volumes can have different surface areas.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

24 m² and 120 m³; 54 cm²; 94 cm²; −1/4; shapes and dimensions affect faces separately from volume.
