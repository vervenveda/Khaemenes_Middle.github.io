Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 31

Arts, Media, and Message Design: Rational Numbers

How do signed quantities describe changes in a designed composition?

Learning goals

Calculate signed integer, decimal, and fraction changes; model number-line distance; average signed deviations; and distinguish model values from real-world physical measurements. Examples are hypothetical classroom design settings.

Monday — Signed creative adjustments

A fictional image editor sets brightness at −3 units, then raises it by 7: −3+7=4. A negative setting is relative to the chosen zero, not a negative amount of light.

Practice: −5+8, 4−9, and explain both answers on a number line.

Interpret signed design adjustments · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: signed quantity, zero reference, net change. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A fictional brightness control begins at −6 units and increases by 9, reaching +3. These are relative interface units, not direct physical light measurements.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Evaluate −7+11.
  2. Evaluate 3−8.
  3. Starting at +2, apply a change of −5.
  4. Explain why a negative adjustment does not imply a negative physical quantity.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

4; −5; −3; signed controls are relative to a chosen zero.

Tuesday — Decimal changes

A sound-mixing exercise starts a fictional level at −2.5 units and raises it by 4.0, reaching +1.5. Find signed change using final minus initial.

Practice: compute change from −3.5 to +2.5 and from +1.5 to −2.0.

Calculate signed decimal differences · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: final minus initial, decimal, direction. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A track setting moves from −4.5 to +2.0 units. Signed change is 2−(−4.5)=+6.5 units.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find change from −2.5 to +3.5.
  2. Find change from +4.5 to −1.0.
  3. From −1.5 apply a decrease of 2.25; find new setting.
  4. Explain the difference between a final value and a signed change.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

+6; −5.5; −3.75; one names position, the other describes movement.

Wednesday — Fractions on the number line

A layout offset starts at −3/4 of a grid unit and shifts +1/2: −3/4+1/2=−1/4. Convert fractions to fourths and justify the signed result.

Practice: −2/3+1/6 and +3/4−5/4.

Combine signed fractions · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: common denominator, rational, number line. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A layout begins at −5/6 units and shifts +1/3, yielding −5/6+2/6=−1/2 unit.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Compute −3/4+1/8.
  2. Compute 2/3−5/6.
  3. Find the distance between −1/2 and +1/4.
  4. Explain why −1/3+3/4 is positive.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

−5/8; −1/6; 3/4; 3/4 exceeds 1/3 by 5/12.

Thursday — Average variation

Five hypothetical soundtrack adjustments are −2, +1, +4, −1, −2 units. Their sum is 0, so average adjustment is 0. This does not mean no individual track was adjusted.

Practice: calculate mean of −3,+2,+1,+4, and explain why a mean hides variation.

Interpret averages of signed changes · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: mean, variation, deviations. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: The adjustments −3,+4,+1,−2 total zero, giving mean 0 even though none of these four individual adjustments equals zero.

Independent mathematical challenges:

  1. Find mean of −4,+1,+5,+2.
  2. Find mean of −3,−1,+2,+6.
  3. Explain why zero average change does not mean all changes were zero.
  4. Recall Week 30: calculate 3/4÷1/8.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

+1; +1; positive and negative observations can cancel; 6.
