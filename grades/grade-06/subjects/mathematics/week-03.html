Week 03 · Mathematics · Five-Day Learning Studio

Rates, Unit Rates, and Informational Text

Essential question: How can rates, tables, graphs, and carefully read data explain change?

Targets and academic vocabulary

Find unit rates by division, identify units and proportional relationships, create a two-column rate table, plot ordered pairs, distinguish total from per-unit data, and critique claims in short informational reports. Rate: comparison of different units. Unit rate: amount per one unit. Coordinate pair (x,y): horizontal value first, vertical value second. Constant of proportionality: the same y/x ratio for every nonzero x.

Monday — How much per one?

A bicyclist travels 42 miles in 3 hours. To find miles per one hour, divide 42 miles by 3 hours: 14 miles/hour. A second cyclist travels 60 miles in 5 hours, or 12 miles/hour. The first rate is greater by 2 miles/hour. Comparing only distances (60 versus 42) would be misleading because the travel times differ.

Worked example: Twelve oranges cost $6.60: $6.60 ÷ 12 = $0.55 per orange. To estimate 20 oranges at the same rate: 20 × $0.55 = $11.00.

Guided practice: 1. A runner completes 24 km in 4 hours. Find km/hour. 2. Nine notebooks cost $13.50. Find dollars/notebook. 3. Explain why 60 miles is not by itself evidence of higher speed than 42 miles.

Compare unit rates fairly · Deep practice

Universal vocabulary: unit rate, per, quotient, unit price. Define each term in your own words and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: A 750 g bag of rice costs $3.90: $3.90 ÷ 750 = $0.0052/g, or $5.20/kg. A 1.2 kg bag costs $6.00: $6.00 ÷ 1.2 = $5.00/kg. Use the same unit before comparing: the 1.2 kg bag costs $0.20 less per kilogram.

Guided check: Convert 900 g to 0.9 kg; at $4.95 for 900 g, the price is $5.50/kg. Explain why comparing $4.95 with $6.00 alone is not sufficient.

Misconception to investigate: Larger packages do not automatically have a better price. Compare equivalent units.

  1. Compute the unit price of 8 oranges costing $5.60.
  2. A 1.5 L bottle costs $2.85 and a 2 L bottle costs $3.60. Find both dollars/L and choose the better unit price.
  3. A runner goes 6.4 km in 0.8 hour. Calculate km/h and explain the division.
  4. Invent two packages with different total prices but identical prices per kilogram.
Check the teacher key after your independent attempt

1. $0.70/orange. 2. $1.90/L versus $1.80/L; the 2 L bottle is cheaper per liter. 3. 6.4 ÷ 0.8 = 8 km/h. 4. Example: 1 kg for $4 and 2 kg for $8, both $4/kg.

Tuesday — Tables, ordered pairs, and graphs

A small pump fills 6 liters in 2 minutes. Its rate is 3 liters per minute. Assume the rate stays constant. Make a table:

Time (min), x012345
Volume (L), y03691215

On graph paper label the horizontal axis time in minutes and the vertical axis volume in liters. Plot (0,0), (1,3), (2,6), (3,9), (4,12), and (5,15). The points fall on a line through the origin because y = 3x. Every nonzero row has y/x = 3. When interpreting graphs, always check scales and units.

Practice: 4. Predict liters in 7 minutes. 5. Find time for 24 liters. 6. Explain what (4,12) means in this setting.

Represent constant rates using tables and graphs · Deep practice

Universal vocabulary: independent variable, dependent variable, ordered pair, origin. Define each term in your own words and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: A printer produces 9 pages per minute. For time x = 0, 1, 2, 4, 6 minutes, pages y = 0, 9, 18, 36, 54. Its equation is y = 9x. The graph contains the origin and each nonzero y/x = 9 pages/minute.

Guided check: At 7 minutes, y = 9 × 7 = 63 pages. Explain why (7,63) is an ordered pair rather than (63,7) when time is on the x-axis.

Misconception to investigate: Equal increases in the output are not enough; compare the correct x-intervals and check the common y/x ratio.

  1. Make a time-volume table for a pump flowing at 2.5 L/min at 0, 2, 4 and 8 minutes.
  2. Find the constant k from the point (3,21) for a proportional relation y = kx.
  3. Explain whether the pairs (1,4), (2,8), (3,13) form a proportional relationship.
  4. Draw and label a graph of y = 2.5x with both axes and units.
Check the teacher key after your independent attempt

1. (0,0), (2,5), (4,10), (8,20). 2. k = 21 ÷ 3 = 7 output units per input unit. 3. Not proportional: 4, 4, and 13/3 are not equal. 4. A straight line through the origin with slope 2.5 L/min and correctly labelled axes.

Wednesday — Read a report critically

Original practice report: “Library helpers shelved 84 books over 7 hours on Tuesday. On Wednesday, helpers shelved 90 books over 9 hours. Wednesday had more books shelved, so the Wednesday team must have worked faster.”

Underline the claim and circle the data. Tuesday's unit rate is 84 ÷ 7 = 12 books/hour. Wednesday's rate is 90 ÷ 9 = 10 books/hour. Therefore the claim about being faster is not supported; Wednesday had more books in total but fewer books per hour.

Inquiry: 7. Write a corrected two-sentence conclusion using both numbers and units. 8. Create a rate table for three hours at Tuesday's constant rate. 9. Name one uncertainty the report leaves out (for example, differing book sizes or number of helpers). 10. Cite the exact numerical evidence that supports your conclusion.

Evaluate claims from data responsibly · Deep practice

Universal vocabulary: claim, evidence, assumption, average rate. Define each term in your own words and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: A recycling report says Saturday collected 144 bottles in 12 worker-hours and Sunday collected 130 bottles in 10 worker-hours. Saturday averaged 12 bottles/worker-hour; Sunday averaged 13. Sunday had a higher rate, even though Saturday's total was larger.

Guided check: Correct the headline 'Saturday was more productive because 144 is greater than 130.' Refer to both rates and explain that these describe averages.

Misconception to investigate: Do not confuse total output with speed, or average rate with a guaranteed constant rate.

  1. Compare 210 visits in 7 hours with 240 visits in 10 hours. Which rate is greater?
  2. A news graphic shows 45 items in 3 hours and 60 items in 5 hours. Draft a two-sentence correction to the claim that the second group is faster.
  3. Identify two missing contextual details that might change the fairness of the recycling comparison.
  4. Describe the difference between a rate observed across an interval and a claim that it was constant at every instant.
Check the teacher key after your independent attempt

1. 30 versus 24 visits/hour; the first is greater. 2. 15 versus 12 items/hour, so the first group averaged more per hour despite a smaller total. 3. Examples: number of people or workers, task difficulty, equipment, weather, interruptions. 4. An average rate summarizes a time interval, not necessarily every moment.

Thursday — Independent application and misconception clinic

  1. A bus travels 210 miles in 3.5 hours. Find its average mph.
  2. Five meters of fabric cost $37.50. Calculate the cost per meter and the projected cost of eight meters.
  3. Machine A packs 96 boxes in 8 minutes; Machine B packs 105 boxes in 7 minutes. Which has the higher rate? Explain.
  4. A tank contains 4 liters after 1 minute and 8 liters after 2 minutes. Assuming constant flow from empty, predict its volume at 6 minutes. Write an equation.
  5. A learner graphs hours vertically and distance horizontally but labels the line as miles per hour. Explain how swapping the axes changes the graph's slope units.

Review: Verify division direction, numeric result, units, and whether the situation reasonably supports assuming a constant rate. If you make a mistake, identify it and correct it.

Reason about dimensions and exceptions · Deep practice

Universal vocabulary: dimension, reciprocal rate, proportional relationship, counterexample. Define each term in your own words and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: If a vehicle travels 180 km in 3 h, the average speed is 60 km/h. The reciprocal 3 h ÷ 180 km = 1/60 h/km answers a different question. Units reveal which quotient was calculated.

Guided check: A machine completes 72 boxes in 9 minutes: 8 boxes/minute. Explain why 9/72 minutes/box is valid but is not the same quantity.

Misconception to investigate: Constant additive change does not automatically make a relationship proportional; check the initial value.

  1. A bike travels 27 km in 1.5 hours. Calculate its average speed and the reciprocal time per kilometer.
  2. Which is the higher production rate: 88 parts in 8 minutes or 108 parts in 9 minutes?
  3. A scooter rental costs $4 to unlock plus $3 per hour. Is total cost proportional to hours? Justify using the origin.
  4. A tank starts with 10 L and gains 2 L/min. Write its equation and explain why volume is not proportional to minutes elapsed.
Check the teacher key after your independent attempt

1. 18 km/h; 1.5/27 = 1/18 h/km. 2. 11 parts/min versus 12 parts/min; second is higher. 3. No, y = 4 + 3x, so x=0 has cost $4 rather than $0. 4. y = 10 + 2x; nonzero initial volume means the graph does not pass through the origin.

Synthesize and transfer mathematical reasoning · Deep practice

Universal vocabulary: justify, model, compare, critique. Define each term in your own words and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: Two delivery services quote 18 packages in 1.5 hours and 26 packages in 2 hours. Their average rates are 12 and 13 packages/hour. The second is faster by 1 package/hour; the conclusion depends on comparable working conditions.

Guided check: Explain why a unit rate is not the same as total delivery count and identify one assumption in the comparison.

Misconception to investigate: Strong explanations show correct calculations, meaningful units, assumptions, and a conclusion supported by evidence.

  1. Calculate rates for 96 pages in 4 hours and 105 pages in 5 hours; identify the greater reading rate.
  2. Create an accurate table and equation for a faucet filling at 3.5 L/min at 0, 2, 4, and 6 minutes.
  3. A presenter says (2,7), (4,14), (6,21) is not proportional because the totals differ. Evaluate the claim using y/x.
  4. Find and correct a misleading headline that compares two unequal time periods by total output only.
  5. Write a complete real-world rate problem with a numerical answer, correct units, and at least one limitation of your model.
Check the teacher key after your independent attempt

1. 24 pages/hour and 21 pages/hour; the first rate is greater. 2. (0,0),(2,7),(4,14),(6,21); y = 3.5x. 3. The presenter is incorrect: 7/2 = 14/4 = 21/6 = 3.5. 4. Answers vary; compare outputs per same unit of time. 5. Teacher reviews arithmetic, units, validity and limitations.
