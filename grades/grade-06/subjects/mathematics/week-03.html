Week 03 · Mathematics · Five-Day Learning Studio

Rates, Unit Rates, and Informational Text

Essential question: How can rates, tables, graphs, and carefully read data explain change?

Targets and academic vocabulary

Find unit rates by division, identify units and proportional relationships, create a two-column rate table, plot ordered pairs, distinguish total from per-unit data, and critique claims in short informational reports. Rate: comparison of different units. Unit rate: amount per one unit. Coordinate pair (x,y): horizontal value first, vertical value second. Constant of proportionality: the same y/x ratio for every nonzero x.

Monday — How much per one?

A bicyclist travels 42 miles in 3 hours. To find miles per one hour, divide 42 miles by 3 hours: 14 miles/hour. A second cyclist travels 60 miles in 5 hours, or 12 miles/hour. The first rate is greater by 2 miles/hour. Comparing only distances (60 versus 42) would be misleading because the travel times differ.

Worked example: Twelve oranges cost $6.60: $6.60 ÷ 12 = $0.55 per orange. To estimate 20 oranges at the same rate: 20 × $0.55 = $11.00.

Guided practice: 1. A runner completes 24 km in 4 hours. Find km/hour. 2. Nine notebooks cost $13.50. Find dollars/notebook. 3. Explain why 60 miles is not by itself evidence of higher speed than 42 miles.

Tuesday — Tables, ordered pairs, and graphs

A small pump fills 6 liters in 2 minutes. Its rate is 3 liters per minute. Assume the rate stays constant. Make a table:

Time (min), x012345
Volume (L), y03691215

On graph paper label the horizontal axis time in minutes and the vertical axis volume in liters. Plot (0,0), (1,3), (2,6), (3,9), (4,12), and (5,15). The points fall on a line through the origin because y = 3x. Every nonzero row has y/x = 3. When interpreting graphs, always check scales and units.

Practice: 4. Predict liters in 7 minutes. 5. Find time for 24 liters. 6. Explain what (4,12) means in this setting.

Wednesday — Read a report critically

Original practice report: “Library helpers shelved 84 books over 7 hours on Tuesday. On Wednesday, helpers shelved 90 books over 9 hours. Wednesday had more books shelved, so the Wednesday team must have worked faster.”

Underline the claim and circle the data. Tuesday's unit rate is 84 ÷ 7 = 12 books/hour. Wednesday's rate is 90 ÷ 9 = 10 books/hour. Therefore the claim about being faster is not supported; Wednesday had more books in total but fewer books per hour.

Inquiry: 7. Write a corrected two-sentence conclusion using both numbers and units. 8. Create a rate table for three hours at Tuesday's constant rate. 9. Name one uncertainty the report leaves out (for example, differing book sizes or number of helpers). 10. Cite the exact numerical evidence that supports your conclusion.

Thursday — Independent application and misconception clinic

  1. A bus travels 210 miles in 3.5 hours. Find its average mph.
  2. Five meters of fabric cost $37.50. Calculate the cost per meter and the projected cost of eight meters.
  3. Machine A packs 96 boxes in 8 minutes; Machine B packs 105 boxes in 7 minutes. Which has the higher rate? Explain.
  4. A tank contains 4 liters after 1 minute and 8 liters after 2 minutes. Assuming constant flow from empty, predict its volume at 6 minutes. Write an equation.
  5. A learner graphs hours vertically and distance horizontally but labels the line as miles per hour. Explain how swapping the axes changes the graph's slope units.

Review: Verify division direction, numeric result, units, and whether the situation reasonably supports assuming a constant rate. If you make a mistake, identify it and correct it.

Friday — Independent mastery demonstration

A (2 points): Find the unit rate for 75 pages read in 5 hours; label units. B (3 points): Complete a table for a tap filling 4 L/min at 0, 2, and 5 minutes, then write y = kx and identify k. C (3 points): Compare 72 items in 6 minutes and 80 items in 8 minutes; calculate each rate and defend which is faster. D (2 points): A report claims the second group is faster because it produced more items. Explain the flaw using evidence.

Rubric: 10 points total; 8/10 = 80% for this teacher-reviewed demonstration. This lesson's written activity does not itself set a verified mastery flag or unlock a subsequent week. The Academy's official assessment process remains authoritative.
