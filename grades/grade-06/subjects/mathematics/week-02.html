Week 02 · Mathematics · Five-Day Learning Studio

Evidence, Annotation, and Ratios

How can a ratio table provide evidence for a fair comparison?

Learning goals and vocabulary

Review Week 1 ratios and unit rates. This week, interpret part-to-part versus part-to-whole ratios; generate and test equivalent ratios; use double number lines and tables; and defend or reject a proportional claim using calculated evidence. Part-to-part compares two groups; part-to-whole compares a group with the total. A proportional relationship keeps a constant multiplier between two quantities.

Monday — Ratios tell different stories

A garden has 6 tomato plants and 4 pepper plants. The tomato-to-pepper ratio is 6:4 = 3:2. The tomato-to-all-plants ratio is 6:10 = 3:5. These represent different questions! Label both categories before writing a ratio. An annotation should explain what each number counts.

Worked example: In a club, 8 students chose chess and 12 chose art. Chess:art = 8:12 = 2:3; chess:total = 8:20 = 2:5. The ratios cannot be swapped because their denominators have different meanings.

Guided questions: (1) If 9 learners walk and 6 cycle, find walk:cycle in simplest form. (2) Find walk:total. (3) Explain which ratio answers “What fraction of all learners walk?”

Tuesday — Build equivalent-ratio tables

A recipe uses 2 cups of oats for every 3 cups of milk. Multiply both quantities by the same factor to preserve taste. A ratio table makes the reasoning visible:

Oats (cups)2468
Milk (cups)36912

Double number line: Mark matching points (0,0), (2,3), (4,6), (6,9). Both lines start at zero, and paired intervals use consistent scaling. For 10 cups oats, 10 ÷ 2 = 5 batches; milk = 3 × 5 = 15 cups.

Practice: (4) Complete 3:5 = 12:__. (5) Fill in milk for 14 cups of oats. (6) Is 8:12 equivalent to 2:3? Prove it using division or multiplication.

Wednesday — Test a claim with evidence

Team A says 4 blue tiles for every 6 red tiles creates the same color ratio as Team B's 10 blue for every 15 red. Claim: They match. Evidence: A = 4:6 = 2:3; B = 10:15 = 2:3. Reasoning: Both ratios reduce to the same pair, so the claim is supported.

Investigation: Compare Plan C (6 cleanups in 9 weeks) and Plan D (10 cleanups in 15 weeks). Write the two rates, simplify them, sketch equivalent pairs, and decide whether the pace is the same. Record what the comparison does not tell you—for example, the quality or duration of each cleanup.

Thursday — Challenge, errors, and revision

  1. A box contains 12 green and 18 yellow beads. Find green:yellow and green:total, both simplified.
  2. Complete 5:8 = 25:__.
  3. A student states 3:4 = 9:16 because both numbers were increased. Explain and correct the mistake.
  4. Decide whether 12:20 and 18:30 are equivalent. Show your proof.
  5. Two drawings use widths and heights of 6 cm × 9 cm and 10 cm × 15 cm. Do they have the same shape proportions? Explain.

Self-review: Mark what each quantity measures; test whether a single multiplier acts on both terms; correct one error with a short explanation.

Optional preview, not a Week 2 mastery prerequisite: The fraction 2/5 is equivalent to 40/100, or 40%. Formal percent instruction follows later in the sequence.

Friday — Independent mastery demonstration

(A) 15 runners and 10 walkers: express runners:walkers and runners:total in simplest form (3 points). (B) Complete a 4:7 ratio for a first quantity of 20; show the scale factor (2 points). (C) Are 9:12 and 15:20 equivalent? Show two steps of evidence (3 points). (D) Explain how a part-to-whole ratio differs from part-to-part (2 points).

Rubric: 10 points; 8/10 meets the 80% mastery standard for teacher review. This lesson does not itself record or unlock verified mastery. Use the Academy's separately authorized assessment and progress records.

Worked answers — review only after trying the activities

1. 3:2; 2. 3:5; 3. walk:total. 4. 20; 5. 21 cups milk; 6. yes, multiply 2:3 by four. Investigation: 6:9 = 2:3 and 10:15 = 2:3, equal rates. 7. 2:3 and 2:5; 8. 40; 9. 3:4 = 9:12, not 9:16; 10. yes, both 3:5; 11. yes, both 2:3. Friday A = 3:2 and 3:5; B = 35, scale factor 5; C = yes, both 3:4; D = distinguish group:group from group:total.

If below mastery: Revisit the two worked examples, draw two labeled models, correct your errors, and take a different authorized mastery check. Do not count a practice worksheet as a scored pass.
