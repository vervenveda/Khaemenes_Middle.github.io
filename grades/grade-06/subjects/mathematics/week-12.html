Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 12

Geometry: Area, Surface Area, and Design

How do measurements help designers plan efficient spaces?

Learning targets

Calculate area of triangles and rectangles, decompose composite figures, identify nets, calculate closed and lidless rectangular-prism surface areas, and label appropriate square units. Explain the constraints and limits of geometric design models.

Monday — Area of polygons

Area counts square units covering a flat surface. A rectangle 8 cm by 5 cm has area 40 cm². A triangle with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm has half that area: 20 cm². Height must be perpendicular to the base, not a sloping side.

Practice: calculate a 7 × 4 rectangle and a triangle with base 12 and height 6; draw the shape and label units.

Tuesday — Decompose composite figures

An L-shaped floor can be divided into nonoverlapping rectangles. For a 10 m × 8 m outer rectangle with a 4 m × 3 m corner removed, area is 80 − 12 = 68 m². Another valid approach adds the remaining rectangles. Avoid counting the removed corner twice.

Practice: find the area of a 9 m × 7 m rectangle with a 2 m × 3 m corner removed; compare subtraction and subdivision.

Wednesday — Nets and surface area

Surface area is the total area of all outside faces of a three-dimensional object, measured in square units. A closed rectangular box with length 6 cm, width 4 cm, height 3 cm has surface area 2(6×4 + 6×3 + 4×3) = 108 cm². A net unfolds the six faces without changing their total area.

Practice: sketch and label a net for a 5 × 3 × 2 cm closed prism. Calculate its surface area and identify each pair of matching faces.

Thursday — Design constraints and measurement

Suppose a fictional art display needs a lidless box measuring 8 cm long, 5 cm wide, and 4 cm high. Include the base and four sides, but no top: 8×5 + 2(8×4) + 2(5×4) = 144 cm². A closed box would need another 40 cm² for the top. Explain design choices, materials, and whether seams or waste would increase actual material needs.

Practice: design a lidless 6 × 4 × 3 cm box; calculate its surface area and explain why the closed-box formula would overcount.

Friday — Independent mastery evidence

A (2 points): area of a triangle with base 10 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm. B (2 points): area of a 12 × 9 m rectangle with a 3 × 4 m corner removed. C (3 points): surface area of a closed 4 × 3 × 2 cm prism, showing all face pairs. D (3 points): area of material for a lidless 5 × 4 × 3 cm box, with a labeled net and explanation.

Teacher-reviewed work is 10 points; 8/10 is an instructional threshold, not an automatic verified record or unlock. Revisit diagrams and unit labels for remediation, and complete the formal assessment separately.
