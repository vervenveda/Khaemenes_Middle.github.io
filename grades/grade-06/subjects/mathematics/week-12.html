Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 12

Geometry: Area, Surface Area, and Design

How do measurements help designers plan efficient spaces?

Learning targets

Calculate area of triangles and rectangles, decompose composite figures, identify nets, calculate closed and lidless rectangular-prism surface areas, and label appropriate square units. Explain the constraints and limits of geometric design models.

Monday — Area of polygons

Area counts square units covering a flat surface. A rectangle 8 cm by 5 cm has area 40 cm². A triangle with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm has half that area: 20 cm². Height must be perpendicular to the base, not a sloping side.

Practice: calculate a 7 × 4 rectangle and a triangle with base 12 and height 6; draw the shape and label units.

Justify polygon area · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: perpendicular height, square unit. Define the terms and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: A triangle with base 9 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm has area 1/2 ×9×4=18 cm²; the slanted side is not necessarily the height.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find a triangle's area with base 14 m and perpendicular height 5 m.
  2. Find a rectangle area measuring 3.5 cm by 4 cm.
  3. Explain why doubling only the height doubles triangle area.
  4. A triangle's area is 24 cm² and base 8 cm. Find perpendicular height.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

35 m²; 14 cm²; area is proportional to base×height; height 6 cm.

Tuesday — Decompose composite figures

An L-shaped floor can be divided into nonoverlapping rectangles. For a 10 m × 8 m outer rectangle with a 4 m × 3 m corner removed, area is 80 − 12 = 68 m². Another valid approach adds the remaining rectangles. Avoid counting the removed corner twice.

Practice: find the area of a 9 m × 7 m rectangle with a 2 m × 3 m corner removed; compare subtraction and subdivision.

Decompose without double counting · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: composite shape, subtract, partition. Define the terms and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: An outer 12×9 rectangle with a 3×4 corner cut away has area 108−12=96 square units.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Subtract a 2×5 cutout from a 10×8 rectangle.
  2. Compute an L-shaped region from 8×6 rectangle less 3×2 cutout.
  3. Explain why overlapping partitions may overcount area.
  4. Recall Week 05: compute 3/4 ÷ 1/8.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

70 square units; 42 square units; overlapped area is added twice; 6.

Wednesday — Nets and surface area

Surface area is the total area of all outside faces of a three-dimensional object, measured in square units. A closed rectangular box with length 6 cm, width 4 cm, height 3 cm has surface area 2(6×4 + 6×3 + 4×3) = 108 cm². A net unfolds the six faces without changing their total area.

Practice: sketch and label a net for a 5 × 3 × 2 cm closed prism. Calculate its surface area and identify each pair of matching faces.

Use nets for closed prisms · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: net, face, surface area. Define the terms and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: A closed prism 4×3×2 cm has face pairs 12,8,6 cm², totaling 2(12+8+6)=52 cm².

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find surface area of a closed 5×4×3 cm prism.
  2. List the three different face dimensions of a 7×2×3 cm prism.
  3. Explain why a complete net needs six nonoverlapping faces.
  4. Calculate a 2×2×2 cube's surface area.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

94 cm²; 7×2,7×3,2×3; six faces compose the prism boundary; 24 square units.

Thursday — Design constraints and measurement

Suppose a fictional art display needs a lidless box measuring 8 cm long, 5 cm wide, and 4 cm high. Include the base and four sides, but no top: 8×5 + 2(8×4) + 2(5×4) = 144 cm². A closed box would need another 40 cm² for the top. Explain design choices, materials, and whether seams or waste would increase actual material needs.

Practice: design a lidless 6 × 4 × 3 cm box; calculate its surface area and explain why the closed-box formula would overcount.

Compare closed and open designs · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: lidless, material, seam allowance. Define the terms and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: For a lidless box 6×4×2 cm, surface area is base 24 plus two 12 and two 8 cm² sides =64 cm². A lid adds 24.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find material area for lidless 8×3×2 cm box.
  2. Compare lidless and closed versions of a 5×4×2 cm box.
  3. Why might actual cardboard needed exceed calculated surface area?
  4. Explain why square units, not cubic units, measure the material.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

68 cm²; lidless 56 cm², closed 76 cm²; tabs, waste and seam allowances; material covers surface faces.

Transfer geometry with review · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: area, net, estimate. Define the terms and use one in a mathematical explanation.

Worked model: A 10×6 m rectangular yard minus a 2×4 m pond has 52 m² usable area; subtracting rather than adding the pond avoids double count.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find area of a triangle with base 16 cm and height 7 cm.
  2. Find surface area of a closed 3×4×5 cm prism.
  3. Recall Week 11: calculate median of 2,3,9,10.
  4. Recall Week 08: expand 3(x+4).
  5. Describe an error from confusing height with a triangle's slanted side.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)
