Week 04 · cumulative retrieval

Percent, proportion, and evidence

Percent means per 100; distinguish an exact calculation from a survey estimate.

Solve independently, record a representation and units where needed, then explain your reasoning before revealing the worked rationale. Repeat after two and seven days.

Reasoning challenge 1

What is 35% of 240? Give the number.

Show reasoning after trying

35/100 × 240 = 84. Check: 30% is 72 and 5% is 12; together 84.

Reasoning challenge 2

18 students out of 60 chose walking. What percent chose walking? Give the percent number.

Show reasoning after trying

18 ÷ 60 = 0.30 = 30%. State the denominator because it defines the whole.

Reasoning challenge 3

A $50 price decreases by 20%. What is the new price in dollars?

Show reasoning after trying

20% of $50 is $10; subtract $10 from $50, giving $40. Do not report the discount alone.

Reasoning challenge 4

A survey finds 24 of 80 respondents favor a park. What fraction of those surveyed is in favor, in simplest form?

Show reasoning after trying

24/80 simplifies by 8 to 3/10, or 30%. The survey result does not automatically represent all residents.

Reasoning challenge 5

A student says 25% of 80 is 25 because the percent is 25. Is that correct? Answer yes or no.

Show reasoning after trying

25% means 25/100, so 0.25 × 80 = 20.

Earlier-learning retrieval 6

Retrieval: A plan uses 3 cups of water for 5 cups of flour. How much water for 20 cups of flour?

Show reasoning after trying

Scale 5 cups of flour to 20 by ×4; water scales 3 × 4 = 12 cups.

Week 05 · cumulative retrieval

Fraction division and written justification

Interpret how many groups fit, model the quotient, and multiply to verify.

Solve independently, record a representation and units where needed, then explain your reasoning before revealing the worked rationale. Repeat after two and seven days.

Reasoning challenge 1

How many one-fourth-cup servings are in 3 cups?

Show reasoning after trying

3 ÷ (1/4) = 3 × 4 = 12. A fraction strip shows four fourths in every whole.

Reasoning challenge 2

Compute 2/3 ÷ 1/6. Enter the whole number.

Show reasoning after trying

2/3 is 4/6. Four sixths fit into 2/3. Check 4 × 1/6 = 2/3.

Reasoning challenge 3

Compute 3/4 ÷ 3/8. Enter the whole number.

Show reasoning after trying

Multiply by the reciprocal: 3/4 × 8/3 = 2. Verify 2 × 3/8 = 3/4.

Reasoning challenge 4

A ribbon 2 1/2 meters long is cut into 1/2-meter pieces. How many pieces?

Show reasoning after trying

Convert 2 1/2 to 5/2; then (5/2) ÷ (1/2) = 5.

Reasoning challenge 5

A student claims 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/8 because we multiply the denominators. Is that correct? Answer yes or no.

Show reasoning after trying

Division asks how many fourths fit in one half: two. Multiply by the reciprocal, not directly by the divisor.

Earlier-learning retrieval 6

Retrieval: What is 15% of 200?

Show reasoning after trying

0.15 × 200 = 30; 10% is 20 and 5% is 10.

Week 06 · cumulative retrieval

Rational numbers, comparisons, and transfer

Locate signed values on a number line; distinguish a number, its opposite, and its distance from zero.

Solve independently, record a representation and units where needed, then explain your reasoning before revealing the worked rationale. Repeat after two and seven days.

Reasoning challenge 1

Which number is smaller: -7 or -3? Enter the smaller integer.

Show reasoning after trying

On a number line −7 lies left of −3, so it is smaller.

Reasoning challenge 2

What is the opposite of -4.5?

Show reasoning after trying

An opposite is the same distance from zero on the other side; it changes the sign.

Reasoning challenge 3

What is the absolute value of -8?

Show reasoning after trying

Absolute value is distance from zero and cannot be negative.

Reasoning challenge 4

Order -1/2 and -0.7: which is greater? Enter -0.5 or -0.7.

Show reasoning after trying

−1/2 = −0.5 and −0.5 is to the right of −0.7 on the number line.

Reasoning challenge 5

A thermometer reads -2 °C then rises by 5 °C. What is the new temperature in °C?

Show reasoning after trying

Start at −2; move five steps right on the number line to +3.

Earlier-learning retrieval 6

Retrieval: How many one-sixth-cup measures fit in 2/3 cup?

Show reasoning after trying

2/3 = 4/6, so four measures of 1/6 fit. This retrieves Week 05 fraction division.
