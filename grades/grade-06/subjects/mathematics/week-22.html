Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 22

Earth Systems and Water: Rational Numbers

How can signed quantities describe Earth-system observations?

Learning goals

Compare signed decimals and fractions, use number lines, calculate differences and changes, and interpret averages of deviations. All observations are hypothetical classroom models.

Monday — Signed elevation

A fictional groundwater sensor sits 4.5 meters below a sea-level reference, represented as −4.5 m. A hill station at +3.5 m is 8 m above it: 3.5 − (−4.5) = 8. Distinguish the reference point from physical water depth.

Practice: calculate the difference between −6 m and +2 m, then between −1.5 m and +3 m.

Represent signed elevation · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: reference, signed, displacement. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: A fictional elevation changes from −7.5 m to +2 m. Signed change is 2−(−7.5)=+9.5 m, relative to the chosen reference.

Guided pathway: Mark both endpoints on a horizontal number line. Subtract final minus initial for signed change; take the absolute value for distance. Explain what the chosen zero represents.

Misconception to avoid: A negative coordinate is a position relative to a reference, not automatically a negative physical amount.

Self-check before the teacher key: Verify that the distance from −8 to +5 is positive, while change from +4 to −3 is negative; attach meters to both.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find distance from −8 m to +5 m.
  2. Find signed change from +4 m to −3 m.
  3. Place −2.5 and −5/2 on a number line and compare.
  4. Explain why 'below reference' does not necessarily mean actual water depth.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

13 m; −7 m; equal points; the chosen zero reference may not be a water surface.

Tuesday — Compare rational temperatures

A simulated winter reading changes from −2.5°C to +1.5°C. The signed change is +4°C. A temperature change of −3°C means cooling, not necessarily a temperature below zero.

Practice: compute changes from +4°C to −1°C and −3.5°C to −0.5°C; justify signs.

Find signed temperature differences · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: signed change, final, initial. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: From −3.5°C to +1°C, final minus initial is 1−(−3.5)=+4.5°C. A negative result would indicate cooling.

Guided pathway: Draw two thermometers and label initial and final readings. Work through final − initial, keeping the signs visible.

Misconception to avoid: A final temperature below zero is not the same as a negative temperature change; warming can end below zero.

Self-check before the teacher key: Substitute −4 − (+6) for the first change and −2 − (−7) for the second; identify which situation warms.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Compute change from +6°C to −4°C.
  2. Compute change from −7°C to −2°C.
  3. From −1°C a 3.5°C decrease reaches what temperature?
  4. Describe why a negative final temperature and negative change are different statements.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

−10°C; +5°C; −4.5°C; reading below zero differs from decreasing temperature.

Wednesday — Rational-number operations

A monitoring station starts at −3/4 meter relative to a reference and changes by +1/2 meter. Adding −3/4 + 1/2 = −1/4 meter. Place fractions on a number line and use common denominators.

Practice: −2/3 + 1/6 and 3/4 − 5/4; check with a number line.

Compute rational-number sums · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: common denominator, opposite, rational. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: −5/6+1/4=−10/12+3/12=−7/12. Use a number line to check the sign.

Guided pathway: Choose a common denominator before adding fractions. Sketch unit segments on a number line so the sign of each result can be checked.

Misconception to avoid: Adding denominators or ignoring the negative sign gives an incorrect rational-number sum.

Self-check before the teacher key: Re-express −3/4 + 1/8 as −6/8 + 1/8, then explain why the result remains negative.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Evaluate −3/4+1/8.
  2. Evaluate 2/3−5/6.
  3. Find the distance between −1/3 and +1/2.
  4. Explain why −1/2+3/4 is positive.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

−5/8; −1/6; 5/6; 3/4 has greater magnitude than 1/2 in the positive direction, total 1/4.

Thursday — Interpret averages cautiously

Fictional rainfall departures from a baseline are −2, +1, +3 and −2 millimeters. Their sum is 0 mm and mean departure is 0 mm, even though individual departures vary. Use signed deviations to describe observations, not absolute rainfall totals.

Practice: average deviations −3,+2,+1,+4; interpret whether every value equals the mean.

Average signed deviations · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: deviation, mean, baseline. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: Deviations −4,+1,+3,0 sum to zero, so their mean is 0 even though none of the first three readings equals zero.

Guided pathway: Build a four-row sign-and-value table. Add the signed deviations first, then divide by the number of observations.

Misconception to avoid: A zero mean does not mean every observation is zero, and a deviation is not an absolute rainfall measurement.

Self-check before the teacher key: Check −5+2+4+3 before dividing by four; compare a canceled sum with individual data points.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find mean deviation of −5,+2,+4,+3.
  2. Find the mean of −3,−1,+2,+6.
  3. Explain why mean departure 0 mm does not mean each day had zero rainfall.
  4. Recall Week 21: compute 3÷1/6.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

+1; +1; positive and negative departures can cancel; 18.

Synthesize Earth-system mathematical models · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: interpret, compare, scale. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: If a fictional map has scale 1 cm:4 km, a 2.5 cm line models 10 km; it does not measure actual route conditions.

Guided pathway: Select a representation for each problem: number line, signed arithmetic, mean table, scale factor or percent model. Label units in every answer.

Misconception to avoid: A fictional map scale gives modeled distance, not travel duration or confirmation of a real route.

Self-check before the teacher key: Use inverse operations to check the map scale: 13.5 km ÷ 3 km per cm = 4.5 cm.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Calculate −4.5+7.
  2. Find distance between −3.5 and +2.5.
  3. Find mean of deviations −2,+1,+3,−2.
  4. A map uses 1 cm:3 km; what modeled distance is 4.5 cm?
  5. Recall Week 20: compare 12/40 and 15/60 as percents.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

2.5; 6; 0; 13.5 km; 30% versus 25%.

Crosswalk extension — Water-use quantities, ratios, percentages and scale

Purpose: Connect the existing rational-number and signed-change lessons with quantitative watershed modeling. All data below are fictional, not measured local conditions.

Ratio and unit comparison

A model classroom collects 12 L of rainwater for a fictional garden, using 3 L for each of four planting beds. Write the ratio of use to total as 3:12 = 1:4, and state that one bed represents 25% of the total.

Percent change

Simulated water consumption drops from 80 L to 60 L. The decrease is 20 L; 20 / 80 = 25%. Explain why a 20 L difference and a 25% reduction describe different but consistent measurements.

Graph and average

Plot daily fictional rainfall amounts of 2, 0, 5, 1 and 2 mm. Label both axes and write the arithmetic mean: (2 + 0 + 5 + 1 + 2) / 5 = 2 mm. Rainfall at a single fictional station does not prove total watershed recharge.

Map scale

A paper map uses 1 cm : 2 km. A 3.5 cm path represents 7 km in the map model. Distinguish map distance from actual travel conditions and show units at each step.

Friday evidence bridge

Prepare a water-use chart, ratio and percent explanation, rainfall graph, and scaled route, with a reasonableness check. Grade with 20 points: computations 6, diagrams 5, units 5, reasoning 4. Retain the existing Friday formative task and answer key as independent practice.
