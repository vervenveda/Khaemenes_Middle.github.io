Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 22

Earth Systems and Water: Rational Numbers

How can signed quantities describe Earth-system observations?

Learning goals

Compare signed decimals and fractions, use number lines, calculate differences and changes, and interpret averages of deviations. All observations are hypothetical classroom models.

Monday — Signed elevation

A fictional groundwater sensor sits 4.5 meters below a sea-level reference, represented as −4.5 m. A hill station at +3.5 m is 8 m above it: 3.5 − (−4.5) = 8. Distinguish the reference point from physical water depth.

Practice: calculate the difference between −6 m and +2 m, then between −1.5 m and +3 m.

Tuesday — Compare rational temperatures

A simulated winter reading changes from −2.5°C to +1.5°C. The signed change is +4°C. A temperature change of −3°C means cooling, not necessarily a temperature below zero.

Practice: compute changes from +4°C to −1°C and −3.5°C to −0.5°C; justify signs.

Wednesday — Rational-number operations

A monitoring station starts at −3/4 meter relative to a reference and changes by +1/2 meter. Adding −3/4 + 1/2 = −1/4 meter. Place fractions on a number line and use common denominators.

Practice: −2/3 + 1/6 and 3/4 − 5/4; check with a number line.

Thursday — Interpret averages cautiously

Fictional rainfall departures from a baseline are −2, +1, +3 and −2 millimeters. Their sum is 0 mm and mean departure is 0 mm, even though individual departures vary. Use signed deviations to describe observations, not absolute rainfall totals.

Practice: average deviations −3,+2,+1,+4; interpret whether every value equals the mean.

Friday — Formative mathematics defense

A (2 points): −4.5 + 7. B (2 points): find distance between −3.5 and +2.5. C (3 points): calculate −3/4 + 1/2 and illustrate on a number line. D (3 points): calculate and interpret the mean deviation of −2,+1,+3,−2.

The 10-point review is formative; only the saved randomized weekly exam records verified mastery.

Crosswalk extension — Water-use quantities, ratios, percentages and scale

Purpose: Connect the existing rational-number and signed-change lessons with quantitative watershed modeling. All data below are fictional, not measured local conditions.

Ratio and unit comparison

A model classroom collects 12 L of rainwater for a fictional garden, using 3 L for each of four planting beds. Write the ratio of use to total as 3:12 = 1:4, and state that one bed represents 25% of the total.

Percent change

Simulated water consumption drops from 80 L to 60 L. The decrease is 20 L; 20 / 80 = 25%. Explain why a 20 L difference and a 25% reduction describe different but consistent measurements.

Graph and average

Plot daily fictional rainfall amounts of 2, 0, 5, 1 and 2 mm. Label both axes and write the arithmetic mean: (2 + 0 + 5 + 1 + 2) / 5 = 2 mm. Rainfall at a single fictional station does not prove total watershed recharge.

Map scale

A paper map uses 1 cm : 2 km. A 3.5 cm path represents 7 km in the map model. Distinguish map distance from actual travel conditions and show units at each step.

Friday evidence bridge

Prepare a water-use chart, ratio and percent explanation, rainfall graph, and scaled route, with a reasonableness check. Grade with 20 points: computations 6, diagrams 5, units 5, reasoning 4. Retain the existing Friday formative task and answer key as independent practice.

Answer key

Check after solving

Monday: 8 m and 4.5 m. Tuesday: −5°C and +3°C. Wednesday: −1/2, −1/2. Thursday: mean departure +1 mm. Friday: 2.5; 6; −1/4; zero mean deviation.
