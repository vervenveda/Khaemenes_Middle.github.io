Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 24

Ancient River Civilizations: Algebraic Expressions

How can expressions describe and compare simplified resource models?

Learning goals

Translate verbal rules into expressions, evaluate variables, apply the distributive property, combine like terms, and critique mathematical assumptions. Scenarios are fictional models and not historical claims.

Monday — Variables for river planning

A fictional settlement stores 4 baskets for each of n farms, plus 6 shared baskets. Write 4n+6. For n=5, storage is 26 baskets. Variables name changing quantities; coefficients scale them, and constants stay fixed.

Practice: write an expression for 3 containers per household plus 8 communal containers; evaluate at 7 households.

Create expressions from situations · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: variable, coefficient, constant. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: A fictional river village keeps five baskets per household and seven shared baskets. For h households, total baskets B=5h+7; at h=4, B=27.

Guided pathway: Name the variable and constant before constructing an expression. Translate 'times' into multiplication and 'more than' into addition outside the product.

Misconception to avoid: Three times a number plus eight is 3n+8, not 3(n+8).

Self-check before the teacher key: Substitute n=2 in both proposed expressions to show they do not give the same value.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Translate 'eight more than three times n' into an expression.
  2. Evaluate 4f+9 for f=6.
  3. Write a rule for 2 jars per household plus 11 common jars.
  4. Explain why 6(x+2) differs from 6x+2.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3n+8; 33; 2h+11; 6x+12 is not equivalent to 6x+2.

Tuesday — Equivalent expressions

A hypothetical irrigation model uses 3(x+4) water units. Distribute to obtain 3x+12. Both expressions produce 27 when x=5. A model's equivalence is algebraic; it does not prove historical measurements.

Practice: expand 4(y+3) and 2(3a+5). Verify one with substitution.

Apply the distributive property · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: distribute, equivalent, model. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: Four rows each hold (x+3) pots: 4(x+3)=4x+12. The same model works with a fixed number of additional pots per row.

Guided pathway: Use an area model or draw equal groups. Multiply the outer factor by both terms inside parentheses, including constants.

Misconception to avoid: Distributing to the variable but not to the constant changes the expression.

Self-check before the teacher key: For 3(2a+4), expand to 6a+12 and verify both sides at a=1.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Expand 5(y+2).
  2. Expand 3(2a+4).
  3. Evaluate 2(x+6) and 2x+12 at x=−1.
  4. Give a counterexample showing 4(p+3) is not 4p+3.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

5y+10; 6a+12; both 10; at p=0, values 12 versus 3.

Wednesday — Combine like terms

A pretend granary receives 5a sacks then 2a sacks and uses 3 sacks: 5a+2a−3=7a−3. You can combine a-terms, but a constant cannot be added as if it were a-quantity.

Practice: simplify 6p+3p+4 and 8m−3m+2+5.

Combine like terms and respect units · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: like terms, factor, simplify. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: A model adds 4c+6c containers and then removes 3 containers: 10c−3, not 7c. A constant cannot be combined with a variable term.

Guided pathway: Underline terms containing the same variable and exponent. Combine only those coefficients; simplify constants separately.

Misconception to avoid: Unlike terms cannot be combined into a product simply because they are adjacent.

Self-check before the teacher key: At x=4, compare 5x+3x−2 with 8x−2 and confirm that both give 30.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Simplify 6n+3n−5.
  2. Simplify 8a−2a+7−4.
  3. Evaluate 5x+3x−2 when x=4.
  4. Explain why 3p+2q is not 5pq.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9n−5; 6a+3; 30; p and q represent different categories or dimensions and cannot be combined as like terms.

Thursday — Translate and critique models

A fictional transport route charges 7 units per trip plus a 12-unit fixed cost, represented by 7t+12. A second plan 5t+20 costs the same at t=4, but differs for other trip counts. An expression describes assumptions, not a verified account of ancient river civilizations.

Practice: evaluate both at t=3 and t=6; compare and explain.

Compare expressions in context · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: evaluate, fixed cost, comparison. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: Two fictional transport plans A=8t+10 and B=5t+25 have equal cost at t=5 because both total 50 units; for t=2 costs are 26 and 35.

Guided pathway: Create a two-column table for plans A=8t+10 and B=5t+25. Substitute each trip count, then solve the equality to locate break-even.

Misconception to avoid: A plan that is cheaper at one trip count is not necessarily cheaper at every trip count.

Self-check before the teacher key: At t=5 both plans cost 50 units. Check t=4 and t=8 to determine which becomes cheaper.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Evaluate A and B at t=4.
  2. Evaluate A and B at t=8.
  3. Find their break-even trip count by solving 8t+10=5t+25.
  4. Explain why a model does not prove actual ancient transport fees.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

42 versus 45, A cheaper; 74 versus 65, B cheaper; t=5; charges were invented for mathematical practice.

Explain algebraic equivalence and retain skills · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: substitution, identity, justify. Define the terms and use one in a clear mathematical statement.

Worked model: For 3(2x+5) and 6x+15, distribution establishes equality for every value of x; testing one value is only a check.

Guided pathway: Explain whether your solution uses algebraic equivalence or a numerical check. Define variables, units and the meaning of the fixed term.

Misconception to avoid: Testing one input supports an algebraic identity but does not prove equivalence for every value.

Self-check before the teacher key: Expand 4(a+3)−2a two ways, then verify the simplified result with a=−2.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Simplify 4(a+3)−2a.
  2. Evaluate 2n²−3 for n=−2.
  3. Recall Week 23: reflect (−3,4) across x-axis.
  4. Recall Week 21: compute 3/4 ÷ 1/8.
  5. Write an original expression with a variable and fixed term and explain its units.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

2a+12; 5; (−3,−4); 6; examples vary, but must define units and variables.
