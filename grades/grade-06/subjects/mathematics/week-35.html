Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 35

Capstone Studio and Oral Defense: Surface Area and Volume

How can geometry defend the design and material choices of a prototype?

Learning goals

Calculate rectangle and triangle area, rectangular- and triangular-prism volume, and surface area of closed and open-top structures. Prepare a concise oral defense with labeled nets, units, verification, and limitations. All dimensions are fictional practice models.

Monday — Build a dimensioned prototype

A fictional presentation display has rectangular base 6 cm by 4 cm and height 3 cm. The footprint area is 24 cm², while interior volume is 72 cm³. Area uses square units; volume uses cubic units.

Practice: find footprint and volume of a 5×3×4 cm model, label units and sketch all dimensions.

Defend area and capacity · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: footprint, dimension, cubic unit. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A proposed display is 7×4×3 cm. Footprint is 28 cm², while interior volume is 84 cm³. Equal units cannot describe these different quantities.

Independent problems:

  1. A model measures 8×5×3 cm. Find its footprint area and volume.
  2. A display floor 6×4 cm remains the same when its height changes from 3 cm to 6 cm. Compare floor areas and volumes.
  3. Explain with an example why cm² cannot label a volume.
  4. Recall Week 34: solve 5+6n≤35 for nonnegative whole n.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

40 cm² and 120 cm³; footprint 24 cm² both times, volume 72 versus 144 cm³; cm² is two-dimensional and cm³ measures three-dimensional space; n=0 through 5.

Tuesday — Surface area and nets

A closed model box 4×3×2 cm needs 2(12+8+6)=52 cm² of exterior material. Draw six faces and identify three pairs of congruent rectangles.

Practice: calculate exterior area for a closed 5×2×3 cm prism and sketch a net.

Unfold a net and count faces · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: net, congruent, surface area. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: For a 5×4×2 cm closed prism, opposite faces have areas 20,10,8 cm²; total surface area is 2(20+10+8)=76 cm².

Independent problems:

  1. Find surface area of a closed 6×3×2 cm prism.
  2. List all six rectangular face dimensions for a 4×3×2 cm prism.
  3. A 5×4×2 cm box has no top. Find its exterior face area without the lid.
  4. Explain why material seams could change the practical estimate.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

72 cm²; two faces each 4×3, 4×2, 3×2; 76−20=56 cm²; construction tabs, overlaps and waste require material beyond ideal faces.

Wednesday — Triangular-prism displays

A right-triangular base has legs 3 cm and 4 cm, so its area is 6 cm². With prism length 10 cm, the volume is 60 cm³.

Practice: calculate volume of a triangular prism with base 6 cm, triangle height 4 cm and prism length 5 cm.

Explain triangle-prism volume · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: perpendicular height, cross-section, length. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A triangle with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm has area 20 cm²; extended 6 cm, its prism volume is 120 cm³.

Independent problems:

  1. Find prism volume with triangular base 12 cm, perpendicular height 4 cm and length 7 cm.
  2. If triangular base area is 15 cm² and volume is 90 cm³, find prism length.
  3. Explain why using a sloping edge instead of perpendicular triangle height is unsafe.
  4. Compare a 4×3×5 rectangular prism with a triangular prism of base area 10 cm² and length 6 cm.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

168 cm³; 6 cm; height must be perpendicular to the chosen base; both volumes are 60 cm³.

Thursday — Critique a prototype assumption

A fictional open-top rectangular container 6×4×2 m has volume 48 m³ but external face area excluding top 24+24+16=64 m². Ask which faces need material and whether thickness, waste or supports have been omitted.

Practice: calculate open-top surface area and volume for a 5×3×2 m model; propose a design revision.

Critique open-top designs · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: lidless, surface, volume. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A hypothetical open 7×3×2 m box holds 42 m³ (ideal) and needs base-and-sides 21+2(14)+2(6)=61 m² of material.

Independent problems:

  1. Calculate ideal capacity and exposed-material area for a lidless 5×4×3 cm box.
  2. Find extra area to add a lid to that same box.
  3. Why might wall thickness reduce usable space?
  4. Recall Week 27: compare ranges of 4,6,8 and 2,6,10.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

Volume 60 cm³, area 20+30+24=74 cm²; add 20 cm²; physical walls occupy some interior volume; ranges 4 and 8.

Friday — Oral mathematical defense

Explain a model's dimensions, formula choices, square versus cubic units, one worked surface-area calculation and one worked volume calculation. Verify a numerical result by an alternative method or a face-by-face net.

This presentation and paper work are formative. The separate randomized examination determines verified mastery.

Present the mathematical defense · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: explain, validate, assumption. Define each term and use one in a justified example.

Worked example: A convincing design defense supplies labeled dimensions, formulas, units, a face-by-face check, and one limitation such as omitted seams.

Independent problems:

  1. For a 3×4×5 cm closed model compute surface area and volume.
  2. Find triangle area with base 9 cm and perpendicular height 8 cm.
  3. Recall Week 33: simplify 3(x+4)+2x.
  4. Recall Week 29: find 25% of 96.
  5. Give one model-based design claim and a numerical check that would support it.
Teacher solution key — open after your attempt

94 cm² and 60 cm³; 36 cm²; 5x+12; 24; original justified claim must include units and a second computation or measured check.
