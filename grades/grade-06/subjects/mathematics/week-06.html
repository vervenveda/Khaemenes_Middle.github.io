Week 06 · Mathematics · Five-Day Learning Studio

Rational Numbers and Ancient Number Systems

How have cultures represented numbers and meaning across time?

Learning targets

Plot, compare, and order integers and rational numbers; recognize equivalent fraction and decimal representations; use opposites and absolute value; interpret practical signed quantities; and recognize historical place-value systems. Vocabulary: integer, rational number, number line, opposite, absolute value, base, place value. Instruction is self-contained and available offline.

Monday — Integers and the number line

Positive numbers represent amounts above a reference point; negative numbers describe positions below it, debts, or temperatures below zero. On a number line, values increase to the right. Example: −4 is left of −1, so −4 < −1. Zero is neither positive nor negative.

Draw a number line from −6 to 6. Plot −5, −2, 0, 3, and 5. Practice: (1) Which is greater, −3 or −7? (2) Arrange 2, −5, 0, −1 in increasing order. (3) A temperature rises from −4°C to 2°C: how many degrees of change?

Tuesday — Rational numbers and equivalent forms

A rational number can be expressed as a fraction a/b with integers a and b, where b is not zero. Integers are rational because −3 = −3/1. Terminating decimals such as 0.75 = 3/4 and repeating decimals such as 0.333… = 1/3 are rational. To compare −1/2 and −3/4, use decimals −0.5 and −0.75: −1/2 is greater.

Worked example: Order −1.25, −3/4, 0, and 1/2: −1.25 < −0.75 < 0 < 0.5. Practice: (4) Convert −0.6 to a simplified fraction. (5) Compare −2/3 and −1/3. (6) Plot −1.5 and −1/2.

Wednesday — Opposites and absolute value

The opposite of −5 is 5: they are the same distance from zero on opposite sides. Absolute value describes distance from zero, so |−5| = 5 and |5| = 5. It is never negative. A bank balance of −$12 and a balance of +$12 are opposites; their absolute values are both 12 dollars, but their meanings differ.

Practice: (7) Find the opposite of 7/4. (8) Compute |−8| and |3/2|. (9) Explain why −9 < −2 although |−9| > |−2|. Model the values on a number line.

Thursday — Mathematical history and applications

Different civilizations developed different numeral systems. Ancient Egyptian numerals used signs for powers of ten; the Babylonian place-value system used base 60, a historical connection to minutes and seconds. These facts concern numeral representation, not whether the underlying quantity changes. Our common decimal positional notation uses base 10 and a zero placeholder.

Example: In base 10, 205 means 2 hundreds + 0 tens + 5 ones. In base 60, a positional numeral with digits (1, 30) represents 1×60 + 30 = 90, not decimal 130. Practice: (10) Explain why a placeholder zero matters in 205. (11) Compute 2×60 + 15. (12) An elevation is −30 m and another is +12 m relative to sea level. Which is lower, and by how many meters?

Friday — Independent mastery demonstration

A (2 points): Order −3/2, −1/4, 0, and 3/4 with a number-line explanation. B (2 points): Express −0.75 as a simplified fraction and compare it with −1/2. C (3 points): Find the opposite and absolute value of −7; explain why the two results have different definitions. D (3 points): Interpret base-60 digits (1, 25) in decimal and explain how place value differs from base 10.

Rubric: 10 points; 8/10 is the instructional 80% threshold. The lesson does not write a verified mastery record or unlock the next week. Official advancement remains governed by authorized Academy assessments and learner records. Below 80%, revise errors using a number line, explain the correction, then take a fresh authorized assessment.
