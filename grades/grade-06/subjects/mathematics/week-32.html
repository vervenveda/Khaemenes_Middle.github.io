Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 32

Health, Identity, and Responsible Choices: Coordinate Planes

How can coordinate grids help us organize ideas without making assumptions about people?

Learning goals

Plot signed coordinates, determine quadrants, measure horizontal and vertical grid distances, and reflect points across axes. All coordinate examples are fictional planning diagrams, not medical or identity assessments.

Monday — Locate points in every quadrant

A fictional wellness-planning grid uses x as schedule adjustment and y as activity-choice adjustment, both measured in arbitrary units relative to a zero baseline. (3,−2) lies in quadrant IV; (−4,2) lies in II. These are invented coordinate values, not health measurements.

Practice: plot (2,3), (−3,4), (−2,−1), (4,−3) and identify quadrants.

Locate meaningful grid points · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: ordered pair, x-axis, quadrant. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: On a hypothetical planning grid (−5,3) means five left and three up; the point lies in Quadrant II. It describes a location, not a learner trait.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Identify quadrants for (4,2), (−3,5), (−2,−6), and (7,−1).
  2. Plot and name a point four left and two down.
  3. Explain why (−4,2) and (2,−4) are different places.
  4. Is (0,−3) in a quadrant? Explain.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

I, II, III, IV; (−4,−2); coordinate order matters; no, the point lies on the negative y-axis.

Tuesday — Coordinate differences

A fictional planning map places station A at (−4,3) and B at (2,3). Their horizontal separation is |2−(−4)|=6 grid units. A pair at (2,−2) and (2,3) is separated vertically by 5 units.

Practice: find horizontal distance from (−5,1) to (3,1) and vertical distance from (2,−4) to (2,5).

Compute distance along axes · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: absolute difference, unit, horizontal. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: On an abstract grid A(−6,4) and B(2,4) are eight units apart horizontally. Calculate |2−(−6)|=8.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find horizontal distance between (−4,5) and (5,5).
  2. Find vertical distance between (3,−7) and (3,2).
  3. A route goes from (−2,−3) to (4,2) using only horizontal and vertical moves. Find total grid steps.
  4. Explain why straight-line route distance need not equal a real walking route.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9 units; 9 units; 6+5=11 steps; route constraints and obstacles may add distance.

Wednesday — Reflect points across axes

Reflecting (−3,2) across the y-axis yields (3,2); reflecting it across the x-axis yields (−3,−2). One coordinate changes sign for each axis reflection, preserving its magnitude.

Practice: reflect (4,−5) across the x-axis and y-axis separately. Draw and label both.

Reflect and verify symmetry · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: reflection, coordinates, x-axis, y-axis. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: Reflect (−5,−2) over the x-axis: (−5,2); over the y-axis: (5,−2). Reflect over both to obtain (5,2).

Independent reasoning:

  1. Reflect (4,−7) across the x-axis.
  2. Reflect (−6,3) across the y-axis.
  3. Reflect (−3,−5) across both axes.
  4. Explain what happens when (0,4) is reflected across the y-axis.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

(4,7); (6,3); (3,5); unchanged because the point lies on the mirror axis.

Thursday — Data versus coordinate location

If fictional points (−2,4), (0,4) and (3,4) share a y-coordinate, they lie on one horizontal line. That does not establish that learners have identical needs, health outcomes or preferences. Separate measurements and context are needed to justify any such claims.

Practice: choose three map positions and explain what cannot be inferred about the people from their coordinates.

Separate coordinate location from evidence · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: model, location, inference. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: Points A(−2,1), B(0,1), C(4,1) share a y-coordinate, but that does not imply identical preferences, needs, or outcomes.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Construct three different points on the line y=2.
  2. State two data fields needed to compare actual activity schedules rather than map locations.
  3. Find the horizontal separation between A(−3,2) and B(5,2).
  4. Explain a limitation of treating invented grid coordinates as measured personal information.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

Examples (−2,2),(0,2),(4,2); relevant separate observations with context and consent; 8 grid units; abstract labels contain no verified personal evidence.

Friday — Independent explanation

A: identify quadrant of (−5,−2). B: distance from (−3,2) to (4,2). C: reflect (2,−4) over each axis. D: plot (−2,3), (2,3), (2,−1), label axes and explain one limitation of the model.

This is formative practice. Only the saved randomized exam independently verifies mastery.

Synthesize coordinates with prior lessons · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: quadrant, justify, cumulative. Define the terms and apply one in your explanation.

Worked model: A point (−2,−4) reflected in the y-axis becomes (2,−4), still four units below x-axis. Compare signs, location, and units.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Reflect (−4,5) across the x-axis.
  2. Find distance from (−5,−1) to (3,−1).
  3. Recall Week 31: calculate −3/4+1/2.
  4. Recall Week 28: simplify 24:36.
  5. Explain why a map alone cannot establish health or identity classifications.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

(−4,−5); 8 units; −1/4; 2:3; no personal or medical conclusions follow from invented coordinates.
