Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 32

Health, Identity, and Responsible Choices: Coordinate Planes

How can coordinate grids help us organize ideas without making assumptions about people?

Learning goals

Plot signed coordinates, determine quadrants, measure horizontal and vertical grid distances, and reflect points across axes. All coordinate examples are fictional planning diagrams, not medical or identity assessments.

Monday — Locate points in every quadrant

A fictional wellness-planning grid uses x as schedule adjustment and y as activity-choice adjustment, both measured in arbitrary units relative to a zero baseline. (3,−2) lies in quadrant IV; (−4,2) lies in II. These are invented coordinate values, not health measurements.

Practice: plot (2,3), (−3,4), (−2,−1), (4,−3) and identify quadrants.

Tuesday — Coordinate differences

A fictional planning map places station A at (−4,3) and B at (2,3). Their horizontal separation is |2−(−4)|=6 grid units. A pair at (2,−2) and (2,3) is separated vertically by 5 units.

Practice: find horizontal distance from (−5,1) to (3,1) and vertical distance from (2,−4) to (2,5).

Wednesday — Reflect points across axes

Reflecting (−3,2) across the y-axis yields (3,2); reflecting it across the x-axis yields (−3,−2). One coordinate changes sign for each axis reflection, preserving its magnitude.

Practice: reflect (4,−5) across the x-axis and y-axis separately. Draw and label both.

Thursday — Data versus coordinate location

If fictional points (−2,4), (0,4) and (3,4) share a y-coordinate, they lie on one horizontal line. That does not establish that learners have identical needs, health outcomes or preferences. Separate measurements and context are needed to justify any such claims.

Practice: choose three map positions and explain what cannot be inferred about the people from their coordinates.

Friday — Independent explanation

A: identify quadrant of (−5,−2). B: distance from (−3,2) to (4,2). C: reflect (2,−4) over each axis. D: plot (−2,3), (2,3), (2,−1), label axes and explain one limitation of the model.

This is formative practice. Only the saved randomized exam independently verifies mastery.

Answer key

Reveal after practice

Monday: I, II, III, IV. Tuesday: 8 and 9 grid units. Wednesday: across x-axis (4,5); across y-axis (−4,−5). Friday: quadrant III, 7 units; across x-axis (2,4), across y-axis (−2,−4).
