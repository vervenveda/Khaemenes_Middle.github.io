Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 21

Ecosystems and Energy Flow: Dividing Fractions

How can fractional groups help quantify samples in an ecosystem model?

Learning goals

Divide whole numbers and fractions by unit fractions, divide fractions by fractions, interpret quotients and remainders, check answers, and distinguish mathematical models from ecological claims. All examples are hypothetical.

Monday — Fraction division as sharing

A fictional conservation team has 3 liters of model nutrient solution and fills sample containers holding 1/2 liter each. The number of containers is 3 ÷ (1/2) = 6. Dividing by a fraction asks how many fractional-size groups fit into the amount.

Practice: calculate 4 ÷ (1/2), 3 ÷ (1/3), and 5 ÷ (1/4). Check by multiplication.

Measure how many portions fit · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: dividend, divisor, quotient. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: Four liters in 1/4-liter containers yield 4÷1/4=16 full containers. Check 16×1/4=4 liters.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Find 3÷1/6 and verify.
  2. Find 5÷1/2.
  3. Explain why 4÷1/4 is greater than 4.
  4. Draw a fraction-strip model for 2÷1/4.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

18; 10; smaller quarter-unit groups fit more times; eight quarter-length groups fit into two whole units.

Tuesday — Fraction by fraction

A hypothetical ecosystem model uses 3/4 kilogram of material, allocated in 1/8 kilogram portions. Compute (3/4) ÷ (1/8) = (3/4) × (8/1) = 6 portions. Explain why multiplying by the reciprocal works with a visual fraction strip.

Practice: (2/3) ÷ (1/6); (3/5) ÷ (1/10); verify units.

Use reciprocals accurately · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: reciprocal, unit fraction, equivalent. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: (5/6)÷(1/3)=5/6×3=15/6=5/2. Multiplying 5/2 by 1/3 returns 5/6.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Compute (3/4)÷(1/6).
  2. Compute (4/5)÷(2/5).
  3. Explain the error in (2/3)÷(1/4)=(2/3)×(1/4).
  4. Check (7/8)÷(7/16) using multiplication.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

9/2; 2; divisor must be inverted, result 8/3; quotient 2 because 2×7/16=7/8.

Wednesday — Interpret remainders

A fictional habitat model has 5/2 liters available, and each container holds 3/4 liter. (5/2) ÷ (3/4) = 10/3 = 3⅓ container capacities. Only three full containers can be filled; 1/4 liter remains. Decide whether a question asks for the number of full containers or an equivalent fractional number of containers.

Practice: 7/2 ÷ 1/2 and 5/3 ÷ 2/3; interpret results in context.

Distinguish partial and full containers · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: remainder, full container, fraction. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: With 11/4 liters and 2/3-liter containers, quotient is (11/4)×(3/2)=33/8=4⅛ containers. Four full containers use 8/3 liters, leaving 1/12 liter.

Independent reasoning:

  1. For 2 1/2 L and 3/4-L containers, find full containers and leftover volume.
  2. For 3 1/2 kg packed in 1/2-kg bags, find full bags.
  3. For 7/4 L and 1/2-L containers, calculate full containers and leftover.
  4. Explain why 3⅓ containers is not the same as 3⅓ liters.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

3 full containers with 1/4 L left; 7 bags; 3 full containers with 1/4 L left; container count is dimensionless and depends on container capacity.

Thursday — Compare models responsibly

One fictional team divides 6 kilograms into 3/4-kilogram samples, making eight full samples. Another divides the same mass into 1/2-kilogram samples, making twelve. Smaller sample size means more samples for a fixed total amount. Do not confuse allocation arithmetic with a claim that energy moves unchanged through a food web.

Practice: compare 4 ÷ (1/2) and 4 ÷ (1/4); explain which produces more samples.

Compare allocation models · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: assumption, quotient, mathematical model. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: Six kilograms divided into 3/4-kg samples yields 8 samples. Six kilograms divided into 2/5-kg samples yields 15, assuming no waste.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Compute 8÷1/4 and 8÷1/2; compare counts.
  2. For 9/2 kg with 3/4-kg portions, find number of portions.
  3. Find 3/4÷3/8 and explain why the answer is an integer.
  4. Explain why sample allocation arithmetic does not determine energy-transfer efficiency.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

32 versus 16; 6 portions; 2 because two 3/8 portions equal 3/4; ecological transfer needs distinct evidence.

Synthesize fraction division and prior skills · Deep practice

Vocabulary studio: verify, portion, reciprocal. Define these terms and use at least one in your mathematical explanation.

Worked model: (2/3)÷(1/9)=6; 6×1/9=2/3. A product check catches reciprocal and division-direction errors.

Independent reasoning:

  1. Compute 5/6÷1/3.
  2. Find 3 3/4÷3/8.
  3. Recall Week 20: find 30% of 180.
  4. Recall Week 17: solve 4x+3=19.
  5. Explain how a leftover volume is calculated after filling all possible full containers.
Teacher solution key (open after attempting)

5/2; 10; 54; x=4; subtract number of full containers times their capacity from available volume.

Crosswalk studio — Food-web ratios, percentages and statistics

Monday bridge: An invented habitat has 40 producers and 20 consumers in a sample. The producer-to-consumer ratio is 40:20 = 2:1; these are not verified population counts.

Tuesday bridge: A simulated count of 40 producers later decreases to 30. The decrease is 10/40 = 25%. Describe the trend, not its cause.

Wednesday bridge: Graph fictional counts 40, 35, 20 for three seasons. Their mean is 95/3 ≈ 31.7 and median is 35. Explain why mean and median differ.

Thursday bridge: Of 60 imaginary organism observations, 15 are decomposers. Compute 15/60=25% and represent all groups in a clearly labeled bar chart. Do not interpret this as energy transfer efficiency.

Friday evidence: Create a food-web quantity table, calculate a simplified ratio, one percent change, center of a dataset and an evidence-limit sentence. Check: 2:1, 25%, mean about 31.7, median 35, 25%.

Answer key

Reveal after practicing

Monday: 8, 9, 20. Tuesday: 4 and 6. Wednesday: 7 and 5/2 = 2½. Thursday: 8 versus 16 samples. Friday: 18; 6; three full containers and 1/4 liter remaining; four one-sixth portions fit in two-thirds.
