Khaemenes Academy · Grade 6 Mathematics · Week 21

Ecosystems and Energy Flow: Dividing Fractions

How can fractional groups help quantify samples in an ecosystem model?

Learning goals

Divide whole numbers and fractions by unit fractions, divide fractions by fractions, interpret quotients and remainders, check answers, and distinguish mathematical models from ecological claims. All examples are hypothetical.

Monday — Fraction division as sharing

A fictional conservation team has 3 liters of model nutrient solution and fills sample containers holding 1/2 liter each. The number of containers is 3 ÷ (1/2) = 6. Dividing by a fraction asks how many fractional-size groups fit into the amount.

Practice: calculate 4 ÷ (1/2), 3 ÷ (1/3), and 5 ÷ (1/4). Check by multiplication.

Tuesday — Fraction by fraction

A hypothetical ecosystem model uses 3/4 kilogram of material, allocated in 1/8 kilogram portions. Compute (3/4) ÷ (1/8) = (3/4) × (8/1) = 6 portions. Explain why multiplying by the reciprocal works with a visual fraction strip.

Practice: (2/3) ÷ (1/6); (3/5) ÷ (1/10); verify units.

Wednesday — Interpret remainders

A fictional habitat model has 5/2 liters available, and each container holds 3/4 liter. (5/2) ÷ (3/4) = 10/3 = 3⅓ container capacities. Only three full containers can be filled; 1/4 liter remains. Decide whether a question asks for the number of full containers or an equivalent fractional number of containers.

Practice: 7/2 ÷ 1/2 and 5/3 ÷ 2/3; interpret results in context.

Thursday — Compare models responsibly

One fictional team divides 6 kilograms into 3/4-kilogram samples, making eight full samples. Another divides the same mass into 1/2-kilogram samples, making twelve. Smaller sample size means more samples for a fixed total amount. Do not confuse allocation arithmetic with a claim that energy moves unchanged through a food web.

Practice: compare 4 ÷ (1/2) and 4 ÷ (1/4); explain which produces more samples.

Friday — Formative defense

A (2 points): 6 ÷ (1/3). B (2 points): (3/4) ÷ (1/8). C (3 points): 5/2 liters divided into 3/4-liter full containers; state number and leftover. D (3 points): explain with a fraction-strip or equation why 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 4.

Ten-point formative practice, not a verified mastery record. The randomized examination separately enforces 80% overall and within the mathematics strand.

Crosswalk studio — Food-web ratios, percentages and statistics

Monday bridge: An invented habitat has 40 producers and 20 consumers in a sample. The producer-to-consumer ratio is 40:20 = 2:1; these are not verified population counts.

Tuesday bridge: A simulated count of 40 producers later decreases to 30. The decrease is 10/40 = 25%. Describe the trend, not its cause.

Wednesday bridge: Graph fictional counts 40, 35, 20 for three seasons. Their mean is 95/3 ≈ 31.7 and median is 35. Explain why mean and median differ.

Thursday bridge: Of 60 imaginary organism observations, 15 are decomposers. Compute 15/60=25% and represent all groups in a clearly labeled bar chart. Do not interpret this as energy transfer efficiency.

Friday evidence: Create a food-web quantity table, calculate a simplified ratio, one percent change, center of a dataset and an evidence-limit sentence. Check: 2:1, 25%, mean about 31.7, median 35, 25%.

Answer key

Reveal after practicing

Monday: 8, 9, 20. Tuesday: 4 and 6. Wednesday: 7 and 5/2 = 2½. Thursday: 8 versus 16 samples. Friday: 18; 6; three full containers and 1/4 liter remaining; four one-sixth portions fit in two-thirds.
