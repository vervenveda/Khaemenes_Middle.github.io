Week 16 · Arts, Music & Media

Forces, Motion, and Systems

How do forces and systems explain motion?

Monday: Sketch kinetic motion

Focus: Sketch kinetic motion.

Learn: A kinetic artwork can show movement with repeated shapes and arrows; the representation is an artistic model, not a measured force diagram.

Practice and evidence: Sketch three frames of an original moving paper sculpture.

Tuesday: Represent rhythm as distance

Focus: Represent rhythm as distance.

Learn: A fictional visual sequence marks positions 0,3,6,9 at seconds 0,1,2,3, corresponding to constant modeled speed.

Practice and evidence: Create a four-beat graphic score and label the numerical changes.

Wednesday: Explore balance

Focus: Explore balance.

Learn: Balance in sculpture is affected by mass distribution and supports, while visual symmetry alone cannot establish physical stability.

Practice and evidence: Compare two paper-only designs and explain an unanswered stability question.

Thursday: Accessibility in kinetic art

Focus: Accessibility in kinetic art.

Learn: Arrows, text descriptions and meaningful captions make motion artwork understandable without animation or color perception.

Practice and evidence: Revise an invented moving-poster design to include a readable static explanation.

Friday: Artist evidence portfolio

Focus: Artist evidence portfolio.

Learn: An artist statement can connect rhythm, motion, geometric forms, interpretation and model limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit a five-frame kinetic design and statement. Rubric: creativity 6, reasoning 5, accessibility 5, revision 4.
