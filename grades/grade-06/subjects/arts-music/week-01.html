Week 01 · Arts, Music & Media

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: Elements of visual composition

Focus: Elements of visual composition.

Learn: Artists arrange line, shape, color, texture, value, and space to guide attention. Contrast makes differences visible; balance distributes visual weight. Symmetry is one type of balance, but asymmetry can also create an intentional composition.

Practice and evidence: Draw two postcard-size compositions of the same subject: one symmetrical, one asymmetrical. Annotate the focal point and explain which elements direct the viewer's eye.

Tuesday: Rhythm in music and art

Focus: Rhythm in music and art.

Learn: Rhythm organizes events through repetition and variation. In music a measure groups beats; in visual art repeated shapes or lines create pattern. A four-beat measure may be counted one-two-three-four, with some beats accented.

Practice and evidence: Clap or tap two original four-beat patterns using long and short sounds, then translate one pattern into a sequence of marks on paper. Describe which repetitions and changes create rhythm.

Wednesday: Original color and mood study

Focus: Original color and mood study.

Learn: A color's hue distinguishes families such as red and blue; value describes lightness or darkness; saturation describes intensity. Colors can influence mood but cultural and personal associations differ. Contrast also affects whether a design is readable.

Practice and evidence: Create a two-column color-and-value chart using pencils or safe digital tools. Design two versions of one message with different color choices, then evaluate contrast and readability.

Thursday: Critique with evidence

Focus: Critique with evidence.

Learn: A useful critique describes what can be observed before offering interpretation. 'Three circles repeat across the upper edge' is observation; 'the pattern feels energetic' is interpretation. Constructive feedback names strengths and a specific possible improvement.

Practice and evidence: Examine your Monday composition. Write three factual observations, two supported interpretations, and one actionable revision. Make the revision and compare before and after.

Friday: Mini exhibition and reflection

Focus: Mini exhibition and reflection.

Learn: Curating a small exhibition involves selecting, labeling, and explaining original work. A strong artist statement names choices in line, balance, color, or rhythm and explains how these choices communicate intent.

Practice and evidence: Display two original artworks and one rhythm notation sample with titles. Write a 100-word artist statement. Rubric (20): technique 6, thoughtful composition 5, evidence-based reflection 5, presentation 4. Keep work in the portfolio.
