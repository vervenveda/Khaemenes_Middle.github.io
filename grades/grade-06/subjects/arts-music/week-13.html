Week 13 · Arts, Music & Media

Volume and Three-Dimensional Modeling

How can 3D models solve real problems?

Monday: Create a solid sculpture

Focus: Create a solid sculpture.

Learn: A rectangular prism model with dimensions 2×2×3 cm occupies 12 cm³. Volume describes enclosed space, not amount of paint.

Practice and evidence: Sketch an original fictional sculpture based on two labeled prisms.

Tuesday: Explore proportion

Focus: Explore proportion.

Learn: If a 2×2×3 cm model is enlarged to 4×4×6 cm, its volume grows from 12 to 96 cm³.

Practice and evidence: Calculate both models and explain why doubled edges yield eight times the volume.

Wednesday: Design a paper net

Focus: Design a paper net.

Learn: A rectangular prism can be constructed from six rectangular faces arranged in a suitable net. Surface area describes its faces, not interior volume.

Practice and evidence: Draw a foldable net for a 2×2×3 cm prism and label each face.

Thursday: Critique craftsmanship

Focus: Critique craftsmanship.

Learn: Design can balance geometry, stability and readable labels. A mathematically accurate drawing does not guarantee a physically stable sculpture.

Practice and evidence: Draft a critique that separates calculation accuracy from artistic and construction choices.

Friday: Exhibit a 3D model

Focus: Exhibit a 3D model.

Learn: An artist statement should explain intentional form, scale, materials, and a measured limitation.

Practice and evidence: Submit model sketches, volume work and a 100-word artist statement. Rubric: form 6, volume 5, explanation 5, revision 4.
