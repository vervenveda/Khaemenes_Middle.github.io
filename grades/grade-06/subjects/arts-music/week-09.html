Week 09 · Arts, Music & Media

Equations and Balanced Arguments

How do balance and evidence make arguments stronger?

Monday: Visual balance

Focus: Visual balance.

Learn: Balance describes distribution of visual emphasis. Symmetrical designs mirror features around an axis; asymmetrical designs can still feel balanced.

Practice and evidence: Create an original pair of compositions, one symmetrical and one asymmetrical. Explain what makes each balanced.

Tuesday: Equality and musical form

Focus: Equality and musical form.

Learn: An equation can model total beats: x+2=6 implies x=4. Musical form can be balanced by equivalent duration without requiring identical patterns.

Practice and evidence: Design two distinct six-beat patterns, verify total durations, and explain the difference between equality and sameness.

Wednesday: Support artistic choices

Focus: Support artistic choices.

Learn: A claim like 'this design directs attention to the center' needs observable evidence such as alignment, size or repetition.

Practice and evidence: Annotate three concrete features of your own composition and explain their likely effects as interpretations.

Thursday: Critique fairly

Focus: Critique fairly.

Learn: A balanced critique names strengths, specific difficulties and a constructive revision rather than relying on likes and dislikes alone.

Practice and evidence: Write a two-sided critique of a fictional poster that has strong composition but unreadable small labels.

Friday: Artistic defense

Focus: Artistic defense.

Learn: An evidence-based artistic statement identifies intent, visual or musical choices, and revisions.

Practice and evidence: Submit two designs or rhythm measures, an accurate equation, and a 100-word artist statement. Rubric 20: composition 5, math 5, evidence 6, revision 4.
