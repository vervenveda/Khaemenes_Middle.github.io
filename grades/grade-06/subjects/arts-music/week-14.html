Week 14 · Arts, Music & Media

Matter: Particles, Properties, and Models

How can invisible particle models explain visible properties?

Monday: Explore material textures

Focus: Explore material textures.

Learn: Art materials differ in texture, flexibility and weight. A material's visual appearance does not establish its composition.

Practice and evidence: Sketch three original objects and annotate observed versus assumed material properties.

Tuesday: Represent particle models

Focus: Represent particle models.

Learn: A simplified solid-particle diagram can help communicate a concept; it is not a literal photograph of atomic positions.

Practice and evidence: Create a collage contrasting two schematic arrangements, with an explicit model disclaimer.

Wednesday: Measure a sculpture model

Focus: Measure a sculpture model.

Learn: A fictional block sculpture with mass 12 g and volume 4 cm³ has modeled density 3 g/cm³. These invented figures are not measurements of actual art supplies.

Practice and evidence: Create a calculation card and label its values simulated.

Thursday: Critique material choices

Focus: Critique material choices.

Learn: An artist can defend wood, paper or clay according to craft goals, availability and intended presentation, while noting untested assumptions.

Practice and evidence: Compare two hypothetical materials with a two-sided critique focused on technique and usability.

Friday: Material-art portfolio

Focus: Material-art portfolio.

Learn: A complete artwork record connects original design, material rationale, correct math and honest model captions.

Practice and evidence: Submit a material study poster and a 100-word artist statement. Rubric: design 6, science 5, clarity 5, reflection 4.
