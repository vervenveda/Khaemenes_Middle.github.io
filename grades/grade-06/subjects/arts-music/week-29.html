Week 29 · Arts, Music & Media

Research Writing: From Question to Thesis

How do researchers build trustworthy explanations?

Monday: Define an artistic research question

Focus: Define an artistic research question.

Learn: Questions about a work's creator, context or audience require sourcing beyond appearance.

Practice and evidence: Choose a documented artwork and write a bounded interpretive question.

Tuesday: Record creator and provenance

Focus: Record creator and provenance.

Learn: Images and performances require attribution and context; resemblance alone is not proof of influence.

Practice and evidence: Create a source card with maker if known, date uncertainty, medium and archive reference.

Wednesday: Compare artistic evidence

Focus: Compare artistic evidence.

Learn: Formal observations are different from claims about historical intent.

Practice and evidence: Compare two sourced artifacts using a fact-versus-interpretation table.

Thursday: Build informative captions

Focus: Build informative captions.

Learn: Good captions use legible typography and distinguish student reconstruction from original work.

Practice and evidence: Draft and peer-review two artifact captions for accessibility and accuracy.

Friday: Curate a mini research gallery

Focus: Curate a mini research gallery.

Learn: An exhibition should communicate evidence without appropriating or inventing cultural authority.

Practice and evidence: Submit two sourced cards and one clearly labeled original response. Rubric: sourcing 7, reasoning 5, presentation 4, reflection 4.
