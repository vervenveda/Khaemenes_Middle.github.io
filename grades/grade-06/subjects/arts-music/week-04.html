Week 04 · Arts, Music & Media

Percent, Proportion, and Civic Decisions

How can percentages help a community make informed decisions?

Monday: Proportion in composition

Focus: Proportion in composition.

Learn: Proportion compares the sizes of elements in an artwork. A poster using one-quarter of its space for a title gives the title 25% of the total area.

Practice and evidence: Draw a four-part grid and design two title placements. Explain the proportions used.

Tuesday: Read audience data

Focus: Read audience data.

Learn: A fictional arts survey has 40 responses: 20 prefer painting, 12 music, and 8 drama. These represent 50%, 30%, and 20% of respondents, not everyone.

Practice and evidence: Calculate the percentages and produce a clear labeled bar chart.

Wednesday: Design an original graphic

Focus: Design an original graphic.

Learn: A chart's visual proportions should reflect the underlying percentages. Using unequal icons without explanation may mislead an audience.

Practice and evidence: Create an original poster with a 50/30/20 proportional design and a clearly visible source label saying simulated data.

Thursday: Critique visual integrity

Focus: Critique visual integrity.

Learn: Charts are creative work and information displays. Contrast, precise labels, and accessible patterns help more people interpret results.

Practice and evidence: Review your poster at a narrow display width and on paper, then revise one visual feature for legibility.

Friday: Artist and evidence statement

Focus: Artist and evidence statement.

Learn: Responsible design explains the relationship between choices, percentages, and audience needs.

Practice and evidence: Submit the revised poster, calculations, and a 100-word artist statement. Rubric: mathematical accuracy 6, composition 5, accessibility 5, reflection 4.
