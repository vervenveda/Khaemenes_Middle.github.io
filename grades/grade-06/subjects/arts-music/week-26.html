Week 26 · Arts, Music & Media

Classical Civilizations and Civic Ideas

How did classical societies influence civic ideas and public life?

Monday: Study public symbols

Focus: Study public symbols.

Learn: Coins, monuments, sculpture and music can convey authority or belonging; what a symbol meant to its audience requires contextual evidence.

Practice and evidence: Sketch and label two documented types of civic symbolism, marking any speculative meanings.

Tuesday: Analyze an object's audience

Focus: Analyze an object's audience.

Learn: Patrons and makers influence public artwork. Archaeological fragments may not preserve their original setting.

Practice and evidence: Compare a teacher-provided image of an artifact with a modern community emblem; identify intended audience and missing context.

Wednesday: Design inclusive visual language

Focus: Design inclusive visual language.

Learn: Contrast, clear labels and readable symbols support access. Avoid copying sacred or culturally sensitive motifs without context.

Practice and evidence: Draft an original symbol for a fictional shared meeting space, explaining shapes and accessibility choices.

Thursday: Review ethical interpretation

Focus: Review ethical interpretation.

Learn: A reconstruction must be labeled as an interpretation, not an original ancient object or performance.

Practice and evidence: Peer-review designs for historical misattribution, cultural respect and legibility; revise using feedback.

Friday: Create a small civic exhibition

Focus: Create a small civic exhibition.

Learn: A responsible exhibition identifies what is documented and what students invented.

Practice and evidence: Produce a labeled two-piece exhibition: one sourced interpretation and one original civic design. Rubric: evidence 6, craft 5, inclusion 5, reflection 4.
