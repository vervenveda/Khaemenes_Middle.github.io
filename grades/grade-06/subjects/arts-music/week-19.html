Week 19 · Arts, Music & Media

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation

How does light shape what we see and how we interpret media?

Monday: Explore light and color

Focus: Explore light and color.

Learn: In visual composition, contrast can direct attention; a chosen color does not scientifically measure light intensity.

Practice and evidence: Draw an original high-contrast composition and label visual choices.

Tuesday: Use reflected-ray geometry

Focus: Use reflected-ray geometry.

Learn: For an ideal plane mirror, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a 30-degree incident and reflected ray as a geometric motif.

Wednesday: Represent point of view

Focus: Represent point of view.

Learn: A change of framing can highlight or conceal context in visual storytelling.

Practice and evidence: Create two cropped sketches of one fictional scene and explain differences.

Thursday: Design accessible visual art

Focus: Design accessible visual art.

Learn: Color alone should not encode essential meaning; captions and distinct shapes enable other interpretations.

Practice and evidence: Add a written description and non-color labels to your image.

Friday: Curate a light-art portfolio

Focus: Curate a light-art portfolio.

Learn: An artist statement separates personal symbolism from scientific claims about optics.

Practice and evidence: Submit original composition, ray motif, and statement. Rubric: originality 6, evidence 5, access 5, reflection 4.
