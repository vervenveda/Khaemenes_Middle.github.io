Week 23 · Arts, Music & Media

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns

How can patterns help communities prepare for change?

Monday: Visualize shifting weather

Focus: Visualize shifting weather.

Learn: Lines, texture and repetition can depict changing conditions, but a blue paint color is not a calibrated temperature measurement.

Practice and evidence: Create an original four-panel fictional weather storyboard with captions.

Tuesday: Turn data into rhythm

Focus: Turn data into rhythm.

Learn: A simulated rainfall series 0,2,5,1 mm over four days can inspire rhythm without suggesting that notes are actual precipitation.

Practice and evidence: Make a graphic score with one beat group per invented rainfall measurement.

Wednesday: Represent long-term patterns

Focus: Represent long-term patterns.

Learn: Weather is short-term atmospheric conditions; climate describes long-term patterns. An art installation may illustrate this distinction.

Practice and evidence: Compare a daily-weather poster to an imagined long-period climate mural and annotate limits.

Thursday: Design accessible artwork

Focus: Design accessible artwork.

Learn: Text descriptions, textured patterns and labeled numerical values communicate meaning without relying on color or audio alone.

Practice and evidence: Add descriptions and readable figures to the fictional climate mural.

Friday: Prepare a climate-art portfolio

Focus: Prepare a climate-art portfolio.

Learn: An artist statement distinguishes simulated figures, factual scientific terms and imaginative choices.

Practice and evidence: Submit storyboard, graphical rhythm, and reflection. Rubric: originality 6, science 5, access 5, editing 4.
