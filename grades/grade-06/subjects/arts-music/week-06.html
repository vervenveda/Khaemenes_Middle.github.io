Week 06 · Arts, Music & Media

Rational Numbers and Ancient Number Systems

How have cultures represented numbers and meaning across time?

Monday: Invent symbols with rules

Focus: Invent symbols with rules.

Learn: Notation translates an idea into symbols. Musical notes indicate durations and pitches; a legend explains an original graphic notation system.

Practice and evidence: Design three original symbols for clap, rest and sustained sound, and publish a legend so another learner can interpret them.

Tuesday: Positive and negative space

Focus: Positive and negative space.

Learn: Positive space is the subject or occupied region; negative space surrounds it. It is a design concept, not a numerical negative value.

Practice and evidence: Create two black-and-white square compositions using the same three shapes, shifting which areas appear dominant.

Wednesday: Represent signed movement visually

Focus: Represent signed movement visually.

Learn: A line above or below a baseline can depict upward and downward change. A signed number represents direction relative to zero; negative does not mean bad.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a five-event timeline with values −2, +1, 0, +3, −1 and a clear legend.

Thursday: Respect historical source traditions

Focus: Respect historical source traditions.

Learn: Historical symbol systems arose in specific contexts. A modern invented artistic alphabet must not be passed off as authentic ancient writing.

Practice and evidence: Produce a fictional merchant-sign design clearly marked contemporary and explain two choices while separating invention from historical evidence.

Friday: Notation exhibition

Focus: Notation exhibition.

Learn: Readable visual systems define signs, apply them consistently and explain the composition's meaning.

Practice and evidence: Submit two original symbol compositions, a legend, and an artist statement distinguishing sign, numerical value and visual space. Rubric: coherence 6, clarity 5, craftsmanship 5, reflection 4.
