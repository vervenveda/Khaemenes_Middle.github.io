Week 08 · Arts, Music & Media

Expressions and Algebraic Thinking

How can symbols represent ideas in math, language, and culture?

Monday: Notation and repeated motifs

Focus: Notation and repeated motifs.

Learn: A motif is a repeated visual or musical idea. We can represent a repeated shape as 3s, meaning three identical units of a symbol s—not necessarily three physical objects of the same size.

Practice and evidence: Create a visual legend with one original motif and draw one, two, then three repetitions.

Tuesday: Represent rhythm algebraically

Focus: Represent rhythm algebraically.

Learn: A rhythm can be described with variables for beat duration. If a four-beat pattern repeats n times, total beats are 4n.

Practice and evidence: Write 4n for n=1, 2, 3; notate an original four-beat pattern and show the repeated version.

Wednesday: Shape and symbolic meaning

Focus: Shape and symbolic meaning.

Learn: A symbol's interpretation depends on a legend and context. A circle in an invented score may mean a clap, while another composer could assign it a rest.

Practice and evidence: Design a three-symbol graphic score with a clear legend; ask whether someone could interpret it without verbal help.

Thursday: Equivalent artistic structures

Focus: Equivalent artistic structures.

Learn: Two rhythms can have equal total beats while using different subdivisions. A four-beat total can be represented as 2+2 or 1+1+1+1.

Practice and evidence: Create two distinct original measures with the same beat total and explain how the representations are equivalent.

Friday: Notation studio portfolio

Focus: Notation studio portfolio.

Learn: An effective studio record combines original artwork, correct algebraic models, legibility, and intentional critique.

Practice and evidence: Submit a graphic score, a 4n repetition table, and a 100-word artist statement. Rubric: pattern accuracy 6, symbols 5, explanation 5, revision 4.
