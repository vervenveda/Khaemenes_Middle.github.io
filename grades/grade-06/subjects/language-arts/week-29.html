Week 29 · Language Arts & Literacy

Research Writing: From Question to Thesis

How do researchers build trustworthy explanations?

Monday: Form a researchable question

Focus: Form a researchable question.

Learn: A useful question is specific, open to evidence, and answerable within available sources.

Practice and evidence: Revise a broad question about public communication into one that specifies place, period and audience.

Tuesday: Distinguish source types

Focus: Distinguish source types.

Learn: Primary sources, scholarly interpretations and summaries serve different research purposes.

Practice and evidence: Sort three fictional source descriptions and record what each could establish.

Wednesday: Take ethical notes

Focus: Take ethical notes.

Learn: Paraphrase by expressing understood ideas in new language; retain citation details and separate quotation from inference.

Practice and evidence: Create four source-note cards with author, title, date and page or URL.

Thursday: Build a defensible thesis

Focus: Build a defensible thesis.

Learn: A thesis answers a research question and can be challenged using evidence.

Practice and evidence: Draft a claim and counterclaim supported by two provided fictional excerpts.

Friday: Write and revise an argument

Focus: Write and revise an argument.

Learn: Research writing needs clear sourcing, organized evidence and acknowledgement of uncertainty.

Practice and evidence: Submit a 220-word mini-essay with thesis, two cited details, a counterargument and revision. Rubric: question 4, evidence 7, reasoning 5, citation 4.
