Week 30 · Language Arts & Literacy

Argument, Debate, and Public Speaking

How can disagreement become disciplined learning?

Monday: Distinguish claims and reasons

Focus: Distinguish claims and reasons.

Learn: A debatable claim can be supported by reasons and relevant evidence; an opinion alone does not prove itself.

Practice and evidence: Annotate a fictional school-library proposal, marking its claim and two supporting reasons.

Tuesday: Evaluate evidence quality

Focus: Evaluate evidence quality.

Learn: Evidence needs provenance, context and a direct relationship to the claim.

Practice and evidence: Compare an invented survey summary and an unsupported statement; explain the stronger support.

Wednesday: Represent a counterclaim fairly

Focus: Represent a counterclaim fairly.

Learn: A counterclaim should accurately express a different perspective rather than ridicule it.

Practice and evidence: Write a fair counterclaim to a fictional longer-library-hours proposal.

Thursday: Practice civil rebuttal

Focus: Practice civil rebuttal.

Learn: A rebuttal responds to reasons and evidence without attacking a speaker.

Practice and evidence: Rehearse a 60-second response and revise one emotionally charged sentence.

Friday: Deliver a balanced speech

Focus: Deliver a balanced speech.

Learn: A persuasive speech uses a clear claim, credible evidence, fair counterclaim and reasoned conclusion.

Practice and evidence: Deliver a two-minute speech with source notes and a self-review. Rubric: reasoning 6, evidence 6, listening 4, delivery 4.
