Week 06 · Language Arts & Literacy

Rational Numbers and Ancient Number Systems

How have cultures represented numbers and meaning across time?

Monday: Numbers in historical writing

Focus: Numbers in historical writing.

Learn: Writing about past number systems requires distinguishing evidence from a modern explanation. Roman numerals use letter symbols such as X for ten and V for five; place-value decimal notation works differently.

Practice and evidence: Write two accurate sentences comparing how X and 10 express the same quantity without claiming all historical cultures used one system.

Tuesday: Compare structures

Focus: Compare structures.

Learn: A compare-and-contrast paragraph identifies a shared feature and a meaningful difference. The Roman numeral XII and decimal 12 represent equal values but use different symbol and place-value conventions.

Practice and evidence: Create a two-column chart for XII and 12, including symbols, order, and the meaning of each representation.

Wednesday: Use careful historical vocabulary

Focus: Use careful historical vocabulary.

Learn: Terms like numeral, quantity, place value, and additive notation have distinct meanings. Avoid suggesting ancient people had no mathematical sophistication merely because their methods differed.

Practice and evidence: Define four terms with original examples, and revise one inaccurate stereotype about historical mathematical traditions.

Thursday: Explain signed numbers

Focus: Explain signed numbers.

Learn: In a modern number line, −3 and 3 are opposites, and |−3|=3 means three units from zero. A modern representation should not be presented as identical to all ancient notation.

Practice and evidence: Draft an explanatory paragraph using a diagram and clearly identify which mathematical conventions are modern.

Friday: Original comparative essay

Focus: Original comparative essay.

Learn: Evidence-based comparisons require accurate examples, precise language and recognition of limits.

Practice and evidence: Produce a 150-word comparison of Roman and decimal notation, add a number-line illustration, and revise for clarity. Rubric: factual precision 6, comparison 5, explanation 5, revision 4.
