Week 04 · Language Arts & Literacy

Percent, Proportion, and Civic Decisions

How can percentages help a community make informed decisions?

Monday: Claims and survey facts

Focus: Claims and survey facts.

Learn: A claim is a statement to be supported. In a fictional student survey, 30 of 50 respondents favored more library hours, or 60%. That describes respondents only, not all students.

Practice and evidence: Mark the claim, evidence and limitation in: 'Most of the 50 respondents favored longer library hours; 30 selected yes.' Explain why the claim cannot be generalized to everyone.

Tuesday: Percent in informational text

Focus: Percent in informational text.

Learn: Percent means per hundred; a survey of 40 with 10 choosing an option gives 25%. Distinguish a percentage of respondents from the percentage of all eligible people.

Practice and evidence: Write a concise caption for a fictional survey chart, including 10/40, 25%, its population and a clear source label.

Wednesday: Words that overstate evidence

Focus: Words that overstate evidence.

Learn: Words like everyone, always and proven can distort limited evidence. 'Of the 20 surveyed' is more accurate than 'all families' when sampling is small.

Practice and evidence: Revise three exaggerated survey claims into cautious, accurate language and explain what information is missing.

Thursday: Evaluate point of view

Focus: Evaluate point of view.

Learn: An editorial can recommend a policy while a report records survey methods. Compare who wrote the document, its purpose, evidence and omissions before accepting a conclusion.

Practice and evidence: Create a two-column fictional comparison: a student recommending a reading club and a school survey report; distinguish opinion and recorded results.

Friday: Write a civic explanation

Focus: Write a civic explanation.

Learn: A persuasive recommendation should combine an actionable claim, numerical evidence, reasoning, counterconsiderations and uncertainty.

Practice and evidence: Write a 150-word recommendation about fictional library hours, cite the 30/50 survey and one limitation, then revise for precise wording. Rubric 20: claim 4, math accuracy 5, explanation 6, limitations 5.
