Week 13 · Language Arts & Literacy

Volume and Three-Dimensional Modeling

How can 3D models solve real problems?

Monday: Explain volume precisely

Focus: Explain volume precisely.

Learn: Volume describes three-dimensional space and uses cubic units. A 3 cm by 2 cm by 4 cm rectangular prism has volume 24 cm³; calling that 24 cm² would confuse volume with area.

Practice and evidence: Write a five-term glossary including edge, face, prism, volume and cubic unit, with an original labeled sketch.

Tuesday: Caption a solid model

Focus: Caption a solid model.

Learn: A useful model caption names the shape, dimensions, units and whether the object is a hypothetical model. Length 3 cm, width 2 cm and height 4 cm give 3×2×4=24 cm³.

Practice and evidence: Write a precise two-sentence caption for a fictional box, showing the multiplication.

Wednesday: Sequence measurement steps

Focus: Sequence measurement steps.

Learn: A technical explanation defines each dimension, multiplies length by width by height, then checks units and reasonableness.

Practice and evidence: Write numbered instructions for a rectangular prism 5×2×3 cm, concluding 30 cm³.

Thursday: Distinguish capacity from volume

Focus: Distinguish capacity from volume.

Learn: The volume of an exterior solid and its usable internal capacity may differ because walls occupy space. A model with no specified wall thickness cannot prove actual capacity.

Practice and evidence: Revise two misleading fictional storage captions to distinguish external dimensions and interior capacity.

Friday: Technical writing portfolio

Focus: Technical writing portfolio.

Learn: Strong technical writing combines spatial vocabulary, consistent units, a clear method and an accurate model limitation.

Practice and evidence: Submit a labeled fictional container drawing and 150-word explanation with one revision. Rubric: accuracy 6, sequencing 5, captions 5, reflection 4.
