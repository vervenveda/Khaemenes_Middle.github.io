Week 23 · Language Arts & Literacy

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns

How can patterns help communities prepare for change?

Monday: Distinguish weather and climate

Focus: Distinguish weather and climate.

Learn: Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions at a place and time; climate describes patterns over long periods, often assessed with decades of observations.

Practice and evidence: Write two accurate captions distinguishing a fictional daily weather chart from a long-term climate summary.

Tuesday: Describe data without overclaiming

Focus: Describe data without overclaiming.

Learn: A fictional four-day temperature record of 18,20,20,22 °C has a mean of 20 °C. It does not establish a location's climate.

Practice and evidence: Calculate the mean and write a qualifying sentence about the four-day sample.

Wednesday: Compare two regions

Focus: Compare two regions.

Learn: A fictional Region A monthly rainfall total of 40 mm and Region B total of 65 mm shows a 25 mm difference for the recorded month, not a permanent ranking.

Practice and evidence: Draft a graph-based comparison identifying timeframe, units and a missing information need.

Thursday: Evaluate causal language

Focus: Evaluate causal language.

Learn: A changing temperature series cannot by itself prove the cause of a weather event or a climate trend. Careful language distinguishes recorded association from causal explanation.

Practice and evidence: Revise a hypothetical headline that claims one hot day proves a long-term trend.

Friday: Publish a data commentary

Focus: Publish a data commentary.

Learn: Clear environmental writing combines labeled evidence, timeframe, correct calculations and an explicit uncertainty.

Practice and evidence: Submit a 160-word fictional weather-versus-climate critique, graph caption and revision. Rubric: concepts 6, evidence 6, clarity 4, reflection 4.
