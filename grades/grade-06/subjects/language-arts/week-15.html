Week 15 · Language Arts & Literacy

Thermal Energy and Phase Change

How does energy explain changes in matter?

Monday: Describe scientific sequence

Focus: Describe scientific sequence.

Learn: A scientific explanation uses temporal signals such as initially, next and finally. Melting is a change from solid to liquid, but the writing must separate observations from interpretations.

Practice and evidence: Write a four-step paragraph describing an invented ice-warming observation sequence without presenting simulated values as real measurements.

Tuesday: Interpret a temperature graph

Focus: Interpret a temperature graph.

Learn: A fictional warming table lists 0, 3, 6 and 6 °C at minutes 0, 1, 2 and 3. A level segment means no recorded temperature rise, not that no energy is transferred.

Practice and evidence: Create a sentence explaining the plotted plateau and identify what further measurements would be needed.

Wednesday: Write claim-evidence-reasoning

Focus: Write claim-evidence-reasoning.

Learn: A claim should be linked to measured evidence and a scientific principle. A single horizontal segment does not by itself prove exactly which phase change occurred.

Practice and evidence: Write a CER paragraph using a clearly simulated flat temperature segment, acknowledging the limits of the data.

Thursday: Avoid false cause and effect

Focus: Avoid false cause and effect.

Learn: Temperature is not identical to total thermal energy. During a phase change, energy transfer may continue while temperature stays nearly constant under suitable conditions.

Practice and evidence: Revise a fictional explanation that incorrectly says a heating plateau means energy transfer stopped.

Friday: Publish a scientific explanation

Focus: Publish a scientific explanation.

Learn: Effective writing combines a time-ordered account, correctly labeled temperature graph, a claim, relevant evidence and qualified reasoning.

Practice and evidence: Submit a 160-word fictional heating-curve explanation with a revised overclaim. Rubric: science 6, evidence 6, sequence 4, editing 4.
