Week 11 · Language Arts & Literacy

Statistics: Questions, Data, and Distribution

How can data answer questions while still requiring judgment?

Monday: Ask statistical questions

Focus: Ask statistical questions.

Learn: Statistical questions expect variation across observations. 'How many pages do students read each week?' invites varied responses, while 'How many pages are in this one book?' asks for a fixed fact.

Practice and evidence: Write four questions and classify whether each anticipates varied data, explaining the difference.

Tuesday: Read a distribution

Focus: Read a distribution.

Learn: A fictional sample of books read is 1, 2, 2, 3, 7. The repeated value 2 is the mode; the larger observation 7 affects how the sample is described.

Practice and evidence: Write three precise observations about the values without assuming they represent every student.

Wednesday: Qualify an evidence claim

Focus: Qualify an evidence claim.

Learn: A sample cannot automatically describe an entire population. If five volunteers answer, 'all students prefer reading' would overstate what was measured.

Practice and evidence: Revise four exaggerated fictional survey claims to specify the source, sample size, and uncertainty.

Thursday: Summarize accurately

Focus: Summarize accurately.

Learn: A strong data summary explains the question, units, distribution and limits. For values 1,2,2,3,7, the median is 2 and the range is 6.

Practice and evidence: Draft a 100-word evidence-based summary and verify the median and range against the original numbers.

Friday: Publish an honest statistics report

Focus: Publish an honest statistics report.

Learn: Reporting must connect an accurate question, a clearly labeled data display, specific supporting examples and appropriate qualifications.

Practice and evidence: Submit an original fictional five-response survey report and revision note. Rubric 20: question 5, numeric accuracy 6, evidence 5, qualifications 4.
