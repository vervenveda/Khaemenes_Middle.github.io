Week 20 · Language Arts & Literacy

Cells, Systems, and Scientific Models

How do small structures support living systems?

Monday: Explain cells as systems

Focus: Explain cells as systems.

Learn: A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms. Technical explanations should distinguish a biological structure from an analogy used to teach its role.

Practice and evidence: Write a glossary defining cell, membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus and model.

Tuesday: Evaluate analogies

Focus: Evaluate analogies.

Learn: A cell may be compared with a city for instructional purposes, but a nucleus is not literally a mayor and a membrane is not a city wall. Analogies require clearly named limitations.

Practice and evidence: Draft a comparison table pairing three cell parts with imagined city features, then explain two ways the analogy fails.

Wednesday: Read a scientific diagram

Focus: Read a scientific diagram.

Learn: A useful diagram caption labels the cell type and parts shown and states when the representation is simplified or not to scale.

Practice and evidence: Write an accurate caption for a fictional labeled plant-cell drawing, noting that not every cell has chloroplasts.

Thursday: Connect structure and function

Focus: Connect structure and function.

Learn: Cell membranes help regulate movement of substances, and chloroplasts perform photosynthesis in plants and some other organisms. Function statements should be supported and carefully scoped.

Practice and evidence: Compose a paragraph explaining the function of two different cell structures with appropriate qualifiers.

Friday: Publish a model-based explanation

Focus: Publish a model-based explanation.

Learn: A strong scientific essay states its topic, defines vocabulary, explains structure-function relationships and identifies limits of diagrams and analogies.

Practice and evidence: Submit a 160-word explanation with a labeled fictional diagram and revision. Rubric: biological accuracy 6, examples 5, clarity 5, reflection 4.
