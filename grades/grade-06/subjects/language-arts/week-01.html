Week 01 · Language Arts & Literacy

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: Read and cite details

Focus: Read and cite details.

Read and learn: Read this original passage: 'Mina arrived early for class. She checked her assignment list and noticed the group map lacked a key. Before anyone asked, she labeled its symbols. Later she wrote down one question for tomorrow.' Textual evidence is a detail or quotation from a source that supports a claim.

Practice and evidence: Claim that Mina is responsible, then quote or paraphrase two precise details and explain how each supports your interpretation.

Tuesday: Observations and inferences

Focus: Observations and inferences.

Read and learn: An observation comes directly from the text; an inference interprets a detail. 'Mina arrived early' is observed. 'Mina plans ahead' is an inference supported but not proven by that action.

Practice and evidence: Make a two-column table with three observations and two inferences. Support each inference with a specific detail, and propose an alternative explanation.

Wednesday: Main idea and summary

Focus: Main idea and summary.

Read and learn: Read this original nonfiction passage: 'A learning plan organizes tasks by urgency and due date. Breaking assignments into smaller steps makes large projects more manageable. Reviewing the plan daily helps students catch missed tasks. A plan should remain flexible when circumstances change.' The main idea covers the whole text, while details support it.

Practice and evidence: Write one sentence stating the main idea, three sentences summarizing the passage, and two original paraphrases. Label the source as Khaemenes Academy original reading.

Thursday: Reasoned paragraph and revision

Focus: Reasoned paragraph and revision.

Read and learn: A strong paragraph makes a claim, offers relevant evidence, and connects that evidence through reasoning. Example: 'A written plan can promote independence. The passage says daily reviews reveal missed tasks. Students can therefore correct problems before a deadline.'

Practice and evidence: Write a 120–180 word response: How do students organize independence and responsibility? Use both original readings, two details, and an explanation; revise one unclear sentence.

Friday: Reading and writing evidence

Focus: Reading and writing evidence.

Read and learn: Review Mina's behavior and the informational reading. An action in a narrative and advice in informational prose can express related ideas through different forms. Explain those differences with textual evidence.

Practice and evidence: Independently compare the two passages using two details. Teacher rubric (20 points): claim 4, evidence 6, reasoning 4, organization 3, revision and conventions 3. At least 16 points meets the teacher-reviewed evidence goal, not the verified digital mastery gate.
