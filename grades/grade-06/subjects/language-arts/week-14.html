Week 14 · Language Arts & Literacy

Matter: Particles, Properties, and Models

How can invisible particle models explain visible properties?

Monday: Define matter accurately

Focus: Define matter accurately.

Learn: Matter has mass and occupies space. A model of matter represents selected properties, but a drawing of particles is not direct observation of individual atoms.

Practice and evidence: Create a glossary of matter, mass, volume, particle, and property. Distinguish a definition from an inference.

Tuesday: Describe a particle model

Focus: Describe a particle model.

Learn: A simplified diagram can depict closely packed particles in a solid and more mobile particles in a liquid, but distances and motion are not necessarily to scale.

Practice and evidence: Write a caption for two fictional particle diagrams, clearly identifying what each illustration omits.

Wednesday: Distinguish observation and cause

Focus: Distinguish observation and cause.

Learn: The statement 'a metal spoon feels cool' is an observation; explaining heat transfer requires a model and additional evidence.

Practice and evidence: Annotate an original paragraph with its observations, explanations and untested assumptions.

Thursday: Use evidence in explanations

Focus: Use evidence in explanations.

Learn: A fictional sample has mass 12 g and volume 4 cm³. Its modeled density is 3 g/cm³, but those numbers alone cannot identify the material.

Practice and evidence: Write an evidence paragraph with the measurement, arithmetic, units and a cautious conclusion.

Friday: Revise a scientific explanation

Focus: Revise a scientific explanation.

Learn: Effective explanatory writing uses clear sequence, measured evidence, qualification and accessible vocabulary.

Practice and evidence: Submit an original 150-word matter-model explanation with a labeled drawing, a corrected overclaim and rubric: accuracy 6, evidence 5, clarity 5, revision 4.
