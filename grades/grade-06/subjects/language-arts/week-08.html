Week 08 · Language Arts & Literacy

Expressions and Algebraic Thinking

How can symbols represent ideas in math, language, and culture?

Monday: Symbols in writing

Focus: Symbols in writing.

Learn: A symbol can represent an idea or quantity. In algebra, n may stand for an unknown number; in a story, a recurring key might symbolize trust. Readers must distinguish a literal object from an interpreted meaning.

Practice and evidence: Write a short original paragraph using a key as a motif and compare its meaning with the variable k in k+3.

Tuesday: Precision and vocabulary

Focus: Precision and vocabulary.

Learn: An expression such as 4x+2 communicates four groups of x plus two, not four times x plus two times something else. Accurate explanation relies on grammatical and mathematical syntax.

Practice and evidence: Explain the difference between 4x+2 and 4(x+2) in two well-formed sentences.

Wednesday: Read mathematical prose

Focus: Read mathematical prose.

Learn: An original text states: 'Mira made three rows of equal-size planters, then placed two additional pots by the gate.' If each row has p planters, the total is 3p+2.

Practice and evidence: Annotate the text for quantities, define p, and write an evidence-based translation to an expression.

Thursday: Compare interpretations

Focus: Compare interpretations.

Learn: A phrase such as 'three more than twice a number' means 2n+3, not 2(n+3). Parentheses and word order change meaning.

Practice and evidence: Write two contrasting examples and explain how punctuation and grouping affect interpretation.

Friday: Original explanatory essay

Focus: Original explanatory essay.

Learn: Strong explanations pair a claim about meaning with accurate symbols, textual evidence and a revision.

Practice and evidence: Write a 150-word guide to translating a fictional garden story into two expressions, with a small glossary and a corrected ambiguity. Rubric: interpretation 6, syntax 5, evidence 5, revision 4.
