Week 19 · Language Arts & Literacy

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation

How does light shape what we see and how we interpret media?

Monday: Read images critically

Focus: Read images critically.

Learn: A photograph or diagram communicates selected information through framing, labels, and choices. A visual source can establish visible features without proving a photographer's intention.

Practice and evidence: Annotate a clearly fictional image description, separating three observations from two inferences.

Tuesday: Interpret a ray diagram

Focus: Interpret a ray diagram.

Learn: An idealized ray diagram uses arrows to represent paths of light. Captions should identify the incoming ray, reflected ray and normal line rather than assuming readers know the symbols.

Practice and evidence: Write an accurate 70-word caption for a plane-mirror diagram, including the angle convention.

Wednesday: Consider point of view

Focus: Consider point of view.

Learn: Cropping, scale and omitted context can change the impression a visual creates. Comparing claims with their sources is stronger than guessing motives.

Practice and evidence: Compare two fictional captions for one diagram and identify unsupported interpretations.

Thursday: Distinguish evidence from persuasion

Focus: Distinguish evidence from persuasion.

Learn: Visual media may employ color, emphasis and composition to persuade. Such choices do not automatically render the underlying information false.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite an invented poster analysis to distinguish graphic technique from its verifiable factual claims.

Friday: Publish a visual-evidence critique

Focus: Publish a visual-evidence critique.

Learn: An effective critique describes a source, interprets labels accurately, checks a claim, and explains one uncertainty.

Practice and evidence: Submit a labeled ray diagram and a 160-word fictional visual-media critique. Rubric: evidence 6, reasoning 6, clarity 4, revision 4.
