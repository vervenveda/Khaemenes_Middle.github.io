Week 02 · Language Arts & Literacy

Evidence, Annotation, and Ratios

How do evidence and comparison help us understand complex ideas?

Monday: Purposeful annotation

Focus: Purposeful annotation.

Learn: Original reading: 'At sunrise, Eli checked the community garden. The seedlings leaned toward a sunlit window. Eli wrote down the date, described the leaves, and measured growth before sharing observations with the group.' Annotation marks important details, questions, and unfamiliar terms.

Practice and evidence: Underline two details showing Eli uses evidence; add a margin question and an inference clearly labeled as an inference.

Tuesday: Quotation and attribution

Focus: Quotation and attribution.

Learn: A quotation copies the source's exact words within quotation marks. In a written explanation identify the original reading, even when it is supplied by the Academy. Evidence needs an explanation, not just a quoted sentence.

Practice and evidence: Answer: How does Eli work carefully? Embed a short accurate quotation and explain what it demonstrates. Identify its source as the original Week 2 garden passage.

Wednesday: Paraphrase without distortion

Focus: Paraphrase without distortion.

Learn: A paraphrase restates specific meaning in fresh language and still identifies the source. A summary reduces a longer passage to its central idea. Replacing one word is not necessarily a meaningful paraphrase.

Practice and evidence: Paraphrase the sentence about measuring growth without copying its wording, then summarize the entire passage in a single sentence.

Thursday: Evaluate evidence strength

Focus: Evaluate evidence strength.

Learn: Compare 'Eli likes sunny mornings' with 'Eli measured growth before sharing observations.' Only the second claim is directly grounded in the passage. Good reading distinguishes fact, interpretation, and unsupported assumption.

Practice and evidence: Write a three-column chart: directly stated, reasonable inference, unsupported claim. Provide one example of each with an explanation.

Friday: Write and revise an evidence paragraph

Focus: Write and revise an evidence paragraph.

Learn: A strong paragraph identifies a claim, provides accurate quoted or paraphrased details, and explains the relationship. Revision checks attribution, grammar, and whether the evidence actually supports the claim.

Practice and evidence: Write 130–180 words explaining how reliable observation supports learning. Cite two details from the passage, then revise. Rubric 20: claim 4, evidence 6, reasoning 6, revision 4.
