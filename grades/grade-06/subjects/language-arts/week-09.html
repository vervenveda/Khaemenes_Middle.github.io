Week 09 · Language Arts & Literacy

Equations and Balanced Arguments

How do balance and evidence make arguments stronger?

Monday: Claims and reasoning

Focus: Claims and reasoning.

Learn: An argument presents a claim and supports it with reasons and evidence. 'A class should add a reading hour' is a proposal; '18 of 30 survey respondents favor it' is simulated evidence with limits.

Practice and evidence: Underline the claim, reason and evidence in an original fictional library proposal; explain why survey results do not represent everyone.

Tuesday: Check both sides

Focus: Check both sides.

Learn: An argument is balanced when it considers credible counterarguments rather than ignoring them. A schedule change may offer study time but require staffing.

Practice and evidence: Make a claim-counterclaim chart about fictional library hours with two reasons on each side.

Wednesday: Counterexamples matter

Focus: Counterexamples matter.

Learn: A counterexample shows why an overbroad statement can fail. 'Every equation with x has x=2' fails for x+3=6, whose solution is 3.

Practice and evidence: Write two overgeneralized classroom claims, test each with a counterexample, and revise for precision.

Thursday: Connect evidence and conclusions

Focus: Connect evidence and conclusions.

Learn: Evidence supports a specific conclusion within its limits. Distinguish a mathematical proof from a preference survey: checking x+4=9 gives x=5, but a poll does not prove a policy is best.

Practice and evidence: Write an evidence paragraph comparing an exact mathematical verification with a cautious civic recommendation.

Friday: Balanced argument essay

Focus: Balanced argument essay.

Learn: An effective essay includes a defensible claim, two grounded reasons, a fair counterargument, a response, and a limited conclusion.

Practice and evidence: Write a 160-word fictional school-library proposal with labeled claim, support, counterclaim and revision. Rubric 20: argument 6, evidence 5, fairness 5, clarity 4.
