Week 11 · Integrated Research Projects

Statistics: Questions, Data, and Distribution

How can data answer questions while still requiring judgment?

Monday: Frame a community question

Focus: Frame a community question.

Learn: A statistical question anticipates variation. A fictional reading-corner team might ask how many books five invented participants choose in a week.

Practice and evidence: Write the question, the fictional sampling plan and a note explaining why the results are not a real community survey.

Tuesday: Collect and plot simulated data

Focus: Collect and plot simulated data.

Learn: For invented responses 1,2,2,3,7 books, a dot plot shows two observations at 2 and one each at 1,3,7.

Practice and evidence: Draw the plot and verify its five observations against a source table.

Wednesday: Interpret the distribution

Focus: Interpret the distribution.

Learn: The fictional data have median 2, mode 2 and range 6. The value 7 affects the spread but should not be dismissed without reason.

Practice and evidence: Calculate all three summaries and write an accurately qualified observation.

Thursday: Stress-test the findings

Focus: Stress-test the findings.

Learn: A sample of five invented responses cannot determine needs for a whole town. Better evidence would require actual voluntary sampling and a stated population.

Practice and evidence: Draft two cautious recommendations and explain what additional evidence would be needed.

Friday: Defend the data project

Focus: Defend the data project.

Learn: A complete project connects the statistical question, records, charts, arithmetic and honest limits.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional community reading report, dot plot and revision log. Rubric: data 6, analysis 5, representation 5, limitations 4.
