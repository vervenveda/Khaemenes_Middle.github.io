Week 28 · Integrated Research Projects

World Languages and Cultural Communication

How does language carry culture and connection?

Monday: Define a language-access challenge

Focus: Define a language-access challenge.

Learn: A public message should identify its intended readers, needed actions and languages without stereotyping.

Practice and evidence: Invent a fictional community-library welcome problem and identify three user needs.

Tuesday: Analyze ratios without stereotypes

Focus: Analyze ratios without stereotypes.

Learn: Part-to-whole ratios summarize invented response counts and must not be generalized to actual language groups.

Practice and evidence: Using 18 English and 12 other-language fictional requests, simplify the ratio and identify the total.

Wednesday: Prototype multilingual communication

Focus: Prototype multilingual communication.

Learn: An accurate interface needs readable labels, source-checked translations and multiple access paths.

Practice and evidence: Create a bilingual paper sign with simple icons and an accessible plain-language alternative.

Thursday: Test with simulated users

Focus: Test with simulated users.

Learn: Testing should account for limited space, different reading preferences and translation uncertainty.

Practice and evidence: Run three fictional usability cases, record errors and revise the design.

Friday: Present an evidence-backed proposal

Focus: Present an evidence-backed proposal.

Learn: Data, ethical language choices and revisions belong together in the portfolio.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page sign prototype with ratio calculations and source notes. Rubric: mathematics 6, communication 5, usability 5, reflection 4.
