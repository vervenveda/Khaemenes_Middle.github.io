Week 10 · Integrated Research Projects

Inequalities and Decision Rules

How do constraints shape solutions and decisions?

Monday: Define a constraint-led project

Focus: Define a constraint-led project.

Learn: A fictional reading corner has space for at most 20 seats and a budget of at most 100 classroom units. These are independent constraints, not real facility regulations.

Practice and evidence: State s≤20 and b≤100 and sketch a fictional proposal that satisfies both.

Tuesday: Build a decision table

Focus: Build a decision table.

Learn: A possible design with 18 seats and cost 95 meets both rules. A design with 22 seats and cost 90 fails the seat condition even though it meets the budget.

Practice and evidence: Test four fictional designs using two-column yes/no checks and justify each result.

Wednesday: Consider nonnumeric criteria

Focus: Consider nonnumeric criteria.

Learn: A proposal can satisfy a cost inequality and still fail accessibility, comfort or fairness goals. Decision making also needs qualitative evidence.

Practice and evidence: Add three clearly defined nonnumeric criteria and explain who would need to review them.

Thursday: Test edge cases

Focus: Test edge cases.

Learn: At-most constraints include the boundary. Exactly 20 seats and a cost of 100 classroom units satisfy the two mathematical inequalities.

Practice and evidence: Test equal-boundary values plus one above each limit; revise any incorrect acceptance rule.

Friday: Present the design

Focus: Present the design.

Learn: A credible defense explains the constraints, checked candidates, tradeoffs, access choices and evidence limits.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional room plan, candidate table, accessible design note and 120-word defense. Rubric: inequalities 6, testing 5, inclusion 5, explanation 4.
