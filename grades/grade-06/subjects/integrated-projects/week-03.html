Week 03 · Integrated Research Projects

Rates, Unit Rates, and Informational Text

How can rates and text structures explain change?

Monday: Choose a comparable question

Focus: Choose a comparable question.

Learn: A strong investigation asks about rates that can be compared fairly, not raw quantities with mismatched durations.

Practice and evidence: Write a question comparing travel speeds for two fictional community routes; specify distance, time, and units.

Tuesday: Build the evidence table

Focus: Build the evidence table.

Learn: Sample fictional routes: A is 12 km in 3 h; B is 15 km in 3 h; C is 18 km in 6 h. Real travel speeds vary with conditions.

Practice and evidence: Create a three-row data table and label every value as simulated rather than collected.

Wednesday: Compute and graph

Focus: Compute and graph.

Learn: The unit rates are A 4 km/h, B 5 km/h, and C 3 km/h. A bar chart represents the three values; a labeled axis keeps interpretation honest.

Practice and evidence: Show divisions, make an original comparative graph, and write two numerical observations.

Thursday: Assess evidence limits

Focus: Assess evidence limits.

Learn: A simulated table demonstrates calculations, but cannot prove why routes differ or what real-world policies should change.

Practice and evidence: Write two limitations and two additional data types needed before recommending a travel route to families.

Friday: Present and defend

Focus: Present and defend.

Learn: A responsible project links a question, reproducible measurements, calculations, visual evidence, and uncertainty.

Practice and evidence: Produce a one-page report with table, graph, unit-rate conclusion, evidence limitations, and a revision. Rubric 20: question 4, data 5, mathematics 5, communication 4, caveats 2.
