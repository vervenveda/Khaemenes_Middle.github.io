Week 06 · Integrated Research Projects

Rational Numbers and Ancient Number Systems

How have cultures represented numbers and meaning across time?

Monday: Define a representation challenge

Focus: Define a representation challenge.

Learn: A practical design goal is to explain the same quantity in multiple notations without suggesting all cultures use identical rules.

Practice and evidence: Propose a portable paper number-system museum card explaining 12, XII and a signed comparison such as −2 and +2.

Tuesday: Collect evidence responsibly

Focus: Collect evidence responsibly.

Learn: A classroom demonstration must distinguish mathematical definitions from claims about historical dates or people. Historic statements need sources; simulated trade entries should be labeled as simulations.

Practice and evidence: Create a source log with separate columns for verified mathematical conventions, fictional examples and historical details needing research.

Wednesday: Build an accurate model

Focus: Build an accurate model.

Learn: An exhibit needs legible numeral examples and clear definitions of symbols, opposite values and distance from zero.

Practice and evidence: Create an original −4 to +4 number line, add opposites and label |−3|=3 using a descriptive caption.

Thursday: Test for misunderstandings

Focus: Test for misunderstandings.

Learn: A reader may confuse a negative sign with a subtraction operator or assume historical Roman numerals used the modern positional zero. Testing should expose and correct such confusion.

Practice and evidence: Write three visitor questions and revise your exhibit to resolve at least two likely misconceptions.

Friday: Present the exhibit

Focus: Present the exhibit.

Learn: An effective interdisciplinary project demonstrates notation, accurate mathematics, historical caution and accessibility.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page museum exhibit including original number line, symbol chart, visitor guide and revision notes. Rubric: math 6, historical care 5, communication 5, accessibility 4.
