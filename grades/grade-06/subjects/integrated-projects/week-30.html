Week 30 · Integrated Research Projects

Argument, Debate, and Public Speaking

How can disagreement become disciplined learning?

Monday: Choose a public question

Focus: Choose a public question.

Learn: An interdisciplinary debate must have a decision, stakeholders and realistic constraints.

Practice and evidence: Frame a fictional classroom resource-allocation proposal with two opposing options.

Tuesday: Reason with fraction division

Focus: Reason with fraction division.

Learn: Fractions can quantify debate-time sharing, but model assumptions must be explicit.

Practice and evidence: A fictional club has 3 minutes and gives 1/2 minute to each speaker: calculate 6 complete turns.

Wednesday: Build a source and calculation table

Focus: Build a source and calculation table.

Learn: Quantitative results and contextual evidence support different parts of an argument.

Practice and evidence: Create an evidence matrix with one calculation, two sources and a missing-perspective note.

Thursday: Stage a constructive discussion

Focus: Stage a constructive discussion.

Learn: Time limits, accessibility options and accurate restatement help all voices be heard.

Practice and evidence: Design a debate agenda with written-response alternatives and neutral moderator prompts.

Friday: Defend an evidence-backed resolution

Focus: Defend an evidence-backed resolution.

Learn: The final recommendation should acknowledge trade-offs and revisions.

Practice and evidence: Present a one-page debate brief with fraction calculations, sources, counterclaim and reflection. Rubric: math 6, sources 6, fairness 4, revision 4.
