Week 14 · Integrated Research Projects

Matter: Particles, Properties, and Models

How can invisible particle models explain visible properties?

Monday: Frame a material comparison

Focus: Frame a material comparison.

Learn: A fictional community is selecting a material for model containers. Useful criteria include mass, volume, durability and access to resources.

Practice and evidence: Define the design question and construct an explicitly simulated evidence table.

Tuesday: Compare density

Focus: Compare density.

Learn: Fictional sample A has 12 g in 4 cm³ (3 g/cm³); sample B has 10 g in 5 cm³ (2 g/cm³). Density alone does not decide suitability.

Practice and evidence: Calculate both densities, label units and identify the lower modeled density.

Wednesday: Study tradeoffs

Focus: Study tradeoffs.

Learn: A material may be relatively light but less durable; such claims need evidence from material testing, not assumptions based on names.

Practice and evidence: Write a two-sided hypothetical design tradeoff table, marking untested claims.

Thursday: Evaluate source quality

Focus: Evaluate source quality.

Learn: Material properties and historical uses should be verified with appropriate measurements or cited sources before becoming factual claims.

Practice and evidence: Prepare source-verification questions for a proposed community material choice.

Friday: Defend the model

Focus: Defend the model.

Learn: The final design should show clear measurements, computations, material choices, accessibility and limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional materials proposal with table, calculations and revision. Rubric: math 6, evidence 5, fairness 5, clarity 4.
