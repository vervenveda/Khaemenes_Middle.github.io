Week 07 · Integrated Research Projects

Coordinate Planes and Geography

How can coordinates help us describe place, movement, and patterns?

Monday: Frame a spatial question

Focus: Frame a spatial question.

Learn: A strong map project identifies the question, the map scale and the difference between a conceptual grid and real geography.

Practice and evidence: Propose a fictional community-garden map showing where students could place information signs; specify a grid origin.

Tuesday: Collect mapped observations

Focus: Collect mapped observations.

Learn: A location table records point names, ordered pairs and what each symbol represents. Simulated observations should never be presented as field measurements.

Practice and evidence: Record three fictional garden landmarks with coordinates and create a matching legend.

Wednesday: Calculate route distances

Focus: Calculate route distances.

Learn: On a square grid, horizontal and vertical distances can be added along a specified right-angle route. A path from (−2,1) to (1,3) needs 3 units east plus 2 north.

Practice and evidence: Draw that route, calculate 5 grid units, and contrast route distance with direct straight-line distance.

Thursday: Test for usability

Focus: Test for usability.

Learn: A design is incomplete until another reader can identify its origin, orientation, scale and destination without help.

Practice and evidence: Test two map-reading tasks and record one ambiguity, including a non-color visual alternative.

Friday: Defend the final map

Focus: Defend the final map.

Learn: Interdisciplinary evidence connects arithmetic, geographic vocabulary, legend quality and an honest explanation of simulated data.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional garden map, data table, route calculation and revision record. Rubric: coordinates 6, route math 5, accessibility 5, explanation 4.
