Week 26 · Integrated Research Projects

Classical Civilizations and Civic Ideas

How did classical societies influence civic ideas and public life?

Monday: Frame a civic design challenge

Focus: Frame a civic design challenge.

Learn: A public-space proposal requires a clear purpose, stakeholders, resource limits and distinction between historical inspiration and present-day needs.

Practice and evidence: Invent a community gathering-space problem and identify four stakeholder perspectives.

Tuesday: Model space and materials

Focus: Model space and materials.

Learn: Floor area and interior volume answer different questions. A rectangular-prism model needs labeled assumptions and units.

Practice and evidence: Create a 6 m by 4 m by 3 m hypothetical hall model; calculate floor area 24 m² and interior volume 72 m³.

Wednesday: Connect evidence across subjects

Focus: Connect evidence across subjects.

Learn: A defensible proposal uses historical context, mathematical reasoning, user feedback and transparent design choices.

Practice and evidence: Build an evidence matrix linking two historical observations, one measurement and one accessibility requirement.

Thursday: Prototype and critique

Focus: Prototype and critique.

Learn: Peer review can reveal cost, exclusion, safety and interpretive problems missed in a first design.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a labeled floor plan and annotate revisions after feedback from two fictional stakeholder roles.

Friday: Present a civic proposal

Focus: Present a civic proposal.

Learn: Historical examples can inspire inquiry but are not ready-made instructions for modern community life.

Practice and evidence: Deliver a one-page proposal with plan, calculations, two cited context notes and inclusion statement. Rubric: reasoning 6, mathematics 6, evidence 4, reflection 4.
