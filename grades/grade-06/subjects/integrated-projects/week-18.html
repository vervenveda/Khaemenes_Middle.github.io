Week 18 · Integrated Research Projects

Waves, Sound, and Communication

How do waves carry energy and information?

Monday: Define a public alert challenge

Focus: Define a public alert challenge.

Learn: A fictional community requires a notice that can reach people with limited connectivity and different access needs.

Practice and evidence: Specify target audience, possible channels and a verifiable fictional message.

Tuesday: Compare communication channels

Focus: Compare communication channels.

Learn: Paper, audio and offline text each present benefits and limitations in reach, speed, power dependence and access.

Practice and evidence: Build a three-channel fictional decision matrix with criteria.

Wednesday: Check information integrity

Focus: Check information integrity.

Learn: Repeated or rapidly shared claims are not automatically true. A clear alert names its hypothetical source and what should be verified.

Practice and evidence: Create an original sample notice and annotate its missing verification information.

Thursday: Build equivalent formats

Focus: Build equivalent formats.

Learn: A public message should communicate the same essential actions in readable print, spoken script and plain text.

Practice and evidence: Produce three compatible sample formats without making a real emergency claim.

Friday: Present a communication capstone

Focus: Present a communication capstone.

Learn: A reasoned proposal explains channels, accessibility, uncertainties and a realistic verification plan.

Practice and evidence: Submit channel matrix, sample notices and reflection. Rubric: evidence 6, access 6, clarity 4, revision 4.
