Week 04 · Integrated Research Projects

Percent, Proportion, and Civic Decisions

How can percentages help a community make informed decisions?

Monday: Ask a civic question

Focus: Ask a civic question.

Learn: A focused civic inquiry might ask which fictional library improvement respondents prefer. A survey is one source of evidence, not a binding community vote.

Practice and evidence: Formulate a neutral question and define a fictional group of 40 respondents.

Tuesday: Calculate support proportions

Focus: Calculate support proportions.

Learn: Simulated results are: 20 prefer longer hours, 12 prefer more books, and 8 prefer study tables. Compute 50%, 30%, and 20% using each category count over 40.

Practice and evidence: Create a labeled results table and show one fraction-to-percent calculation.

Wednesday: Represent results responsibly

Focus: Represent results responsibly.

Learn: Comparative bar graphs should preserve a fair numerical scale. Include number of responses and explicitly mark simulated data.

Practice and evidence: Draw an original three-category graph and write a text description that remains understandable without the image.

Thursday: Weigh trade-offs

Focus: Weigh trade-offs.

Learn: Preferences do not determine cost, feasibility, access, or fairness. Before recommending a change, additional information may be needed.

Practice and evidence: Make a trade-off chart comparing the three improvements by accessibility, estimated complexity, and unanswered questions.

Friday: Defend an evidence-based proposal

Focus: Defend an evidence-based proposal.

Learn: A responsible proposal includes a clear recommendation, supporting proportions, uncertainty, and a feasible next step.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a fictional library proposal with calculations, source note, graph, and counterargument. Rubric: math 5, evidence 5, equity 5, limitations 5.
