Week 01 · Integrated Research Projects

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: Start with a focused inquiry

Focus: Start with a focused inquiry.

Learn: An inquiry question should be answerable with evidence and narrow enough for a project. 'How can students improve their study space?' invites observation and design. 'Is every classroom good?' is too vague without criteria.

Practice and evidence: Write one inquiry question about a study space or fictional classroom, identify intended users, and list three types of information needed. Do not collect anyone's private information.

Tuesday: Plan and evaluate evidence

Focus: Plan and evaluate evidence.

Learn: Evidence can include measurements, sketches, observation notes, and sourced reading. A single observation does not prove a universal rule. Separate actual data from estimates and simulated examples.

Practice and evidence: Create a source-and-evidence log with columns for claim, evidence, origin, date, and limitations. Add at least one original observation or clearly labeled hypothetical example.

Wednesday: Use math and design together

Focus: Use math and design together.

Learn: A scale drawing represents a larger space proportionally. For example, at a scale of 1 cm to 1 m, a 4 m by 3 m room becomes a 4 cm by 3 cm rectangle. Area is length × width and has square units.

Practice and evidence: Draw a fictional 4 m by 3 m study room to scale and find its 12 m² floor area. Mark a desk and an accessible route, explaining which measurements are assumptions.

Thursday: Revise for accessibility and fairness

Focus: Revise for accessibility and fairness.

Learn: Design choices should account for users with different needs. A tidy room is not automatically accessible; reach, legibility, movement, and noise may affect use. Good revisions respond to evidence, not personal taste alone.

Practice and evidence: Evaluate your sketch against four criteria: clear route, readable labels, safe equipment placement, and adequate workspace. Improve two design decisions and state the trade-offs.

Friday: Present and defend the project

Focus: Present and defend the project.

Learn: A final presentation should answer the inquiry question and show the steps from evidence to conclusion. A limitation is information that the project did not establish; acknowledging one makes a claim more trustworthy.

Practice and evidence: Submit inquiry question, evidence log, labeled scale drawing, and revised proposal. Give a two-minute oral explanation or written equivalent. Rubric (20): question 4, evidence 5, math/model 5, equitable design 4, limitations 2. Sixteen points is portfolio proficiency, not automated mastery.
