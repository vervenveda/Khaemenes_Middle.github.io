Week 19 · Integrated Research Projects

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation

How does light shape what we see and how we interpret media?

Monday: Frame a media-literacy inquiry

Focus: Frame a media-literacy inquiry.

Learn: A fictional town is choosing a public poster layout. The goal is to compare clear evidence and inclusive visual communication.

Practice and evidence: Define a testable design question and two hypothetical audiences.

Tuesday: Inspect visual claims

Focus: Inspect visual claims.

Learn: An image may depict a feature while omitting context; source, date and intended purpose require verification.

Practice and evidence: Annotate two invented posters for visible observations and unsupported claims.

Wednesday: Represent light accurately

Focus: Represent light accurately.

Learn: A labeled ray model can illustrate reflection, but it cannot prove how every real surface behaves.

Practice and evidence: Produce a plane-mirror diagram with a matched 30-degree incident and reflected angle.

Thursday: Evaluate accessibility

Focus: Evaluate accessibility.

Learn: Text descriptions, contrast, readable typography and source statements can make a public design easier to interpret.

Practice and evidence: Revise a fictional poster for narrow-screen and print use.

Friday: Defend the media design

Focus: Defend the media design.

Learn: A strong report compares designs, documents sources, includes science reasoning and describes limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit two original posters, a source checklist and critique. Rubric: evidence 6, science 5, access 5, reflection 4.
