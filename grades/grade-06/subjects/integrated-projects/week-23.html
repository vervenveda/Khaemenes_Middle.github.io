Week 23 · Integrated Research Projects

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns

How can patterns help communities prepare for change?

Monday: Frame a regional weather question

Focus: Frame a regional weather question.

Learn: A fictional community needs a weather-ready classroom activity plan using hypothetical data rather than current alerts.

Practice and evidence: State a question and prepare an imaginary four-day temperature and rainfall table.

Tuesday: Analyze sample observations

Focus: Analyze sample observations.

Learn: Simulated daily temperatures 18,20,22,20 °C average 20 °C; that does not establish a regional climate trend.

Practice and evidence: Calculate the mean and graph the series with dates and units.

Wednesday: Explore risk and access

Focus: Explore risk and access.

Learn: A hypothetical public activity plan should account for shelter, communication methods and mobility needs; real hazards demand official guidance.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional decision matrix comparing indoor and outdoor options.

Thursday: Check evidence limits

Focus: Check evidence limits.

Learn: Forecasts, long-term climate statistics and invented lessons differ as evidence. Do not turn practice data into real-world safety advice.

Practice and evidence: Write a source checklist and correct two unsupported claims.

Friday: Present a community design

Focus: Present a community design.

Learn: A strong project connects dated data, accessible communication, uncertainty and clearly fictional planning.

Practice and evidence: Submit graph, decision matrix and reflection. Rubric: data 6, reasoning 5, safety/access 5, limitations 4.
