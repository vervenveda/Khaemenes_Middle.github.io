Week 29 · Integrated Research Projects

Research Writing: From Question to Thesis

How do researchers build trustworthy explanations?

Monday: Choose one investigable problem

Focus: Choose one investigable problem.

Learn: An interdisciplinary research plan needs a question, measurable elements and contextual sources.

Practice and evidence: Select a fictional library-access problem and identify the relevant readers and stakeholders.

Tuesday: Use percentages correctly

Focus: Use percentages correctly.

Learn: The denominator determines the meaning of every percentage.

Practice and evidence: For a simulated review of 18 valid citations out of 24, calculate 75% and describe what it cannot prove.

Wednesday: Combine numeric and contextual evidence

Focus: Combine numeric and contextual evidence.

Learn: Calculations and interviews answer different questions and need separate credibility tests.

Practice and evidence: Build an evidence matrix with one percentage, two sourced facts and one perspective.

Thursday: Prototype a research presentation

Focus: Prototype a research presentation.

Learn: A clear display labels simulations, sources, uncertainties and accessibility decisions.

Practice and evidence: Create a paper infographic with source notes and receive fictional stakeholder feedback.

Friday: Defend a bounded recommendation

Focus: Defend a bounded recommendation.

Learn: A good recommendation accounts for evidence limits and offers a practical revision.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page proposal with thesis, percentage calculations, citation log and reflection. Rubric: research 6, math 6, access 4, revision 4.
