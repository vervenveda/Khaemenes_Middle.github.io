Week 09 · Integrated Research Projects

Equations and Balanced Arguments

How do balance and evidence make arguments stronger?

Monday: Define a fair proposal

Focus: Define a fair proposal.

Learn: An interdisciplinary project can evaluate fictional extended library hours using data, an equation and arguments. The scenario is simulated rather than a real public vote.

Practice and evidence: Write a fictional policy question and identify stakeholders, costs and missing evidence.

Tuesday: Model a budget constraint

Focus: Model a budget constraint.

Learn: A fictional event has a budget of 50 units; fixed materials cost 20, so x+20=50 gives x=30 units for remaining options.

Practice and evidence: Solve, check and graph the budget division without asserting these represent actual prices.

Wednesday: Collect competing evidence

Focus: Collect competing evidence.

Learn: A balanced evidence table includes supporting data, opposing concerns, source labels and uncertainty. A fictional survey may show 18 of 30 respondents supporting a proposal.

Practice and evidence: Make a two-sided chart and state why the poll cannot represent everyone.

Thursday: Test a recommendation

Focus: Test a recommendation.

Learn: A strong draft invites a counterexample: Will the proposal still work if staffing or access changes?

Practice and evidence: Write two stress-test scenarios and revise your recommendation to account for them.

Friday: Defend the project

Focus: Defend the project.

Learn: A completed defense includes a solvable model, survey interpretation, fair counterargument and a feasible next investigation.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page fictional library proposal with x+20=50, balanced evidence table and revision. Rubric 20: equation 5, sources 5, fairness 6, clarity 4.
