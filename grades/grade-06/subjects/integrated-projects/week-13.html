Week 13 · Integrated Research Projects

Volume and Three-Dimensional Modeling

How can 3D models solve real problems?

Monday: Set a storage challenge

Focus: Set a storage challenge.

Learn: A fictional community classroom needs boxes with modeled internal dimensions. A 3×2×4 cm prism holds 24 cm³ geometrically, ignoring wall thickness.

Practice and evidence: Draw a labeled imaginary storage model and calculate its volume.

Tuesday: Compare alternatives

Focus: Compare alternatives.

Learn: Two boxes 3×2×4 and 6×2×2 cm both have volume 24 cm³ despite different footprints.

Practice and evidence: Create a comparison table and explain two practical differences in shape.

Wednesday: Work with scaled models

Focus: Work with scaled models.

Learn: Doubling each length gives eight times the volume. Models must use consistent units and clearly state whether dimensions are internal or external.

Practice and evidence: Scale the 3×2×4 cm box by a factor of two and verify 192 cm³.

Thursday: Review usability

Focus: Review usability.

Learn: Volume alone cannot determine whether objects fit through openings or whether storage is accessible. Real-world dimensions would need verification.

Practice and evidence: Create a peer test checklist for openings, access and clearly labeled fictional assumptions.

Friday: Present a design defense

Focus: Present a design defense.

Learn: An interdisciplinary design combines correct calculations, drawings, constraints and justified recommendations.

Practice and evidence: Submit two sketches, volume and scaling table, an accessibility note, and a revision. Rubric: math 6, models 5, inclusion 5, explanation 4.
