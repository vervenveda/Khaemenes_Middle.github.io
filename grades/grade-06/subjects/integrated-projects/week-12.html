Week 12 · Integrated Research Projects

Geometry: Area, Surface Area, and Design

How do measurements help designers plan efficient spaces?

Monday: Frame a spatial design task

Focus: Frame a spatial design task.

Learn: A fictional reading garden occupies a rectangle 8 m by 5 m, so its planned area is 40 m².

Practice and evidence: Draw a labeled garden outline, define its intended purpose and explain that the site is fictional.

Tuesday: Divide the space

Focus: Divide the space.

Learn: An invented 2 m by 5 m access path uses 10 m², leaving 30 m² if the regions do not overlap.

Practice and evidence: Make a scale sketch dividing the space into the pathway and remaining area.

Wednesday: Estimate surface materials

Focus: Estimate surface materials.

Learn: If two fictional rectangular signs each measure 1 m by 0.5 m, their combined one-sided area is 1 m².

Practice and evidence: Show each sign-area calculation and distinguish one-sided sign area from total surface area of a solid.

Thursday: Test community usability

Focus: Test community usability.

Learn: A correct calculation does not prove a design is accessible, environmentally suitable or approved for construction.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a critique checklist for paths, labels, maintenance, user feedback and needed real-world verification.

Friday: Defend a coherent design

Focus: Defend a coherent design.

Learn: A final evidence packet links measured diagrams, consistent units, nonoverlapping areas and an honest limits statement.

Practice and evidence: Submit a garden scale plan, area table, fictional proposal and revision. Rubric: calculations 6, diagram 5, inclusion 5, explanation 4.
