Week 26 · World Languages & Global Culture

Classical Civilizations and Civic Ideas

How did classical societies influence civic ideas and public life?

Monday: Words for public life

Focus: Words for public life.

Learn: Terms such as citizen, forum, assembly and law have cultural meanings that do not always transfer word for word. A transliteration is not a translation.

Practice and evidence: Create a bilingual glossary of six civic words in the language being studied, checking meaning and pronunciation against approved class materials.

Tuesday: Formal and informal address

Focus: Formal and informal address.

Learn: Polite requests and titles differ across languages and historical settings; modern phrases should not be attributed to ancient speakers.

Practice and evidence: Write two modern dialogues inviting classmates to a community meeting, one formal and one informal.

Wednesday: Translate with context

Focus: Translate with context.

Learn: False friends and grammatical choices can change what a civic statement means. Explain choices instead of claiming literal equivalence.

Practice and evidence: Annotate three short modern civic sentences, comparing a first translation and a revised one.

Thursday: Listen and confirm meaning

Focus: Listen and confirm meaning.

Learn: Independent listening assesses comprehension beyond memorizing spelling. Use teacher-read or locally recorded modern speech.

Practice and evidence: Listen twice to a teacher-read meeting invitation and write the topic, location and requested action; verify with the script afterward.

Friday: Present an inclusive invitation

Focus: Present an inclusive invitation.

Learn: A public invitation should be comprehensible to language learners and should avoid historically unsupported phrases.

Practice and evidence: Deliver a 45-second modern bilingual civic invitation and a translation note. Rubric: meaning 6, comprehensibility 5, language 5, self-correction 4.
