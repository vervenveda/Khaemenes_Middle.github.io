Week 11 · World Languages & Global Culture

Statistics: Questions, Data, and Distribution

How can data answer questions while still requiring judgment?

Monday: Ask a respectful survey question

Focus: Ask a respectful survey question.

Learn: A survey question should be clear, optional and culturally considerate. Spanish ¿Cuántos? asks how many, while ¿Con qué frecuencia? asks how often.

Practice and evidence: Write an anonymous fictional bilingual question about weekly reading frequency and check the meaning of its words.

Tuesday: Use frequency words

Focus: Use frequency words.

Learn: Spanish siempre means always, a veces means sometimes, and nunca means never. These words are broad categories, not precise counts unless the survey defines them.

Practice and evidence: Create a bilingual response key and explain why a numeric frequency would give a different type of data.

Wednesday: Count and compare

Focus: Count and compare.

Learn: A fictional survey of six responses reports 2 siempre, 3 a veces and 1 nunca. Three is the largest category, but not a majority of six.

Practice and evidence: Draw a labeled bar chart and write two accurate comparison sentences.

Thursday: Protect respondents

Focus: Protect respondents.

Learn: Surveys should avoid collecting unnecessary identifying data or pressuring people to explain personal answers. Translation uncertainty should be noted.

Practice and evidence: Revise an intrusive fictional survey to be voluntary, anonymous and understandable in both languages.

Friday: Bilingual data report

Focus: Bilingual data report.

Learn: A useful report retains the original question, response categories, accurate totals and clear limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional six-response chart, bilingual glossary and five-sentence summary. Rubric 20: translations 5, arithmetic 5, respect 6, clarity 4.
