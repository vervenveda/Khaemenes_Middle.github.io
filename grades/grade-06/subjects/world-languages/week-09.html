Week 09 · World Languages & Global Culture

Equations and Balanced Arguments

How do balance and evidence make arguments stronger?

Monday: Respectful agreement

Focus: Respectful agreement.

Learn: Spanish estoy de acuerdo means I agree; no estoy de acuerdo means I disagree. The statements require context, and polite reasons should address ideas rather than the person.

Practice and evidence: Write a four-line bilingual dialogue about a fictional library schedule, including a reason for each view.

Tuesday: Reasons and evidence

Focus: Reasons and evidence.

Learn: Spanish porque introduces a reason. A reason is not automatically evidence: a fictional poll of 18 of 30 respondents favoring an idea is limited to those respondents.

Practice and evidence: Translate and annotate two short statements using porque, keeping the survey numbers accurate.

Wednesday: Clarify a disagreement

Focus: Clarify a disagreement.

Learn: A good exchange asks questions and paraphrases fairly. Spanish ¿Por qué? asks why, while entiendo means I understand.

Practice and evidence: Compose a six-turn fictional conversation with a clarification question and an accurate summary of the other view.

Thursday: Balanced discussion

Focus: Balanced discussion.

Learn: A disagreement can still produce a shared objective. Avoid translating expressions literally without checking their use in a real setting.

Practice and evidence: Create a bilingual table of two claims, evidence, and a respectful reply. Note one uncertain phrase requiring verification.

Friday: Oral-argument portfolio

Focus: Oral-argument portfolio.

Learn: A respectful exchange includes claims, supporting details, clarification and fair acknowledgment of other opinions.

Practice and evidence: Submit an eight-line bilingual dialogue with glossary and a revision note. Rubric 20: language 5, reasons 5, respect 6, checking 4.
