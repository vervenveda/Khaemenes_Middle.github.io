Week 05 · World Languages & Global Culture

Fraction Division and Procedural Writing

How can models explain division when the numbers are fractions?

Monday: Sequence expressions

Focus: Sequence expressions.

Learn: Instructional language uses sequence words. Spanish primero means first, después means afterward, and finalmente means finally. Words can vary by audience and region.

Practice and evidence: Create a bilingual three-step plan for constructing a paper fraction strip and label every step in order.

Tuesday: Precise classroom directions

Focus: Precise classroom directions.

Learn: Commands must make quantities and materials clear. Spanish divide en cuatro partes iguales means divide into four equal parts. Confirm intended meaning with a reliable dictionary.

Practice and evidence: Illustrate a paper circle split into fourths and write three short directions in English and a selected target language.

Wednesday: Fractions and units

Focus: Fractions and units.

Learn: A fractional quantity needs an object or unit. Spanish un medio is one half and un cuarto is one quarter. Avoid assuming that terms mean the same thing in every context.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a bilingual chart for 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, and 2/3 with visual models and checked vocabulary.

Thursday: Check translation clarity

Focus: Check translation clarity.

Learn: Literal word-for-word translation can conceal meaning. A successful procedure should allow a second reader to reproduce the result.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite an ambiguous fictional direction in both languages, showing what materials, fractions, and actions were omitted.

Friday: Bilingual procedural guide

Focus: Bilingual procedural guide.

Learn: Communication demonstrates precise sequence, accurate quantities, and consideration of the learner who will follow it.

Practice and evidence: Make a five-step bilingual fraction-strip guide and a glossary with source notes. Rubric: sequence 5, vocabulary 5, mathematical meaning 5, clarity/revision 5.
