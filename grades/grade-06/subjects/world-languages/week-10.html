Week 10 · World Languages & Global Culture

Inequalities and Decision Rules

How do constraints shape solutions and decisions?

Monday: Permission and limits

Focus: Permission and limits.

Learn: Spanish se permite means is permitted and no se permite means is not permitted. Rules should state the action, conditions and exceptions clearly.

Practice and evidence: Make a checked bilingual sign for an invented reading room; distinguish permitted activities from prohibited activities.

Tuesday: Compare quantities

Focus: Compare quantities.

Learn: Spanish más que means more than and menos que means less than. 'Fewer than 10' excludes the number 10, whereas '10 or fewer' includes it.

Practice and evidence: Write four bilingual quantity comparisons; illustrate x<10 and x≤10 with boundary examples.

Wednesday: If and then

Focus: If and then.

Learn: Spanish si often introduces if. 'Si hay espacio' means if there is space; a full conditional statement also specifies what follows.

Practice and evidence: Write three bilingual fictional club rules using if-then structure and confirm each intended meaning.

Thursday: Ask for clarification

Focus: Ask for clarification.

Learn: A learner may need to ask whether a limit is inclusive. Clear communication matters more than a guessed translation.

Practice and evidence: Create a six-turn bilingual conversation asking whether a fictional event permits exactly 20 attendees.

Friday: Inclusive language portfolio

Focus: Inclusive language portfolio.

Learn: An effective bilingual rule guide conveys permission, prohibition, conditions and the meaning of boundaries without invented legal claims.

Practice and evidence: Submit a bilingual sign and 8-line dialogue with glossary and verification note. Rubric: accuracy 6, conditions 5, respect 5, revision 4.
