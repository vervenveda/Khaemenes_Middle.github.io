Week 01 · World Languages & Global Culture

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: Greetings across languages

Focus: Greetings across languages.

Learn: Languages express greetings differently depending on context, time, and familiarity. English 'hello,' Spanish 'hola,' French 'bonjour,' and Arabic 'مرحبا' are examples, but pronunciation and social use vary. Respectful communication includes checking how a person prefers to be addressed.

Practice and evidence: Make a four-language greeting chart: language, greeting, pronunciation source if available, and setting. Practice a polite self-introduction in one selected language. Do not assume all speakers use the same phrase.

Tuesday: Names and introductions

Focus: Names and introductions.

Learn: An introduction can communicate a name, a question, and a polite response. In Spanish, 'Me llamo Ana' means 'My name is Ana'; '¿Cómo te llamas?' asks a familiar person their name. 'Mucho gusto' expresses pleasure at meeting someone. Forms and politeness rules differ across communities.

Practice and evidence: Write a four-line fictional exchange in Spanish and English using those phrases. Highlight which phrase is a question. A learner choosing another language may prepare a parallel exchange with checked vocabulary.

Wednesday: Listening and sound patterns

Focus: Listening and sound patterns.

Learn: Language learning includes listening for syllables, stress, and meaning, not just copying written words. Spanish vowels usually correspond to relatively stable sounds, while English vowel spelling is less predictable. Accent differences are normal, not mistakes.

Practice and evidence: Divide 'hola,' 'amigo,' and 'familia' into syllables; read them aloud slowly and then naturally. Make an optional private audio recording, or demonstrate orally without recording. Compare the role of rhythm in English and Spanish.

Thursday: Language, culture, and context

Focus: Language, culture, and context.

Learn: A phrase has meaning in a social situation. 'Buenos días' is used as a morning greeting in many Spanish-speaking communities; 'buenas tardes' is used later in the day. Usage varies by place, so a learner should observe context and ask questions rather than stereotype speakers.

Practice and evidence: Create two short situational dialogues, one formal and one informal. Include a respectful question about preferred language. Reflect on why translation is not always word-for-word.

Friday: Language portfolio demonstration

Focus: Language portfolio demonstration.

Learn: A language portfolio should demonstrate comprehension, communication, and cultural humility. A copied list alone cannot demonstrate conversational use. Choose one target language and label any information that you verified with a dictionary or speaker.

Practice and evidence: Perform or write a greeting dialogue of at least six lines, provide a two-column translation, identify four vocabulary terms, and explain one cultural or contextual consideration. Rubric: communication 8, accurate vocabulary 4, comprehension 4, reflection 4; 16/20 is a portfolio target, not the verified examination score.
