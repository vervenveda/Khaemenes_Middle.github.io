Week 08 · World Languages & Global Culture

Expressions and Algebraic Thinking

How can symbols represent ideas in math, language, and culture?

Monday: Words and symbols

Focus: Words and symbols.

Learn: Words, symbols, and gestures can represent related ideas but are not interchangeable. Spanish número means number and símbolo means symbol; a letter such as n can stand for an unspecified quantity.

Practice and evidence: Build a bilingual glossary of number, symbol, variable, and expression; check each target-language term with a dictionary.

Tuesday: Patterned sentences

Focus: Patterned sentences.

Learn: Sentence structure guides meaning. English 'three more than twice n' means 2n+3, not 2(n+3); translations need the same grouping.

Practice and evidence: Write two precise English quantity phrases, their expressions, and a checked translation into a chosen language.

Wednesday: Roots and meaning

Focus: Roots and meaning.

Learn: Related words may share roots while differing in present-day use. Cognates help suggest meaning but need contextual confirmation.

Practice and evidence: Compare English mathematical and Spanish matemático; identify the shared word family and an example sentence for each.

Thursday: Translate instructions responsibly

Focus: Translate instructions responsibly.

Learn: A bilingual learning guide should define symbols, units, and mathematical order before translating a procedure.

Practice and evidence: Create a four-line bilingual dialogue about substituting n=4 in n+3, and explain why its answer is 7.

Friday: Multilingual notation guide

Focus: Multilingual notation guide.

Learn: Reliable communication combines correct mathematical meaning with accessible vocabulary and appropriately qualified translation.

Practice and evidence: Submit a bilingual glossary, two translated expression examples, and a revision note. Rubric: meaning 6, wording 5, notation 5, verification 4.
