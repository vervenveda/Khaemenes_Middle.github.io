Week 07 · World Languages & Global Culture

Coordinate Planes and Geography

How can coordinates help us describe place, movement, and patterns?

Monday: Location vocabulary

Focus: Location vocabulary.

Learn: Spanish norte, sur, este and oeste mean north, south, east and west. A map should establish orientation before any translated instruction.

Practice and evidence: Make a bilingual four-direction compass and verify the meaning of each word with a trusted language reference.

Tuesday: Describe relative positions

Focus: Describe relative positions.

Learn: Spanish a la derecha and a la izquierda indicate to the right and to the left; these depend on the observer's orientation. Coordenadas are coordinates.

Practice and evidence: On a fictional neighborhood grid, label a library at (2,1) and school at (−1,1). Explain their relative positions in English and Spanish.

Wednesday: Create a bilingual legend

Focus: Create a bilingual legend.

Learn: Map legends turn symbols into readable information. A map with unlabeled symbols cannot be followed reliably across languages.

Practice and evidence: Draw three original place icons and include clear bilingual captions for park, school, and library, verifying regional terms.

Thursday: Explain a route

Focus: Explain a route.

Learn: Directions should specify starting place and steps, not simply 'over there.' Coordinate steps can be communicated alongside ordinary place words.

Practice and evidence: Write a five-step bilingual route from (−2,0) to (1,2) using horizontal and vertical movements.

Friday: Map communication portfolio

Focus: Map communication portfolio.

Learn: An accurate bilingual map keeps place words, compass directions, map key and numeric positions consistent.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional map, glossary, and six-sentence bilingual guide. Rubric: vocabulary 5, orientation 5, accurate steps 6, review 4.
