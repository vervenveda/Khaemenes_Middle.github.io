Week 13 · World Languages & Global Culture

Volume and Three-Dimensional Modeling

How can 3D models solve real problems?

Monday: Name three-dimensional forms

Focus: Name three-dimensional forms.

Learn: Spanish cubo means cube and prisma rectangular means rectangular prism. A label must include measurements to communicate an object's size.

Practice and evidence: Draw and label two fictional solids in English and Spanish, with their three dimensions.

Tuesday: Discuss volume units

Focus: Discuss volume units.

Learn: Spanish volumen is volume and centímetro cúbico means cubic centimeter. A 2×3×4 cm box has volume 24 cm³.

Practice and evidence: Make bilingual captions for a 2×3×4 cm model and show the multiplication.

Wednesday: Distinguish size and capacity

Focus: Distinguish size and capacity.

Learn: Spanish capacidad is capacity. External volume and usable internal capacity differ when a container has walls.

Practice and evidence: Write four bilingual comparison sentences and explain one translation ambiguity.

Thursday: Describe proportional models

Focus: Describe proportional models.

Learn: Doubling all three dimensions multiplies volume by eight. Numbers and units must retain their meaning across translation.

Practice and evidence: Compare 1×2×3 and 2×4×6 cm prisms; label both with bilingual descriptions.

Friday: Build a bilingual model guide

Focus: Build a bilingual model guide.

Learn: Clear instructions combine shape vocabulary, correct units, a labeled drawing and checked meaning.

Practice and evidence: Submit a bilingual model poster with glossary, calculation and reflection. Rubric: language 6, math 6, labels 4, verification 4.
