Week 15 · World Languages & Global Culture

Thermal Energy and Phase Change

How does energy explain changes in matter?

Monday: Temperature words

Focus: Temperature words.

Learn: Spanish temperatura means temperature, caliente means hot, and frío means cold. A scientific caption should always give a measurement unit rather than rely only on a subjective adjective.

Practice and evidence: Create an English-Spanish weather and temperature glossary, checking accents and meanings.

Tuesday: Describe changing states

Focus: Describe changing states.

Learn: Spanish derretirse refers to melting and congelarse to freezing. These verbs describe phase changes, not every rise or fall in temperature.

Practice and evidence: Write bilingual captions for fictional ice and water diagrams with correct states.

Wednesday: Compare readings

Focus: Compare readings.

Learn: Más caliente means hotter and más frío means colder in common contexts. A fictional sample rising from 2 °C to 6 °C increases by 4 °C.

Practice and evidence: Compare two invented measurements in complete bilingual sentences with degrees Celsius.

Thursday: Read a heating curve

Focus: Read a heating curve.

Learn: A level section of a heating graph means the measured temperature did not change during that interval. It does not always prove a phase change.

Practice and evidence: Annotate a bilingual simulated time-temperature table and distinguish observations from interpretations.

Friday: Bilingual thermal report

Focus: Bilingual thermal report.

Learn: Reliable scientific communication keeps units, graph labels and tentative interpretation consistent between languages.

Practice and evidence: Submit a labeled bilingual graph, glossary and short comparison. Rubric: vocabulary 6, data 5, accuracy 5, revision 4.
