Week 31 · World Languages & Global Culture

Arts, Media, and Message Design

How do artists and media makers shape meaning?

Monday: Read multilingual signs

Focus: Read multilingual signs.

Learn: Public signs vary in language, script, reading direction and register.

Practice and evidence: Document six verified words or phrases for a modern school exhibition in the studied language.

Tuesday: Compare figurative language

Focus: Compare figurative language.

Learn: Idioms and metaphors often resist word-for-word translation.

Practice and evidence: Explain why a fictional slogan may require meaning-based translation.

Wednesday: Write a short caption

Focus: Write a short caption.

Learn: Clear target-language captions should fit the audience, not merely mirror English word order.

Practice and evidence: Write three modern captions using supported vocabulary and check them against class materials.

Thursday: Listen for intended message

Focus: Listen for intended message.

Learn: Independent listening checks what a speaker communicates before revealing transcripts.

Practice and evidence: Listen to a teacher-read exhibition announcement and record place, topic and requested action.

Friday: Present a bilingual exhibit

Focus: Present a bilingual exhibit.

Learn: A useful bilingual presentation combines accurate meaning, understandable delivery and revision.

Practice and evidence: Give a 45-second bilingual exhibit introduction and translation note. Rubric: meaning 6, comprehension 6, clarity 4, revision 4.
