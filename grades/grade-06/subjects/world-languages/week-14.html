Week 14 · World Languages & Global Culture

Matter: Particles, Properties, and Models

How can invisible particle models explain visible properties?

Monday: Describe materials

Focus: Describe materials.

Learn: Spanish madera means wood, metal means metal, and vidrio means glass. Labels describe materials but do not alone show their properties.

Practice and evidence: Create a bilingual glossary for five materials and label an original fictional object.

Tuesday: Compare properties

Focus: Compare properties.

Learn: Spanish duro can describe hard and flexible means flexible. Use measured evidence rather than assuming every sample of a material has identical properties.

Practice and evidence: Write four bilingual comparisons of invented objects, qualifying statements where testing is missing.

Wednesday: Scientific vocabulary

Focus: Scientific vocabulary.

Learn: Spanish masa means mass and volumen means volume; 12 g divided by 4 cm³ gives a modeled density of 3 g/cm³.

Practice and evidence: Caption a simulated material table with bilingual headings, units and calculations.

Thursday: Clarify observations

Focus: Clarify observations.

Learn: A color description or word such as light can be ambiguous. Bilingual scientific reports should identify whether light refers to brightness or mass.

Practice and evidence: Write a brief two-language dialogue asking for clarification about a fictional sample's mass.

Friday: Bilingual material guide

Focus: Bilingual material guide.

Learn: Good scientific communication connects a verified glossary, clear measured properties, an accurate model and translation limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit a bilingual material comparison chart and short explanation. Rubric: vocabulary 6, evidence 5, units 5, verification 4.
