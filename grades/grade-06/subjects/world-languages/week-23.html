Week 23 · World Languages & Global Culture

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns

How can patterns help communities prepare for change?

Monday: Describe weather in context

Focus: Describe weather in context.

Learn: Spanish hace calor means it is hot; hace frío means it is cold. These expressions describe conditions, not long-term regional climate by themselves.

Practice and evidence: Create bilingual weather vocabulary with checked spelling and measurement labels.

Tuesday: Recognize seasons

Focus: Recognize seasons.

Learn: Spanish primavera, verano, otoño and invierno describe seasons. Their local weather patterns vary by hemisphere and region.

Practice and evidence: Draft a respectful two-language comparison of two invented regional weather observations.

Wednesday: Interpret forecasts

Focus: Interpret forecasts.

Learn: A forecast is a prediction with uncertainty, and its time window matters. A translated warning should preserve the original date, probability and instructions.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite a fictional weather forecast into clear bilingual text, retaining all units and caveats.

Thursday: Compare climate data

Focus: Compare climate data.

Learn: A fictional three-day temperature series cannot establish decades-long climate trends. Spanish clima refers to climate and tiempo atmosférico to weather.

Practice and evidence: Create bilingual captions distinguishing a daily chart from long-term climate averages.

Friday: Publish an accessible forecast guide

Focus: Publish an accessible forecast guide.

Learn: Communication works through verified terms, context, dated weather statements and an optional readable transcript.

Practice and evidence: Submit glossary, original dialogue and captioned forecast. Rubric: vocabulary 6, context 5, accuracy 5, revision 4.
