Week 19 · World Languages & Global Culture

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation

How does light shape what we see and how we interpret media?

Monday: Describe visual context

Focus: Describe visual context.

Learn: Spanish luz means light, imagen image, color color, and forma shape. Descriptions should name visible details without guessing motives.

Practice and evidence: Create a checked bilingual glossary and describe an invented geometric poster.

Tuesday: Use shape and direction terms

Focus: Use shape and direction terms.

Learn: Spanish izquierda means left and derecha right. Directions should specify the viewer's perspective.

Practice and evidence: Write bilingual captions for a fictional image with a circle on the left and rectangle on the right.

Wednesday: Explain an optical diagram

Focus: Explain an optical diagram.

Learn: Spanish espejo means mirror and reflejo reflection. A ray diagram is a model, not a photograph of vision.

Practice and evidence: Label an imaginary mirror diagram in two languages and explain its symbols.

Thursday: Evaluate caption bias

Focus: Evaluate caption bias.

Learn: Image framing may suggest an interpretation; translated captions must preserve uncertainty and factual meaning.

Practice and evidence: Compare two hypothetical bilingual headlines and identify unsupported claims.

Friday: Publish a visual glossary

Focus: Publish a visual glossary.

Learn: An accessible bilingual portfolio uses alt-text-like descriptions, verified vocabulary, legible labels, and respectful interpretation.

Practice and evidence: Submit a two-language illustrated glossary and reflection. Rubric: language 6, accuracy 5, access 5, revision 4.
