Week 08 · Health, PE & SEL

Expressions and Algebraic Thinking

How can symbols represent ideas in math, language, and culture?

Monday: Variables for optional choices

Focus: Variables for optional choices.

Learn: A letter may represent a chosen count or duration without implying a health judgment. Let w be minutes of optional walking and r minutes of rest; total time is w+r.

Practice and evidence: Design a fictional flexible activity schedule with w=6 and r=4, then compute its total duration.

Tuesday: Calculate planned repetitions

Focus: Calculate planned repetitions.

Learn: If a fictional routine includes three rounds of x optional arm movements, the total is 3x. Alternatives can replace any activity when needed.

Practice and evidence: Create a table for x=2, 4, 6, labeling the results as planning counts, not fitness scores.

Wednesday: Safe conditional choices

Focus: Safe conditional choices.

Learn: A wellness plan can express alternatives: choose a seated or standing option, stop with discomfort, and change a plan when appropriate.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite an overly rigid fictional activity instruction into an accessible choice sequence.

Thursday: Check the model's limits

Focus: Check the model's limits.

Learn: An expression such as 3x counts repetitions but does not establish intensity, benefit, or suitability. Wellness planning requires context and consent.

Practice and evidence: Identify three things 3x does not tell us about a learner, and propose an inclusive procedure.

Friday: Planning reflection

Focus: Planning reflection.

Learn: Assessment should value safe decision-making, accurate variable interpretation, access, and privacy rather than physical performance.

Practice and evidence: Present a fictional w+r time model, a 3x practice table, and revised accessible routine. Rubric: algebra 5, safety 6, inclusion 5, explanation 4.
