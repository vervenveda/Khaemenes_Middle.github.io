Week 15 · Health, PE & SEL

Thermal Energy and Phase Change

How does energy explain changes in matter?

Monday: Recognize safe boundaries

Focus: Recognize safe boundaries.

Learn: Learners should not be asked to touch hot liquids or extreme temperatures to prove a concept. A diagram or simulated thermometer is a valid alternative.

Practice and evidence: Write safety instructions for a paper-based warming-curve activity.

Tuesday: Observe simulated temperatures

Focus: Observe simulated temperatures.

Learn: A fictional digital temperature record 18,19,21,21 °C gives a gradual increase then plateau, without indicating a medical condition.

Practice and evidence: Graph the invented data and label the measurement and time units.

Wednesday: Practice evidence versus advice

Focus: Practice evidence versus advice.

Learn: Temperature graphs from classroom simulations cannot determine appropriate body-temperature or health recommendations.

Practice and evidence: Identify two unsupported health conclusions and rewrite them as properly limited observations.

Thursday: Design inclusive procedures

Focus: Design inclusive procedures.

Learn: A thermal-energy activity can include reading, graphing, seated discussion and an option to skip physical demonstrations.

Practice and evidence: Create an inclusive procedure with clear instructions and learner choice.

Friday: Safety and reflection

Focus: Safety and reflection.

Learn: Portfolio evidence should reward accurate data interpretation and responsible choices, not physical exposure to heat.

Practice and evidence: Submit a simulated graph and safe activity plan. Rubric: data 6, safety 6, inclusion 4, reflection 4.
