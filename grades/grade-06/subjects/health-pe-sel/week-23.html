Week 23 · Health, PE & SEL

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns

How can patterns help communities prepare for change?

Monday: Read weather information safely

Focus: Read weather information safely.

Learn: Public forecasts can help plan outdoor activities, but a classroom diagram is not a real-time hazard alert.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional choice chart for indoor or outdoor learning without requiring physical activity.

Tuesday: Respect hydration and heat safety

Focus: Respect hydration and heat safety.

Learn: General heat-safety learning should consider shade, water access, rest and local guidance; avoid prescribing individual medical needs.

Practice and evidence: Create an inclusive fictional outdoor lesson safety checklist.

Wednesday: Consider storm warnings

Focus: Consider storm warnings.

Learn: Weather hazards require current official local alerts rather than relying on simulated class data.

Practice and evidence: Explain how a hypothetical class should verify an official warning before a planned activity.

Thursday: Practice group care

Focus: Practice group care.

Learn: Accessibility and learner choice matter when plans change because of weather. A written or seated alternative can be equally valid evidence.

Practice and evidence: Write a respectful fictional communication plan for rescheduling an activity.

Friday: Build a safe learning portfolio

Focus: Build a safe learning portfolio.

Learn: Evidence should document forecast reading and safety decisions without collecting personal health information.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional weather-response plan and reflection. Rubric: evidence 6, safety 6, inclusion 4, clarity 4.
