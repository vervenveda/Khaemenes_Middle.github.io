Week 11 · Health, PE & SEL

Statistics: Questions, Data, and Distribution

How can data answer questions while still requiring judgment?

Monday: Ask a safe statistical question

Focus: Ask a safe statistical question.

Learn: A statistics activity can use invented routine-planning times without collecting students' bodies, medical details or actual activity levels.

Practice and evidence: Formulate a voluntary fictional question: 'How many minutes do five sample routines allot to setup?' and state its unit.

Tuesday: Represent fictional durations

Focus: Represent fictional durations.

Learn: The invented setup durations 2,3,3,4,8 minutes give different observations. A dot plot displays counts and variation.

Practice and evidence: Draw a dot plot with labeled minute values and check that it includes all five entries.

Wednesday: Use summaries carefully

Focus: Use summaries carefully.

Learn: For 2,3,3,4,8, median is 3 minutes and range is 6 minutes. This does not prescribe an appropriate routine for any person.

Practice and evidence: Calculate median and range, stating why a personal choice cannot be determined by the statistics alone.

Thursday: Respect preferences and privacy

Focus: Respect preferences and privacy.

Learn: Voluntary participation and accessible alternatives are essential. Even anonymous small-group data may reveal individuals when categories are too specific.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite a fictional class survey to use made-up durations, avoid personal disclosures, and allow opting out.

Friday: Inclusive statistics reflection

Focus: Inclusive statistics reflection.

Learn: Evidence should highlight mathematical reasoning, safe choices and accessibility, not fitness ranking.

Practice and evidence: Submit the fictional data plot, summaries and a privacy-conscious plan. Rubric: calculations 6, representation 5, safety 5, reflection 4.
