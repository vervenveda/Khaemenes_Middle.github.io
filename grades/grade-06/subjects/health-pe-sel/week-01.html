Week 01 · Health, PE & SEL

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: Build a safe movement plan

Focus: Build a safe movement plan.

Learn: Physical activity can include walking, stretching, play, and sports. Different activities build endurance, strength, flexibility, and coordination. A plan should fit a person's abilities, environment, and needs; there is no single activity appropriate for everyone.

Practice and evidence: Draft a flexible 15-minute beginner movement session with warm-up, activity, and cool-down. Offer a seated alternative. Identify signs to stop, such as pain or dizziness, and seek adult guidance when needed.

Tuesday: Measure effort thoughtfully

Focus: Measure effort thoughtfully.

Learn: The talk test is a simple way to describe moderate effort: a person can generally speak but may not sing comfortably. Breathing and exertion vary. Avoid treating comparisons between classmates as measures of health or worth.

Practice and evidence: Design a private activity log with activity, duration, effort description, and how you felt. Use a hypothetical example if movement is not accessible. Do not require public disclosure of personal health details.

Wednesday: Stress and self-regulation

Focus: Stress and self-regulation.

Learn: Stress can influence concentration and mood. Skills such as choosing a manageable next action, asking for help, taking an appropriate break, and noticing emotions can support self-regulation. A coping strategy is not a substitute for professional support in a crisis.

Practice and evidence: Write a fictional scenario about a learner with too many assignments. Identify stress signals, two supportive strategies, one trusted adult or resource, and a practical next step.

Thursday: Teamwork and inclusion

Focus: Teamwork and inclusion.

Learn: Fair play includes consent, clear rules, respectful communication, and adapting activities for access. A team benefits when members contribute in different ways. Competitive outcomes do not define a student's value.

Practice and evidence: Design a cooperative game for three players, including an accessible seated version, safe play area, role rotation, and a conflict-resolution rule. Explain why each adaptation matters.

Friday: Personal wellness portfolio

Focus: Personal wellness portfolio.

Learn: Healthy routines are flexible and involve movement, rest, hydration, relationships, and manageable work. Students should be able to select safe participation modes without being graded on personal physical characteristics.

Practice and evidence: Submit the movement plan, fictional or private effort log, and cooperative-game design. Explain one adjustment after feedback. Rubric: safety and accessibility 6, planning 5, reflection 5, respectful teamwork 4; 16/20 is an adult-reviewed portfolio target only.
