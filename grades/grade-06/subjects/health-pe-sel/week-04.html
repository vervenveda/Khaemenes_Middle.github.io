Week 04 · Health, PE & SEL

Percent, Proportion, and Civic Decisions

How can percentages help a community make informed decisions?

Monday: Understanding survey samples

Focus: Understanding survey samples.

Learn: A fictional survey of 20 learners finds 12 enjoy walking, 5 enjoy dancing, and 3 prefer stretching. The results describe those who responded, not health status or what everyone should do.

Practice and evidence: Find 60%, 25%, and 15%, then explain why wellness preferences are not a measure of fitness.

Tuesday: Goals and percentages

Focus: Goals and percentages.

Learn: Percent progress compares completed actions with a chosen goal. Finishing 3 of 4 planned optional activities represents 75% of the plan, not a grade on personal health.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional weekly choice log and calculate progress without asking for personal medical information.

Wednesday: Respect in group data

Focus: Respect in group data.

Learn: Health-related information should be private. Group surveys can use anonymous fictional cases, and participation should offer accessible alternatives.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite a fictional survey so it avoids asking for diagnoses, weight, or private health details.

Thursday: Design inclusive options

Focus: Design inclusive options.

Learn: A group may prefer different activities. Fair planning includes accessible and seated alternatives, safe pacing, and flexible choices.

Practice and evidence: Design a cooperative 15-minute fictional activity session with at least three optional roles and two accessibility modifications.

Friday: Wellness decision brief

Focus: Wellness decision brief.

Learn: Numerical data can inform activity options but never justify stereotypes or unsafe participation rules.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a one-page fictional activity recommendation using the 20-person preference survey and its limitations. Rubric: computation 5, safety 6, inclusion 5, reflection 4.
