Week 01 · Science & Engineering

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: States of matter

Focus: States of matter.

Learn: Matter has mass and occupies space. Solid particles remain relatively fixed, liquid particles move past one another, and gas particles move freely through available space.

Practice and evidence: Draw three labeled particle models for solid, liquid, and gas. Explain what each model reveals and what it cannot show.

Tuesday: Measurement and units

Focus: Measurement and units.

Learn: Measure mass in grams and liquid volume in milliliters. Measurements require units and a recorded method. A model and a data table should be labeled.

Practice and evidence: Create a three-row table for simulated samples of 50, 100, and 150 mL water. Clearly label simulated data and explain why 100 without a unit is ambiguous.

Wednesday: Physical changes

Focus: Physical changes.

Learn: Melting turns solid water into liquid water; evaporation changes liquid water into vapor. In a closed system, matter does not disappear during a physical change.

Practice and evidence: Sketch melting ice in a sealed container and predict its total mass before and after. Explain why invisible water vapor is still matter.

Thursday: Fair-test design

Focus: Fair-test design.

Learn: A fair investigation changes one variable while controlling other important conditions. Observations are different from interpretations. Repeated trials test the reliability of results.

Practice and evidence: Design a safe paper-towel absorbency comparison. Identify the independent and dependent variables, three controls, a data table, and a way to repeat trials.

Friday: CER mastery studio

Focus: CER mastery studio.

Learn: A scientific claim answers the question; evidence uses observations or measurements; reasoning connects evidence to scientific principles. A particle model is useful but simplified.

Practice and evidence: Define matter, model, mass, and volume; compare three states; explain one state change using a claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph. Teacher rubric: scientific accuracy 8, models and units 4, investigation 4, reasoning 4. A 16/20 performance result supports portfolio evidence, not automatic verified mastery.
