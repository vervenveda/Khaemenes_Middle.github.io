Week 10 · Science & Engineering

Inequalities and Decision Rules

How do constraints shape solutions and decisions?

Monday: Define acceptable ranges

Focus: Define acceptable ranges.

Learn: Engineering designs work within constraints. A fictional temperature chamber target of 18–22 °C is represented by 18≤T≤22; this is a classroom model, not a universal safety standard.

Practice and evidence: Plot the acceptable interval on a number line, checking T=18, 20, 22, and 23.

Tuesday: Measurements and uncertainty

Focus: Measurements and uncertainty.

Learn: An instrument might round to the nearest whole degree. A recorded reading of 22 °C can represent a range of true values, so thresholds near a boundary deserve caution.

Practice and evidence: Make a simulated table with 17, 18, 20, 22 and 23 °C, identifying which recorded readings meet the fictional design criterion.

Wednesday: Compare proposed designs

Focus: Compare proposed designs.

Learn: Two fictional containers have recorded temperatures 19 °C and 24 °C against a 18–22 °C target. Only the first reported reading fits the target; the evidence does not explain why.

Practice and evidence: Write an evidence comparison and identify two other variables that would need controlling.

Thursday: Test a decision rule

Focus: Test a decision rule.

Learn: A sensor algorithm might flag T<18 or T>22. Test boundary cases to ensure the rule does not wrongly reject acceptable values.

Practice and evidence: Draw a decision flow and test T=17, 18, 22, 23 with the expected result.

Friday: Explain thresholds scientifically

Focus: Explain thresholds scientifically.

Learn: A report defines the target, records simulated measurements, checks inclusive endpoints and acknowledges model limits.

Practice and evidence: Submit a table, number line, decision rule and CER paragraph on the fictional chamber. Rubric: inequalities 6, measurements 5, reasoning 5, limitations 4.
