Week 23 · Science & Engineering

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns

How can patterns help communities prepare for change?

Monday: Define atmospheric patterns

Focus: Define atmospheric patterns.

Learn: Weather involves current temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity and pressure. Climate concerns long-term statistical patterns, not one day's observations.

Practice and evidence: Make a comparison table of daily weather observations versus long-term climate descriptors.

Tuesday: Read short-term data

Focus: Read short-term data.

Learn: A simulated four-day temperature series 18,20,20,22 °C has mean 20 °C and range 4 °C. This short record cannot identify a regional climate.

Practice and evidence: Plot the invented readings and calculate mean and range with units.

Wednesday: Study regional differences

Focus: Study regional differences.

Learn: Latitude, elevation, land and water influences can shape regional patterns, but a two-region comparison needs an appropriately long and representative dataset.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional two-region monthly rainfall chart and identify two possible factors requiring investigation.

Thursday: Interpret hazard uncertainty

Focus: Interpret hazard uncertainty.

Learn: Forecasts express estimates with uncertainty. A paper emergency-planning activity should use official local guidance for real-world decisions, not invented forecast numbers.

Practice and evidence: Create an entirely fictional hazard bulletin and annotate its uncertainty and verification needs.

Friday: Present weather science CER

Focus: Present weather science CER.

Learn: A reasoned explanation links a claim to a labeled time series, distinguishes short from long timescales, and states what the model cannot show.

Practice and evidence: Submit temperature graph, regional comparison and CER paragraph. Rubric: data 6, definitions 5, inference 5, limitations 4.
