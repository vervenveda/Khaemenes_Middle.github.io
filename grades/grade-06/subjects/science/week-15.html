Week 15 · Science & Engineering

Thermal Energy and Phase Change

How does energy explain changes in matter?

Monday: Distinguish heat and temperature

Focus: Distinguish heat and temperature.

Learn: Temperature indicates the thermal state of a system, while thermal energy relates to microscopic motion and interactions. Energy transferred because of a temperature difference is heat.

Practice and evidence: Make a labeled concept map distinguishing temperature, heat transfer and thermal energy.

Tuesday: Model particle motion

Focus: Model particle motion.

Learn: In a solid, particles vibrate around positions; a liquid's particles can move past one another. Particle drawings simplify real interactions and are not microscope images.

Practice and evidence: Draw and annotate simple solid and liquid particle models, identifying one model limitation.

Wednesday: Read a heating curve

Focus: Read a heating curve.

Learn: A classroom simulation may record temperatures 0,2,4,4,4,6 °C at equally spaced intervals. A flat region could be consistent with a phase transition, but the graph alone does not prove material identity.

Practice and evidence: Plot the invented readings with a time axis and identify the flat interval.

Thursday: Explain phase changes

Focus: Explain phase changes.

Learn: Melting and freezing involve solid-liquid transitions. In a pure substance under suitable conditions, energy transfer during a phase transition can occur without a large temperature change.

Practice and evidence: Use a CER organizer to explain a possible warming-curve plateau and name one alternative explanation to test.

Friday: Evaluate safe investigations

Focus: Evaluate safe investigations.

Learn: Investigation design should avoid unsafe heating. Simulated datasets and teacher-supervised safe demonstrations can teach modeling without burners or boiling liquids.

Practice and evidence: Submit a simulation graph, particle models and CER report. Rubric: graph 6, definitions 5, reasoning 5, safety 4.
