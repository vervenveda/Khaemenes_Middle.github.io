Week 19 · Science & Engineering

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation

How does light shape what we see and how we interpret media?

Monday: Describe light as an energy transfer

Focus: Describe light as an energy transfer.

Learn: Light is electromagnetic radiation and can travel through empty space. Simplified straight-ray diagrams often model propagation in uniform media.

Practice and evidence: Draw a source, ray and screen diagram, stating what the model omits.

Tuesday: Explain reflection

Focus: Explain reflection.

Learn: For ideal specular reflection from a plane mirror, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, each measured from the normal.

Practice and evidence: Draw a labeled 30° incident ray and a reflected ray at 30° on the other side of the normal.

Wednesday: Trace seeing an object

Focus: Trace seeing an object.

Learn: Seeing a nonluminous object generally depends on light reaching it and then entering the eye. An opaque object may absorb or reflect some incident light.

Practice and evidence: Make a source-object-eye model and distinguish incoming from reflected light.

Thursday: Compare models and observations

Focus: Compare models and observations.

Learn: A ray drawing illustrates expected geometry but is not experimental proof of a real mirror's quality or a person's visual perception.

Practice and evidence: Write a paper investigation plan varying the modeled incident angle and record predicted reflection angles.

Friday: Prepare a light-science CER

Focus: Prepare a light-science CER.

Learn: Scientific explanation links correctly labeled geometric evidence to a narrow conclusion and describes model limits.

Practice and evidence: Submit two ray diagrams and a CER paragraph. Rubric: rays 6, angle reasoning 5, explanation 5, limitations 4.
