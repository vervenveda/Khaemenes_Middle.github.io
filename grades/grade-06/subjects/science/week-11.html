Week 11 · Science & Engineering

Statistics: Questions, Data, and Distribution

How can data answer questions while still requiring judgment?

Monday: Design measurable questions

Focus: Design measurable questions.

Learn: A scientific statistical question anticipates variation, such as 'How does the measured cooling time vary across trials?' A single measurement cannot show a distribution.

Practice and evidence: Write one measurable statistical question for a fictional cooling trial and identify the measured unit.

Tuesday: Collect repeated observations

Focus: Collect repeated observations.

Learn: A simulated series of cooling times might be 4,5,5,6,10 minutes. All observations need the same measurement method and units.

Practice and evidence: Build a labeled five-trial table and dot plot; identify which value repeats.

Wednesday: Describe variation

Focus: Describe variation.

Learn: In the fictional set 4,5,5,6,10, the median is 5 and the range is 6. The high value 10 is not automatically a faulty reading.

Practice and evidence: Calculate and explain both measures, stating what additional information might clarify the unusually long trial.

Thursday: Separate pattern and noise

Focus: Separate pattern and noise.

Learn: Small samples can show apparent patterns without proving a cause. Different room conditions, measurement timing, and inconsistent setup could influence trials.

Practice and evidence: Propose two controlled improvements for the fictional cooling experiment and predict what the improved data would help test.

Friday: Scientific data portfolio

Focus: Scientific data portfolio.

Learn: An evidence-based explanation includes repeated data, clear visualization, numerical summaries, and limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit a simulated measurement table, dot plot, and CER report. Rubric 20: observation design 5, plotting 5, statistics 6, caution 4.
