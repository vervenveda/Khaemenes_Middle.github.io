Week 09 · Science & Engineering

Equations and Balanced Arguments

How do balance and evidence make arguments stronger?

Monday: Equations in investigations

Focus: Equations in investigations.

Learn: A model can express measurable relationships. In a fictional experiment a sensor records d=3t, where d is distance in meters and t is time in seconds. That equality depends on the assumed constant rate.

Practice and evidence: Create a data table for t=0,1,2,3 and name both variables with units.

Tuesday: Balance and inverse operations

Focus: Balance and inverse operations.

Learn: To find t from 3t=12, divide both sides by 3: t=4. Applying the same valid operation to both sides preserves equality.

Practice and evidence: Show the two equation steps and substitute the result to verify 3(4)=12.

Wednesday: Claim versus measurement

Focus: Claim versus measurement.

Learn: If a measured reading differs from a model prediction, identify the difference without inventing a cause. Three readings do not establish that every situation follows d=3t.

Practice and evidence: Compare fictional measurements 3, 6, 10 meters at t=1,2,3 with model predictions; identify the mismatch.

Thursday: Investigate possible errors

Focus: Investigate possible errors.

Learn: Measurement units, time recording, rounding and uncontrolled conditions can explain differences, but a hypothesis still needs tests.

Practice and evidence: List three plausible reasons for the fictional discrepancy and a controlled follow-up for each.

Friday: Scientific argument

Focus: Scientific argument.

Learn: A careful scientific claim includes exact observations, an equation, a check and limitations.

Practice and evidence: Write a CER report with a d=3t table, the 3t=12 solution, and two limits. Rubric 20: equation 6, evidence 6, interpretation 4, limits 4.
