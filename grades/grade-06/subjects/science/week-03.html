Week 03 · Science & Engineering

Rates, Unit Rates, and Informational Text

How can rates and text structures explain change?

Monday: Rate of change

Focus: Rate of change.

Learn: Rate measures how much a quantity changes per unit time. Simulated seedling heights are 2 cm on Day 0 and 6 cm on Day 4. Average growth rate = (6−2)÷4 = 1 cm/day; actual daily growth could vary.

Practice and evidence: Calculate average growth for a second simulated plant from 3 cm on Day 0 to 9 cm on Day 3. Show units.

Tuesday: Construct a data table

Focus: Construct a data table.

Learn: A data table needs variables, units, and recorded observations. Fictional readings of plant height on Days 0, 2, 4 are 2, 4, 6 cm. These describe a straight trend but only at sampled times.

Practice and evidence: Draw the table, label it simulated, and write one observation and one inference.

Wednesday: Graph and interpret

Focus: Graph and interpret.

Learn: A graph of height (cm) against time (days) places time on the horizontal axis. The slope of a straight line represents rate of change. A line connecting samples is a model, not proof of every intermediate reading.

Practice and evidence: Graph the three simulated measurements and compute the slope. State why the graph cannot establish cause.

Thursday: Compare investigations

Focus: Compare investigations.

Learn: Plant A changes from 2 to 6 cm over four days; Plant B from 3 to 9 cm over three days. Their average rates are 1 and 2 cm/day. Different conditions may affect interpretation.

Practice and evidence: Compare rates in a clear sentence and propose three conditions to control in a future fair test.

Friday: Evidence-supported conclusion

Focus: Evidence-supported conclusion.

Learn: A strong scientific conclusion identifies the question, calculation, evidence, limits, and an achievable follow-up.

Practice and evidence: Write a claim-evidence-reasoning comparison of Plants A and B, show a labeled graph, and propose a controlled experiment. Rubric: units 4, table/graph 6, calculation 5, limitations 5.
