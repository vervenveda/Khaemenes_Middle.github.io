Week 12 · Science & Engineering

Geometry: Area, Surface Area, and Design

How do measurements help designers plan efficient spaces?

Monday: Surface area and structure

Focus: Surface area and structure.

Learn: Surface area is the combined area of a solid's outer faces. A closed cube with edges of 2 cm has surface area 6×(2 cm×2 cm)=24 cm².

Practice and evidence: Draw a labeled cube net, calculate its surface area, and explain what the model does not describe.

Tuesday: Compare shapes fairly

Focus: Compare shapes fairly.

Learn: A cuboid with dimensions 2×2×4 cm has surface area 2(4+8+8)=40 cm². Its exposed area depends on geometry, not merely the longest edge.

Practice and evidence: Calculate the faces and compare the model with a 2 cm cube, observing that the solids have different volumes.

Wednesday: Measurement uncertainty

Focus: Measurement uncertainty.

Learn: If a ruler measurement is approximate, calculated area is also approximate. A reported length of 2 cm does not necessarily mean exactly 2.000 cm.

Practice and evidence: Make a table of measured lengths and assumed uncertainty for a paper model; explain why the calculated area should not claim impossible precision.

Thursday: Test a structure-function idea

Focus: Test a structure-function idea.

Learn: Surface area can affect exposure to the surroundings, but an experiment must control material, mass and environmental conditions before inferring a mechanism.

Practice and evidence: Design a safe fictional comparison of two paper models; name controlled variables and one measurable observation.

Friday: Present a qualified explanation

Focus: Present a qualified explanation.

Learn: Scientific models need clearly stated dimensions, mathematical computations, observations and limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit labeled nets, the two calculations and a CER paragraph explaining a testable question. Rubric: geometry 6, units 5, reasoning 5, limitations 4.
