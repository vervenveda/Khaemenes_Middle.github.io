Week 04 · Science & Engineering

Percent, Proportion, and Civic Decisions

How can percentages help a community make informed decisions?

Monday: Frequency of observations

Focus: Frequency of observations.

Learn: Frequency is the number of times an event occurs. In a simulated 20-day observation log, a particular cloud pattern appears on 5 days, or 25% of entries.

Practice and evidence: Create a 20-row fictional tally summary and calculate the proportion of days with the pattern.

Tuesday: Percent composition

Focus: Percent composition.

Learn: Percent composition compares a part with the whole. A simulated mixture has 15 blue beads and 5 yellow beads; blue represents 15/20 = 75%. Beads model counting, not chemical composition.

Practice and evidence: Calculate both color percentages, verify they total 100%, and explain what a bead model cannot prove about actual matter.

Wednesday: Visual data displays

Focus: Visual data displays.

Learn: Bar charts compare category frequencies; pie charts show part-to-whole proportions. Both require a clear total, labels and disclosure of simulated data.

Practice and evidence: Draw a bar chart for 15 blue and 5 yellow beads, label axes, and add a caption explaining 75% versus 25%.

Thursday: Samples and limitations

Focus: Samples and limitations.

Learn: A small or biased sample cannot establish a universal pattern. Observations must note when and how data were gathered. A percent does not establish a cause.

Practice and evidence: List three limitations of a fictional schoolyard sample and design a more representative observation plan.

Friday: Explain data scientifically

Focus: Explain data scientifically.

Learn: A CER argument states a bounded claim, reports counts or percentages, and reasons from the evidence without inventing causation.

Practice and evidence: Write a CER report on the simulated cloud log and bead model, including correct calculations and two limitations. Rubric: arithmetic 6, labeled display 5, evidence 5, caution 4.
