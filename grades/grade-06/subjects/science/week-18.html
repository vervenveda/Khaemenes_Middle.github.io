Week 18 · Science & Engineering

Waves, Sound, and Communication

How do waves carry energy and information?

Monday: Identify mechanical sound waves

Focus: Identify mechanical sound waves.

Learn: Sound propagates through a material medium via vibrations. Unlike electromagnetic waves, sound cannot travel through a vacuum.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a labeled vibrating source, medium and receiver; identify one model limitation.

Tuesday: Explore frequency

Focus: Explore frequency.

Learn: Frequency counts cycles per second in hertz. A fictional signal with 12 cycles over 3 seconds has frequency 4 Hz.

Practice and evidence: Compute frequency for 12 cycles/3 seconds and 20 cycles/5 seconds, showing units.

Wednesday: Compare amplitude

Focus: Compare amplitude.

Learn: For otherwise similar sound waves, larger pressure oscillations generally correspond to greater sound intensity; amplitude and frequency describe different wave features.

Practice and evidence: Draw two fictional wave traces of equal frequency but different amplitude and explain the distinction.

Thursday: Trace information transfer

Focus: Trace information transfer.

Learn: A microphone can convert sound vibrations into electrical signals; speakers convert electrical signals into vibrations that produce sound.

Practice and evidence: Make a simplified sender-microphone-channel-speaker-listener system diagram and state a limitation.

Friday: Present a scientific CER

Focus: Present a scientific CER.

Learn: Graphs and models can support explanations of observed pattern, but a drawing alone cannot prove measured loudness or hearing outcomes.

Practice and evidence: Submit labeled wave traces, two frequency calculations and a CER paragraph. Rubric: model 6, math 5, reasoning 5, caution 4.
