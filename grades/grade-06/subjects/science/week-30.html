Week 30 · Science & Engineering

Argument, Debate, and Public Speaking

How can disagreement become disciplined learning?

Monday: Separate scientific claims from preference

Focus: Separate scientific claims from preference.

Learn: Scientific claims are testable and are strengthened or weakened by measurements.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite a fictional claim about classroom materials as a testable question.

Tuesday: Identify variables

Focus: Identify variables.

Learn: A fair trial holds major conditions constant while changing one independent variable.

Practice and evidence: Plan a safe paper-airplane-distance comparison with identical launch conditions.

Wednesday: Inspect results honestly

Focus: Inspect results honestly.

Learn: Measurements can vary; a small trial does not prove a universal result.

Practice and evidence: Record a fictional three-trial data table and describe two uncertainties.

Thursday: Debate model limitations

Focus: Debate model limitations.

Learn: Respectful scientific disagreement identifies measurement weaknesses and alternative explanations.

Practice and evidence: Role-play researchers defending two interpretations of simulated data.

Friday: Present a scientific argument

Focus: Present a scientific argument.

Learn: A conclusion distinguishes observation, inference and untested speculation.

Practice and evidence: Present a claim-evidence-reasoning poster with a counterargument. Rubric: method 5, evidence 7, limitations 4, clarity 4.
