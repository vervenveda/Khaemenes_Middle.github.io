Week 06 · Science & Engineering

Rational Numbers and Ancient Number Systems

How have cultures represented numbers and meaning across time?

Monday: Signed temperature

Focus: Signed temperature.

Learn: Temperature change can be positive or negative. Moving from 2 °C to −3 °C is a change of −5 °C; a negative temperature is not the same as a negative amount of heat.

Practice and evidence: Plot 2 and −3 on a Celsius number line, calculate change, and describe the direction.

Tuesday: Compare magnitudes

Focus: Compare magnitudes.

Learn: Absolute value describes distance from zero, not whether a temperature is warmer. |−8|=8 and |5|=5, while −8 °C is colder than 5 °C.

Practice and evidence: Create a comparison table of four fictional temperature readings and order them from coldest to warmest.

Wednesday: Data and time

Focus: Data and time.

Learn: Measurements require units, time, and context. Fictional temperatures at 8:00, 10:00 and 12:00 are −2, 1 and 3 °C; changes are +3 then +2 °C.

Practice and evidence: Create a labeled line graph of the simulated readings; describe the two intervals without asserting a cause.

Thursday: Distinguish change from reading

Focus: Distinguish change from reading.

Learn: A final temperature and its change have different meanings: from −4 to 2 °C is a rise of 6 °C. A rise is not proof of what caused it.

Practice and evidence: Solve three temperature-change problems and explain a possible measurement limitation.

Friday: Scientific evidence brief

Focus: Scientific evidence brief.

Learn: A strong conclusion names the data, explains signed values correctly, and distinguishes measurements from inference.

Practice and evidence: Submit an original number-line temperature diagram, simulated data table, and CER paragraph. Rubric: signed arithmetic 6, graph 5, evidence 5, limitations 4.
