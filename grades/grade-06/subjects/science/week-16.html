Week 16 · Science & Engineering

Forces, Motion, and Systems

How do forces and systems explain motion?

Monday: Build a motion model

Focus: Build a motion model.

Learn: A force is an interaction that can change an object's motion. A simulated distance-time table can establish speed but does not alone measure the force applied.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional table of distances 0,3,6,9 meters at times 0,1,2,3 seconds.

Tuesday: Identify independent variables

Focus: Identify independent variables.

Learn: In a designed investigation, a controlled input may be ramp height; distance traveled is a possible measured outcome. A fair test holds other relevant conditions constant.

Practice and evidence: Design a paper-only investigation varying one fictional ramp height while controlling object and surface.

Wednesday: Calculate and graph speed

Focus: Calculate and graph speed.

Learn: Average speed equals distance divided by elapsed time. A fictional model traveling 12 m in 4 s has average speed 3 m/s.

Practice and evidence: Plot a simulated distance-time graph and calculate its average speed.

Thursday: Interpret competing claims

Focus: Interpret competing claims.

Learn: Equal average speeds do not prove identical forces or motion throughout a trip. Data resolution and model conditions limit interpretation.

Practice and evidence: Compare two fictional tables that both total 12 m in 4 s but differ during intermediate intervals.

Friday: Document a fair-test proposal

Focus: Document a fair-test proposal.

Learn: A safe science report specifies the system boundary, input, outcome, controls, evidence, and limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit a simulated test plan, labeled graph, and CER paragraph. Rubric: design 6, calculations 5, evidence 5, limitations 4.
