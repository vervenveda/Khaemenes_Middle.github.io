Week 05 · Science & Engineering

Fraction Division and Procedural Writing

How can models explain division when the numbers are fractions?

Monday: Replicable experiments

Focus: Replicable experiments.

Learn: A scientific method describes what changes, what is observed, the materials and steps, and how results are recorded. Written procedures should never encourage unsafe or unsupervised experiments.

Practice and evidence: Write a safe paper-only method for comparing simulated paper-strip lengths; identify variable, units and control.

Tuesday: Fractional measurements

Focus: Fractional measurements.

Learn: A measurement of 3/4 cm divided into 1/4 cm intervals contains 3 segments. Fraction strips or a ruler sketch can make the relationship visible.

Practice and evidence: Draw a labeled 3/4 cm strip marked every 1/4 cm and record the quotient in a two-column measurement table.

Wednesday: Precision and units

Focus: Precision and units.

Learn: Every recorded number should identify a quantity and unit. Rounding changes precision and a procedure must specify whether estimates are acceptable.

Practice and evidence: Create a simulated measurement log with 1/2 cm, 3/4 cm and 1 cm strips. Add a unit column and note the instrument's limitations.

Thursday: Find procedural errors

Focus: Find procedural errors.

Learn: If one learner measures the strip from the zero mark and another begins at one centimeter, the records may not be comparable. Reproducibility requires identical instructions.

Practice and evidence: Identify three possible errors in a fictional paper-measurement investigation and rewrite the relevant instructions.

Friday: Report and repeat

Focus: Report and repeat.

Learn: A scientific report includes a question, clearly labeled simulated measurements, a method, results, and limits. A numerical result cannot establish a universal scientific claim.

Practice and evidence: Submit your paper-only procedure, original measurement diagram and a paragraph explaining how an independent learner could repeat it. Rubric: clarity 5, units 5, repeatability 6, limitations 4.
