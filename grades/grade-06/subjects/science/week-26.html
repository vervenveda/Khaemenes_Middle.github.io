Week 26 · Science & Engineering

Classical Civilizations and Civic Ideas

How did classical societies influence civic ideas and public life?

Monday: Materials of public structures

Focus: Materials of public structures.

Learn: Stone, fired clay, wood and metal differ in strength, water response, availability and environmental cost. Archaeological interpretations need physical evidence.

Practice and evidence: Make a property table for three classroom-safe model materials; distinguish observation from assumption.

Tuesday: Design a fair strength test

Focus: Design a fair strength test.

Learn: A fair comparison changes one material variable at a time while holding model size, load placement and testing procedure constant.

Practice and evidence: Plan a paper-column load test using equal dimensions; identify the variable, controls and safety precautions.

Wednesday: Record reliable observations

Focus: Record reliable observations.

Learn: Repeated measurements and consistent units make comparisons useful. A model does not prove how a real ancient building performed.

Practice and evidence: Conduct three gentle tests with classroom materials and record loads in identical units; no heavy objects.

Thursday: Interpret engineering limits

Focus: Interpret engineering limits.

Learn: A successful model can reveal one structural principle but cannot establish a historical construction method without supporting sources.

Practice and evidence: Graph the results, describe variation and name two limitations of the experiment.

Friday: Defend a model-based conclusion

Focus: Defend a model-based conclusion.

Learn: A scientific claim must be supported by measured data and must not turn a classroom simulation into a historical fact.

Practice and evidence: Submit a test table, labeled graph and 120-word claim-evidence-reasoning conclusion. Rubric: method 5, data 6, interpretation 6, limitations 3.
