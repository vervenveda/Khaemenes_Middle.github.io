Week 20 · Science & Engineering

Cells, Systems, and Scientific Models

How do small structures support living systems?

Monday: Understand cells as systems

Focus: Understand cells as systems.

Learn: Living cells have membranes, cytoplasm and processes that sustain life. A plant cell often has a wall and large central vacuole, while animal cells lack cell walls.

Practice and evidence: Draw distinct simplified plant and animal cells with labeled shared and different structures.

Tuesday: Investigate structure and function

Focus: Investigate structure and function.

Learn: The plasma membrane regulates exchange; ribosomes help synthesize proteins; mitochondria perform important energy-processing functions in eukaryotic cells. These systems interact.

Practice and evidence: Create a three-column structure-function-evidence chart for a fictional eukaryotic cell.

Wednesday: Explain microscopy and scale

Focus: Explain microscopy and scale.

Learn: Microscopy can reveal some cellular structures, but resolving smaller features depends on the instrument and preparation. A drawing is not proof of a direct observation.

Practice and evidence: Compare an imagined microscope observation with a textbook-style schematic and note two scale limitations.

Thursday: Use responsible analogy

Focus: Use responsible analogy.

Learn: An analogy of a cell as a factory may clarify interacting processes, but real cells do not have human workers or identical organizational plans.

Practice and evidence: Construct a labeled cell-as-factory analogy and identify two misleading comparisons.

Friday: Present a cell-science CER

Focus: Present a cell-science CER.

Learn: Explanations of cell structures should use trusted established scientific knowledge, clearly distinguish drawings from observations and state what the model cannot prove.

Practice and evidence: Submit two labeled cell models and a CER argument about cell membranes. Rubric: structures 6, functions 6, evidence 4, limits 4.
