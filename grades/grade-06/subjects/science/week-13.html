Week 13 · Science & Engineering

Volume and Three-Dimensional Modeling

How can 3D models solve real problems?

Monday: Measure a rectangular prism

Focus: Measure a rectangular prism.

Learn: The volume of a rectangular prism equals length times width times height. A solid 3×2×4 cm occupies 24 cm³ by this geometric model.

Practice and evidence: Draw a labeled model and calculate cubic centimeters, explaining the units.

Tuesday: Explore fractional edge lengths

Focus: Explore fractional edge lengths.

Learn: A fictional prism with edges 2 cm, 1.5 cm, 3 cm has volume 9 cm³. Fractional dimensions are handled with the same formula.

Practice and evidence: Compute 2×1.5×3 and show how the answer represents nine unit cubes of volume.

Wednesday: Compare volume and capacity

Focus: Compare volume and capacity.

Learn: Internal liquid capacity may differ from external solid volume because container walls take up space. A liter is 1000 cm³, but a geometric exterior estimate is not automatically a measured capacity.

Practice and evidence: Write a model-versus-measurement table and list two quantities requiring real measurements.

Thursday: Use scale with caution

Focus: Use scale with caution.

Learn: If all three edge lengths double, modeled volume increases by 2³=8. Doubling a single edge doubles the volume instead.

Practice and evidence: Compare 2×2×2 and 4×4×4 cm cubes and explain the eightfold change.

Friday: Scientific modeling portfolio

Focus: Scientific modeling portfolio.

Learn: A careful science explanation pairs computed predictions with units, measurement uncertainty and limits of the model.

Practice and evidence: Submit dimension table, two volume calculations and a CER-style explanation. Rubric: model 6, units 5, reasoning 5, limits 4.
