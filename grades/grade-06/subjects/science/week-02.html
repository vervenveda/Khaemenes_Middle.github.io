Week 02 · Science & Engineering

Evidence, Annotation, and Ratios

How do evidence and comparison help us understand complex ideas?

Monday: Observations versus interpretations

Focus: Observations versus interpretations.

Learn: An observation records what a tool or sense detects, while an inference explains what it might mean. Example: a hypothetical beaker reads 24 °C; claiming sunlight caused that temperature needs additional evidence.

Practice and evidence: Sort five statements into observations or inferences. Explain which further measurements could test the claim about sunlight.

Tuesday: Reliable measurement tables

Focus: Reliable measurement tables.

Learn: Data tables include headings, units, conditions, and repeated observations. In fictional measurements at minutes 0, 2, and 4, the temperatures are 20, 22, and 24 °C. These measurements show a trend but do not prove why it happened.

Practice and evidence: Draw an original table of time (min) and temperature (°C), then calculate change in temperature from first to last measurement. State one limitation.

Wednesday: Compare two fictional data sets

Focus: Compare two fictional data sets.

Learn: Consider two simulated cups: Cup A reads 20, 22, 24 °C at minutes 0, 2, 4; Cup B reads 20, 21, 22 °C. A fair test keeps the same thermometer and comparable conditions.

Practice and evidence: Plot both sets on an original graph with labeled axes, compare changes, and propose one environmental variable worth investigating.

Thursday: Design a repeatable fair test

Focus: Design a repeatable fair test.

Learn: A testable question identifies the change and what will be measured. Repeated trials and measurement precision matter. Correlation in a table alone cannot prove cause.

Practice and evidence: Write a safe paper-only investigation plan for measuring cooling water temperatures using simulated data, including hypothesis, controls, units, repetitions, and safety.

Friday: Evidence-based explanation

Focus: Evidence-based explanation.

Learn: Use claim-evidence-reasoning: Claim identifies a pattern, evidence cites the numerical changes, reasoning explains why the measurements support the limited conclusion.

Practice and evidence: Write a CER paragraph comparing Cup A and Cup B, cite two numbers and acknowledge uncertainty. Rubric 20 points: correct data 5, graph 5, reasoning 6, limitations 4.
