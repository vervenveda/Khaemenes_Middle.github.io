Week 14 · Science & Engineering

Matter: Particles, Properties, and Models

How can invisible particle models explain visible properties?

Monday: Observe material properties

Focus: Observe material properties.

Learn: Scientists describe measurable properties such as mass, volume and density. Different materials can share a property, so one measurement alone rarely identifies a substance.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional observations table with mass, volume, appearance and units; label all data simulated.

Tuesday: Model particles

Focus: Model particles.

Learn: Particle diagrams help explain why solids retain shape and why liquids flow. These schematic models simplify real molecular behavior and should not be interpreted as microscopic photographs.

Practice and evidence: Draw two labeled model panels for a solid and liquid, identifying one useful feature and one limitation.

Wednesday: Calculate density

Focus: Calculate density.

Learn: Density is mass divided by volume. A simulated block with mass 12 g and volume 4 cm³ has density 3 g/cm³.

Practice and evidence: Compute densities for fictional blocks 12 g / 4 cm³ and 10 g / 5 cm³, showing units.

Thursday: Compare evidence

Focus: Compare evidence.

Learn: A simulated block A with density 3 g/cm³ and block B with 2 g/cm³ differs in mass per unit volume. Density alone cannot prove material identity.

Practice and evidence: Build a comparison chart and write one supported conclusion plus one claim the data do not establish.

Friday: Present a model-based CER

Focus: Present a model-based CER.

Learn: Claim-evidence-reasoning links observations and calculations to an explanation while noting measurement limitations.

Practice and evidence: Submit two diagrams, a density table and CER paragraph; rubric: model 5, units 5, calculation 6, caution 4.
