Week 14 · Social Studies & Civics

Matter: Particles, Properties, and Models

How can invisible particle models explain visible properties?

Monday: Materials shape choices

Focus: Materials shape choices.

Learn: A community might select wood, stone, clay or metal based on local availability, workability, durability and cost. A material choice needs historical and geographic context.

Practice and evidence: Make a fictional community construction comparison table showing two materials, benefits and limitations.

Tuesday: Consider trade and resources

Focus: Consider trade and resources.

Learn: Raw material access can influence transportation and trading choices, but no single resource determines a society's history.

Practice and evidence: Write two plausible, qualified reasons a fictional settlement might trade for a building material.

Wednesday: Interpret material properties

Focus: Interpret material properties.

Learn: A model storage vessel made of clay may serve different needs from a flexible fabric bag. Design involves material properties and users' priorities.

Practice and evidence: Compare two invented containers by weight, potential durability and intended use, clearly labeling untested assumptions.

Thursday: Verify claims about history

Focus: Verify claims about history.

Learn: An illustration of an ancient tool does not prove where or how it was made. Historical claims require reliable provenance and relevant archaeological or documentary evidence.

Practice and evidence: Draft two fictional source cards and a list of evidence required before an authentic historical conclusion.

Friday: Write a responsible material brief

Focus: Write a responsible material brief.

Learn: A civic design brief should compare materials, supply questions, sustainability and source limits without inventing historical facts.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page fictional community material-selection brief; rubric: comparison 6, context 5, source reasoning 5, clarity 4.
