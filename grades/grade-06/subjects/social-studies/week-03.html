Week 03 · Social Studies & Civics

Rates, Unit Rates, and Informational Text

How can rates and text structures explain change?

Monday: Economic data with units

Focus: Economic data with units.

Learn: A rate such as people per square kilometer compares two measurements. A fictional district has 1,200 residents across 40 km², so its population density is 30 people/km². A rate does not describe where individuals live within the district.

Practice and evidence: Compute the density of a second fictional district with 900 people across 30 km². Compare the two.

Tuesday: Check denominators

Focus: Check denominators.

Learn: A raw total can mislead comparisons when groups differ in size. In fictional towns A and B, 40 and 30 students bicycle to school; if A has 100 students and B has 50, the percentages are 40% and 60%.

Practice and evidence: Explain why raw totals and participation rates give different rankings. Show calculations.

Wednesday: Read geographic graphs

Focus: Read geographic graphs.

Learn: Maps show where events occur; graphs show numerical patterns. Both require headings, a legend, units, a date, and a source. A missing scale or misleading axis may distort comparisons.

Practice and evidence: Design a bar chart of the two fictional towns' cycling percentages. Label the data simulated and explain one misleading graph design to avoid.

Thursday: Interpret data responsibly

Focus: Interpret data responsibly.

Learn: An average or rate cannot explain personal motives. Economic and geographic patterns should be interpreted with context and multiple perspectives.

Practice and evidence: Write a 100-word note comparing the towns and identify two further facts needed before recommending a transport policy.

Friday: Evidence-based policy brief

Focus: Evidence-based policy brief.

Learn: A community decision considers safety, equity, costs, and reliable data. A supported recommendation is conditional when evidence is limited.

Practice and evidence: Draft a one-page fictional safe-cycling brief using a chart and two properly labeled rates. Rubric: calculations 6, source/context 5, policy reasoning 5, caveats 4.
