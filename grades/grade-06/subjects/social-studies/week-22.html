Week 22 · Social Studies & Civics

Earth Systems and Water

How do Earth systems interact and affect human life?

Monday: Consider water and settlement

Focus: Consider water and settlement.

Learn: Communities often locate settlements with attention to water access, terrain, transportation and risk, but actual historical choices require contextual sources.

Practice and evidence: Map a clearly fictional river settlement with wells, farms and higher-ground homes.

Tuesday: Interpret a watershed map

Focus: Interpret a watershed map.

Learn: Watershed boundaries identify land draining toward a shared outlet. A sketch without scale cannot prove distances or flood risk.

Practice and evidence: Draw an invented drainage-basin map with a labeled legend and an explicit not-to-scale notice.

Wednesday: Compare water allocation

Focus: Compare water allocation.

Learn: A fictional community has 100 units of water and considers 40 for homes, 35 for farms and 25 for habitat. These numbers are a policy exercise, not a real allocation.

Practice and evidence: Construct a table and explain two equity or ecological tradeoffs.

Thursday: Examine scarcity and evidence

Focus: Examine scarcity and evidence.

Learn: Water scarcity can concern availability, infrastructure, demand and governance. A fictional dry-season account cannot establish the cause of a real crisis.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a source checklist naming evidence needed before making geographic or historical claims.

Friday: Write a balanced water brief

Focus: Write a balanced water brief.

Learn: A civic proposal should recognize water access, agriculture, ecosystems and uncertainty as well as competing community perspectives.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page fictional watershed policy brief with map. Rubric: geography 6, evidence 5, fairness 5, clarity 4.
