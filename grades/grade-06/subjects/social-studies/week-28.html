Week 28 · Social Studies & Civics

World Languages and Cultural Communication

How does language carry culture and connection?

Monday: Map linguistic diversity

Focus: Map linguistic diversity.

Learn: Language families reflect historical relationships; modern political borders rarely match language boundaries.

Practice and evidence: Label a sourced regional map with multiple languages and an explicit date.

Tuesday: Study contact and borrowing

Focus: Study contact and borrowing.

Learn: Trade, migration and cultural contact can influence vocabulary without making languages identical.

Practice and evidence: Investigate three documented loanwords, keeping dates and etymological uncertainty visible.

Wednesday: Examine language and power

Focus: Examine language and power.

Learn: Official-language choices can affect access to schools, courts and public services.

Practice and evidence: Compare fictional community policies from the perspectives of three language users.

Thursday: Respect endangered languages

Focus: Respect endangered languages.

Learn: Language revitalization is led by communities; outsiders should not claim ownership of cultural knowledge.

Practice and evidence: Analyze a publicly documented revitalization initiative with a source and ethical sharing note.

Friday: Create an inclusive communication exhibit

Focus: Create an inclusive communication exhibit.

Learn: Language history requires reliable sourcing, community context and care about generalizations.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a three-panel exhibit with sources, an access recommendation and reflection. Rubric: context 6, evidence 6, equity 4, clarity 4.
