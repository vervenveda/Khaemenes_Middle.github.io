Week 05 · Social Studies & Civics

Fraction Division and Procedural Writing

How can models explain division when the numbers are fractions?

Monday: Procedures and historical evidence

Focus: Procedures and historical evidence.

Learn: Historical records can describe procedures such as measuring land or distributing grain. Reconstructing one requires its time, place, source and context; a fictional classroom account is not an authentic primary source.

Practice and evidence: Examine this simulated source: 'A village clerk counts baskets before distribution.' Identify what it states and three important facts it does not provide.

Tuesday: Resource shares

Focus: Resource shares.

Learn: A fictional village stores 12 baskets and assigns one-quarter to a reserve, or 3 baskets. A proportional distribution is a mathematical model; it does not prove how any particular historical community acted.

Practice and evidence: Calculate the reserve and the remainder. Draw a four-part diagram and label the story as fictional.

Wednesday: Sequence and cause

Focus: Sequence and cause.

Learn: An order of actions may matter: counting a harvest, recording shares, storing a reserve, then distributing the rest. A historical explanation must distinguish recorded events from supposed motives.

Practice and evidence: Write a four-event fictional timeline and explain one plausible consequence of distributing supplies before checking the inventory.

Thursday: Compare viewpoints

Focus: Compare viewpoints.

Learn: A clerk, worker and household might view distribution differently. Perspectives do not by themselves verify a policy's fairness; missing voices and records matter.

Practice and evidence: Write three short fictional statements about a 12-basket distribution and annotate each perspective and one limitation.

Friday: Evidence-based history brief

Focus: Evidence-based history brief.

Learn: Historical reasoning names the source, reports computations carefully, identifies uncertainty and avoids presenting invented stories as fact.

Practice and evidence: Create a one-page classroom simulation brief with the 12-basket fraction calculation, a sequence diagram, two perspectives and source limitations. Rubric: calculation 5, chronology 5, perspective 5, qualifications 5.
