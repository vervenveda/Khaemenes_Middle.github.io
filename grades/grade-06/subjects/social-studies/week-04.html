Week 04 · Social Studies & Civics

Percent, Proportion, and Civic Decisions

How can percentages help a community make informed decisions?

Monday: Civic survey literacy

Focus: Civic survey literacy.

Learn: A survey records answers from a particular group. In a fictional community poll 36 of 60 respondents prefer an additional crossing, or 60%. The result does not automatically represent every resident.

Practice and evidence: Compute 36/60 as a percentage and write a correctly qualified headline.

Tuesday: Majority and minority

Focus: Majority and minority.

Learn: A simple majority means more than half of the votes counted; voting rules differ by setting. Minority interests still deserve consideration and protection.

Practice and evidence: In a fictional 60-vote council advisory ballot, compare 36 yes, 20 no, and 4 abstentions. Explain why the denominator matters.

Wednesday: Sample size and fairness

Focus: Sample size and fairness.

Learn: A poll of one classroom cannot reliably represent an entire city. Sampling methods, question wording, nonresponse and timing affect interpretation.

Practice and evidence: Draft a neutral survey question about a fictional crosswalk and identify two potential selection biases.

Thursday: Create a civic-data visual

Focus: Create a civic-data visual.

Learn: A graph title, exact total, category labels, source and date let readers evaluate percentages. Cropped axes and missing denominators may mislead.

Practice and evidence: Draw a bar chart of 36 yes, 20 no and 4 abstentions, including raw counts and percentages of all 60 ballots.

Friday: Recommend with safeguards

Focus: Recommend with safeguards.

Learn: Responsible civic reasoning weighs safety, cost, accessibility, competing needs and data quality. Survey results are input to a decision, not the sole authority.

Practice and evidence: Write a one-page fictional crosswalk recommendation citing 60%, one opposing concern and one further piece of evidence required. Rubric: calculations 5, sourcing 5, fairness 5, reasoning 5.
