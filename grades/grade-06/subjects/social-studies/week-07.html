Week 07 · Social Studies & Civics

Coordinate Planes and Geography

How can coordinates help us describe place, movement, and patterns?

Monday: Read maps and legends

Focus: Read maps and legends.

Learn: A map conveys location through a title, orientation, symbols, scale and legend. A classroom coordinate grid is useful for practice but does not replace geographic latitude and longitude.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional town map marking river, market and school. Include north arrow, legend and coordinate grid.

Tuesday: Scale and distance

Focus: Scale and distance.

Learn: Map scale relates diagram distances to real-world distances. On a fictional map with 1 square = 2 kilometers, a three-square straight horizontal path represents 6 kilometers.

Practice and evidence: Calculate distances between (−1,2) and (3,2), then explain that direct-line distance differs from a road route.

Wednesday: Geographic coordinate foundations

Focus: Geographic coordinate foundations.

Learn: Latitude measures position north or south of the equator; longitude measures east or west of the prime meridian. Their degree coordinates are not identical to a flat x-y school grid.

Practice and evidence: Label the equator and prime meridian on a sketch and explain one key difference from a classroom coordinate plane.

Thursday: Human and physical features

Focus: Human and physical features.

Learn: Rivers and hills are physical features; roads and settlements are human features. A map may show relationships without explaining why people live where they do.

Practice and evidence: Annotate a fictional map with three physical and three human features and identify one correlation that would need further evidence.

Friday: Defend a map-based explanation

Focus: Defend a map-based explanation.

Learn: A responsible geographic argument explains the map source, legend, scale, measured relationships, and the limits of the representation.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a one-page town map and short explanatory report comparing two locations with exact distance, map evidence and uncertainty. Rubric: symbols 5, scale 6, geographic reasoning 5, sourcing 4.
