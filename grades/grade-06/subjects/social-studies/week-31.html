Week 31 · Social Studies & Civics

Arts, Media, and Message Design

How do artists and media makers shape meaning?

Monday: Study public images in context

Focus: Study public images in context.

Learn: Civic art and public monuments express selected political and cultural viewpoints.

Practice and evidence: Describe one documented civic artifact using a source, date and stated uncertainty.

Tuesday: Compare messages and audiences

Focus: Compare messages and audiences.

Learn: Public imagery may aim to persuade different groups in different historical settings.

Practice and evidence: Build a sourced comparison chart for two civic messages from distinct contexts.

Wednesday: Consider absent voices

Focus: Consider absent voices.

Learn: Official public art does not necessarily describe every resident's experience.

Practice and evidence: Write questions about audiences excluded from a historical civic depiction.

Thursday: Check historical reconstruction

Focus: Check historical reconstruction.

Learn: Modern images of the past can blend evidence and artistic invention.

Practice and evidence: Annotate a hypothetical historical scene as verified, inferred or invented.

Friday: Create a contextual exhibit

Focus: Create a contextual exhibit.

Learn: Ethical exhibitions give provenance and avoid treating civic symbolism as universal.

Practice and evidence: Submit two sourced artifact cards and an inclusion note. Rubric: context 6, sources 6, perspective 4, reflection 4.
