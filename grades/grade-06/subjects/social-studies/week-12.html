Week 12 · Social Studies & Civics

Geometry: Area, Surface Area, and Design

How do measurements help designers plan efficient spaces?

Monday: Maps and built spaces

Focus: Maps and built spaces.

Learn: Plans use geometry and scale to represent spaces. A fictional rectangular public garden 8 m by 5 m covers 40 m²; its size alone does not determine whether it serves a community well.

Practice and evidence: Draw a scaled fictional community space and distinguish measured area from social benefit.

Tuesday: Human needs and design

Focus: Human needs and design.

Learn: Communities may consider access, shared use, privacy and maintenance when designing parks or gathering places. A map can show dimensions but not residents' lived experiences.

Practice and evidence: Compare two original fictional layouts with equal area and discuss different access routes.

Wednesday: Understand land-use tradeoffs

Focus: Understand land-use tradeoffs.

Learn: A fictional lot 10 m by 6 m covers 60 m². Reserving a 2 m by 6 m path uses 12 m², leaving 48 m², assuming nonoverlapping rectangular regions.

Practice and evidence: Make a labeled area diagram and explain the benefit and tradeoff of the accessible path.

Thursday: Evaluate evidence and sources

Focus: Evaluate evidence and sources.

Learn: A proposed plan, a resident interview and a finalized policy offer different kinds of evidence. Do not label imagined sources as authentic.

Practice and evidence: Create fictional planning-source cards and list what would need confirmation before claiming a real town followed the plan.

Friday: Make a balanced planning brief

Focus: Make a balanced planning brief.

Learn: A persuasive geography-and-civics proposal combines a scale diagram, correct area, accessibility questions and fair treatment of alternative needs.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page fictional shared-space plan with calculated area and a balanced rationale. Rubric: scale 5, mathematics 6, civic reasoning 5, source honesty 4.
