Week 19 · Social Studies & Civics

Light, Vision, and Media Interpretation

How does light shape what we see and how we interpret media?

Monday: Identify visual-source context

Focus: Identify visual-source context.

Learn: A historical image must be interpreted with its creator, date, location, purpose and preservation history when available. A cropped image alone rarely establishes the entire event.

Practice and evidence: Create a source-analysis card for a clearly fictional historical illustration, marking unknown fields.

Tuesday: Read map design choices

Focus: Read map design choices.

Learn: A map's legend, projection, scale and selected boundaries affect interpretation. A diagram without scale cannot establish true travel distance.

Practice and evidence: Annotate a fictional map with legend, title, scale status and one possible visual misreading.

Wednesday: Distinguish persuasion from evidence

Focus: Distinguish persuasion from evidence.

Learn: Propaganda and other persuasive images may rely on symbols, emotional appeals and omission; analysis should identify techniques without assuming every claim is false.

Practice and evidence: Analyze a made-up civic poster, distinguishing its persuasive features and testable assertions.

Thursday: Corroborate visual claims

Focus: Corroborate visual claims.

Learn: A single image may support a claim about what it depicts but not the author's motive or all wider circumstances. Multiple relevant sources can clarify context.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a checklist of independent evidence needed to confirm two fictional image captions.

Friday: Write a media-literacy civic brief

Focus: Write a media-literacy civic brief.

Learn: A balanced civic analysis combines careful image description, context, limitations and clear source labels.

Practice and evidence: Submit two visual-source cards and a 150-word comparison. Rubric: sourcing 6, interpretation 5, fairness 5, clarity 4.
