Week 09 · Social Studies & Civics

Equations and Balanced Arguments

How do balance and evidence make arguments stronger?

Monday: Compare civic proposals

Focus: Compare civic proposals.

Learn: An evidence-based civic argument weighs benefits and trade-offs. In a fictional town, residents discuss extending library hours; cost, accessibility and participation matter.

Practice and evidence: Write a balanced claim chart with two benefits and two concerns; identify what evidence would be needed.

Tuesday: Interpret a poll

Focus: Interpret a poll.

Learn: A fictional survey has 18 supporters and 12 opponents among 30 respondents. Those totals equal 60% and 40% of respondents, not all residents.

Practice and evidence: Compute both percentages, state the denominator and identify a possible selection bias.

Wednesday: Consider competing sources

Focus: Consider competing sources.

Learn: A student opinion letter and a town budget memo have different purposes. Neither should automatically be dismissed, but each needs source context.

Practice and evidence: Create two clearly fictional source cards, annotate perspective, strengths and missing evidence.

Thursday: Reason through priorities

Focus: Reason through priorities.

Learn: Public decisions cannot be resolved only by a simple equation: staffing, safety, budgets and unequal access all affect the recommendation.

Practice and evidence: Draft an evidence table showing a numerical consideration, a nonnumerical consideration and one unanswered question.

Friday: Civic evidence brief

Focus: Civic evidence brief.

Learn: A strong conclusion states a proposal, accurately interprets multiple viewpoints and acknowledges uncertainty.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a fictional 1-page library-hours policy brief with the survey figures, fair counterargument and follow-up evidence request. Rubric: numeracy 5, sources 5, fairness 6, writing 4.
