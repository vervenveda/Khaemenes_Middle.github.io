Week 15 · Social Studies & Civics

Thermal Energy and Phase Change

How does energy explain changes in matter?

Monday: Heat control in communities

Focus: Heat control in communities.

Learn: People have developed different shelter, clothing and food-storage methods in response to climate and local resources. A technology should be studied in its own place and time rather than attributed universally.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional community comparison chart linking two climate conditions and possible heat-management needs.

Tuesday: Storage and phase change

Focus: Storage and phase change.

Learn: Cooling and freezing technologies can change food preservation possibilities, but their adoption depends on infrastructure, access, costs and historical context.

Practice and evidence: Write a qualified explanation of how reliable refrigeration might affect an invented town's food distribution.

Wednesday: Interpret energy-use data

Focus: Interpret energy-use data.

Learn: A fictional community table shows 100, 120 and 90 energy units over three months. This describes modeled use and does not establish why demand changed.

Practice and evidence: Graph the simulated values and distinguish two observations from two hypotheses.

Thursday: Examine tradeoffs

Focus: Examine tradeoffs.

Learn: Heating or cooling choices can offer comfort and preservation while raising questions about affordability, environment and unequal access.

Practice and evidence: Create a balanced decision matrix for two fictional temperature-control options, identifying missing evidence.

Friday: Create a contextual policy brief

Focus: Create a contextual policy brief.

Learn: A responsible civic and historical analysis names its setting, distinguishes simulation from fact and considers multiple community needs.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a one-page fictional community heat-management brief with a graph and two verified-source questions. Rubric: interpretation 6, context 5, fairness 5, sourcing 4.
