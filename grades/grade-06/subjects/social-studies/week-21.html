Week 21 · Social Studies & Civics

Ecosystems and Energy Flow

How do energy and matter move through ecosystems?

Monday: Recognize human-environment interactions

Focus: Recognize human-environment interactions.

Learn: Communities use land and water resources while also affecting habitats. Impacts and priorities depend on the particular place and historical context.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional village resource-use map with labeled fields, river and habitat area.

Tuesday: Compare competing needs

Focus: Compare competing needs.

Learn: A proposed land-use change may affect farming, housing, biodiversity and recreation. Responsible discussion includes more than one stakeholder perspective.

Practice and evidence: Construct a two-sided fictional land-use matrix with benefits and costs.

Wednesday: Interpret environmental data

Focus: Interpret environmental data.

Learn: A simulated wetland table showing 50,40,35 habitat units documents change but does not identify the cause on its own.

Practice and evidence: Graph the values and write one supported conclusion and two unanswered questions.

Thursday: Evaluate source claims

Focus: Evaluate source claims.

Learn: A local plan, scientific study and personal testimony provide different kinds of information. Any authentic claim needs verifiable source details.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional source checklist identifying author, date, methods and possible limitations.

Friday: Write a stewardship brief

Focus: Write a stewardship brief.

Learn: Civic proposals should consider ecosystems, community needs, accessibility and environmental uncertainty without treating simulated data as observed history.

Practice and evidence: Submit a one-page fictional watershed plan with a map and source checklist. Rubric: context 6, evidence 5, fairness 5, clarity 4.
