Week 30 · Social Studies & Civics

Argument, Debate, and Public Speaking

How can disagreement become disciplined learning?

Monday: Describe public deliberation

Focus: Describe public deliberation.

Learn: Civic systems have varied widely in who could speak, vote and exercise authority.

Practice and evidence: Compare fictional assembly rules with a sourced historical example, noting exclusions.

Tuesday: Establish historical context

Focus: Establish historical context.

Learn: Speeches and laws reflect audiences and conditions; translations can change nuance.

Practice and evidence: Create two provenance cards for teacher-provided civic documents.

Wednesday: Include differing stakeholders

Focus: Include differing stakeholders.

Learn: A policy may produce different consequences across groups.

Practice and evidence: Construct a stakeholder chart for a fictional public-space policy.

Thursday: Debate without presentism

Focus: Debate without presentism.

Learn: Modern values may guide discussion, but historical interpretation needs period-specific context.

Practice and evidence: Develop two sourced arguments about an ancient institution and qualify comparisons.

Friday: Convene an evidence-based civic forum

Focus: Convene an evidence-based civic forum.

Learn: A fair forum distinguishes historical findings from modern proposals.

Practice and evidence: Submit a moderated fictional forum transcript with citations and reflection. Rubric: context 6, sources 6, fairness 4, reasoning 4.
