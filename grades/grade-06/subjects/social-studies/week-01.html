Week 01 · Social Studies & Civics

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: Mapping our world

Focus: Mapping our world.

Learn: Geography studies places and relationships between people and environments. A map title tells what is shown, a compass rose identifies direction, a legend explains symbols, and a scale connects map distances to real distances.

Practice and evidence: Draw an original map of an imaginary neighborhood with a title, north arrow, legend, and three landmarks. Write two directions using cardinal directions.

Tuesday: Location and evidence

Focus: Location and evidence.

Learn: Absolute location uses a coordinate or address; relative location describes one place in relation to another. Latitude measures position north or south of the Equator; longitude measures east or west of the prime meridian.

Practice and evidence: On a hand-drawn latitude-longitude grid, mark 0°,0° and 20°N,30°E. Explain why a coordinate is more precise than saying 'near the river'.

Wednesday: Human and physical geography

Focus: Human and physical geography.

Learn: Physical features include rivers, mountains, and climate. Human features include roads, settlements, and buildings. People adapt to environments but can also change them. A river may support transport and farming, but flooding creates risks.

Practice and evidence: Compare two fictional settlement sites: Site A beside a river on low ground and Site B on higher ground farther from water. List two advantages, two risks, and a supported recommendation.

Thursday: Reading maps critically

Focus: Reading maps critically.

Learn: Maps are designed for purposes and cannot show everything. Choices about scale, symbols, categories, and omitted information shape what readers notice. Historical maps are sources whose author, date, and purpose should be considered.

Practice and evidence: Review your Monday map. Identify one detail it omits and how that could affect decisions. Design a second map emphasizing accessibility or flood safety; explain the different purpose.

Friday: Geography and civic responsibility

Focus: Geography and civic responsibility.

Learn: Good civic decisions use evidence, consider people affected, and acknowledge trade-offs. An accurate map is one kind of evidence, not a substitute for listening to a community.

Practice and evidence: Capstone: Draw a fictional community map, propose a location for a public library, and support the decision with three geographic facts and one accessibility concern. Teacher rubric (20): mapping 5, accurate vocabulary 4, evidence 6, trade-offs 5. At least 16/20 meets the portfolio performance target, not the verified formal assessment gate.
