Week 23 · Social Studies & Civics

Weather, Climate, and Human Patterns

How can patterns help communities prepare for change?

Monday: Compare climate and settlement

Focus: Compare climate and settlement.

Learn: Communities adapt to local resources, rainfall, heat and hazards; climate alone does not determine settlement choices or culture.

Practice and evidence: Draw a map of two fictional communities with different water and shelter needs.

Tuesday: Analyze regional water patterns

Focus: Analyze regional water patterns.

Learn: A fictional monthly precipitation table gives 40 mm in Region A and 65 mm in Region B. One month is insufficient to classify either climate.

Practice and evidence: Compare the totals and specify what longer-term records would be required.

Wednesday: Consider hazard resilience

Focus: Consider hazard resilience.

Learn: Flood, storm and heat planning can involve evacuation routes, communication formats and safe public spaces. Real plans require authoritative local instructions.

Practice and evidence: Develop a fictional town preparedness checklist with offline and accessible communication.

Thursday: Weigh policy tradeoffs

Focus: Weigh policy tradeoffs.

Learn: A community decision can involve shelter, affordability, land use and ecological protection. An invented example should not be attributed to a real population.

Practice and evidence: Write a balanced fictional shelter-location comparison including conflicting needs.

Friday: Publish a civic geography brief

Focus: Publish a civic geography brief.

Learn: Responsible analysis uses labeled maps, stated timescales, evidence limits and community perspectives.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional two-community climate-resilience brief with sourced-verification questions. Rubric: geography 6, evidence 5, fairness 5, clarity 4.
