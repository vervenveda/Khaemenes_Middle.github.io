Week 29 · Social Studies & Civics

Research Writing: From Question to Thesis

How do researchers build trustworthy explanations?

Monday: Frame a historical inquiry

Focus: Frame a historical inquiry.

Learn: A research question requires a particular time, place, people and evidence base.

Practice and evidence: Narrow 'How did laws work?' to one historically bounded question.

Tuesday: Check historical provenance

Focus: Check historical provenance.

Learn: A translated primary source differs from a modern textbook overview, and sources reflect their makers' viewpoints.

Practice and evidence: Create provenance cards for two instructor-provided sources.

Wednesday: Seek missing perspectives

Focus: Seek missing perspectives.

Learn: Surviving written records may omit people who lacked access to official archives.

Practice and evidence: Identify an absent stakeholder and frame an ethical research question about them.

Thursday: Compare interpretations

Focus: Compare interpretations.

Learn: Historians may disagree because evidence is incomplete or differently weighted.

Practice and evidence: Write a comparison of two interpretations, separating agreement, disagreement and uncertainty.

Friday: Defend a historical thesis

Focus: Defend a historical thesis.

Learn: Responsible historical argument avoids universal claims and labels speculation.

Practice and evidence: Create a three-source mini-exhibit with thesis, counterpoint and citations. Rubric: context 6, sourcing 6, argument 5, ethics 3.
