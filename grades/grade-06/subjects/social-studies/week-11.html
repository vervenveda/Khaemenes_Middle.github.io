Week 11 · Social Studies & Civics

Statistics: Questions, Data, and Distribution

How can data answer questions while still requiring judgment?

Monday: Understand population and sample

Focus: Understand population and sample.

Learn: A population is the whole group of interest; a sample is the subset observed. A fictional survey of 20 volunteers does not necessarily represent 200 town residents.

Practice and evidence: Define the population and sample for a fictional library-use survey; discuss whether participation was random.

Tuesday: Interpret category counts

Focus: Interpret category counts.

Learn: In a simulated survey, 12 of 20 respondents prefer extended library hours and eight do not. That is 60% and 40% of respondents, not every resident.

Practice and evidence: Draw an original bar chart with a labeled denominator and write one accurate percentage statement.

Wednesday: Identify misleading claims

Focus: Identify misleading claims.

Learn: A headline may hide sampling limits. 'The whole town supports longer hours' is unjustified when only 20 volunteers responded.

Practice and evidence: Rewrite three misleading invented headlines to reflect sample size, uncertainty, and the actual result.

Thursday: Compare two groups fairly

Focus: Compare two groups fairly.

Learn: Group comparisons require consistent questions and denominators. Ten supporters among 20 respondents is 50%; five among 10 is also 50%, despite different counts.

Practice and evidence: Calculate both rates and explain why raw totals alone may mislead.

Friday: Civic data brief

Focus: Civic data brief.

Learn: A careful public argument states the survey source, figures, representation limits and the information still needed.

Practice and evidence: Write a one-page fictional public-library data brief with a bar chart, two qualified findings and a proposed improved sampling method. Rubric: computation 6, sources 5, fairness 5, clarity 4.
