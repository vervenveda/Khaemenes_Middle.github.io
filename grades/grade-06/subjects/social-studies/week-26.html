Week 26 · Social Studies & Civics

Classical Civilizations and Civic Ideas

How did classical societies influence civic ideas and public life?

Monday: Locate diverse classical societies

Focus: Locate diverse classical societies.

Learn: Classical is a period label used differently by scholars; study Greek and Roman civic institutions alongside other contemporaneous societies without declaring one universal model.

Practice and evidence: Build a dated map key and mark Athens, Rome and a selected comparison region; label uncertainties in borders.

Tuesday: Distinguish civic participation

Focus: Distinguish civic participation.

Learn: Participation and legal standing varied with time, city, gender, age, citizenship and status. Athens and Rome cannot be treated as a single system.

Practice and evidence: Create a comparison chart for a selected Athenian period and Roman Republican period, noting who could participate and who could not.

Wednesday: Investigate public institutions

Focus: Investigate public institutions.

Learn: Councils, courts and assemblies handled different functions. Surviving accounts often reflect elite perspectives.

Practice and evidence: For two teacher-provided secondary-source excerpts, identify institution, author perspective and one missing viewpoint.

Thursday: Evaluate civic legacies

Focus: Evaluate civic legacies.

Learn: Modern ideas of civic duty, representation and law have multiple histories; continuity is not the same as direct equivalence.

Practice and evidence: Write a sourced comparison of an ancient institution and a present-day practice, naming two major differences.

Friday: Curate a civic history exhibit

Focus: Curate a civic history exhibit.

Learn: Responsible public history separates verified facts, interpretations and invented illustrations.

Practice and evidence: Submit a three-panel exhibit with chronology, two cited sources, an exclusion/inclusion note and reflection. Rubric: accuracy 7, context 5, evidence 5, ethics 3.
