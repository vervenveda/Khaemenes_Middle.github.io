Week 25 · Social Studies & Civics

Belief Systems, Law, and Culture

How do beliefs and laws shape community life?

Monday: Study laws in historical context

Focus: Study laws in historical context.

Learn: Written law collections can reveal stated social obligations but do not tell us how every case was enforced. The Code of Hammurabi is one historical example requiring contextual study.

Practice and evidence: Create a source card noting author or authority, approximate era, audience, translation and unknowns.

Tuesday: Compare belief and custom

Focus: Compare belief and custom.

Learn: Communities can have diverse religious, ethical and cultural traditions. A source about one group or era cannot describe all its members.

Practice and evidence: Analyze two fictional communities' customs without ranking cultures or asserting universality.

Wednesday: Examine unequal consequences

Focus: Examine unequal consequences.

Learn: Rules may allocate rights and penalties differently by social position. Historical analysis should describe unequal provisions accurately and separately consider present-day fairness.

Practice and evidence: Write a fictional case comparison showing whom a rule protects and whom it disadvantages.

Thursday: Check historical evidence

Focus: Check historical evidence.

Learn: An inscription, modern translation and dramatized story offer different levels of evidence. Translation and surviving-record bias should be noted.

Practice and evidence: Build a source reliability table with provenance, date, interpretation and gaps.

Friday: Write a balanced law brief

Focus: Write a balanced law brief.

Learn: A historical argument needs documented context, precise description of sources and empathy for affected people.

Practice and evidence: Submit a two-source comparison and 150-word legal-culture brief. Rubric: accuracy 6, context 5, fairness 5, sourcing 4.
