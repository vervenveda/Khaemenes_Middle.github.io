Week 18 · Social Studies & Civics

Waves, Sound, and Communication

How do waves carry energy and information?

Monday: Compare communication systems

Focus: Compare communication systems.

Learn: Communities have shared messages through oral traditions, writing, postal networks, broadcast media and digital systems; their uses vary by place, era and resources.

Practice and evidence: Construct a timeline of three communication methods with clearly marked illustrative dates rather than invented historical facts.

Tuesday: Explore access and participation

Focus: Explore access and participation.

Learn: A message can reach different groups unevenly depending on language, literacy, disability access and available infrastructure.

Practice and evidence: Create a fictional town emergency-notice plan with print, audio and plain-language options.

Wednesday: Examine source reliability

Focus: Examine source reliability.

Learn: Rapid transmission does not establish truth. Source identity, corroboration and context are needed for informed public decisions.

Practice and evidence: Compare two invented news alerts and list evidence needed before treating either as factual.

Thursday: Weigh technology tradeoffs

Focus: Weigh technology tradeoffs.

Learn: An internet notice may be fast but fail during a power outage; a printed notice may be slower but can function offline.

Practice and evidence: Build a fictional communication-options matrix considering speed, reach, accessibility and outage resilience.

Friday: Write a civic communication plan

Focus: Write a civic communication plan.

Learn: An effective community proposal names its audience, dissemination channels, source-checking practices and possible access gaps.

Practice and evidence: Submit an inclusive fictional public-alert plan and source checklist. Rubric: reasoning 6, access 5, evidence 5, clarity 4.
