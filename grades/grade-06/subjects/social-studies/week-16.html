Week 16 · Social Studies & Civics

Forces, Motion, and Systems

How do forces and systems explain motion?

Monday: Transportation as a system

Focus: Transportation as a system.

Learn: Transportation depends on routes, vehicles, energy, labor and infrastructure. Its effects on communities vary across place, era and access.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a fictional settlement map with two routes and explain their different users.

Tuesday: Compare modeled journeys

Focus: Compare modeled journeys.

Learn: A fictional travel route of 12 km covered in 3 hours has average speed 4 km/h. A second route of 12 km in 2 hours averages 6 km/h; neither rate proves a particular real historic journey.

Practice and evidence: Create a two-route table and compute both average speeds with units.

Wednesday: Investigate technology and context

Focus: Investigate technology and context.

Learn: Wheels, boats and rail networks have affected transportation opportunities in different historical contexts. Avoid assuming every community followed one path of technological development.

Practice and evidence: Make two hypothetical transport-source cards and identify evidence needed before asserting historical specifics.

Thursday: Weigh transportation tradeoffs

Focus: Weigh transportation tradeoffs.

Learn: A faster route may cost more or limit access. Travel time, environmental effects, safety and affordability all matter in policy.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a balanced fictional town-route decision matrix with two numeric and two nonnumeric criteria.

Friday: Present a systems evidence brief

Focus: Present a systems evidence brief.

Learn: Good civic reasoning integrates accurate speed calculations, map context, competing community needs and clear source labels.

Practice and evidence: Submit a fictional transit brief with route comparison and limitations. Rubric: numeracy 6, social context 5, fairness 5, sources 4.
