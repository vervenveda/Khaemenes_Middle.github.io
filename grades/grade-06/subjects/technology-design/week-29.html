Week 29 · Technology, Design & Tools

Research Writing: From Question to Thesis

How do researchers build trustworthy explanations?

Monday: Organize research files

Focus: Organize research files.

Learn: Consistent descriptive filenames and folders distinguish sources, notes and drafts.

Practice and evidence: Sketch an offline folder layout with dates and version labels.

Tuesday: Track citation metadata

Focus: Track citation metadata.

Learn: Capture source titles, dates, creator and access information without fabricating missing details.

Practice and evidence: Design a four-column citation log with one deliberately incomplete example.

Wednesday: Prevent accidental overwrites

Focus: Prevent accidental overwrites.

Learn: Keep source copies separate from revisions and provide recovery without exposing learner data.

Practice and evidence: Draft an offline backup and revision policy for a student research project.

Thursday: Test credibility and accessibility

Focus: Test credibility and accessibility.

Learn: A readable, keyboard-friendly bibliography helps learners verify sources.

Practice and evidence: Design a 255px-wide citation list and test long links, empty fields and screen-reader labels.

Friday: Deliver a reliable research archive

Focus: Deliver a reliable research archive.

Learn: Ethical archives reveal uncertainties and protect identity and original sources.

Practice and evidence: Submit a three-source offline index with revision history and accessible labels. Rubric: records 6, provenance 6, usability 4, privacy 4.
