Week 14 · Technology, Design & Tools

Matter: Particles, Properties, and Models

How can invisible particle models explain visible properties?

Monday: Store a material record

Focus: Store a material record.

Learn: A simple dataset can describe material name, mass in grams and volume in cm³. Unit consistency is essential for comparisons.

Practice and evidence: Make a table of three clearly fictional samples and their labeled values.

Tuesday: Calculate density

Focus: Calculate density.

Learn: An offline formula density=mass/volume can calculate a numeric model. Simulated values 12 g and 4 cm³ give 3 g/cm³.

Practice and evidence: Write calculator pseudocode and show the sample calculation.

Wednesday: Validate inputs

Focus: Validate inputs.

Learn: Zero volume causes division by zero; missing, negative or nonnumeric entries should produce clear feedback in this classroom model.

Practice and evidence: Write six test cases including zero volume and a positive fractional volume.

Thursday: Create accessible output

Focus: Create accessible output.

Learn: A 255px interface should identify units, avoid color-only status, wrap its labels and make calculations understandable.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a narrow-screen mass-and-volume form with a readable text result and error notice.

Friday: Test the data tool

Focus: Test the data tool.

Learn: A good prototype records expected answers, observed outputs and corrections without pretending simulated data are measured facts.

Practice and evidence: Submit pseudocode, test log and accessible wireframe. Rubric: formula 6, input tests 6, access 5, documentation 3.
