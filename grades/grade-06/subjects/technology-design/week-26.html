Week 26 · Technology, Design & Tools

Classical Civilizations and Civic Ideas

How did classical societies influence civic ideas and public life?

Monday: Describe a reliable archive

Focus: Describe a reliable archive.

Learn: A record needs title, origin, date or date uncertainty, maker if known and a way to distinguish transcription from interpretation.

Practice and evidence: Draft metadata for a fictional assembly inscription, flagging unknown details rather than inventing them.

Tuesday: Model provenance and versions

Focus: Model provenance and versions.

Learn: A digital exhibit should preserve the original record separately from corrected text and new commentary.

Practice and evidence: Draw a provenance flow from artifact or source image to transcription, translation and public summary.

Wednesday: Design accessible civic information

Focus: Design accessible civic information.

Learn: Clear headings, keyboard navigation and sufficient contrast help people use historical archives.

Practice and evidence: Build a paper or offline HTML wireframe for a civic timeline with descriptive labels.

Thursday: Test errors and ambiguity

Focus: Test errors and ambiguity.

Learn: Archive interfaces should warn users when evidence is incomplete, dates disputed or translations uncertain.

Practice and evidence: Test the wireframe with three invented records, including a missing date and conflicting descriptions; record fixes.

Friday: Publish an ethical mini archive

Focus: Publish an ethical mini archive.

Learn: An archive should not expose learner identifiers or present an imaginative reconstruction as authentic history.

Practice and evidence: Submit a three-record offline archive mockup with provenance labels, accessibility notes and privacy check. Rubric: metadata 6, accuracy 6, access 4, privacy 4.
