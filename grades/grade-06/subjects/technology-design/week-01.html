Week 01 · Technology, Design & Tools

Middle School Scholar Launch

How do middle school scholars organize independence, evidence, and responsibility?

Monday: Files, naming, and privacy

Focus: Files, naming, and privacy.

Learn: A file name should help its creator find work later. Descriptive names and dated versions reduce confusion. Personal records deserve extra care: a public website is not an appropriate place for passwords, private addresses, or classmates' information.

Practice and evidence: Propose a folder system with grade, subject, and week folders. Rename three fictional files using descriptive names such as grade06_science_week01_notes.txt. Explain why each is easier to recognize.

Tuesday: Keyboard and accessible navigation

Focus: Keyboard and accessible navigation.

Learn: Keyboard navigation helps users who cannot or prefer not to use a mouse. Tab moves through many controls, Shift+Tab moves backward, and Enter activates focused links and buttons. Accessible design should show a visible focus indicator.

Practice and evidence: On a safe page, navigate using only a keyboard if available. Record whether the focus indicator is visible, whether the reading order makes sense, and one improvement. Offer a touch-only observation alternative.

Wednesday: Algorithms and debugging

Focus: Algorithms and debugging.

Learn: An algorithm is an ordered set of instructions. A condition can change what happens, and a loop repeats steps. Debugging tests whether instructions produce the intended result. Computers follow instructions but do not infer missing directions.

Practice and evidence: Write pseudocode for packing a school bag: check the schedule, add each needed book, then verify nothing is missing. Test with a hypothetical Tuesday schedule. Find and correct one ambiguous instruction.

Thursday: Design for a real learner

Focus: Design for a real learner.

Learn: Good design considers purpose, audience, usability, and constraints. A narrow phone display may require single-column content and controls that fit the screen. Clear labels help prevent errors more effectively than decorative effects.

Practice and evidence: Sketch a low-fidelity 255px-wide lesson portal with heading, reading area, progress notice, and one clearly labeled action. Explain font sizing, tap targets, and how long words wrap.

Friday: Prototype and test

Focus: Prototype and test.

Learn: A prototype is an early testable design, not a finished product. Evaluating a prototype means checking observable tasks, describing failures, and revising before release.

Practice and evidence: Make a paper or offline digital prototype of a study organizer. Ask an adult or peer to try three fictional tasks, or self-test with a checklist. Record three observations and revise one part. Rubric: usability 6, clear algorithm 5, privacy 5, revision 4.
