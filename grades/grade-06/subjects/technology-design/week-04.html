Week 04 · Technology, Design & Tools

Percent, Proportion, and Civic Decisions

How can percentages help a community make informed decisions?

Monday: Collect usable data

Focus: Collect usable data.

Learn: A clean survey table identifies a question, categories, counts, total, and whether entries are fictional. Missing or duplicate responses may affect a percentage.

Practice and evidence: Create a 20-response fictional survey table with 12, 5, and 3 responses; annotate its source.

Tuesday: Percent formulas

Focus: Percent formulas.

Learn: A spreadsheet proportion formula is count divided by total. With a count in B2 and total in B5, =B2/$B$5 gives a reusable fractional result that can be formatted as a percent.

Practice and evidence: Write or simulate formulas for all three categories, and verify 60%, 25%, and 15%.

Wednesday: Make an honest chart

Focus: Make an honest chart.

Learn: A graph needs labeled categories, a clear scale and sample size. Decorative charts must not change numeric proportions or omit zero without explanation.

Practice and evidence: Draw a bar chart starting at zero and supply a short text alternative summarizing each category.

Thursday: Audit survey quality

Focus: Audit survey quality.

Learn: Data design includes privacy, wording and accessible input. A biased survey question or identifiable responses can reduce trust and safety.

Practice and evidence: Redesign a leading fictional survey question and remove a private-information field.

Friday: Test and revise

Focus: Test and revise.

Learn: A successful data interface lets another reader interpret totals, proportions, and source without guessing.

Practice and evidence: Produce a paper or offline dashboard for a 255px-wide screen, test readability and arithmetic, and document one revision. Rubric: data integrity 6, accessibility 5, privacy 5, revision 4.
