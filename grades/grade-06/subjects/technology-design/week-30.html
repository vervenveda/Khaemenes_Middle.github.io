Week 30 · Technology, Design & Tools

Argument, Debate, and Public Speaking

How can disagreement become disciplined learning?

Monday: Trace digital claims

Focus: Trace digital claims.

Learn: Online arguments require distinguishable authorship, dates and source provenance.

Practice and evidence: Create a source log for two fictional web claims.

Tuesday: Spot misleading presentation

Focus: Spot misleading presentation.

Learn: Cropped charts, unsupported headlines and missing denominators can distort evidence.

Practice and evidence: Annotate a fictional chart whose axis is truncated and identify its misleading effect.

Wednesday: Design an accessible debate page

Focus: Design an accessible debate page.

Learn: Semantic headings, labels and keyboard controls help readers evaluate competing claims.

Practice and evidence: Sketch an offline 255px-wide claim-evidence-counterclaim layout.

Thursday: Check misinformation safely

Focus: Check misinformation safely.

Learn: Verification involves consulting evidence and noting uncertainty, not republishing unverified allegations.

Practice and evidence: Prepare a four-step fact-check checklist for an invented claim.

Friday: Publish a transparent argument archive

Focus: Publish a transparent argument archive.

Learn: Privacy and source integrity matter even in a classroom simulation.

Practice and evidence: Submit a three-claim offline debate index with citation and accessibility checks. Rubric: evidence 6, structure 6, access 4, privacy 4.
